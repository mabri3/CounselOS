## Summary

Juniper Ledger is an entirely fictional U.S. financial-technology company created only for this UX experiment. It has no affiliation with any real company, bank, regulator, or person, and its name, facts, and operating details are invented. Juniper Ledger provides a business money-management platform for small and midsize online merchants and professional-services firms. Its business model combines a monthly software subscription with usage fees on payments and account services. Customers are U.S.-based small businesses that need simple cash management, vendor payments, invoicing, and visibility across operating accounts. Products include a web dashboard, business sub-ledger accounts, ACH vendor payments, domestic wires, virtual expense cards, invoice collection, and read-only financial reporting. Juniper Ledger does not itself hold a banking charter. For deposit and payment services it works with fictional sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A., under a program-management and banking-services agreement; Cedar Harbor is the named bank for deposit accounts and payment rails, while Juniper Ledger owns the software, customer experience, and first-line operations. The company operates from Seattle, Washington, with remote staff across the United States and a small operations team in Toronto, Canada; it serves U.S. customers only. Teams include product and engineering, customer operations, payments operations, compliance and risk, finance, security, sales, and a two-person legal function. Key legal topics are bank-partner oversight, money transmission and payments regulation, AML and sanctions controls, KYC and beneficial ownership, privacy and data security, commercial contracts, card-network rules, marketing review, employment, and incident response. Its risk posture is conservative for regulated activity: it uses documented controls, escalation for unusual transactions, least-privilege access, audit logs, and human review for higher-risk cases, while accepting moderate product experimentation outside regulated decisioning. Operating principles are customer clarity, accurate records, privacy by design, explainable decisions, prompt escalation, measurable controls, and shipping a useful first pass with clear assumptions. The fictional legal owner is Maya Chen, General Counsel and Chief Compliance Officer, who owns legal intake, regulatory interpretation, partner oversight, and final legal escalation decisions.

## Business Model

Monthly software subscription plus usage fees on payments and account services.

## Products Services

Web dashboard, business sub-ledger accounts, ACH vendor payments, domestic wires, virtual expense cards, invoice collection, and read-only financial reporting.

## Jurisdictions

United States (customers and operations); company based in Seattle, WA with remote staff across the U.S. and a small operations team in Toronto, Canada.

## Regulatory Context

Operates as a fintech without a banking charter; relies on sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. for deposit and payment services. Subject to money transmission and payments regulation, AML and sanctions, KYC and beneficial ownership, privacy and data security, card-network rules, and bank-partner oversight.

## Data Practices

Privacy by design; least-privilege access; audit logs; human review for higher-risk cases; documented controls for regulated activity.

## Risk Posture

Conservative for regulated activity with documented controls, escalation for unusual transactions, and human review; moderate product experimentation outside regulated decisioning.
