# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Automated beneficial-owner background screening for small-business operating accounts; FCRA/ECOA/Reg B and bank-partner oversight are the primary legal workstreams.

## Decision question

Advise on which background-check data sources and checks are legally acceptable for automated beneficial-owner screening of small-business operating accounts, what notices and dispute rights are required, which decisions must remain with a human or the sponsor bank (Cedar Harbor), and what controls are needed before a launch targeted six months out.

## Material facts

- Juniper Ledger plans to offer faster approval for small-business operating accounts by running automated background checks on owners, controllers, and authorized users.
- The proposed flow collects beneficial-owner information, retrieves identity and public-record data, screens sanctions and politically exposed person (PEP) lists, and assigns a risk result.
- Low-risk businesses would receive an account and virtual card within minutes; higher-risk cases go to Juniper Ledger's compliance team and, where required, the sponsor bank.
- Participants include Product, compliance, the sponsor bank (Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A.), applicants, and the screening vendor.
- Target launch is six months from now.
- A 25% beneficial-ownership threshold applies.
- The flow must support manual review.
- Product has not settled which records may be used, how criminal or civil records would affect eligibility, how discrepancies will be handled, or whether an owner may see and challenge the result.
- The permissible use of information about non-applicant owners, the retention period, and the language for an account denial are not yet known.
- Is the background-check/screening vendor a consumer-reporting agency under the FCRA (i.e., does it furnish consumer reports that would be used to deny an account)? — Not sure — need to check the vendor contract

## Assumptions

- Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. is the named bank for deposit accounts and payment rails under the program-management agreement.
- The screening vendor may be a consumer-reporting agency, which would trigger FCRA adverse-action and dispute duties on denial.
- Beneficial-owner screening may implicate ECOA/Reg B and FCRA notice obligations depending on how the data is used and whether the account is consumer or business in nature.
- The screening vendor may be a consumer-reporting agency, which would trigger FCRA adverse-action and dispute duties on denial; this is unresolved pending review of the vendor contract.

## Issues and workstreams

- Which background-check data sources and records are legally permissible for automated screening of beneficial owners
- Whether the screening vendor is a consumer-reporting agency under the FCRA, driving adverse-action and dispute obligations
- What notices and dispute rights are required for denials based on automated screening
- Which decisions must remain with a human or the sponsor bank rather than automated decisioning
- Permissible use and retention of information about non-applicant owners
- Controls needed before launch (manual review, escalation, vendor oversight)
