# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Determine which background-check checks and data sources are legally acceptable for automated screening of beneficial owners, what notices and dispute rights are required, which decisions must remain with a human or the sponsor bank, and what controls are needed before launch of faster small-business account approval.

## Decision question

Determine which background-check checks and data sources are legally acceptable for automated screening of beneficial owners, what notices and dispute rights are required, which decisions must remain with a human or the sponsor bank, and what controls are needed before launch of faster small-business account approval.

## Material facts

- Juniper Ledger plans to offer faster approval for small-business operating accounts by running automated background checks on owners, controllers, and authorized users.
- The proposed flow collects beneficial-owner information, retrieves identity and public-record data, screens sanctions and politically exposed person (PEP) lists, and assigns a risk result.
- Low-risk businesses would receive an account and virtual card within minutes; higher-risk cases go to Juniper Ledger's compliance team and, where required, the sponsor bank.
- Participants include Product, compliance, the sponsor bank (Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A.), applicants, and the screening vendor.
- Target launch is six months from now.
- A 25% beneficial-ownership threshold applies.
- The flow must support manual review.
- Product has not settled which records may be used, how criminal or civil records would affect eligibility, how discrepancies will be handled, or whether an owner may see and challenge the result.
- The permissible use of information about non-applicant owners, the retention period, and the language for an account denial are not yet known.
