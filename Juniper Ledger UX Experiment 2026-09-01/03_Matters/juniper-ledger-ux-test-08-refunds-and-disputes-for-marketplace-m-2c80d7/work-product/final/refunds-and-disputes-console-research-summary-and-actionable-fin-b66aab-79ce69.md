# Refunds and Disputes Console — Substantive Legal Response for Launch

**Matter:** Juniper Ledger UX Test — 08 — Refunds and Disputes for Marketplace Merchants
**Scope:** Visa and Mastercard only, multiple processors; mixed merchant-of-record and buyer-notice ownership; ~3-month launch window; legal answer needed before launch.
**Status:** Draft for review. This is a recommendation, not a recorded decision.

---

## 1. Executive answer

Juniper Ledger can launch a unified refunds and disputes console, but only if it is designed around **hard, network-defined dispute deadlines** and **does not assume a single merchant-of-record model**. The two highest-risk items are (a) deadline tracking and missed-deadline liability, and (b) the allocation of duties that depends on who is the merchant of record for each transaction.

The console should:
- **Track every dispute deadline per network and per processor** against a single reference clock, because Visa and Mastercard deadlines are calendar-day, network-specific, and missing one generally means automatic loss.
- **Route duties by merchant-of-record model per transaction** — the console must support both merchant-of-record and payment-facilitator/marketplace structures without assuming one liability or notice model.
- **Resolve provisional credits as a decision before launch**, not leave them open.
- **Allocate missed-deadline liability contractually** (with processors and with merchants) and back it with operational controls (deadline alerts, escalation, audit logs).
- **Define evidence authentication and record retention up front**, aligned to network reason codes.

Exact network day counts, evidence matrices, and program-level obligations must be confirmed against the **current Visa Core Rules, Mastercard Chargeback Guide, and the Cedar Harbor Bank program-management agreement** before launch. This draft does not invent those numbers; items requiring confirmation are labeled below.

---

## 2. Allocation of duties by actor and by merchant-of-record model

### 2.1 By actor

| Actor | Primary duties |
|---|---|
| **Merchant** | Issue refunds; respond to disputes (accept liability, contest, or upload evidence); provide accurate transaction and delivery records; send buyer notices where the merchant is the merchant of record; fund valid adjustments. |
| **Buyer** | Receive refunds and dispute notices; submit dispute claims through the card issuer; provide evidence where required. |
| **Juniper Ledger** | Operate the console; route messages to the processor and network; track deadlines; show status; debit merchants for valid adjustments; authenticate evidence; maintain records; allocate duties correctly per merchant-of-record model. |
| **Sponsor bank (Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A.)** | Hold the program relationship with the networks; carry network-level obligations and settlement; set program-level dispute and evidence requirements. *(Confirm scope against the program-management agreement.)* |
| **Processor** | Transmit dispute and refund messages to the network; relay network responses; meet network deadlines on the rails it operates. |
| **Card networks (Visa, Mastercard)** | Define dispute cycles, deadlines, evidence requirements, and arbitration; adjudicate disputes that reach arbitration. |

### 2.2 By merchant-of-record model

Because merchant of record is **mixed (depends on the merchant/product)**, the console must determine, per transaction, which model applies and route duties accordingly:

- **Merchant-of-record model (merchant is the cardholder-facing party):** The merchant owns dispute liability, buyer notices, and network obligations. Juniper Ledger's role is operational (routing, tracking, debiting), and the merchant bears the economic and compliance consequences of disputes.
- **Payment-facilitator / marketplace model (Juniper Ledger or the sponsor bank is the merchant of record):** The facilitator/marketplace carries the network-level merchant obligations, dispute liability, and buyer-notice duties for those transactions. *(Confirm the exact treatment against the current network rules and the Cedar Harbor Bank program-management agreement.)*

**Recommendation:** The console must carry a per-transaction merchant-of-record attribute and route duties (liability, notices, evidence, deadlines) from that attribute. Do not build a single-model assumption into the data model or the liability logic.

---

## 3. Liability and indemnity for missed deadlines

- **Network deadlines are hard and calendar-day.** Missing a representment or response deadline generally results in automatic loss of the dispute for the merchant. This is the highest-risk operational control.
- **Missed-deadline liability is not yet allocated** when Juniper Ledger's API or a processor causes the miss. This must be resolved contractually before launch.

**Recommendation — three layers:**
1. **Processor agreements:** Allocate liability for deadlines missed due to processor failure, and require the processor to meet network deadlines and provide timely status/acknowledgment. *(Confirm processor contract terms.)*
2. **Merchant agreements:** Allocate liability for deadlines missed due to merchant inaction (e.g., merchant fails to respond or upload evidence in time), with clear notice and escalation before the deadline.
3. **Operational controls:** Deadline alerts with buffer, escalation to human review, and audit logs of every deadline event and action. Juniper Ledger should carry liability only for failures within its own control (e.g., its API failing to route a merchant's timely submission), and should define that scope explicitly.

---

## 4. Buyer and merchant communications

- **Who sends notices to buyers is mixed (depends on the merchant/product).** The duty follows the merchant-of-record allocation: the cardholder-facing party (merchant or facilitator/marketplace) owns buyer notices.
- **Consumer-protection notice duties** (e.g., Regulation Z for credit, Regulation E for debit/ACH) attach to the cardholder-facing party and may require specific disclosures and timing. *(Confirm which rail and which regulation applies per product.)*

**Recommendation:**
- Route buyer-notice duty from the merchant-of-record attribute, consistent with the allocation in Section 2.
- Define, per product, who sends refund confirmations, dispute status updates, and provisional-credit notices to buyers.
- Ensure merchant-facing communications (dashboard status, deadline alerts, debit notifications for valid adjustments) are clear and timely, and that merchants receive notice before any debit for a valid adjustment.

---

## 5. Refund timing and practices for digital goods, subscriptions, and delayed services

- **Full and partial refunds are standard, permitted merchant actions** and are distinct from disputes/chargebacks. Refunds are processed through the network and subject to the merchant's agreement with its processor and the sponsor bank.
- **Digital goods, subscriptions, and services with delayed delivery** raise timing questions because the "delivery" trigger for refunds and disputes is less clear than for physical goods. Network reason codes and evidence expectations differ for these categories. *(Confirm the current network treatment for digital/subscription/delayed-delivery reason codes.)*

**Recommendation:**
- Support full and partial refunds as distinct from disputes, with clear labeling in the console.
- For digital goods, subscriptions, and delayed services, define refund timing and cancellation/refund policies per product, and ensure the console captures the delivery/fulfillment evidence needed to defend disputes in these categories.
- Do not treat a refund as a substitute for responding to a dispute; a refund may resolve some claims but does not automatically close a network dispute.

---

## 6. Provisional credits

- **Whether Juniper Ledger may make provisional credits to buyers before a dispute resolves is undecided** and was flagged for legal guidance.
- Networks and consumer-protection rules permit provisional credits in some dispute scenarios (e.g., Regulation E provisional-credit requirements for certain errors), but a **merchant-initiated provisional credit before a dispute resolves is a product decision** with network-rule and consumer-protection implications. *(Confirm permissibility for the specific use case against the current rulebooks and the sponsor-bank agreement.)*

**Recommendation:**
- Treat provisional credits as a **decision to be made before launch**, not an open question.
- If adopted, define: when a provisional credit may be issued, the notice to the buyer, the conditions for reversal if the dispute is resolved against the buyer, and the funding source (who bears the float).
- If not adopted, state that explicitly and design the console to issue credits only on final resolution.

---

## 7. Record retention

- Network rules and regulations require retention of transaction and dispute records for defined periods (commonly several years). The exact period depends on the network rules and applicable regulations. *(Confirm the exact retention period against the current rulebooks and the sponsor-bank agreement.)*

**Recommendation:**
- Define a retention policy for transaction records, dispute records, evidence submissions, and communications, aligned to the longer of the network-required period and any applicable regulatory period.
- Retain evidence in a form that can be re-submitted if a dispute is reopened or escalated.
- Confirm the retention period with the sponsor bank and processors so the console's storage and deletion schedules match program requirements.

---

## 8. Minimum API contract and operational controls for launch

**Minimum API contract:**
- Per-transaction **merchant-of-record attribute** that drives liability, notice, and evidence routing.
- **Network and processor identifiers** per transaction so deadlines and evidence rules are tracked per network and per processor.
- **Dispute lifecycle status** (received, evidence window open, representment submitted, pre-arbitration, arbitration, resolved) with timestamps.
- **Deadline fields** (deadline date, reference timezone, buffer) computed from the network and processor rules.
- **Evidence upload** with reason-code mapping and authentication metadata.
- **Refund endpoints** for full and partial refunds, distinct from dispute responses.
- **Debit/adjustment records** for valid adjustments, with merchant notice.

**Minimum operational controls:**
- **Single reference clock** and defined timezone/cut-off policy for all deadline calculations. *(Confirm the reference-timezone and cut-off policy.)*
- **Deadline alerts with buffer** and escalation to human review before the network deadline.
- **Audit logs** of every deadline event, action, and status change.
- **Evidence authentication** method that matches network reason codes. *(Confirm the authentication method.)*
- **Retention and deletion** schedules per Section 7.
- **Processor integration monitoring** so a processor outage or missed acknowledgment is detected and escalated.

## 9. Assumptions, missing facts, and unverified leads

### Assumptions (positions taken for this analysis)
- Juniper Ledger operates as a fintech without a banking charter, relying on sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A.; card-network and sponsor-bank program rules govern much of the dispute mechanics.
- Juniper Ledger operates as a payment facilitator / marketplace model for at least some merchants, given the mixed merchant-of-record answer.
- The console must support both merchant-of-record and payment-facilitator/marketplace models without assuming a single liability or notice model.
- Visa and Mastercard network dispute timelines and evidence rules apply; multiple processors require consistent routing and deadline tracking.

### Missing facts / open questions (must be resolved before launch)
1. **Evidence authentication method** for card disputes — how uploaded evidence is authenticated and matched to the reason code.
2. **Deadline calculation policy across time zones** — reference timezone and cut-off policy for network deadlines.
3. **Liability allocation for missed deadlines** caused by Juniper Ledger's API or a processor — contractual and operational allocation.
4. **Provisional credits** — whether Juniper Ledger may make provisional credits to buyers before a dispute resolves.
5. **Buyer-notice ownership** — who sends notices to buyers about refunds and disputes (mixed, depends on merchant/product).

### Unverified leads (directionally correct, needs confirmation)
- **Payment-facilitator / marketplace liability model** — whether Juniper Ledger is the merchant of record depends on the program structure and the sponsor-bank agreement. *(Confirm against current network rules and the Cedar Harbor Bank program-management agreement.)*
- **Buyer-notice duties** — network rules and consumer-protection regimes impose notice duties on the cardholder-facing party; which party that is depends on the merchant-of-record allocation. *(Confirm per product.)*
- **Provisional credits** — permissibility for the specific use case. *(Confirm against current rulebooks and the sponsor-bank agreement.)*
- **Record retention** — exact retention period. *(Confirm against current rulebooks and the sponsor-bank agreement.)*
- **Exact network day counts and evidence matrices** — must be pulled from the current Visa Core Rules and Mastercard Chargeback Guide; not invented here.

## 10. Practical recommendation

**This is a recommendation, not a recorded decision.**

1. **Build the console around network deadlines and a single reference clock.** This is the highest-risk control and should be designed first.
2. **Carry a per-transaction merchant-of-record attribute** and route liability, notices, evidence, and deadlines from it. Do not assume a single model.
3. **Resolve provisional credits as a decision before launch** — either adopt with defined conditions and funding, or explicitly decline.
4. **Allocate missed-deadline liability contractually** with processors and merchants, and back it with deadline alerts, escalation, and audit logs.
5. **Define evidence authentication and record retention up front**, aligned to network reason codes and the sponsor-bank program.
6. **Confirm the current Visa Core Rules, Mastercard Chargeback Guide, and the Cedar Harbor Bank program-management agreement** for exact day counts, evidence matrices, provisional-credit permissibility, and retention periods before launch.

**Recommended next step:** Confirm the three authoritative sources (current network rulebooks and the sponsor-bank agreement) and resolve the five open questions in Section 9. Once those are confirmed, this draft can be finalized and, if you approve, recorded as a durable decision.
