      checks sanctions/AML procedures, and records a reasoned disposition. A case
      may not remain frozen without a dated next review.


      C. Restore

      the decision record.

      30 days while continuing. Limits must not be a disguised indefinite closure.

Matter: Juniper Ledger UX Test — 04 — Deactivating Accounts After Repeated Fraud Alerts

Classification: Generated analysis based on the supplied request. Not a verified legal opinion.

1. Scope and assumptions

Recorded facts: both consumer and business accounts are in scope; alerts may concern unusual card purchases, account takeover, synthetic identity indicators, or suspicious transfers; Product wants production by November 1, 2026; the business needs the legal answer before launch; and there is no existing threshold policy.

Assumptions: Juniper Ledger is a program manager and first-line operator under an unreviewed Cedar Harbor Bank agreement. Cedar Harbor is the bank for deposit accounts and payment rails. The agreement's authority allocation is unverified. The process must be split by consumer and business account type. Jurisdiction, account contracts, state law, license status, and transaction facts are not supplied.

Missing facts: the Cedar Harbor agreement and sponsor-bank procedures; account and state coverage; alert scoring and false-positive data; customer contracts; funds-return rails; complaint SLA; and whether a law-enforcement request or SAR is involved. These items must be confirmed before production.

2. Recommendation in brief

Adopt a risk-based, reversible control. A single credible high-severity signal (account takeover, synthetic identity, or active suspicious-transfer pattern) may trigger an immediate temporary freeze. Otherwise require two corroborated alerts in 30 days or investigator approval. Do not deactivate from an automated score alone.

Freeze only the necessary functions: stop card authorization and outbound transfers, preserve access to case status and lawful inbound credits, and allow essential inbound payroll/refunds unless Compliance directs a documented exception. Open an investigator case at once. Decide restore, limits, or deactivate within five business days; use a shorter 24-hour review for high-risk cases and never use the operational target to shorten a mandatory legal error-resolution period.

Use neutral notices. State that access is restricted for security and regulatory review, what functions are affected, how to submit an unauthorized-transaction dispute, and how to contact support. Do not disclose a SAR, a confidential law-enforcement request, or facts that would tip off an investigation.

Deactivate only after documented human review, policy checks, and required Cedar Harbor approval. Return any undisputed positive balance promptly through a verified method, subject to lawful holds and pending items. Do not use a negative balance to erase an unresolved consumer dispute.

3. Trigger and action matrix

High severity: credible account takeover, synthetic identity evidence, active money-laundering or suspicious-transfer indicators, or a confirmed unauthorized transaction pattern. Action: immediate temporary freeze; preserve evidence; notify Compliance and Cedar Harbor within one business day; investigator review within 24 hours.

Medium severity: two corroborated alerts within 30 days, or one alert plus a material identity, device, beneficiary, or transaction mismatch. Action: targeted limits or temporary freeze after documented rationale; investigator review within one business day.

Low or uncorroborated signal: unusual purchase without corroboration, customer travel/device change, or a single low-confidence model alert. Action: step-up verification, monitoring, or a narrow limit. No deactivation.

The trigger must be explainable. Record alert type, source, timestamp, affected function, score or rationale, analyst, and disposition. Review threshold performance monthly for false positives and disparate impact. A model score is a lead, not the legal ground for closure.

4. Process by phase

A. Freeze / limited access

1. Fraud Operations opens a case and records the trigger.

2. The control service freezes card authorization and outbound transfers. It must not block inbound credits by default.

3. Permit lawful inbound payroll and refunds where the rail and risk assessment allow. If an inbound item cannot be credited, return it to source or place it in a documented suspense process with a customer-facing explanation that does not reveal confidential investigation details.

4. Route high-severity cases to Compliance and Cedar Harbor within one business day. Preserve logs and evidence.

5. Send the applicable customer notice promptly, unless Compliance documents a lawful reason to delay or limit it.

B. Investigation

The investigator confirms identity, reviews transaction history and device/beneficiary signals, separates unauthorized-transaction claims from fraud-risk signals, checks sanctions/AML procedures, and records a reasoned disposition. A case may not remain frozen without a dated next review.

C. Restore

Restore access promptly when the signal is cleared or risk is acceptably controlled. Remove only the controls that are no longer needed. Correct any fees or service impact caused by an erroneous restriction. Send a restoration notice and preserve the decision record.

D. Impose limits

Use the narrowest limit that controls the risk, with a reason, start date, review date, and appeal path. Reassess within five business days and at least every 30 days while continuing. Limits must not be a disguised indefinite closure.

E. Deactivate

Require human approval from Fraud Operations and Compliance, a documented basis, a check for open Reg E disputes, pending credits, legal holds, and Cedar Harbor approval where the agreement requires it. Apply the same criteria to similarly situated customers. Schedule closure and funds return; do not destroy records.

5. Decision timing and notices

Operational SLA: high-severity case review within 24 hours; all other freezes reviewed within one business day; final restore/limit/deactivate decision within five business days, with a dated reason for any extension. This is an internal control, not a replacement for law.

Consumer accounts: handle unauthorized electronic-transfer claims under Reg E error-resolution rules. Provide the required acknowledgment, investigation, provisional-credit, and written-result notices within the applicable regulatory timelines. A ten-business-day internal target is useful for an error claim, but it does not authorize withholding a required provisional credit. Apply any permitted extended investigation period and notice rules only after Compliance confirms the facts and account type.

Business accounts: apply the account agreement, UCC Article 4A allocation-of-risk terms, and applicable funds-availability and payment rules. Notice timing may differ. Do not assume consumer Reg E protections apply to a business account.

Notice content: affected functions; effective time; safe support channel; dispute/error-reporting route; documents the customer may provide; expected review window; and, at closure, the balance and funds-return method. Use language such as: “We restricted some account functions while we complete a security and regulatory review. We cannot provide details that could compromise that review. You may contact support about access, transactions, or an unauthorized-transaction claim.” Never state or imply that a SAR was filed.

6. Funds, refunds, payroll, negative balances, and disputes

Pending refunds: identify the original transaction and accept a legitimate refund where the rail permits. Credit the account or return to the original source after risk review. If neither is possible, track the amount in suspense and give the customer a status update.

Payroll deposits: do not impose a blanket hold. Allow verified recurring payroll where lawful and operationally safe. If the account is closed, coordinate a return or alternate verified delivery and explain the next step. Document any exception.

Deactivation funds return: calculate ledger balance, pending debits/credits, holds, and disputed amounts. Return undisputed positive funds through a verified account or check within five business days after closure and any required Cedar Harbor approval. Keep an audit trail and send a completion notice. Apply longer timing only when a specific lawful hold or rail rule requires it, with a review date.

Negative balance: do not net against an unresolved consumer error claim or funds that must be provisionally credited. For an undisputed debt, apply only contractually and legally permitted setoff after notice. Otherwise refer collection activity to Compliance/Collections, with hardship and complaint controls.

Disputed transactions: open a separate error/dispute case. Preserve rights and deadlines even if the fraud-risk case continues. Do not treat a dispute as proof of fraud or use closure to avoid investigation duties.

7. Compliance, sponsor bank, and escalation

Juniper Ledger may execute delegated operational controls and customer support. It should recommend disposition and maintain the case record only within the Cedar Harbor delegation. Cedar Harbor decides or approves bank-level account closure, payment-rail restrictions, regulatory reporting, and any action reserved by the agreement. Compliance owns AML/SAR review and regulatory escalation. Product cannot override those decisions.

Escalate immediately for suspected money laundering, sanctions concerns, identity fraud rings, law-enforcement requests, repeat losses, material customer harm, or a system-wide false-positive pattern. Compliance decides whether a SAR or other report is required. Do not disclose a SAR or tip off a subject. Confirm the agreement's notice, approval, record-retention, and incident-reporting requirements before launch.

8. Complaints, fairness, records, and controls

Customer Support logs every complaint, provides a case number, routes unauthorized-transaction complaints to the applicable error-resolution team, and escalates unresolved complaints to Compliance. Set a two-business-day acknowledgment target and a ten-business-day substantive response target unless a legal rule or investigation requires more time. Do not promise restoration or disclose protected investigative details.

Retain the alert, decision, notice, evidence, approvals, funds calculation, return proof, complaint, and appeal outcome under the applicable retention schedule. Audit monthly for consistency, false positives, disparate impact, notice timeliness, pending-credit handling, and overdue reviews. Provide a manual override with reason and second-level review.

9. Launch gate and open items

Before the November 1, 2026 launch, Product must confirm the Cedar Harbor agreement allocation, consumer/business account segmentation, jurisdiction and contracts, alert thresholds using real performance data, Reg E workflows, refund/payroll rails, negative-balance rules, complaint ownership, reporting paths, training, and a controlled pilot. The agreement review and the policy draft are high-priority work items.

Recommendation: proceed to a controlled pilot only after Cedar Harbor signs off on the delegation and the compliance workflow is tested. Keep the policy marked “working draft” until those facts are verified.
