# Regulation E error-resolution and provisional-credit timing for consumer account

## Question

Regulation E error-resolution and provisional-credit timing for consumer accounts

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Juniper Ledger, a fintech serving both consumer and business accounts through sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A., is building a fraud-response flow that freezes card authorization and outbound transfers, allows limited inbound funds, and routes cases to a fraud investigator, with outcomes of restore, limit, or deactivate and return of funds. Counsel is involved because no fraud-alert threshold or escalation policy exists, and the freeze/deactivation mechanics intersect with Regulation E error-resolution rights for consumer accounts. Product has confirmed a November 1, 2026 production target, leaving roughly eight weeks for pilot, training, and sponsor-bank sign-off.
## Decision question
When a consumer account is frozen or deactivated on repeated fraud alerts, what Regulation E error-resolution and provisional-credit deadlines must Juniper Ledger's process meet (10 business days to investigate or provisionally credit, 45/90-day outer limits, 3-business-day reporting), and can the proposed freeze-and-investigate flow be sequenced so those clocks are never breached before the November 1, 2026 launch?

- Does the Cedar Harbor program-management agreement allocate freeze/deactivation decision authority, and does it make Cedar Harbor (as the account-holding bank) the Reg E "financial institution" of record, with Juniper Ledger acting as its agent? (Unverified; treated as delegated program authority for now.)
- Will the fraud-alert freeze ever overlap with a customer's own error notice (e.g., account-takeover alerts where the customer also reports unauthorized EFTs), which would start Reg E clocks independently of the internal investigation?
- Are the virtual expense cards and sub-ledger accounts structured so that consumer accounts can receive EFTs subject to Reg E (payroll deposits are confirmed in scope), and are any accounts prepaid or payroll-card accounts under Reg E's expanded definitions?
- What is the operational handoff between the fraud investigator and whoever owns Reg E error claims, so a fraud freeze does not silently suspend a pending error-resolution clock?
- Does the deactivation-and-return-of-funds step ever occur while a Reg E investigation is still open, which would require resolving (and paying) the error claim before or at closure?
## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.** The following is model-only analysis of the governing framework; counsel should verify against current regulatory text before relying on it.
Likely controlling framework for the consumer side (12 C.F.R. Part 1005, Regulation E, implementing the Electronic Fund Transfer Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1693 et seq.):
- **Trigger.** Reg E error-resolution duties are triggered by the *consumer's notice* of an "error" (including unauthorized EFTs), not by Juniper Ledger's internal fraud alerts. 12 C.F.R. § 1005.11(a). An internal fraud freeze does not itself start the Reg E clock — but if the customer contacts support about the same activity (likely in account-takeover cases), the clock starts on that notice regardless of the internal investigation's status.
- **Core timing.** Once notice is received, the financial institution must investigate promptly and either (a) complete the investigation and report results within **10 business days**, or (b) **provisionally credit** the alleged error amount (including interest, if any) within 10 business days and complete the investigation within **45 calendar days** (90 days for new accounts, point-of-sale, and foreign-initiated transfers). § 1005.11(c). Results must be reported to the consumer within **3 business days** after completing the investigation, and corrections made within **1 business day** of determining an error occurred. § 1005.11(c)(2).
- **Freeze interaction.** A fraud freeze that blocks outbound transfers does not toll Reg E deadlines. If a frozen consumer account has a pending error claim, the investigation and provisional-credit obligations continue to run. Deactivating the account and returning funds without resolving an open error claim risks a direct Reg E violation plus UDAAP exposure.
- **Liability tiers.** Consumer liability for unauthorized EFTs is capped at $50 / $500 / unlimited depending on notification timing (§ 1005.6), which matters when the fraud alerts concern account takeover and the customer disputes transactions during or after the freeze.
- **Notices.** If the freeze or deactivation is communicated, care is needed: Reg E requires specific content in error-resolution reports, and fraud-investigation confidentiality (including SAR tipping-off constraints under 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)) limits what can be said about the *reasons* — but cannot be used to avoid Reg E disclosures owed on the customer's own claim.
- **Business accounts.** Reg E does not apply; UCC Article 4A, NACHA rules, and the account agreement govern. The policy should keep the two tracks procedurally separate so consumer deadlines are never missed because a case was treated as business.
## Viable paths
1. **Dual-track design (recommended starting point).** Run the fraud-investigation track and the Reg E error-resolution track in parallel with a mandatory cross-check: any customer contact alleging unauthorized activity on a consumer account is logged as a Reg E error notice and routed to a Reg E owner, independent of the fraud case. Freeze decisions proceed on the fraud track; error claims proceed on statutory clocks.
2. **Provisional-credit default for frozen consumer accounts.** Where a consumer account under fraud freeze also has an open error claim that cannot be resolved in 10 business days, provisionally credit rather than let the freeze substitute for investigation. This is operationally conservative and fits Juniper Ledger's stated risk posture.
3. **Closure gate.** No consumer deactivation closes until (a) all open Reg E error claims are resolved and paid, and (b) the funds-return calculation accounts for provisional/final credits. Negative-balance and disputed-transaction handling should be netted only after Reg E credits are applied.
4. **Contract check first.** Confirm with Cedar Harbor who is the Reg E "financial institution" and who operationally receives error notices; if Juniper Ledger's support team is the intake point, the agreement should say so and allocate the 10-business-day clock accordingly.
## Facts that could change the answer
- If Cedar Harbor is the Reg E institution of record and insists on running error resolution itself, Juniper Ledger's role shifts to referral-with-clock-monitoring rather than direct compliance.
- If the affected consumer accounts are structured as prepaid accounts, the 90-day (rather than 45-day) extended window and other Subpart A prepaid rules may apply.
- If Product narrows scope to business accounts only, Reg E drops out and the analysis shifts to UCC 4A / NACHA / contract.
- If fraud alerts frequently coincide with customer-reported errors (account takeover), the volume of parallel Reg E clocks may require staffing changes before launch.
