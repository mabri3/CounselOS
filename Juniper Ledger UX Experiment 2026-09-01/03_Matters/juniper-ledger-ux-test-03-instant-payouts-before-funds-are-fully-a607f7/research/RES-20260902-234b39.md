# What reserve and negative-balance collection practices are permissible under UDA

## Question

What reserve and negative-balance collection practices are permissible under UDAAP and card-network rules for merchant payout programs?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Juniper Ledger, a fintech working with sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A., wants to offer online merchants instant payouts from card-acceptance proceeds before those transactions fully settle. The proposed flow would maintain a reserve, evaluate merchant history and fraud signals, and later debit the reserve or merchant account for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments. Counsel is involved because the legal characterization of the reserve/advance, permissible collection practices, required merchant agreement terms, and sponsor-bank approvals are unresolved. Product wants to launch a pilot with 10 established merchants on October 15, 2026, with a $25,000 per-payout cap and $100,000 aggregate daily cap, subject to compliance and sponsor-bank review.
## Decision question
Should Juniper Ledger structure its instant payout feature as a hold on unsettled card proceeds, a loan or advance funded by its own capital, or another form of advance, and what reserve, negative-balance collection, liability-duration, fee-disclosure, and payout-suspension terms must be in the merchant agreement before launching a 10-merchant pilot on October 15, 2026, given that Cedar Harbor Bank has not yet been engaged and the program-agreement requirements are unknown?
## Open questions
- How will Juniper Ledger characterize the reserve/advance — a hold on unsettled card proceeds, a loan or advance funded by Juniper Ledger's own capital, or another structure — and who funds the instant payout before settlement?
- Who bears the credit risk if the merchant's card proceeds are insufficient to cover chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments after an instant payout?
- How long will merchants remain liable after an instant payout for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments?
- How does Juniper Ledger intend to collect negative balances if the reserve or merchant account is insufficient?
- What does the Cedar Harbor Bank program agreement require for this feature, and what sponsor-bank approvals are needed before pilot launch?

### UDAAP (Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices)
Under the Dodd-Frank Act (12 U.S.C. §§ 5531, 5536) and analogous state UDAP statutes, the CFPB and state regulators can pursue practices that are unfair, deceptive, or abusive. For a merchant-facing instant payout product, the key UDAAP risks are:
- **Deception risk in fee and reserve disclosures.** Merchants see an "estimated fee" and an "explanation of the reserve" before confirming. If the actual fee differs materially from the estimate, or if the reserve explanation does not clearly disclose the conditions under which funds will be held, debited, or recouped, that creates deception exposure. The disclosure must be clear, conspicuous, and made before the merchant commits.
- **Unfairness risk in negative-balance collection.** If Juniper Ledger debits a merchant's operating account or external account for chargebacks and reversals after payout, and the merchant did not clearly understand that liability could extend beyond the reserve, that could be challenged as unfair — particularly if the debit causes substantial injury (e.g., overdrawing the merchant's account) that the merchant could not reasonably avoid.
- **Abusiveness risk in liability duration.** If merchants remain liable for chargebacks and network adjustments for an extended or indefinite period after payout, and that duration is not clearly disclosed, a regulator could argue the practice takes unreasonable advantage of the merchant's lack of understanding.
- **Suspension discretion.** Juniper Ledger's ability to suspend payouts when fraud risk increases must be exercised under disclosed, objective criteria. Undisclosed or arbitrary suspension could be challenged as deceptive or unfair, particularly if merchants rely on expected payout timing.
### Card-network rules
Visa and Mastercard operating regulations impose obligations on acquirers and their agents (which would include Juniper Ledger and Cedar Harbor Bank in this flow) regarding:
- **Reserve requirements.** Networks permit and sometimes require reserves against merchant chargeback exposure. The reserve must be calculated and administered consistent with network rules and the acquirer's underwriting standards.
- **Chargeback liability allocation.** Network rules define the timeline and conditions under which chargebacks can be initiated (typically 120 days for most reason codes, up to 540 days for certain categories). The merchant agreement must align with these timelines.
- **Prohibited practices.** Networks prohibit certain practices that could expose the acquirer to excessive risk, including advancing funds against unsettled transactions without adequate controls. The sponsor bank (as the acquiring entity or program sponsor) will need to confirm the feature complies with its network registrations and obligations.
### Money transmission and lending characterization
- If the instant payout is characterized as a **hold on the merchant's own unsettled proceeds** (i.e., Juniper Ledger is simply accelerating access to funds the merchant has already earned), the money transmission analysis is simpler — Juniper Ledger is facilitating payment, not extending credit.
- If the payout is characterized as a **loan or advance funded by Juniper Ledger's capital**, state lending and licensing laws may apply. Several states regulate commercial lending or advances, and some require licenses for commercial financing transactions. The characterization also affects usury analysis, disclosure obligations, and whether the fee is treated as interest.
- If structured as a **purchase of receivables** (factoring), different rules apply, but the recourse provisions (Juniper Ledger's right to debit the reserve or account for chargebacks) could cause the arrangement to be recharacterized as a loan in some states.
### Sponsor-bank program agreement
Cedar Harbor Bank, as the named bank for deposit accounts and payment rails, will have oversight obligations under its program-management and banking-services agreement with Juniper Ledger. The bank's regulators (OCC or state banking department, depending on charter) expect the bank to oversee third-party risk, including new product features that affect payment flows, reserves, and consumer or merchant protections. The bank has not yet been engaged, which is a critical path item.

## Viable paths
### Path A: Hold/acceleration structure (lower regulatory risk)
Structure the instant payout as an acceleration of the merchant's own card-acceptance proceeds, not a loan. Juniper Ledger (through Cedar Harbor) releases funds from the merchant's settlement before the standard settlement date, based on the merchant's history and fraud signals. The reserve is a holdback from the merchant's own proceeds, not collateral for a loan.
- **Advantages:** Avoids lending license issues in most states. Simpler money transmission analysis. Aligns with how many payment processors structure early-settlement features.
- **Requirements:** Clear merchant agreement language that the payout is an acceleration of the merchant's own funds, not a loan. Fee disclosed as a service fee, not interest. Reserve disclosed as a holdback, not a security deposit for credit.
- **Residual risk:** Some states may still scrutinize the arrangement if the fee is disproportionate or if the recourse provisions (debit rights) make it look like a loan in substance.
### Path B: Loan/advance structure (higher regulatory burden)
Structure the instant payout as a loan or advance funded by Juniper Ledger's own capital, repaid from future card proceeds.
- **Advantages:** More flexibility in structuring fees and recourse. Clear credit-risk allocation.
- **Requirements:** State lending license analysis in all states where merchants are located. Commercial lending disclosure requirements. Usury analysis. Potentially higher sponsor-bank scrutiny.
- **Residual risk:** Licensing burden may be significant for a pilot. Some states have broad definitions of "loan" or "advance" that could capture this structure even for commercial borrowers.
### Path C: Receivables purchase (factoring)
Structure the instant payout as a purchase of the merchant's card receivables at a discount, with recourse to the merchant for chargebacks and reversals.
- **Advantages:** Avoids lending characterization in most states if structured as a true sale. Well-established legal framework for factoring.
- **Requirements:** True-sale analysis (the recourse provisions must not be so extensive that the arrangement is recharacterized as a loan). UCC Article 9 filing considerations. Clear merchant agreement language.
- **Residual risk:** Recharacterization risk if recourse is unlimited or if Juniper Ledger retains too much control over the receivables.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Who funds the payout.** If Cedar Harbor Bank funds the payout (rather than Juniper Ledger), the lending analysis shifts to the bank, and Juniper Ledger's role may be limited to program management. This would reduce Juniper Ledger's licensing burden but increase the bank's regulatory obligations.
- **Merchant locations.** If pilot merchants are concentrated in states with aggressive commercial lending or factoring regulation (e.g., California, New York), the licensing analysis becomes more complex.
- **Fee structure.** If the fee is a flat service fee rather than a percentage of the payout, the usury and lending analysis is simpler. If the fee varies with the payout amount or duration, it looks more like interest.
- **Liability duration.** If merchant liability for chargebacks is capped at the network chargeback window (typically 120–180 days), the UDAAP risk is lower. If liability is indefinite or extends beyond network timelines, the risk increases.
- **Collection mechanics.** If negative balances are collected only from future card proceeds (not from the merchant's external bank account), the unfairness risk is lower. If Juniper Ledger can debit external accounts, the risk increases.
- **Program agreement terms.** The Cedar Harbor program agreement may already address reserve requirements, new product approvals, or merchant-facing features. If it does, the approval path may be faster. If it is silent, the bank may require a formal amendment.

5. **UDAAP review of disclosures.** Have counsel review the merchant-facing disclosures (fee estimate, reserve explanation, liability terms, suspension criteria) for clarity, conspicuousness, and completeness before the pilot launches.
