# What sponsor-bank and card-network approvals and program-agreement terms are typ

## Question

What sponsor-bank and card-network approvals and program-agreement terms are typically required for pre-settlement payout features?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Juniper Ledger, a fintech working with sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A., wants to let online merchants receive instant payouts from card-acceptance proceeds before the underlying transactions fully settle. The payments team proposes maintaining a reserve, evaluating merchant history and fraud signals, and later debiting the reserve or merchant account for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments. Counsel is involved because the legal characterization of the advance (hold vs. loan), sponsor-bank approvals, card-network compliance, and merchant agreement terms remain undetermined. Product wants to launch a pilot with 10 established merchants on October 15, 2026, with a $25,000 per-payout cap and $100,000 aggregate daily cap.
## Decision question
Should Juniper Ledger structure the instant payout feature as a hold on the merchant's own unsettled card proceeds (funded by the sponsor bank or processor) or as a loan/advance funded by Juniper Ledger's own capital, and what specific sponsor-bank approvals, card-network notifications, and merchant agreement terms must be in place before the October 15 pilot launch with 10 merchants and $100,000 daily exposure?
## Open questions
- Has Cedar Harbor Bank's program agreement been reviewed to determine whether pre-settlement payouts require explicit bank approval, amendments to the agreement, or additional reserve/collateral requirements?
- Do Visa and Mastercard rules (or the processor's program terms) require registration, notification, or specific disclosures for early access to settlement funds, and do they impose limits on reserve practices or fee structures?
- Will Juniper Ledger fund the payouts from its own balance sheet (creating potential lending/credit characterization and state licensing issues) or will the sponsor bank or processor fund them (reducing Juniper's regulatory exposure but requiring bank approval)?
- What is the maximum merchant liability period after payout for chargebacks and network adjustments, and does it align with card-network chargeback windows (typically 120–180 days)?
- How will negative balances be collected if the reserve and future proceeds are insufficient—through ACH debit authorization, personal guarantees, or other means—and are those collection practices permissible under state law and card-network rules?

## Likely rules and issues
### 1. Sponsor-bank program agreement requirements
**Issue:** Juniper Ledger operates under a program-management and banking-services agreement with Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. Pre-settlement payouts likely constitute a new product feature or material change to the program that requires bank approval.
**Typical requirements:**
- **Advance notice and approval:** Most sponsor-bank agreements require 30–90 days' advance written notice and explicit bank approval before launching new payment features, especially those involving credit risk, reserves, or early access to settlement funds.
- **Risk assessment and controls:** The bank will likely require a written risk assessment, reserve adequacy analysis, fraud controls, and compliance review before approving the feature.
- **Reserve and collateral:** The bank may require Juniper Ledger to maintain a separate reserve account, post collateral, or meet minimum capital requirements to cover potential losses from chargebacks and negative balances.
- **Amendment or side letter:** If the current agreement does not contemplate pre-settlement payouts, the bank may require a formal amendment or side letter defining the feature, risk allocation, and approval conditions.
- **Ongoing reporting:** The bank may require periodic reporting on payout volumes, reserve balances, chargeback rates, and negative balance collections.
**Internal support:** the internal record and the internal record confirm Cedar Harbor Bank has not yet been engaged and the program-agreement requirements are undetermined.
### 2. Card-network rules and processor terms
**Issue:** Visa and Mastercard impose rules on settlement timing, reserves, chargebacks, and merchant agreements. The payment processor (which sits between Juniper Ledger and the networks) may also have program terms that restrict or require approval for early payout features.
**Typical requirements:**
- **Settlement timing:** Card networks generally require settlement within a defined window (e.g., 24–48 hours). Early payout features must not violate network settlement rules or create unauthorized extensions of credit.
- **Reserve practices:** Networks allow reserves but may impose limits on reserve amounts, duration, and release conditions. Excessive or indefinite reserves can trigger network scrutiny or fines.
- **Chargeback liability:** Networks hold merchants liable for chargebacks for a defined period (typically 120–180 days from transaction date). The merchant agreement must clearly allocate this liability and preserve Juniper Ledger's right to debit the reserve or account.
- **Registration or notification:** Some networks or processors require registration or notification of new payment features, especially those involving early access to funds or non-standard reserve practices.
- **Prohibited practices:** Networks may prohibit certain high-risk practices, such as payouts to merchants with excessive chargeback rates or payouts that exceed a defined percentage of expected settlement proceeds.
**Assumption:** The specific card-network and processor requirements are not in the matter file and would need to be confirmed by reviewing the processor agreement and network rules or consulting with the processor.
### 3. Legal characterization: hold vs. loan vs. advance
**Issue:** The characterization of the instant payout determines the regulatory framework, required disclosures, and state licensing obligations.
**Options and implications:**
**Option A: Hold on merchant's own unsettled proceeds (preferred)**
- **Structure:** Juniper Ledger (or the sponsor bank/processor) releases the merchant's own card proceeds before final settlement, subject to a reserve and the merchant's obligation to cover chargebacks and adjustments.
- **Regulatory treatment:** Generally treated as a payment service, not a loan. No state lending license required. Money transmission laws still apply, but the feature fits within Juniper Ledger's existing money transmission framework.
- **Risk allocation:** The merchant bears the risk of chargebacks and adjustments. Juniper Ledger's risk is limited to negative balances if the reserve and future proceeds are insufficient.
- **Disclosure:** Must clearly disclose the reserve, fee, timing, and merchant's ongoing liability for chargebacks.
**Option B: Loan or advance funded by Juniper Ledger's capital**
- **Structure:** Juniper Ledger advances its own funds to the merchant before settlement, secured by the expected card proceeds.
- **Regulatory treatment:** May be characterized as a loan or credit product under state law, triggering state lending license requirements, usury limits, and Truth in Lending Act (TILA) or state disclosure requirements. Some states have specific laws governing merchant cash advances or business-purpose loans.
- **Risk allocation:** Juniper Ledger bears the credit risk if the merchant's proceeds are insufficient. This increases regulatory scrutiny and capital requirements.
- **Disclosure:** Must comply with state lending laws, which may require APR disclosure, repayment terms, and other consumer-style disclosures (even for business-purpose loans in some states).
**Option C: Factoring or purchase of receivables**
- **Structure:** Juniper Ledger purchases the merchant's card receivables at a discount before settlement.
- **Regulatory treatment:** Factoring is generally not treated as a loan if structured as a true sale, but the line between factoring and lending is fact-specific and varies by state. Some states regulate merchant cash advances as loans.
- **Risk allocation:** Juniper Ledger bears the credit risk, but may have recourse to the merchant if structured as recourse factoring.
- **Disclosure:** Must clearly disclose the purchase price, discount rate, and recourse terms.
**Recommendation:** Option A (hold on merchant's own proceeds) is the lowest-risk structure and aligns with Juniper Ledger's conservative risk posture for regulated activity. It avoids state lending license issues and keeps the feature within the existing money transmission and payments framework.
### 4. Merchant agreement terms and disclosures
**Issue:** The merchant agreement must clearly define the feature, allocate risk, and comply with card-network rules and state law.
**Required terms:**
- **Optional nature:** Clearly state that the service is optional and the merchant may choose standard settlement timing instead.
- **Fee disclosure:** Disclose the fee amount or formula, when it is charged, and how it is calculated (e.g., percentage of payout amount, flat fee, or tiered based on merchant history).
- **Reserve explanation:** Explain the reserve amount or percentage, how it is calculated, when it is released, and the conditions for release.
- **Timing:** State when funds will be available (e.g., within minutes, same day) and any conditions or delays.
- **Merchant liability:** Clearly state that the merchant remains liable for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments for a defined period (e.g., 180 days) after payout, and that Juniper Ledger may debit the reserve or account for these amounts.
- **Negative balance collection:** Explain how negative balances will be collected (e.g., ACH debit authorization, offset against future proceeds, demand for payment) and the merchant's obligation to maintain sufficient funds.
- **Suspension rights:** State that Juniper Ledger may suspend payouts when fraud risk increases, and provide examples of triggering events (e.g., unusual transaction patterns, excessive chargebacks, fraud alerts).
- **Termination:** Explain how the merchant may opt out of the service and any conditions or notice requirements.
- **Compliance with card-network rules:** Include a representation that the merchant will comply with card-network rules and not engage in prohibited practices.
**UDAAP considerations:** The disclosures must be clear, conspicuous, and not misleading. The fee, reserve, and risk allocation must be prominently disclosed before the merchant confirms the payout. Avoid fine print or buried terms.
### 5. Permissible reserve and collection practices
**Issue:** Reserve and collection practices must comply with card-network rules, state law, and UDAAP principles.
**Reserve practices:**
- **Reasonable amount:** The reserve should be reasonably related to the merchant's chargeback and fraud risk. Excessive reserves may be challenged as unfair or deceptive.
- **Defined duration:** The reserve should be released within a defined period (e.g., 180 days) after the underlying transactions settle, unless there are outstanding chargebacks or disputes.
- **Segregation:** The reserve should be held in a separate account or clearly identified in the merchant's account ledger to avoid commingling and ensure transparency.
- **Interest:** State law and card-network rules may require or prohibit payment of interest on reserves. This should be confirmed and disclosed.
**Collection practices:**
- **ACH debit authorization:** The merchant agreement should include ACH debit authorization for negative balances, but the authorization must comply with NACHA rules and state law (e.g., clear disclosure, right to revoke).
- **Offset against future proceeds:** Juniper Ledger may offset negative balances against future card proceeds, but this right must be clearly disclosed and comply with card-network rules.
- **Demand for payment:** If the reserve and future proceeds are insufficient, Juniper Ledger may demand payment, but collection practices must comply with state debt collection laws (even for business debts in some states).
- **Personal guarantees:** For higher-risk merchants, Juniper Ledger may require a personal guarantee from the business owner, but this must be clearly disclosed and comply with state law.
**State law considerations:** Some states have specific laws governing reserves, holds, and collection practices for payment services. These should be reviewed for the states where pilot merchants are located.

## Viable paths
### Path 1: Hold on merchant's own proceeds, funded by sponsor bank or processor (recommended)
**Structure:**
- Cedar Harbor Bank or the payment processor releases the merchant's own card proceeds before final settlement.
- Juniper Ledger maintains a reserve (funded by a percentage of the payout or a holdback from future proceeds) to cover chargebacks and adjustments.
- The merchant remains liable for chargebacks and adjustments for 180 days after payout.
- Juniper Ledger debits the reserve or merchant account for chargebacks and adjustments, and collects negative balances through ACH debit or offset against future proceeds.
**Approvals and steps:**
1. Review the Cedar Harbor Bank program agreement to determine approval requirements and any required amendments.
2. Engage Cedar Harbor Bank with a written proposal, risk assessment, and reserve adequacy analysis.
3. Confirm card-network and processor requirements (registration, notification, reserve limits, disclosure requirements).
4. Draft merchant agreement terms and disclosures (fee, reserve, timing, liability, suspension, termination).
5. Implement fraud and risk controls (merchant history evaluation, fraud signals, suspension criteria).
6. Obtain Cedar Harbor Bank approval and any required card-network or processor approvals.
7. Launch pilot with 10 established merchants, $25,000 per-payout cap, and $100,000 aggregate daily cap.
**Timeline:** 6–8 weeks (assuming Cedar Harbor Bank approval takes 4–6 weeks and card-network/processor confirmation takes 1–2 weeks).
**Risk:** Low. This structure avoids state lending license issues, keeps the feature within the existing money transmission framework, and aligns with Juniper Ledger's conservative risk posture.
### Path 2: Loan or advance funded by Juniper Ledger's capital
**Structure:**
- Juniper Ledger advances its own funds to the merchant before settlement, secured by the expected card proceeds.
- The merchant repays the advance from settlement proceeds, and Juniper Ledger retains a reserve to cover chargebacks and adjustments.
- The merchant remains liable for chargebacks and adjustments for 180 days after payout.
**Approvals and steps:**
1. Determine whether the advance is characterized as a loan under state law and whether a state lending license is required.
2. If a lending license is required, obtain the license or partner with a licensed lender.
3. Comply with state usury limits, disclosure requirements, and TILA (if applicable).
4. Review the Cedar Harbor Bank program agreement and obtain bank approval.
5. Confirm card-network and processor requirements.
6. Draft merchant agreement terms and disclosures (including APR, repayment terms, and state-specific disclosures).
7. Implement fraud and risk controls.
8. Launch pilot.
**Timeline:** 12–16 weeks (assuming state lending license analysis and approval takes 8–12 weeks).
**Risk:** High. This structure may trigger state lending license requirements, usury limits, and additional disclosure obligations. It also increases Juniper Ledger's credit risk and regulatory scrutiny.
### Path 3: Factoring or purchase of receivables
**Structure:**
- Juniper Ledger purchases the merchant's card receivables at a discount before settlement.
- The merchant receives the purchase price (minus the discount and reserve) before settlement.
- Juniper Ledger collects the receivables from settlement proceeds and retains the reserve to cover chargebacks and adjustments.
**Approvals and steps:**
1. Determine whether the factoring arrangement is characterized as a true sale or a loan under state law.
2. If characterized as a loan, comply with state lending license requirements and usury limits.
3. Review the Cedar Harbor Bank program agreement and obtain bank approval.
4. Confirm card-network and processor requirements.
5. Draft merchant agreement terms and disclosures (purchase price, discount rate, recourse terms).
6. Implement fraud and risk controls.
7. Launch pilot.
**Timeline:** 8–12 weeks (assuming state law analysis and bank approval take 6–10 weeks).
**Risk:** Medium. Factoring is generally not treated as a loan if structured as a true sale, but the line is fact-specific and varies by state. This structure also increases Juniper Ledger's credit risk.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Cedar Harbor Bank program agreement terms:** If the agreement already contemplates pre-settlement payouts or grants Juniper Ledger broad discretion to launch new features, the approval process may be faster and simpler. If the agreement prohibits early access to settlement funds or requires extensive bank review, the timeline may be longer.
- **Card-network and processor requirements:** If Visa/Mastercard or the processor require registration, notification, or specific disclosures, the timeline and compliance burden may increase. If they prohibit certain reserve practices or fee structures, the feature design may need to change.
- **State lending license requirements:** If the pilot merchants are located in states with broad definitions of "loan" or "credit" (e.g., California, New York), the hold structure may still trigger lending license requirements. A state-by-state analysis is needed.
- **Merchant chargeback rates:** If pilot merchants have high chargeback rates, the reserve requirement may be higher, and the feature may be uneconomic or require additional risk controls.
- **Funding source:** If Juniper Ledger funds the payouts from its own balance sheet (rather than the sponsor bank or processor), the regulatory characterization and risk profile change significantly.
