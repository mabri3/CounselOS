# What approvals and program-agreement requirements apply when a fintech (Juniper

## Question

What approvals and program-agreement requirements apply when a fintech (Juniper Ledger) that relies on a sponsor bank (Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A.) for deposit and payment services launches an instant-payout feature that makes card-acceptance proceeds available to merchants before settlement, maintains a reserve, and debits it later for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments? Identify the sponsor-bank approvals, program-agreement implications, and bank-partner oversight considerations.

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Juniper Ledger, a fintech that relies on sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. for deposit and payment services, wants to launch an instant-payout feature that advances card-acceptance proceeds to online merchants before those transactions fully settle. The proposed flow would maintain a reserve, evaluate merchant history and fraud signals, and later debit the reserve or merchant account for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments. Counsel is involved because the feature's legal characterization (hold vs. loan vs. other advance), required merchant agreement terms, reserve and collection practices, and sponsor-bank approvals are all undetermined. Product wants to pilot with 10 established merchants starting October 15, 2026, with a $25,000 per-payout cap and $100,000 aggregate daily cap, but Cedar Harbor Bank has not yet been engaged.
## Decision question
Before Juniper Ledger launches an instant-payout pilot on October 15, 2026 with 10 merchants and $25,000/$100,000 caps, what legal characterization should the advance receive (hold on unsettled proceeds vs. loan vs. other structure), what merchant agreement terms and disclosures are required, what reserve and collection practices are permissible, and what approvals must Cedar Harbor Bank provide under the program agreement?
## Open questions
- How will Juniper Ledger characterize the instant payout and reserve — as a hold on the merchant's own unsettled card proceeds, a loan or advance funded by Juniper Ledger's capital, or another structure? This drives money transmission, lending, and UDAAP exposure.
- Who funds the payout before settlement, and who bears credit risk if the merchant's proceeds are insufficient to cover chargebacks and reversals?
- How long will merchants remain liable after an instant payout for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments?
- How will Juniper Ledger collect negative balances if the reserve or account is insufficient (e.g., debit authorization, ACH pull, offset against future settlements)?
- What does the Cedar Harbor Bank program agreement require for new product features, and what specific approvals or amendments are needed before pilot launch?

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.**
Based on the matter facts and general regulatory framework for fintech-bank partnerships:
### 1. Legal characterization drives regulatory exposure
The core issue is whether the instant payout is:
- **A hold/release timing change on the merchant's own funds** — If Juniper Ledger is simply accelerating access to proceeds the merchant has already earned from card sales, this may be characterized as a payment service or funds availability feature, not credit. This is the lowest-risk characterization but requires that the merchant have a present right to the underlying proceeds and that Juniper Ledger is not advancing its own capital.
- **A loan or advance funded by Juniper Ledger's capital** — If Juniper Ledger fronts its own money before settlement and later recoups from the merchant's proceeds, this looks like a short-term business loan or merchant cash advance. This triggers state lending laws, usury limits, licensing requirements (unless a bank-partner exemption applies), and UDAAP risk if fees or terms are not clearly disclosed.
- **A hybrid structure** — Some instant-payout products are structured as a purchase of future receivables or a secured advance against expected settlement proceeds. These can avoid lending characterization if properly documented but require careful drafting to avoid recharacterization as a disguised loan.
The characterization affects:
- **Money transmission** — Whether Juniper Ledger is "receiving money for transmission" or providing credit may change the analysis under state money transmitter laws.
- **Lending and usury** — If characterized as a loan, state lending licenses, rate caps, and disclosure requirements apply unless the sponsor-bank model provides an exemption.
- **UDAAP** — Fees, timing, reserve terms, and suspension criteria must be clearly disclosed to avoid unfair, deceptive, or abusive practices.
### 2. Merchant agreement terms and disclosures
The merchant agreement must clearly address:
- **Nature of the service** — Whether it is a funds availability feature, a loan, an advance, or a purchase of receivables.
- **Fees** — The estimated fee must be disclosed before confirmation, and the final fee calculation method must be clear.
- **Reserve** — How the reserve is calculated, how long it is held, when it is released, and whether it earns interest.
- **Liability period** — How long the merchant remains liable for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments after payout.
- **Collection rights** — Juniper Ledger's right to debit the reserve, the merchant's operating account, or external accounts; authorization for ACH debits; and offset rights against future settlements.
- **Suspension criteria** — When and why Juniper Ledger may suspend payouts (e.g., fraud risk, excessive chargebacks), and whether the merchant has notice or appeal rights.
- **Risk allocation** — Who bears the risk if settlement proceeds are insufficient, and whether the merchant's obligation is contingent or absolute.
### 3. Reserve and collection practices
Permissible practices depend on characterization:
- If the payout is a **hold on the merchant's own funds**, the reserve is a risk-management tool, and debiting it for chargebacks is a contractual setoff. This is generally permissible if disclosed.
- If the payout is a **loan or advance**, the reserve functions as collateral or a repayment source. Collection practices must comply with lending laws, and debiting the merchant's account may require specific authorization under EFTA/Regulation E (if consumer accounts are involved) or UCC Article 9 (if a security interest is taken).
- **Negative balance collection** — If the reserve and account are insufficient, Juniper Ledger must have a clear contractual right to collect the deficiency, either through ACH debit authorization, offset against future settlements, or a personal guarantee. The method must be disclosed and legally enforceable.
### 4. Sponsor-bank approvals and program-agreement implications
Cedar Harbor Bank has not yet been engaged, which is a critical gap. The program agreement likely requires:
- **New product approval** — Most sponsor-bank agreements require bank review and approval before launching new features that affect deposit accounts, payment rails, or regulatory compliance.
- **Risk and compliance review** — The bank will want to assess money transmission, lending, AML/sanctions, and UDAAP risk, and may require changes to the feature or additional controls.
- **Amendment or side letter** — If the instant-payout feature is not contemplated by the current program agreement, an amendment or side letter may be required to allocate responsibilities, indemnities, and oversight rights.
- **Bank-partner oversight** — Cedar Harbor will likely require ongoing reporting, audit rights, and escalation procedures for the pilot, especially given the credit and fraud risk.
The bank's primary concerns will be:
- **Safety and soundness** — Whether the feature creates credit risk, operational risk, or compliance risk for the bank.
- **Regulatory compliance** — Whether the feature complies with money transmission, lending, AML, and consumer protection laws.
- **Reputation risk** — Whether the feature could result in customer complaints, regulatory scrutiny, or network rule violations.
### 5. Card network and processor rules
Card networks (Visa, Mastercard) and the payment processor may have rules about:
- **Funds availability** — Whether proceeds can be released before settlement.
- **Reserve requirements** — Whether reserves must be held in specific accounts or meet specific criteria.
- **Chargeback liability** — How chargebacks are allocated when payouts are accelerated.
These rules may limit Juniper Ledger's flexibility or require additional disclosures or controls.

## Viable paths
### Path 1: Characterize as a hold on merchant's own proceeds (lowest risk)
Structure the feature as an accelerated release of the merchant's own card proceeds, not a loan or advance. This requires:
- The merchant has a present, earned right to the proceeds (i.e., the card transactions are complete and not subject to dispute).
- Juniper Ledger is not advancing its own capital; it is simply releasing funds earlier than the normal settlement cycle.
- The reserve is a risk-management holdback, not collateral for a loan.
**Advantages:** Avoids lending characterization, reduces money transmission risk, and simplifies disclosures.
**Disadvantages:** May not be economically viable if Juniper Ledger must fund the payout before receiving settlement proceeds from the processor or networks.
### Path 2: Characterize as a loan or advance under the sponsor-bank model
Structure the feature as a short-term business loan or advance made by Cedar Harbor Bank (or by Juniper Ledger under the bank's lending authority), with the reserve and future settlements as repayment sources.
**Advantages:** Provides clear legal framework, may allow for higher fees, and leverages the bank's lending authority.
**Disadvantages:** Requires bank approval, triggers lending and usury analysis, and increases UDAAP and disclosure risk.
### Path 3: Characterize as a purchase of receivables or merchant cash advance
Structure the feature as a purchase of the merchant's future card receivables at a discount, with the reserve as a holdback for chargebacks and adjustments.
**Advantages:** May avoid lending characterization if properly structured as a true sale.
**Disadvantages:** Requires careful drafting to avoid recharacterization as a disguised loan, and may still trigger state licensing requirements.
### Path 4: Delay pilot until bank approval and legal characterization are resolved
Do not launch the pilot on October 15, 2026. Instead:
- Engage Cedar Harbor Bank immediately to determine program-agreement requirements and obtain approval.
- Decide on the legal characterization and draft the merchant agreement and disclosures.
- Implement required controls and reporting.
**Advantages:** Reduces legal and regulatory risk, ensures bank support, and allows for a compliant launch.
**Disadvantages:** Delays the pilot and may miss the quarter.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Funding source** — If Juniper Ledger funds the payout from its own capital, lending characterization is more likely. If the payout is funded by accelerating the merchant's own proceeds, hold characterization is more viable.
- **Merchant's right to proceeds** — If the merchant does not have a present, earned right to the proceeds (e.g., because transactions are not final), the payout cannot be characterized as a hold on the merchant's funds.
- **Fee structure** — If the fee is a percentage of the payout and varies with risk, it looks more like interest or a discount, which supports lending or receivables-purchase characterization. If it is a flat fee for accelerated access, it looks more like a service fee.
- **Recourse** — If the merchant's obligation to repay is absolute (i.e., not contingent on settlement proceeds), the payout looks more like a loan. If repayment is contingent on settlement, it looks more like a receivables purchase.
- **Bank's risk appetite** — If Cedar Harbor Bank is unwilling to support the feature without significant changes or additional controls, the pilot may not be viable on the proposed timeline.
- **State licensing** — If the feature is characterized as a loan or money transmission, Juniper Ledger may need state licenses it does not currently hold, which could delay or block the pilot.

## Recommended last-mile verification
Before approving the pilot, Juniper Ledger should:
1. **Engage Cedar Harbor Bank immediately** — Determine what the program agreement requires for new product features, what approvals are needed, and whether an amendment or side letter is required. Obtain written approval before launch.
2. **Decide on legal characterization** — Choose whether the feature is a hold, a loan, an advance, or a receivables purchase, and document the analysis. This decision drives all other legal requirements.
3. **Draft merchant agreement and disclosures** — Prepare a clear, conspicuous merchant agreement that addresses fees, reserve, liability period, collection rights, suspension criteria, and risk allocation. Ensure disclosures comply with UDAAP and any applicable lending or money transmission laws.
4. **Verify state licensing** — Confirm whether the chosen characterization requires state lending or money transmitter licenses, and whether Juniper Ledger or Cedar Harbor Bank holds the necessary licenses.
5. **Review card network and processor rules** — Confirm that the feature complies with Visa, Mastercard, and processor rules on funds availability, reserves, and chargebacks.
6. **Implement controls and reporting** — Establish fraud monitoring, reserve management, negative balance collection, and escalation procedures. Ensure audit logs and human review for higher-risk cases, consistent with Juniper Ledger's risk posture.
7. **Obtain legal sign-off** — Have Maya Chen (General Counsel and Chief Compliance Officer) review and approve the legal characterization, merchant agreement, disclosures, and bank approval before pilot launch.
