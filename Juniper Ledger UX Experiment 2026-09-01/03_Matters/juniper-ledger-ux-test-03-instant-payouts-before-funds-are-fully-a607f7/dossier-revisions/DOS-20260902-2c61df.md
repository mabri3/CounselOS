# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Juniper Ledger, a fintech working with sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A., wants to offer online merchants instant payouts from card-acceptance proceeds before those transactions fully settle. The proposed flow would maintain a reserve, evaluate merchant history and fraud signals, and later debit the reserve or merchant account for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments. Counsel is involved because the legal characterization of the reserve/advance, permissible collection practices, required merchant agreement terms, and sponsor-bank approvals are unresolved. Product wants to launch a pilot with 10 established merchants on October 15, 2026, with a $25,000 per-payout cap and $100,000 aggregate daily cap, subject to compliance and sponsor-bank review.

## Decision question

Should Juniper Ledger structure its instant payout feature as a hold on unsettled card proceeds, a loan or advance funded by its own capital, or another form of advance, and what reserve, negative-balance collection, liability-duration, fee-disclosure, and payout-suspension terms must be in the merchant agreement before launching a 10-merchant pilot on October 15, 2026, given that Cedar Harbor Bank has not yet been engaged and the program-agreement requirements are unknown?

## Issues and workstreams

- Legal characterization of the reserve/advance (hold vs. loan vs. other form of advance) and resulting regulatory exposure (money transmission, lending/credit, UDAAP).
- Required merchant agreement terms and disclosures for fees, timing, risk allocation, and payout suspension.
- Permissible reserve and collection practices, including how negative balances are collected and how long merchant liability persists.
- Sponsor-bank (Cedar Harbor) approvals and program-agreement implications; bank not yet engaged.
- Decisions Juniper Ledger must make before approving the pilot (characterization, funding source, liability period, collection mechanics).
