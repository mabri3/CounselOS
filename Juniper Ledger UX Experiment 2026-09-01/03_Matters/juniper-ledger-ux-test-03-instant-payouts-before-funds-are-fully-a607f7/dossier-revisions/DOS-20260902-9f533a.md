# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Juniper Ledger, a fintech working with sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A., wants to let online merchants receive instant payouts from card-acceptance proceeds before the underlying transactions fully settle. The proposed flow would maintain a reserve, evaluate merchant history and fraud signals, and later debit the reserve or merchant account for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments. Counsel is involved because the legal characterization of this arrangement—whether it is a settlement/hold, a loan, or another form of advance—drives regulatory exposure under money transmission and lending law, and because required merchant agreement terms, disclosures, reserve practices, and sponsor-bank approvals remain undefined. Product wants to launch a pilot with 10 established merchants on October 15, 2026, with a $25,000 per-payout cap and a $100,000 aggregate daily cap.

## Decision question

Should Juniper Ledger characterize its instant payout feature as a settlement/hold on unsettled card proceeds, a loan or advance funded by its own capital, or another structure, and what merchant agreement terms, disclosures, reserve and collection practices, and sponsor-bank approvals are required before launching the October 15, 2026 pilot with 10 merchants?
