# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Advise on the legal characterization, required merchant agreement terms and disclosures, permissible reserve and collection practices, sponsor-bank approvals, and the decisions Juniper Ledger must make before approving a pilot that lets established online merchants receive instant payouts from card-acceptance proceeds before the underlying transactions fully settle.

## Decision question

Advise on the legal characterization, required merchant agreement terms and disclosures, permissible reserve and collection practices, sponsor-bank approvals, and the decisions Juniper Ledger must make before approving a pilot that lets established online merchants receive instant payouts from card-acceptance proceeds before the underlying transactions fully settle.

## Material facts

- The proposed service would let online merchants receive instant payouts from card-acceptance proceeds before the underlying transactions fully settle.
- The proposed flow would maintain a reserve, evaluate merchant history and fraud signals, and make funds available to a Juniper Ledger operating account or an external account.
- Juniper Ledger would later debit the reserve or account for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments.
- Merchants would see an estimated fee and an explanation of the reserve before confirming.
- The service would be optional for merchants.
- Juniper Ledger may suspend payouts when fraud risk increases.
- Product wants a pilot with established merchants this quarter.
- Participants include Juniper Ledger, the merchant, the sponsor bank (Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A.), card networks, and the payment processor.
- How does Juniper Ledger intend to characterize the reserve/advance — a hold on unsettled card proceeds, a loan or advance funded by Juniper Ledger's own capital, or another structure? — Not yet determined — that's part of what we need advice on
- The characterization of the reserve/advance (hold vs. loan vs. other form of advance) is not yet determined.
- Who funds the instant payout before settlement, and who bears the credit risk if the merchant's proceeds are insufficient? — Not yet determined

## Assumptions

- Juniper Ledger operates without a banking charter and relies on sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. for deposit and payment services under a program-management and banking-services agreement.
- The pilot is limited to established merchants (existing history and fraud signals available).
- The service is U.S.-only, consistent with Juniper Ledger's customer base.

## Issues and workstreams

- Legal characterization of the reserve/advance (hold vs. loan vs. other form of advance) and resulting regulatory exposure (money transmission, lending/credit, UDAAP).
- Required merchant agreement terms and disclosures for fees, timing, risk allocation, and payout suspension.
- Permissible reserve and collection practices, including how negative balances are collected and how long merchant liability persists.
- Sponsor-bank (Cedar Harbor) approvals and program-agreement implications.
- Decisions Juniper Ledger must make before approving the pilot.

## Open questions

- Who funds the instant payout before settlement and who bears the credit risk (Juniper Ledger capital vs. sponsor bank vs. third-party funder).
- How long merchants remain liable after payout for chargebacks, refunds, reversals, and network adjustments.
- How negative balances will be collected if the reserve or account is insufficient.
- The fee structure and how fees are disclosed.
- The sponsor bank's (Cedar Harbor) position and required approvals under the program agreement.
- The payout suspension criteria and how they are disclosed.
- Target pilot timing and scope (launch date, number of merchants, payout caps).
- How does Juniper Ledger intend to collect negative balances if the reserve or account is insufficient?
