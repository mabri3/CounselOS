# Juniper Ledger is considering a household feature that lets an

## Question

Juniper Ledger is considering a household feature that lets an adult account holder issue a virtual or physical card to a teenager, with the adult setting spending categories/limits, viewing transactions, and approving merchants. Product wants research-ready legal and product requirements for a limited beta next summer. Advise on age and consent rules, account/cardholder status, privacy and marketing restrictions, dispute liability, required disclosures, parental controls, and the product decisions that must be made before user testing.

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Juniper Ledger, a fintech that operates through sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A., is designing a household feature that would let an adult primary account holder issue a virtual or physical card to a teenager who may be under 18. The adult would set spending categories and limits, view transactions, and approve merchants; the teen could make purchases and receive refunds but could not initiate ACH or account-to-account transfers. Counsel is involved because issuing payment cards to minors implicates age and consent rules, cardholder status, COPPA and financial privacy law, dispute liability, and sponsor-bank and card-network requirements. Product wants research-ready requirements for a limited beta targeted for next summer.
## Decision question
Can Juniper Ledger launch a limited beta next summer that issues restricted debit cards to teenagers under an adult's primary account, and if so, must the teen be structured as an authorized user rather than a customer, what minimum age applies, what consent and privacy notices are required, and how must disputes, marketing, and data use be handled to satisfy COPPA, Reg E, GLBA/Reg P, and Cedar Harbor's program rules?
## Open questions
- Will the teenager be a Juniper Ledger customer in their own right or an authorized user on the adult's account? (Not decided; this drives KYC, privacy, disclosure, and liability analysis.)
- What minimum age will Product set, and will any participants be under 13? (Under-13 triggers COPPA verifiable parental consent; 13+ may avoid COPPA but still implicates state minor-privacy laws.)
- How will the adult's consent be collected and documented, and who receives privacy notices (GLBA/Reg P and, if applicable, COPPA direct notice)?
- How will disputes and unauthorized use be allocated between the adult, the teen, Juniper Ledger, and Cedar Harbor under Reg E and card-network rules?
- Have Cedar Harbor's program requirements and card-network age rules been reviewed and confirmed for this design? (Not yet reviewed; sponsor approval is a gating item.)

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.** The following is generated analysis based on the matter record and general legal principles; it is not retrieved authority.
### Age and consent
- **Contract capacity.** Minors generally cannot enter binding contracts. If the teen is a party to the cardholder agreement, the agreement may be voidable. The safer structure is to keep the adult as the sole contracting customer and treat the teen as an authorized user or restricted supplementary cardholder.
- **COPPA.** If any teen is under 13, the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act requires verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information online, a direct notice to the parent, and child-directed privacy controls. This is a hard gate for under-13 participation.
- **State minor-privacy laws.** Even for teens 13–17, several states (e.g., California's Age-Appropriate Design Code, other state comprehensive privacy laws) impose heightened duties for minors' data, including default privacy-protective settings and restrictions on targeted advertising and profiling.
### Account and cardholder status
- **Customer vs. authorized user.** If the teen is a "customer," Juniper Ledger may need to perform KYC/CIP on the teen, provide Reg E and privacy disclosures directly, and treat the teen as an account holder for dispute purposes. If the teen is an authorized user, the adult remains the customer, KYC runs on the adult, and the teen's access is derivative and restricted.
- **Sponsor bank and network rules.** Cedar Harbor's program agreement and card-network rules (Visa/Mastercard) may impose minimum ages, require the primary cardholder to be 18+, or restrict supplementary card issuance to minors. These have not been reviewed and are a gating constraint.
### Privacy and marketing restrictions
- **GLBA/Reg P.** The adult, as the customer, receives the initial and annual privacy notices. If the teen is also a customer, the teen (or the parent, depending on age and structure) may need to receive notices.
- **COPPA.** For under-13 users, marketing, behavioral analytics, and data sharing require verifiable parental consent and are heavily restricted.
- **State law.** For teens 13–17, state laws may restrict sale/share of personal information, targeted advertising, and profiling without opt-in consent. The conservative default is to keep personalized marketing, ad targeting, and behavioral analytics based on teen data off by default and separate any optional consent from card access.
### Dispute and unauthorized-use liability
- **Reg E.** If the card accesses a consumer asset account, Regulation E limits consumer liability for unauthorized electronic fund transfers and imposes error-resolution duties. The teen's status affects who is the "consumer" and who can assert errors.
- **Card-network rules.** Networks have zero-liability policies and chargeback rules that interact with Reg E. The adult's control over the teen's card (limits, merchant approval, pause/freeze) may affect whether a transaction is "unauthorized."
- **Allocation.** The product must decide whether the adult is liable for all teen transactions, whether the teen can initiate disputes, and how refunds and chargebacks are handled.
### Required disclosures
- **Cardholder agreement.** Must be provided to the contracting party (the adult). If the teen is a customer, a separate age-appropriate agreement or notice may be needed.
- **Reg E disclosures.** Initial disclosures of rights and liabilities, and periodic statements or access to transaction history.
- **Privacy notices.** GLBA/Reg P initial and annual notices; COPPA direct notice and privacy policy if under-13.
- **Change-in-terms and error-resolution notices.** As applicable.
### Parental controls
- The proposed controls (spending categories, limits, transaction visibility, merchant approval) are consistent with an authorized-user model and help mitigate risk. Additional controls to consider: pause/freeze, real-time notifications, geographic restrictions, cash-access prohibition, and age-appropriate teen interface.

## Viable paths
1. **Authorized-user model (recommended).** The adult is the sole customer and funder; the teen is a named restricted supplementary cardholder. This minimizes KYC, privacy, and contract-capacity issues. Start at age 13+ to avoid COPPA; do not include under-13 users unless COPPA-compliant verifiable parental consent and child-directed controls are implemented.
2. **Customer model with adult co-signer.** The teen is a customer with the adult as co-signer or joint account holder. This is more complex: KYC on the teen, direct disclosures, potential state minor-contract issues, and higher privacy compliance burden. Not recommended for a limited beta.
3. **Custodial or trust structure.** Overly complex for this use case and not aligned with the product goal.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Minimum age.** If Product includes under-13 users, COPPA applies and the design must add verifiable parental consent, direct notice, and child-directed privacy controls.
- **Customer status.** If the teen is a customer, KYC, Reg E, privacy, and disclosure obligations expand significantly.
- **Sponsor bank and network rules.** Cedar Harbor or the card networks may prohibit minor cardholders, impose a higher minimum age, or require specific controls. This could block or reshape the feature.
- **State scope.** If the beta includes residents of states with strict minor-privacy laws (e.g., California), additional defaults and restrictions may apply.
- **Credit or overdraft features.** If the card ever involves credit, overdraft, or teen-controlled cash-out, TILA/Reg Z, state lending laws, and additional Reg E issues arise. The current debit-only, no-ACH design avoids these.
