# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Juniper Ledger, a fintech operating through sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A., is designing a household feature that would let an adult primary account holder issue a virtual or physical card to a teenager who may be under 18. The adult would invite the teen, set spending categories and limits, view transactions, and approve selected merchants; the teen could make purchases and receive refunds but could not initiate ACH or account-to-account transfers. Counsel is involved because the teen's age and undecided customer status drive consent, privacy, disclosure, and liability obligations. Product wants research-ready requirements for a limited beta next summer.

## Decision question

For a limited beta next summer, can Juniper Ledger issue a restricted debit card to a minor as a named authorized user on the adult's account — with the adult bearing primary liability under Reg E and network rules — and what minimum age, consent evidence, and dispute-handling terms must be locked before user testing so unauthorized-use losses and chargebacks are allocated predictably?

## Issues and workstreams

- Age and consent: minors generally cannot enter binding contracts, so card issuance to a teenager requires adult consent and careful structuring of who is the cardholder and who bears liability.
- Account/cardholder status: whether the teenager is a customer of Juniper Ledger or an authorized user on the adult's account changes KYC, privacy, and disclosure obligations.
- Privacy: COPPA applies to online collection of personal information from children under 13; state privacy laws and GLBA/Reg P may also apply depending on data flows.
- Marketing and analytics: use of a minor's transaction and behavioral data for marketing raises COPPA and state privacy concerns.
- Dispute and unauthorized-use liability: Reg E and card-network rules allocate liability, but the teenager's status and the adult's control affect who bears loss.
- Disclosures: required cardholder and privacy disclosures depend on whether the teenager is a cardholder and who receives them.
- Sponsor bank and card-network rules: Cedar Harbor's program requirements and network age rules may constrain the design.

## Open questions

- Will the teen be a Juniper Ledger customer in their own right or an authorized user on the adult's account? This drives who is the "consumer" under Reg E and who receives disclosures and error-resolution rights.
- What minimum age will Product set, and will any users be under 13 (triggering COPPA verifiable parental consent for data collection)?
- Have Cedar Harbor's program agreement and the card networks' age/cardholder rules been confirmed to permit minor authorized users, and on what terms?
- How will disputes and unauthorized use be allocated contractually between the adult, Juniper Ledger, and Cedar Harbor — including whether the adult's grant of card access to the teen is treated as furnishing an access device?
- Who receives Reg E initial disclosures, periodic statements, and privacy notices — the adult only, or the teen as well?
