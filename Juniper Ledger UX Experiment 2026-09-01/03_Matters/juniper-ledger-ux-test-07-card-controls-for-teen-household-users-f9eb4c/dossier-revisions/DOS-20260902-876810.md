# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Product change: household feature allowing an adult to issue a virtual or physical card to a teenager, with spending controls, for a limited beta next summer. Key legal workstreams: age/consent, account and cardholder status, privacy (COPPA, GLBA/Reg P, state law), marketing/analytics restrictions, dispute and unauthorized-use liability, required disclosures, and sponsor-bank/card-network constraints.

## Decision question

Advise on the legal and regulatory requirements for a Juniper Ledger household feature that lets an adult account holder issue a virtual or physical card to a teenager (under 18), including age and consent rules, account/cardholder status, privacy and marketing restrictions, dispute liability, required disclosures, and parental controls, and identify the product decisions that must be made before a limited beta next summer.

## Material facts

- The adult is the primary account holder on the Juniper Ledger account.
- The teenager may be under 18.
- The proposed flow requires the adult to invite the teenager, set spending categories and limits, view transactions, and approve selected merchants.
- The teenager could use the card for purchases and receive refunds, but could not initiate ACH or account-to-account transfers.
- Product wants research-ready requirements for a limited beta next summer.
- Juniper Ledger does not hold a banking charter and relies on sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. for deposit and payment services.

## Assumptions

- The feature is limited to U.S. customers, consistent with Juniper Ledger's U.S.-only operations.
- The teenager is a minor under 18 and not an adult co-owner of the account.
- The card is issued under the sponsor bank's program and card-network rules apply.
- A limited beta next summer implies a target launch window of roughly mid-2027.

## Issues and workstreams

- Age and consent: minors generally cannot enter binding contracts, so card issuance to a teenager requires adult consent and careful structuring of who is the cardholder and who bears liability.
- Account/cardholder status: whether the teenager is a customer of Juniper Ledger or merely an authorized user on the adult's account changes KYC, privacy, and disclosure obligations.
- Privacy: COPPA applies to online collection of personal information from children under 13; state privacy laws and GLBA/Reg P may also apply depending on data flows.
- Marketing and analytics: use of a minor's transaction and behavioral data for marketing raises COPPA and state privacy concerns.
- Dispute and unauthorized-use liability: Reg E (electronic fund transfers) and card-network rules allocate liability, but the teenager's status and the adult's control affect who bears loss.
- Disclosures: required cardholder and privacy disclosures depend on whether the teenager is a cardholder and who receives them.
- Sponsor bank and card-network rules: Cedar Harbor's program requirements and network age rules may constrain the design.

## Open questions

- Minimum age for the teenager (e.g., 13, 16, or 18)
- Whether the teenager becomes a Juniper Ledger customer or remains a cardholder on the adult's account
- How parental/adult consent is collected and documented
- Who receives privacy notices (adult, teenager, or both)
- How disputes and unauthorized-use liability are allocated
- Whether marketing, transaction data, and behavioral analytics may include the teenager
- Whether the card is virtual, physical, or both, and which card network(s) are involved
- Whether the sponsor bank (Cedar Harbor) has approved the program and its own age/consent requirements
- What is the proposed minimum age for the teenager?
- Will the teenager be a Juniper Ledger customer or an authorized user on the adult's account?
- How does Juniper Ledger intend to collect and document adult consent?
