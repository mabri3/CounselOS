# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Juniper Ledger, a fintech operating through sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A., is designing a household feature that would let an adult primary account holder issue a virtual or physical card to a teenager who may be under 18. The adult would invite the teen, set spending categories and limits, view transactions, and approve selected merchants; the teen could make purchases and receive refunds but could not initiate ACH or account-to-account transfers. Counsel is involved because issuing cards to minors implicates card-network rules, sponsor-bank program requirements, age and consent law, privacy and marketing restrictions, and dispute-liability allocation. Product wants research-ready requirements for a limited beta next summer, and Cedar Harbor's program requirements and card-network age rules have not yet been reviewed.

## Decision question

Before the limited beta next summer, can Juniper Ledger structure teen card issuance as an authorized-user model under the adult's account — with the teen as a named restricted supplementary cardholder starting at age 13 or older — in a way that satisfies Visa/Mastercard network rules and Cedar Harbor's program requirements, or must the teen be a full customer, and what minimum age, consent, and control conditions do the networks and sponsor bank impose?

## Issues and workstreams

- Age and consent: minors generally cannot enter binding contracts, so card issuance to a teenager requires adult consent and careful structuring of who is the cardholder and who bears liability.
- Account/cardholder status: whether the teenager is a customer of Juniper Ledger or an authorized user on the adult's account changes KYC, privacy, and disclosure obligations.
- Privacy: COPPA applies to online collection of personal information from children under 13; state privacy laws and GLBA/Reg P may also apply depending on data flows.
- Marketing and analytics: use of a minor's transaction and behavioral data for marketing raises COPPA and state privacy concerns.
- Dispute and unauthorized-use liability: Reg E and card-network rules allocate liability, but the teenager's status and the adult's control affect who bears loss.
- Disclosures: required cardholder and privacy disclosures depend on whether the teenager is a cardholder and who receives them.
- Sponsor bank and card-network rules: Cedar Harbor's program requirements and network age rules may constrain the design.

## Open questions

- What minimum age, if any, do Visa and Mastercard network rules and Cedar Harbor's card-program agreement impose for supplementary or authorized-user cardholders, and do those rules differ for virtual versus physical cards?
- Does Cedar Harbor's program agreement require the teen to be treated as a customer (with independent KYC/CIP and disclosures) or permit a pure authorized-user/supplementary-cardholder structure under the adult's account?
- If any teen under 13 is included, can Juniper Ledger implement COPPA-compliant verifiable parental consent and child-directed privacy controls in time for the beta, or should the beta be restricted to 13+?
- How do the network rules and Cedar Harbor allocate liability for unauthorized use and disputes on a minor's card when the adult controls the account and funding?
- What disclosures, privacy notices (GLBA/Reg P and, if applicable, COPPA), and marketing/data-use restrictions apply to teen transaction and behavioral data under the chosen structure?
