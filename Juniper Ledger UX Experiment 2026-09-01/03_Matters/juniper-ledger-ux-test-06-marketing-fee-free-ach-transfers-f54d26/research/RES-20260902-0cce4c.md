# Sponsor bank program-management agreement obligations for marketing deposit/paym

## Question

Sponsor bank program-management agreement obligations for marketing deposit/payment services

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Juniper Ledger, a fintech operating through sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A., wants to advertise "fee-free ACH transfers" to consumers and small businesses across paid ads, the app, email, and partner landing pages. The proposed flow would waive Juniper Ledger's fee on a set number of standard ACH transfers, while expedited transfers, returns, and certain external-bank actions may still carry charges, and network or third-party fees may still occur. Counsel is involved because the claim's net impression could be misleading, the fee schedule and eligibility rules are not final, and sponsor bank oversight obligations are unresolved. The campaign is scheduled to launch in eight weeks (target 2026-10-28).
## Decision question
Can Juniper Ledger launch the "fee-free ACH transfers" campaign by 2026-10-28 without Cedar Harbor Bank's written approval, or must the bank approve the marketing and fee structure first under the program-management agreement and bank oversight rules — and if approval is required but not obtained in time, should the campaign be delayed or narrowed to "No Juniper Ledger fee on standard ACH transfers"?
## Open questions
- What does the program-management and banking-services agreement with Cedar Harbor actually say about marketing review and approval rights for deposit and payment services?
- Has Cedar Harbor already reviewed or approved this campaign or the underlying fee structure, and if not, what is its approval timeline relative to the 2026-10-28 launch?
- Which transfer types will actually carry a fee under the final fee schedule, and what are the free-transfer caps, measurement period, and eligibility conditions?
- What is the final geographic scope of the offer, which determines which state UDAP laws apply?
- Will consumer testing be completed before launch to show whether a typical customer understands "fee-free" to exclude expedited, third-party, and external-bank charges?

## Likely rules and issues
**Bank-partner oversight and third-party risk management.** Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. is the named bank for deposit accounts and payment rails. Federal banking regulators expect banks to manage risks from third-party relationships, including fintech program managers that market deposit and payment services on the bank's behalf. The OCC, Federal Reserve, and FDIC have each issued third-party risk management guidance (e.g., OCC Bulletin 2013-29 and the 2023 interagency guidance) that treats marketing of bank products by a third party as an activity requiring bank oversight. In practice, sponsor bank agreements almost always include marketing review and approval rights, compliance with law representations, and sometimes specific advertising standards. The exact contractual requirement here is unconfirmed.
**FTC Act Section 5 and "free" claims.** For consumers, the claim is evaluated under FTC Act Section 5's prohibition on deceptive acts or practices. The FTC's guidance on "free" claims (16 C.F.R. Part 251) requires that all material conditions be disclosed clearly and conspicuously at the outset and in close conjunction with the claim. A "fee-free" claim where expedited transfers, returns, external-bank actions, network fees, and third-party fees may still apply risks a misleading net impression unless those limits are disclosed with equal prominence.
**State UDAP laws.** State unfair and deceptive acts and practices statutes in the final geographic scope will apply to both consumer and, in many states, small-business recipients. Coverage and standards vary.
**UDAAP risk for the bank.** If the marketing is deceptive, the bank itself could face UDAAP exposure under Dodd-Frank Section 1031/1036, which is why sponsor banks typically insist on approval rights.

## Viable paths
**Path 1 — Delay launch until bank approval and facts are final.** Obtain Cedar Harbor's written approval after delivering the final fee schedule, eligibility rules, geographic scope, and creative for all four channels. This is the lowest-risk path but may miss the 2026-10-28 target if the bank's review cycle is slow.
**Path 2 — Narrow the claim and proceed with conditions.** Use "No Juniper Ledger fee on standard ACH transfers" with complete disclosures, obtain bank approval of the narrower claim, and launch on time if the bank can review quickly. This reduces deception risk but still requires bank sign-off.
**Path 3 — Launch without bank approval.** Not recommended. Even if the contract is silent, the bank's oversight duties and the relationship risk make this a poor choice. If the contract does require approval, launching without it is a breach.
## Facts that could change the answer
- If the program-management agreement expressly requires bank approval of all marketing, the answer is clear: approval is mandatory.
- If Cedar Harbor has already approved the campaign or fee structure, the approval question is resolved and the focus shifts to disclosure adequacy.
- If the final fee schedule shows that no transfer type carries a Juniper Ledger fee and no third-party fees are passed through, the "fee-free" claim becomes more supportable.
- If consumer testing shows that typical customers understand the claim correctly, the net impression risk decreases.
- If the geographic scope is limited to states with narrow UDAP statutes, state law exposure may be reduced.
