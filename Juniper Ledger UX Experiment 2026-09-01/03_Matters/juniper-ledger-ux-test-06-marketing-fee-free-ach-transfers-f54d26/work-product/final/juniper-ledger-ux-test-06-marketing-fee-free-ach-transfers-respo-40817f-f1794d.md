  ## Bottom line


  Do not launch the phrase “fee-free ACH transfers” as currently designed. The fee
  schedule, qualifying conditions, geographic scope, disclosure placement, and returned/unauthorized-transaction
  treatment are not final. The phrase risks a misleading net impression because a
  typical customer may read it to mean that no fee will be paid by the customer, while
  expedited transfers, returns, external-bank actions, network fees, and third-party
  fees may still apply.


  The lower-risk path is to use a narrower claim such as “No Juniper Ledger fee on
  standard ACH transfers,” subject to the same material limits. If Marketing keeps
  “fee-free,” launch only after the fee schedule and eligibility rules are fixed,
  the sponsor bank gives written approval, and consumer testing shows that the full
  presentation is understood.


  ## Required qualifiers and presentation


  Every channel must state the scope next to the claim, in clear and conspicuous text.
  State the number of free inbound and outbound transfers, the measurement period,
  eligibility, and any plan, balance, geographic, or account limits. State that the
  offer covers standard ACH only if that is the actual product rule. State that expedited
  transfers, returned items, unauthorized-transaction handling, and specified external-bank
  actions may carry charges. State that network or third-party bank fees may still
  apply. State that availability and posting timing depend on risk review, settlement,
  cutoff times, and holds.


  Do not put a material limit only behind a link, tooltip, or checkout screen. Paid
  ads and partner landing pages should place a short qualifier beside each prominent
  claim and link to full terms. In the app, show the fee and timing disclosure before
  the user selects a transfer type and again before confirmation. In email, put the
  main qualifier in the body near the headline, not only in a footer. Partner pages
  must use the same approved language and version control. Disclosures must not contradict
  the headline or use unreadable type.


  ## Rules and audiences


  For consumers, evaluate the overall net impression under FTC Act section 5 and the
  FTC guidance on “free” claims, plus the state unfair/deceptive acts and practices

# Legal review: “Fee-free ACH transfers” campaign

## Executive answer

The claim is not ready to use as proposed. The product rules and fee schedule are not final, and the phrase “fee-free ACH transfers” can create the impression that the customer will pay no fee for any ACH-related activity. That impression may be misleading when expedited transfers, returned or unauthorized items, certain external-bank actions, network charges, or third-party bank fees can apply.

A narrower claim is safer: “No Juniper Ledger fee on standard ACH transfers.” Even that claim must match the final product terms and include material limitations. If Marketing keeps “fee-free,” use it only after the fee schedule, eligibility, state scope, disclosures, and returned-item treatment are final; complete consumer testing; and obtain written Cedar Harbor approval.

## Facts and assumptions

Known facts are that the offer will cover a set number of inbound and outbound transfers, the claim will run in paid ads, the app, email, and partner landing pages, and transfer availability depends on risk and settlement timing. Network and third-party fees may still occur. Product has not finalized the fee schedule, qualifying conditions, geographic scope, disclosure placement, or treatment of returned and unauthorized transactions. No consumer research has tested how a typical customer reads “fee-free.”

I assume U.S. advertising law applies and Juniper Ledger offers payment services through Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. The final Cedar Harbor program-management and banking-services agreements are not in the record. The current research packet states that no external authority was retrieved. Its legal discussion is a model-generated lead, not verified authority.

## Supportability and qualifiers

A “free” claim must be true for the service as a typical customer understands it. The headline and the disclosure together must produce a truthful overall impression. A material limitation cannot be hidden in a footer, a separate terms page, a tooltip, or a checkout screen.

At minimum, each presentation must say, next to the claim:

- the exact number of free inbound and outbound transfers;
- the measurement period and whether unused transfers roll over;
- who qualifies, including account, plan, balance, activity, and geographic limits;
- that the offer is limited to standard ACH if expedited transfers cost more;
- the fee treatment for returned and unauthorized transactions;
- which external-bank actions can create a fee;
- that network and third-party fees may still apply; and
- that availability, posting, and settlement timing depend on risk review, cutoffs, holds, and settlement.

Do not use “fee-free ACH transfers” until Product can state these terms precisely. If the schedule includes any fee for a transfer that the headline reasonably covers, change the headline. A possible qualified version is: “Up to [X] standard ACH transfers per [month] with no Juniper Ledger fee. Expedited transfers, returns, certain external-bank actions, and third-party fees may apply. Transfers are subject to risk review, cutoff times, and settlement.”

## Channel controls

For paid ads, place a short qualifier in immediate proximity to every prominent claim. Do not make a click-through the only place that explains a cap or possible fee. The landing page must repeat the same claim and show full terms before sign-up.

In the app, display the fee and timing information before the user selects the transfer type and again before final confirmation. Show standard versus expedited clearly. Identify any fee before the customer submits the transfer.

In email, put the key limit and possible charges in the body near the headline. Do not rely on a footer or a generic terms link. Keep the wording and version aligned with the paid creative.

For partner landing pages, provide approved copy and a controlled version. Partners must not shorten “fee-free” or omit the qualifier. Review every page before publication.

## Consumer and small-business rules

For consumer audiences, assess the complete net impression under FTC Act section 5 and applicable state unfair or deceptive acts and practices laws. The FTC “free” guidance and any current enforcement materials should be verified before final reliance. The geographic scope is still open, so the state-law review cannot be complete.

The FTC Act can also apply to small-business advertising. State coverage varies, but do not assume that a small business will understand “fee-free” to exclude bank or network charges. Use the same clear, conspicuous disclosure standard for both audiences unless documented testing supports a different presentation.

Substantiate the claim before launch. Keep the final fee schedule, eligibility rules, fee examples, timing rules, creative, testing results, and approval record together. Review returned and unauthorized transactions separately because an exception that appears after a customer initiates a transfer can defeat the headline impression.

## Sponsor bank approval

Cedar Harbor should give written approval before any paid ad, app copy, email, or partner page is published. The exact contractual requirement must be confirmed from the program-management agreement. If the agreement requires approval, launch without it would breach the program process. Even if the contract is silent, the bank may have oversight and regulatory exposure for third-party marketing of its deposit or payment services. Keep the approval and the final approved creative in the matter record.

## Recommended path and launch gate

Use “No Juniper Ledger fee on standard ACH transfers” as the default path. Before launch, Product must finalize and sign off on the fee schedule, caps, eligibility, states, external-bank fee handling, returned and unauthorized transactions, and risk and settlement timing. Marketing must supply final creative for all four channels. Counsel must verify the current FTC and state authorities, obtain consumer testing, and obtain written Cedar Harbor approval.

The eight-week date may be achievable only if these items close early enough for a final review. If any material item remains open, delay the campaign or launch only the narrower claim with complete disclosures. Do not record this recommendation as a final business decision until the responsible lawyer chooses the path.

## Questions for Product and Marketing

1. What exact fee applies to each standard, expedited, returned, unauthorized, and external-bank scenario?
2. How many inbound and outbound transfers are free, over what period, and for which accounts or plans?
3. Which states and customer types are in scope?
4. What exact timing and availability language is supported by operations?
5. Has Cedar Harbor reviewed the campaign and fee schedule?
6. What consumer testing will establish how people understand “fee-free”?

## Status

Recommendation: hold the current claim; prefer the narrower claim; require all launch-gate items and Cedar Harbor written approval.

Decision: not recorded. This draft is a first-pass legal response and contains assumptions and unverified authority leads.
