# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Product change: freelancer consumer spending-account onboarding flow with variable income. Key legal workstreams: account classification (consumer vs. business), Reg E/Reg CC consumer protections, ECOA/Reg B and FCRA adverse-action notices, BSA/CIP data retention, sponsor-bank (Cedar Harbor) review triggers, and the permitted point of virtual-card issuance and ACH enablement.

## Decision question

Advise on the permitted onboarding sequence for a freelancer consumer spending-account flow (identity/TIN/occupation/volume/funding collection, automated ID verification, sanctions screening, device-risk, small initial deposit), including required disclosures and adverse-action notices, account classification by intended use, review triggers, data-retention, appeal of failed verification, and the exact point at which Juniper Ledger may issue a virtual/physical card or enable ACH. Product wants to launch a test with selected applicants next quarter.

## Material facts

- Proposed flow collects identity details, tax identification information, occupation, expected monthly volume, and funding source.
- Flow uses automated identity verification, sanctions screening, device-risk signals, and a small initial deposit.
- Customer may receive a virtual card after preliminary approval; physical cards, ACH transfers, and higher limits remain restricted until review.
- Actors in the flow: Juniper Ledger, sponsor bank (Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A.), identity-verification provider, and customer-support team.
- Product wants to launch a test with selected applicants next quarter.
- Freelancers may have irregular income and may use one account for both personal and business expenses.
- Product has not decided whether to classify the account by intended use, whether to request proof of income, or when sponsor-bank review must occur.

## Assumptions

- The account is a consumer spending account (Reg E/Reg CC consumer protections likely apply) unless Product decides otherwise.
- The identity-verification provider may be a consumer-reporting agency, which would trigger FCRA adverse-action duties on denial.
- Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. is the named bank for deposit accounts and payment rails under the program-management agreement.

## Issues and workstreams

- Account classification: whether the account is a consumer deposit account, a business account, or a hybrid given freelancers may use one account for personal and business expenses; classification drives Reg E, Reg CC, and disclosure obligations.
- Regulatory sequencing: whether a virtual card may be issued after preliminary approval before full sponsor-bank review, and the exact point at which ACH and physical cards may be enabled.
- Adverse-action and notice obligations for automated identity-verification and sanctions-screening denials (ECOA/Reg B adverse action, FCRA adverse action if a consumer report is used, and any sponsor-bank notice requirements).
- Data-retention period for identity, verification, and screening records.
- Whether a failed verification can be appealed and what that process must look like.
- Sponsor-bank (Cedar Harbor) review trigger points and program-management agreement obligations.

## Open questions

- Proposed transaction and balance limits for the preliminary (virtual-card) stage and the full-review stage.
- Whether the account will be classified by intended use (consumer vs. business) and whether proof of income will be requested.
- When sponsor-bank review must occur in the sequence.
- Required adverse-action notices and whether the identity-verification provider is a consumer-reporting agency.
- Data-retention period for identity, verification, and screening records.
- Whether a failed verification can be appealed.
- Target launch date for the test.
