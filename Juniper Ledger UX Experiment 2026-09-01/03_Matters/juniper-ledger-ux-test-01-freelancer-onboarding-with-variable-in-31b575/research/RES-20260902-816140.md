# CIP and sponsor-bank review requirements for issuing a virtual card

## Question

CIP and sponsor-bank review requirements for issuing a virtual card before full review

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Juniper Ledger, a fintech without a banking charter, is designing a consumer spending account for freelancers with sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. Product wants to reduce onboarding abandonment by issuing a virtual card after preliminary approval while restricting physical cards, ACH, and higher limits until full review. Counsel is involved because the account classification, sequencing, and disclosure obligations under Reg E and Reg CC are undecided, and the launch is planned for next quarter.
## Decision question
Can Juniper Ledger issue a virtual card to freelancers after preliminary approval but before full sponsor-bank review, and if so, what CIP, disclosure, and limit conditions must be met before enabling ACH and physical cards?
## Open questions
- Is the identity-verification provider a consumer-reporting agency under the FCRA? (Needs vendor contract check.)
- What are the final transaction and balance limits for the preliminary and full-review stages? (Working assumptions are $250 balance/$100 daily card spend pre-review; $5,000 balance/$2,500 daily card spend post-review.)
- When must sponsor-bank (Cedar Harbor) review occur in the flow? (Options: before virtual card issuance, before ACH enablement, or both.)
- What data-retention period will apply to identity, verification, and screening records? (Working assumption: five years after account closure or the record, as applicable.)
- What specific adverse-action notices are required if the IDV provider is not a consumer-reporting agency?
---
### Likely rules/issues
- **CIP and identity verification**: The USA PATRIOT Act and FinCEN rules require a Customer Identification Program (CIP) for new accounts. Juniper Ledger must collect and verify identity details before issuing any card or enabling transactions.
- **Reg E (Electronic Fund Transfer Act)**: Applies to consumer accounts. Requires disclosures, error-resolution procedures, and limits on liability for unauthorized transactions.
- **Reg CC (Expedited Funds Availability Act)**: Governs funds-availability policies for deposit accounts. Disclosures required.
- **ECOA/Reg B (Equal Credit Opportunity Act)**: If the account involves credit (e.g., overdraft or card spend), adverse-action notices are required for denials or unfavorable terms.
- **FCRA (Fair Credit Reporting Act)**: If the IDV provider furnishes consumer reports, FCRA adverse-action notices and disclosures are required.
- **Sanctions screening**: OFAC compliance is mandatory. Failed or ambiguous results must be escalated for manual review.
- **Privacy and data retention**: GLBA and state privacy laws require clear privacy notices and data-retention schedules.
### Viable paths
1. **Issue virtual card after preliminary approval, before full sponsor-bank review**
- Conditions: CIP satisfied, sanctions screening cleared, device-risk checks passed, and Cedar Harbor has approved the program’s preliminary stage.
- Limits: $250 balance, $100/day card spend, no ACH, cash withdrawal, or card-to-card transfers.
- Disclosures: Privacy notice, deposit terms, virtual-card restrictions, and funding-source terms.
2. **Delay virtual card issuance until after full sponsor-bank review**
- Lower risk, but higher abandonment.
- All features (ACH, physical card, higher limits) enabled only after review.
3. **Hybrid approach**
- Issue virtual card after preliminary approval, but require sponsor-bank review before any funds can be loaded or spent.
- ACH and physical card enabled only after full review.
### Facts that could change the answer
- If the IDV provider is a consumer-reporting agency, FCRA adverse-action notices are required.
- If the account is classified as business or hybrid, Reg E and Reg CC may not apply, but other obligations (e.g., UDAAP, state law) may.
- If Cedar Harbor requires review before any card issuance, the sequence must change.
- If Product decides to request proof of income, additional disclosures and retention rules may apply.
