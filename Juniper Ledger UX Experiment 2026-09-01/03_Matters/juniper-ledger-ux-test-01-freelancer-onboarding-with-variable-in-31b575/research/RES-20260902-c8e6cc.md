# Advise on the permitted onboarding sequence, required disclosures and notices

## Question

Advise on the permitted onboarding sequence, required disclosures and notices, account classification, review triggers, and the exact point at which Juniper Ledger may issue a card or enable ACH for a freelancer consumer spending account test launching next quarter.

## Matter summary
Juniper Ledger, a fintech without a banking charter, wants to test a streamlined onboarding flow for freelancers opening consumer spending accounts, working through its sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. The proposed flow would collect identity, tax, occupation, volume, and funding information; run automated identity verification, sanctions screening, and device-risk checks; and issue a virtual card after preliminary approval while restricting physical cards, ACH, and higher limits until further review. Counsel is involved because the flow implicates CIP/KYC, sanctions, consumer-protection, adverse-action, privacy, and sponsor-bank oversight obligations, and Product needs answers before a planned launch next quarter. Key open items include account classification, transaction limits, adverse-action notices, data retention, and whether failed verifications can be appealed.
## Decision question
For next quarter's freelancer consumer spending account pilot, what onboarding sequence, disclosures, account classification, review triggers, and specific control points must Juniper Ledger implement before it may issue a virtual card or enable ACH, given that Cedar Harbor Bank is the named bank and the identity-verification provider's FCRA status is unconfirmed?
## Open questions
- Is the identity-verification provider a consumer-reporting agency under the FCRA? (Needs vendor contract review.)
- What are the final approved transaction and balance limits for the preliminary and post-review stages? (Working assumptions are $250 balance / $100/day card spend preliminary; $5,000 balance / $2,500/day post-review.)
- When must Cedar Harbor's sponsor-bank review occur in the flow — before virtual-card issuance, before ACH enablement, or at another trigger point?
- What data-retention period will Product and the sponsor bank commit to for identity, verification, and screening records? (Working assumption is at least five years after account closure.)
- Will the account be classified as consumer, business, or hybrid, and will proof of income be requested by default or only under specific risk triggers?

## Likely rules and issues
**Customer Identification Program (CIP) and KYC.** Before opening any account, Juniper Ledger and Cedar Harbor must collect and verify identity information sufficient to form a reasonable belief that the customer is who they claim to be. The proposed collection of identity details, tax ID, occupation, expected volume, and funding source aligns with CIP and risk-based KYC. Automated verification is permissible, but failed or ambiguous results must route to a documented manual review process.
**Sanctions screening.** OFAC sanctions screening must occur before account opening and on an ongoing basis. A true sanctions match blocks account opening entirely. Screening logic and results must not be disclosed to the applicant in a way that tips off monitoring.
**Account classification — consumer vs. business.** This drives which consumer-protection regulations apply. If the account is marketed and structured as a consumer spending account, Regulation E (electronic fund transfers), Regulation CC (funds availability), and other consumer protections apply even if some freelancers use it for business expenses. A "hybrid" label does not reduce regulatory obligations. If Product later offers a separate business account, that account would need its own disclosures, eligibility criteria, and controls.
**Adverse action — ECOA/Reg B and FCRA.** If the identity-verification provider furnishes a consumer report (i.e., it is a consumer-reporting agency under the FCRA), then any denial or adverse action based on that report triggers FCRA adverse-action notice requirements, including the provider's name and contact information and the applicant's right to dispute. Even if the provider is not a CRA, ECOA/Regulation B may require adverse-action notices if the account is considered credit. At minimum, clear status messages and a human appeal path are needed.
**Privacy and data use.** Collecting tax ID, occupation, income expectations, and device-risk signals requires a concise privacy notice explaining the purpose of each field, how data will be used, and with whom it will be shared (including the IDV provider and sponsor bank). Data-retention periods must be documented and consistent with the sponsor bank's schedule and applicable law.
**Sponsor-bank oversight.** Cedar Harbor, as the named bank for deposits and payment rails, must approve the program's preliminary stage and define when its review occurs. Juniper Ledger's program-management agreement with Cedar Harbor will control the exact sequencing and approval gates.
**Card-network rules.** Virtual-card issuance and any subsequent physical-card fulfillment must comply with applicable card-network rules, which typically require issuer (bank) approval and may impose additional identity-verification or risk requirements.
## Viable paths
**Path A — Conservative staged rollout (recommended).** Treat the account as a consumer account. Collect all required information upfront with clear privacy notices. Run CIP, sanctions screening, and device-risk checks. Route failures to manual review. Issue a virtual card only after Cedar Harbor approves the preliminary stage, CIP is satisfied, and sanctions screening is cleared, with a $250 balance cap and $100/day card spend. Do not enable ACH, cash withdrawal, or card-to-card transfers. Complete full sponsor-bank review and identity resolution before enabling ACH, physical cards, or higher limits. Send all required adverse-action and approval notices. Offer a human appeal/reconsideration process.
**Path B — Faster preliminary approval with deferred bank review.** Issue the virtual card after automated checks pass but before Cedar Harbor's formal review. This increases speed but raises risk: if the bank later declines the customer, Juniper Ledger must unwind the account and card. This path requires explicit Cedar Harbor approval and robust unwind procedures. It is not recommended for a first pilot.
**Path C — Business account classification.** Classify the account as a business account to reduce consumer-protection obligations. This is not recommended for this pilot because the product is marketed as a consumer spending account, and freelancers may use it for personal expenses. Misclassification creates regulatory and reputational risk.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **IDV provider's FCRA status.** If the provider is a consumer-reporting agency, FCRA adverse-action notices are mandatory. If not, only ECOA/Reg B (if applicable) and general fairness principles apply.
- **Cedar Harbor's review timing.** If the bank requires review before any card issuance, the virtual card cannot be issued at the preliminary stage. If the bank delegates preliminary approval to Juniper Ledger, the sequence can be faster.
- **Final transaction and balance limits.** Higher limits may trigger enhanced due diligence or proof-of-income requirements. Lower limits may reduce risk but also reduce product utility.
- **Account classification decision.** If Product decides to market the account as a business account, different regulations apply, but the product must then be structured and disclosed accordingly.
- **Proof-of-income requirement.** If Product decides to request proof of income by default, this adds friction and requires a clear privacy and retention rationale. If requested only under risk triggers, the triggers must be documented.
- **Appeal process scope.** If the appeal process is limited to automated denials only, it may not satisfy fairness expectations. A broader human-review path is preferable.
## Recommended last-mile verification
Before launch, counsel should:
1. **Review the IDV provider contract** to confirm whether it is a consumer-reporting agency under the FCRA and what adverse-action obligations apply.
2. **Confirm Cedar Harbor's program agreement** to determine the exact point at which sponsor-bank review must occur and what approval gates exist for virtual-card issuance, ACH enablement, and limit increases.
3. **Finalize transaction and balance limits** with Product and Cedar Harbor, and document the risk rationale.
4. **Draft and review all customer-facing disclosures**, including privacy notices, deposit and funds-availability terms, virtual-card restrictions, adverse-action templates, and appeal-process instructions.
5. **Document the data-retention schedule** for identity, verification, and screening records, consistent with the sponsor bank's schedule and applicable law (working assumption: at least five years after account closure).
6. **Test the manual review and appeal process** to ensure failed or ambiguous verifications route correctly and applicants receive clear, actionable status messages.
7. **Confirm card-network rules** with Cedar Harbor or the card issuer to ensure virtual-card issuance and subsequent physical-card fulfillment comply with network requirements.
