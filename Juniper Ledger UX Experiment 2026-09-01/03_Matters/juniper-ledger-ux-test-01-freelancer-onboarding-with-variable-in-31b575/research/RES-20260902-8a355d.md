# ECOA/Reg B and FCRA adverse-action notice requirements for automated identity-ve

## Question

ECOA/Reg B and FCRA adverse-action notice requirements for automated identity-verification and sanctions-screening denials

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Juniper Ledger, a fintech without a banking charter, is designing a consumer spending account for freelancers with sponsor bank Cedar Harbor Bank, N.A. Product wants to reduce onboarding abandonment by issuing a virtual card after preliminary approval while restricting physical cards, ACH, and higher limits until full review. Counsel is involved because the account classification, sequencing, and disclosure obligations under Reg E and Reg CC are undecided, and the identity-verification provider's status as a consumer-reporting agency under FCRA is unverified. Product wants to launch a test with selected applicants next quarter.
## Decision question
Before next quarter's pilot launch, what adverse-action notices must Juniper Ledger send when its automated identity-verification or sanctions-screening systems deny a freelancer's application for a consumer spending account, and do those obligations change if the identity-verification provider is a consumer-reporting agency under the FCRA?
## Open questions
- Is the identity-verification provider a consumer-reporting agency that furnishes consumer reports under the FCRA? (Vendor contract check is pending.)
- What are the final transaction and balance limits for the preliminary virtual-card stage and after full review? (Working assumptions are $250 balance/$100 daily spend preliminary; $5,000 balance/$2,500 daily spend post-review.)
- When must sponsor-bank (Cedar Harbor) review occur in the flow — before virtual-card issuance, or only before physical card/ACH enablement?
- What data-retention period will Product set for identity, verification, and sanctions-screening records? (Working assumption is five years after account closure.)
- Will the account be classified as consumer, business, or hybrid, given freelancers may use one account for personal and business expenses?
## Likely rules and issues
**ECOA/Reg B adverse-action requirements.** If Juniper Ledger denies a credit application (including a consumer spending account with overdraft or credit features) based on automated identity verification or sanctions screening, ECOA and Regulation B require an adverse-action notice. The notice must include: (1) the specific reasons for the action taken, or (2) a statement that the applicant has the right to request the reasons within 60 days, along with the name, address, and telephone number of the person to contact. The notice must be sent within 30 days of the adverse action. If the denial is based in whole or in part on information from a consumer-reporting agency, the notice must also include the agency's name, address, and telephone number, and a statement that the agency did not make the decision and cannot explain the reasons.
**FCRA adverse-action requirements.** If the identity-verification provider is a consumer-reporting agency and furnishes a consumer report that contributes to the denial, FCRA § 615(a) requires a separate adverse-action notice. That notice must include: (1) the name, address, and telephone number of the consumer-reporting agency; (2) a statement that the agency did not make the decision and cannot explain the reasons; (3) notice of the applicant's right to obtain a free copy of the consumer report within 60 days; and (4) notice of the applicant's right to dispute the accuracy or completeness of the report. If Juniper Ledger uses a credit score in the decision, FCRA § 615(a) also requires disclosure of the score and key factors.
**Sanctions-screening denials.** OFAC sanctions-screening denials are not credit decisions and do not trigger ECOA/Reg B adverse-action notices. However, if the denial is based on a consumer report (e.g., a sanctions list furnished by a consumer-reporting agency), FCRA adverse-action requirements apply. Juniper Ledger should avoid revealing the specific sanctions hit or screening logic to prevent tipping off the applicant, but must still provide a general adverse-action notice if FCRA applies.
**Device-risk signals.** If device-risk signals contribute to a denial and are furnished by a consumer-reporting agency, FCRA adverse-action requirements apply. If the signals are generated internally or by a non-CRA vendor, ECOA/Reg B may still require a reason if the denial is a credit decision. Juniper Ledger should not treat a device-risk score alone as a denial reason without human review.
**Appeal/reconsideration process.** Juniper Ledger has committed to offering an appeal/reconsideration process for failed verification. This process should allow the applicant to submit additional documentation or correct errors, and should include a human review. The adverse-action notice should inform the applicant of this right and the process for requesting reconsideration.
## Viable paths
**Path 1: Assume FCRA applies and send combined ECOA/FCRA notices.** If the identity-verification provider is a consumer-reporting agency, Juniper Ledger should send a combined adverse-action notice that satisfies both ECOA/Reg B and FCRA. The notice should include the specific reasons for denial (or the right to request them), the CRA's contact information, the right to a free report, the right to dispute, and the appeal/reconsideration process. This is the conservative approach and avoids the risk of under-noticing.
**Path 2: Confirm the IDV provider's status before launch.** Juniper Ledger should review the vendor contract to confirm whether the identity-verification provider is a consumer-reporting agency. If it is not, FCRA does not apply, and only ECOA/Reg B notices are required (if the denial is a credit decision). This path requires legal review of the contract before launch.
**Path 3: Separate notices for sanctions-screening denials.** If a denial is based solely on a sanctions hit (not a consumer report), Juniper Ledger should send a general adverse-action notice that does not reveal the specific sanctions list or screening logic, but informs the applicant of the denial and the appeal process. This notice should be reviewed by counsel to ensure it does not violate OFAC tipping-off prohibitions.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **IDV provider's FCRA status.** If the provider is not a consumer-reporting agency, FCRA adverse-action requirements do not apply, and only ECOA/Reg B notices are required (if the denial is a credit decision).
- **Account classification.** If the account is classified as a business account (not consumer), ECOA/Reg B may not apply, and FCRA applies only if the applicant is a natural person and the report is used for personal, family, or household purposes.
- **Credit features.** If the account does not include overdraft or credit features, ECOA/Reg B may not apply, and only FCRA (if applicable) governs.
- **Sanctions-screening source.** If the sanctions list is not furnished by a consumer-reporting agency, FCRA does not apply to sanctions-screening denials.
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Confirm the IDV provider's FCRA status.** Review the vendor contract to determine whether the identity-verification provider is a consumer-reporting agency. If it is, confirm that Juniper Ledger's adverse-action notices include all required FCRA content.
2. **Confirm account classification.** Decide whether the account is consumer, business, or hybrid. If consumer, ensure ECOA/Reg B and FCRA notices are in place. If business, confirm whether ECOA/Reg B applies and whether FCRA applies to natural-person applicants.
3. **Draft and review adverse-action notice templates.** Prepare templates for ECOA/Reg B and FCRA notices, including the specific reasons for denial, CRA contact information, free-report and dispute rights, and the appeal/reconsideration process. Have counsel review the templates before launch.
4. **Confirm sponsor-bank review timing.** Decide when Cedar Harbor's review must occur in the flow — before virtual-card issuance, or only before physical card/ACH enablement. Ensure the adverse-action notices reflect the correct review stage.
5. **Document the appeal/reconsideration process.** Ensure the process is documented, includes human review, and is communicated to applicants in the adverse-action notice.
