    text: '


      For each candidate country, counsel should determine, in order:


      1. **Does the activity fall within the local "money transmission" / "payment
      service" definition?** This turns on custody/control of funds, receipt-for-transmission,
      and whether Mosaic Relay is the contracting "provider" of the payment service.

      2. **Who holds the regulatory permission?** Mosaic Relay''s own license, a licensed
      partner''s permission (agent/partner-of-record), or a third-party provider''s
      permission with Mosaic Relay purely as technology.

      3. **Is an agent/partner-of-record model available?** Many regimes (PSD2/EEA,
      UK PSRs) allow agents of a licensed principal, but the agent must be registered
      and the principal remains liable. Some regimes do not offer a clean agent path.

      4. **What does the contracting entity drive?** The entity that contracts with
      merchants and sellers may be deemed the "provider," triggering licensing in
      that entity''s jurisdiction. A single global entity may need licenses in every
      target country; local entities may need licenses per country.

# Country-Approval Gate Framework — International Expansion and Local Payment Methods

**Matter:** Mosaic Relay — International Expansion and Local Payment Methods
**Work product type:** Draft (first-pass, model-only — no external authority retrieved)
**Status:** For review; do not rely on as final legal analysis until local counsel confirms per-country requirements.

---

## 0. How to read this document

This is the **country-approval gate framework**: the reusable documentation package each candidate country must clear before it can be added to the launch list. It is deliberately structured so that reported facts, assumptions, missing facts, unverified leads, recommendations, and decisions are kept separate and auditable.

- **Reported facts** are what is established from the matter record and request.
- **Assumptions** are what the analysis leans on that is not yet established.
- **Missing facts** are the inputs that must be supplied before any country is approved.
- **Unverified leads** are model-only regulatory observations that have not been confirmed by local counsel or external authority.
- **Recommendations** are the paths proposed for decision.
- **Decisions** are recorded only when the lawyer explicitly directs them; none are recorded in this draft.

The target country list is **not yet selected**, and funds-flow custody, contracting entities, payout providers, and data locations are all **still undetermined**. Those open items materially change the per-country answer, so this framework is structured as a decision framework plus a jurisdiction map, not a final per-country determination.

---

## 1. Reported facts (established)

The following are established from the matter record and request:

1. Mosaic Relay is **not a bank**, does **not hold deposits**, and works through external payment and infrastructure providers.
2. The proposed experience uses **one global onboarding flow**, local payment options at checkout, automated currency conversion, and cross-border payouts to sellers and contractors.
3. Product development would begin **this quarter**, with a **pilot in six months** and broader release after operational testing.
4. The business goal is to help existing customers expand without building separate payment operations for each country.
5. The target countries are **not yet selected**; the country list is a launch gate.
6. Funds-flow custody, contracting entity, payout provider, and data locations are **all still being designed** (not yet determined).
7. Mosaic Relay's existing footprint is North America, the UK, the EU, and selected other markets.

---

## 2. The controlling question (applies to every workstream)

The single most decision-changing question across the entire framework is: **Does Mosaic Relay ever take custody or control of customer funds at any point in the cross-border flow?**

- **If yes (even momentarily):** Mosaic Relay is likely a money transmitter / payment institution in most target jurisdictions, needs its own license (or a registered agent arrangement with a licensed principal that remains liable), and carries direct AML/KYC, consumer, and disclosure obligations.
- **If no (pure technology layer, all funds held and moved by licensed acquiring/local payment and payout providers):** Mosaic Relay may avoid licensure in some jurisdictions, or operate as an agent/partner-of-record of a locally licensed entity, with a materially lighter compliance footprint.

Because funds-flow custody is not yet determined, this framework treats both branches as live and maps what each implies for every workstream.

## 4. Workstream A — Licensing / agent-partner-of-record

### 4.1 Decision framework (how to classify each candidate country)

For each candidate country, counsel should determine, in order:

1. **Does the activity fall within the local "money transmission" / "payment service" definition?** This turns on custody/control of funds, receipt-for-transmission, and whether Mosaic Relay is the contracting "provider" of the payment service.
2. **Who holds the regulatory permission?** Mosaic Relay's own license, a licensed partner's permission (agent/partner-of-record), or a third-party provider's permission with Mosaic Relay purely as technology.
3. **Is an agent/partner-of-record model available?** Many regimes (PSD2/EEA, UK PSRs) allow agents of a licensed principal, but the agent must be registered and the principal remains liable. Some regimes do not offer a clean agent path.
4. **What does the contracting entity drive?** The entity that contracts with merchants and sellers may be deemed the "provider," triggering licensing in that entity's jurisdiction. A single global entity may need licenses in every target country; local entities may need licenses per country.
5. **What do the local payment methods require?** Some local methods (e.g., iDEAL, Sofort) have scheme rules that require a local licensed partner regardless of Mosaic Relay's own status.

### 4.2 Jurisdiction map (unverified leads — model-only)

> **Important:** This section is drawn from the first-pass research packet and general regulatory frameworks. It is **model-only** — no external authority was retrieved (the external research run timed out). Every entry must be verified by local counsel before any country is approved.

**United States (federal and state)**
- Money transmission: 48 states plus DC and territories require money transmitter licenses for entities that "receive money for transmission" or "transmit money." The definition generally hinges on custody and control of funds.
- If Mosaic Relay never takes custody and acts solely as a technology layer, it may avoid licensure in some states under "agent of the payee" or "payment processor" exemptions. If it contracts directly with merchants and controls the flow of funds (even via third-party custodians), it may be deemed a money transmitter.
- Agent of the payee exemption: available in many states (e.g., California, New York, Texas) if Mosaic Relay acts as the merchant's agent and the merchant's obligation to the customer is satisfied upon payment to Mosaic Relay. Requires careful contractual structuring and disclosure.
- Federal MSB registration: if Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter, it must register with FinCEN as a Money Services Business and implement a BSA/AML program.

**European Union / EEA**
- PSD2: requires authorization as a Payment Institution (PI) or Electronic Money Institution (EMI) for "payment services" (money remittance, payment initiation, account information). If Mosaic Relay provides payment services, it needs a license in an EU member state and can passport across the EEA.
- Agent model: PSD2 allows PIs to operate through registered agents, with the PI remaining liable. Mosaic Relay could act as an agent of a licensed PI (e.g., its acquiring or payout partner) if it does not hold funds.
- Local payment methods: some local methods (e.g., iDEAL in the Netherlands, Sofort in Germany) may have scheme rules requiring local licensing or partnership with a local PI.

**United Kingdom**
- Payment Services Regulations 2017 (PSRs): mirror PSD2. Requires authorization as an Authorized Payment Institution (API) or Small Payment Institution (SPI) for payment services; FCA-regulated.
- Agent model: APIs can operate through FCA-registered agents. Mosaic Relay could act as an agent of a licensed API.

**Canada**
- Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA): requires registration with the Bank of Canada for "retail payment activities," including electronic funds transfers. Mosaic Relay's facilitation of merchant payments likely falls in scope.
- Provincial money services business laws: Quebec requires a money services business license; other provinces may have similar requirements.

**Other markets (examples)**
- Australia: Australian Financial Services Licence (AFSL) for "non-cash payment facilities" unless an exemption applies.
- Singapore: Major Payment Institution (MPI) or Standard Payment Institution (SPI) license under the Payment Services Act for account issuance, domestic/cross-border money transfer, and merchant acquisition.
- Japan: Funds Transfer Service Provider (FTSP) license for money transmission.
- Brazil: authorization as a Payment Institution (IP) under the Central Bank of Brazil.
- Mexico: Fintech Law license for electronic payment funds / related services.

### 4.3 Viable paths (recommendations for decision — not yet decided)

1. **Agent/Partner-of-Record Model (recommended for pilot):** Mosaic Relay acts as agent/partner-of-record for a locally licensed PI in each country. Avoids license time/cost; leverages partner permissions; faster to market. Trade-offs: limited compliance control, potential liability for partner actions, not available in all jurisdictions.
2. **Obtain Licenses in Key Markets:** Mosaic Relay obtains its own money transmitter (US), PI/EMI (EU/UK), and equivalent licenses elsewhere. Full control but slow (6–18 months per jurisdiction) and costly (fees, capital, local presence/directors).
3. **Hybrid Model:** Own licenses in key markets; agent/partner-of-record in smaller or less strategic markets. Balances control and speed; adds complexity.
4. **Pure Technology Provider Model:** Structure services to avoid "payment services"/"money transmission" definitions by never taking custody and acting solely as a technology layer. Avoids licensing but may not be feasible everywhere and limits service scope.

## 5. Workstream B — Cross-border AML/KYC and sanctions

### 5.1 What is established
- Mosaic Relay does not hold deposits and works through external providers.
- The proposed flow includes one global onboarding flow and cross-border payouts to sellers and contractors.

### 5.2 Likely obligations (unverified leads — model-only)
- **AML/KYC:** Each target country has its own AML/KYC regime. Likely obligations include customer due diligence (CDD), enhanced due diligence (EDD) for high-risk customers, and ongoing monitoring. Even if Mosaic Relay avoids licensing, it may still be subject to AML/KYC obligations as a financial institution or designated non-financial business or profession (DNFBP) in some jurisdictions.
- **Sanctions screening:** Mosaic Relay must screen payouts against relevant sanctions lists, which vary by country and change over time. Requires a robust system and clear ownership.
- **Transaction monitoring:** Effective transaction monitoring must detect and report suspicious activity per local rules, including thresholds and unusual-pattern detection.
- **KYB/KYC for merchants and recipients:** The global onboarding flow must include KYB for merchants and KYC for sellers/contractors/recipients, with risk-based controls and human review for material exceptions.

### 5.3 Controls to design (recommendations)
- A single sanctions-screening engine with jurisdiction-specific list coverage and dynamic list updates.
- KYB/KYC program covering merchants and recipients, with EDD for high-risk categories.
- Transaction monitoring with per-jurisdiction thresholds and suspicious-activity reporting (SAR/STR) obligations.
- Clear allocation of AML responsibility between Mosaic Relay and its licensed partners (who files, who monitors).

## What would change this

- **Working assumption — no custody:** The analysis assumes Mosaic Relay will not take custody of customer funds at any point. If it does take custody (even momentarily), it will likely need a money transmitter or payment institution license in most jurisdictions, materially increasing time and cost, and will carry direct AML/KYC, consumer, and disclosure obligations. This is the single biggest swing factor and is unresolved.
- **Open fork — target countries:** The analysis assumes the new countries sit within Mosaic Relay's existing footprint (North America, UK, EU, selected other markets). If the list includes markets with restrictive or complex licensing regimes (e.g., China, India, Russia), the paths and analysis change significantly. The country list is not yet selected.
- **Open fork — contracting entity:** Whether Mosaic Relay uses a single global entity or local entities determines which jurisdiction(s) are deemed the "provider," where licensing is triggered, tax presence, and consumer-law applicability. Undetermined.
- **Not examined — local payment method scheme rules:** The specific rules for each local payment method (e.g., iDEAL, Sofort, Alipay, WeChat Pay) were not examined; some require local licensing or a local licensed partner.
- **Not examined — external authority:** This framework is model-only. No external legal authority was retrieved (the external research run timed out), and no local counsel opinions were obtained. Every per-country entry must be verified before reliance.
- **Not examined — tax, consumer, sanctions, and privacy specifics:** These workstreams are flagged and structured but not developed to per-country specificity in this draft; they are inputs that require local counsel confirmation.
