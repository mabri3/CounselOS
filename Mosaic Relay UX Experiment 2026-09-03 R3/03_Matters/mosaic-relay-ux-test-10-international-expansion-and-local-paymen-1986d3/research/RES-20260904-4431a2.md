      Advise on the required regulatory analysis before Mosaic Relay launches international

      ## Matter summary

      Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company (not a bank, does not hold deposits),
      plans to support merchants accepting cards, ACH, and selected local payment
      methods across several new countries. The proposed experience uses one global
      onboarding flow, local checkout options, automated currency conversion, and
      cross-border payouts to sellers and contractors. Counsel is involved because
      the expansion triggers licensing, AML/KYC, privacy, consumer protection, tax,
      sanctions, disclosure, and payout requirements that vary by country and must
      be documented before any country is added to the launch list. Product development
      begins this quarter, with a pilot in six months and broader release after operational
      testing.

      ## Decision question

      Which specific countries can Mosaic Relay add to its international launch list,
      and what minimum facts and documentation must each candidate country clear —
      covering licensing/agent structure, funds flow, AML/KYC, privacy, consumer,
      tax, sanctions, disclosure, and payout requirements — before it is approved
      for the six-month pilot?

      ## Open questions

      - Which specific countries are the target for this expansion? (Drives all downstream
      analysis — licensing, AML/KYC, privacy, disclosures are country-specific.)

      - In the proposed cross-border flow, who holds customer funds at each step —
      does Mosaic Relay ever take custody, or do acquiring/local payment providers
      and payout providers hold and move all funds?

      - Which Mosaic Relay entity would contract with merchants, sellers, and local
      payment providers in the new countries — a single global entity, or local/regional
      entities per country?

      - Which payout provider(s) would carry the cross-border payouts to sellers and
      contractors?

      - Where will customer and merchant data be located for the new countries — existing
      Mosaic Relay data centers/regions, or in-country/local data storage?

      ---

      ## Likely rules and issues

      ### 1. Money transmission and licensing exposure

      Mosaic Relay is not a bank and does not hold deposits, but cross-border payouts
      and local payment acceptance may still trigger licensing or agent/partner-of-record
      structures in each target country. The critical question is whether Mosaic Relay
      ever takes custody of funds or acts as a principal in the payment flow. If it
      does, it may need money transmitter licenses (US state-by-state), payment institution
      licenses (EU/UK), or local equivalents. If it operates purely as a technology
      layer with licensed partners holding and moving funds, it may avoid direct licensing
      but must ensure partner agreements clearly allocate regulatory responsibility.

      ### 2. Cross-border AML/KYC controls

      Each new country requires sanctions screening (OFAC, EU, UN, local lists), KYB/KYC
      for merchants and recipients, and transaction monitoring across jurisdictions.
      The one global onboarding flow must accommodate country-specific identity verification
      requirements, beneficial ownership thresholds, and enhanced due diligence triggers.
      Cross-border payouts to sellers and contractors require screening of both senders
      and recipients, plus monitoring for structuring, sanctions evasion, and fraud
      patterns.

      ### 3. Consumer protection and funds-flow disclosures

      Local payment methods often carry specific consumer cancellation rights, refund
      rules, and disclosure requirements. Cross-border payouts may be subject to remittance
      transfer rules (e.g., US Remittance Rule, EU Payment Services Directive) requiring
      pre-payment disclosures, cancellation rights, and error resolution procedures.
      Automated currency conversion must disclose exchange rates, fees, and the total
      cost to the payer.

      ### 4. Privacy and data protection

      Data locations for the new countries are not yet decided. Cross-border data
      transfers require adequacy determinations, standard contractual clauses, or
      other transfer mechanisms. The one global onboarding flow must accommodate country-specific
      data localization requirements (e.g., Russia, China, India, certain EU member
      states for certain data types). Controller/processor allocation between Mosaic
      Relay, merchants, and local payment providers must be documented.

      ### 5. Contracting structure

      Which Mosaic Relay entity contracts with merchants, sellers, and local payment
      providers in each country affects tax, regulatory, and liability exposure. A
      single global entity simplifies operations but may create permanent establishment
      risks and complicate local licensing. Local/regional entities add complexity
      but may be required for licensing, tax, or data localization reasons.

      ### 6. Marketing limits and local disclosure requirements

      Marketing claims about payment acceptance, speed, cost, and availability must
      comply with local advertising standards and financial promotion rules. Some
      countries restrict marketing of payment services to licensed entities or require
      specific disclosures about fees, exchange rates, and consumer rights.

      ### 7. Tax obligations

      Cross-border payouts may trigger withholding tax, VAT/GST on fees, and reporting
      obligations (e.g., DAC7 in the EU, 1099-K in the US). The contracting structure
      and funds flow determine which entity has tax collection and reporting responsibility.

      ---

      ## Viable paths

      ### Path A: Agent/partner-of-record model (recommended first pass)

      Mosaic Relay''s existing acquiring/processing partners and local payment providers
      carry the licensed money-transmission activity in each new country. Mosaic Relay
      operates as a technology layer, never taking custody of funds. This minimizes
      direct licensing burden but requires:

      - Partner agreements that clearly allocate regulatory responsibility

      - Due diligence on each partner''s licenses and compliance posture

      - Funds-flow diagrams showing Mosaic Relay never touches customer funds

      - Country-by-country confirmation that the partner''s license covers the proposed
      activity

      ### Path B: Direct licensing

      Mosaic Relay obtains money transmitter licenses, payment institution licenses,
      or local equivalents in each target country. This provides maximum control but
      is slow, expensive, and may not be justified for a pilot. Consider only if the
      partner model cannot support the desired product experience or if a key market
      requires direct licensing.

      ### Path C: Hybrid model

      Direct licensing in one or two anchor markets (e.g., EU via a single payment
      institution license passported across member states) plus partner model elsewhere.
      This balances control and speed but requires careful structuring to avoid regulatory
      arbitrage concerns.

      ---

      ## Country-approval gate framework

      No country is approved for launch until its licensing, funds flow, AML/KYC,
      privacy, consumer, tax, sanctions, disclosure, and payout requirements are documented.
      The minimum facts and documentation for each candidate country:

      ### Licensing/agent

      - [ ] Confirm whether Mosaic Relay needs a license or can operate under a partner''s
      license

      - [ ] If partner model: obtain partner''s license details, regulatory standing,
      and confirmation of coverage for proposed activity

      - [ ] If direct licensing: identify license type, application timeline, capital
      requirements, and local presence requirements

      ### Funds flow

      - [ ] Document step-by-step funds flow showing who holds customer funds at each
      step

      - [ ] Confirm Mosaic Relay never takes custody of funds (or document custody
      points and associated licensing)

      - [ ] Identify all payment providers, acquiring banks, and payout providers
      in the flow

      ### AML/KYC

      - [ ] Map country-specific KYC/KYB requirements (identity documents, beneficial
      ownership thresholds, enhanced due diligence triggers)

      - [ ] Confirm sanctions screening coverage (OFAC, EU, UN, local lists)

      - [ ] Document transaction monitoring rules and reporting obligations

      ### Privacy

      - [ ] Determine data localization requirements

      - [ ] Document cross-border data transfer mechanisms

      - [ ] Allocate controller/processor roles between Mosaic Relay, merchants, and
      local providers

      ### Consumer

      - [ ] Map cancellation rights, refund rules, and disclosure requirements for
      each local payment method

      - [ ] Confirm remittance transfer rule applicability for cross-border payouts

      - [ ] Document currency conversion disclosure requirements

      ### Tax

      - [ ] Identify withholding tax obligations on cross-border payouts

      - [ ] Determine VAT/GST treatment of fees

      - [ ] Confirm reporting obligations (DAC7, 1099-K, local equivalents)

      ### Sanctions

      - [ ] Confirm country is not subject to comprehensive sanctions

      - [ ] Document screening procedures for local sanctions lists

      ### Disclosure

      - [ ] Identify required pre-contractual and transactional disclosures

      - [ ] Confirm marketing material approval requirements

      ### Payout

      payouts

      - [ ] Document payout timing, currency, and fee disclosure requirements
