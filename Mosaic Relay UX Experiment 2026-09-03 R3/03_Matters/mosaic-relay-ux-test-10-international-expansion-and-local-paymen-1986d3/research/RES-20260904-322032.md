      ## Question

      ## Working Analysis


      **No external authority retrieved**


      ## Matter summary

      Mosaic Relay, a non-bank payment infrastructure provider, plans to expand into
      several new countries to support merchant acceptance of cards, ACH, and local
      payment methods, alongside automated currency conversion and cross-border payouts
      to sellers and contractors. The company does not hold deposits and relies on
      external acquiring, processing, and payout partners. Counsel is involved because
      the six-month pilot timeline requires immediate determination of money-transmission
      licensing or agent/partner-of-record requirements in each target jurisdiction
      before the country list is finalized. The specific countries remain unselected,
      and funds-flow custody, contracting entities, and data locations are still undetermined.

      ## Decision question

      Which specific countries will Mosaic Relay target for its six-month pilot, and
      for each, will it operate under its own money-transmitter or payment-institution
      license, act as an agent or partner-of-record for a locally licensed entity,
      or rely entirely on third-party providers to hold the regulatory permissions—given
      that the company does not hold deposits and has not yet finalized funds-flow
      custody or contracting entities?

      ## Open questions

      - Which specific countries are targeted for the pilot and broader release? (This
      determines whether Mosaic Relay needs its own license, can passport under EU/EEA
      rules, or must use an agent model.)

      - Does Mosaic Relay ever take custody of customer funds at any point in the
      cross-border flow, or do acquiring/local payment providers and payout providers
      hold and move all funds? (Custody triggers licensing; pure technology provision
      may not.)

      - Which Mosaic Relay entity will contract with merchants, sellers, and local
      payment providers in each country—a single global entity or local/regional entities?
      (This affects licensing jurisdiction, tax presence, and consumer protection
      applicability.)

      - Which payout provider(s) will carry the cross-border payouts, and what licenses
      do they hold in each target country? (Determines whether Mosaic Relay can rely
      on their permissions or needs its own.)

      - Where will customer and merchant data be located for the new countries? (Affects
      data-protection compliance and may influence local licensing requirements.)

      ## Likely rules and issues

      **No external authority retrieved.**

      Based on the matter facts and general regulatory frameworks applicable to non-bank
      payment infrastructure providers, the following rules and issues are likely
      to govern the licensing analysis:

      **United States (Federal and State)**

      - **Money Transmission Laws:** 48 states plus DC and territories require money
      transmitter licenses for entities that "receive money for transmission" or "transmit
      money." The definition typically hinges on custody and control of funds. If
      Mosaic Relay never takes custody (even momentarily) and acts solely as a technology
      layer, it may avoid licensure in some states under "agent of the payee" or "payment
      processor" exemptions. However, if it contracts directly with merchants and
      controls the flow of funds (even via third-party custodians), it may be deemed
      a money transmitter.

      - **Agent of the Payee Exemption:** Available in many states (e.g., California,
      New York, Texas) if Mosaic Relay acts as the agent of the merchant (payee) and
      the merchant''s obligation to the customer is satisfied upon payment to Mosaic
      Relay. This requires careful contractual structuring and disclosure.

      - **Federal MSB Registration:** If Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter, it must
      register with FinCEN as a Money Services Business (MSB) and implement a BSA/AML
      program.

      **European Union / European Economic Area**

      - **Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2):** Requires authorization as a Payment
      Institution (PI) or Electronic Money Institution (EMI) for "payment services"
      including money remittance, payment initiation, and account information services.
      If Mosaic Relay provides "payment services" (e.g., executing payment transactions,
      issuing payment instruments), it needs a license in an EU member state and can
      passport across the EEA.

      - **Agent Model:** PSD2 allows PIs to operate through agents, but the agent
      must be registered and the PI remains liable. Mosaic Relay could act as an agent
      of a licensed PI (e.g., its acquiring or payout partner) if it does not hold
      funds.

      - **Local Payment Methods:** Some local payment methods (e.g., iDEAL in the
      Netherlands, Sofort in Germany) may have specific scheme rules requiring local
      licensing or partnership with a local PI.

      **United Kingdom**

      - **Payment Services Regulations 2017 (PSRs):** Mirror PSD2. Requires authorization
      as an Authorized Payment Institution (API) or Small Payment Institution (SPI)
      for payment services. The FCA has a "payment services" definition similar to
      PSD2.

      - **Agent Model:** APIs can operate through agents, but the agent must be registered
      with the FCA. Mosaic Relay could act as an agent of a licensed API.

      **Canada**

      - **Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA):** Requires registration with the Bank
      of Canada for "retail payment activities" including electronic funds transfers.
      Mosaic Relay''s activities likely fall within scope if it facilitates payments
      for merchants.

      - **Provincial Money Services Business Laws:** Quebec requires a money services
      business license; other provinces may have similar requirements.

      **Other Markets (e.g., Australia, Singapore, Japan, Brazil, Mexico)**

      - **Australia:** Requires an Australian Financial Services Licence (AFSL) for
      "non-cash payment facilities" unless an exemption applies.

      - **Singapore:** Requires a Major Payment Institution (MPI) or Standard Payment
      Institution (SPI) license under the Payment Services Act for "account issuance,"
      "domestic money transfer," "cross-border money transfer," and "merchant acquisition"
      services.

      - **Japan:** Requires a Funds Transfer Service Provider (FTSP) license for money
      transmission.

      - **Brazil:** Requires authorization as a Payment Institution (IP) under the
      Central Bank of Brazil''s regulations.

      - **Mexico:** Requires a Fintech Law license for "crowdfunding," "electronic
      payment funds," or "virtual asset" services.

      **Key Issues Across Jurisdictions**

      - **Custody vs. Non-Custody:** The critical determinant is whether Mosaic Relay
      ever takes custody of funds. If it does, licensing is almost certainly required.
      If it does not, it may avoid licensing or operate under an agent/partner-of-record
      model.

      - **Contracting Entity:** The entity that contracts with merchants and sellers
      may be deemed the "provider" of payment services, triggering licensing in that
      entity''s jurisdiction.

      - **Cross-Border Payouts:** Payouts to sellers and contractors may trigger "money
      remittance" or "cross-border money transfer" licensing requirements in both
      the sending and receiving countries.

      - **Local Payment Methods:** Some local payment methods (e.g., bank transfers,
      e-wallets) may require local licensing or partnership with a local licensed
      entity.

      - **AML/KYC:** Even if Mosaic Relay avoids licensing, it may still be subject
      to AML/KYC requirements as a "financial institution" or "designated non-financial
      business or profession" (DNFBP) in some jurisdictions.

      ## Viable paths

      **Path 1: Agent/Partner-of-Record Model (Recommended for Pilot)**

      - **Structure:** Mosaic Relay acts as an agent or partner-of-record for a locally
      licensed payment institution (e.g., its acquiring partner, payout provider,
      or a third-party PI) in each target country.

      - **Advantages:** Avoids the time and cost of obtaining licenses; leverages
      existing partner licenses; faster time to market.

      - **Disadvantages:** Limited control over compliance; potential liability for
      partner''s actions; may not be available in all jurisdictions.

      - **Implementation:** Contract with a licensed PI in each country; register
      as an agent where required; ensure the PI holds all necessary licenses for the
      services Mosaic Relay provides.

      **Path 2: Obtain Licenses in Key Markets**

      - **Structure:** Mosaic Relay obtains its own money transmitter licenses (US),
      PI/EMI licenses (EU/UK), and equivalent licenses in other target countries.

      - **Advantages:** Full control over compliance; ability to offer a broader range
      of services; no reliance on partners.

      - **Disadvantages:** Time-consuming (6–18 months per jurisdiction); costly (application
      fees, legal fees, capital requirements); may require local presence or directors.

      - **Implementation:** Prioritize licenses in the largest markets (e.g., US,
      EU, UK); use passporting for EU/EEA; consider acquiring a licensed entity to
      accelerate the process.

      **Path 3: Hybrid Model**

      - **Structure:** Obtain licenses in key markets (e.g., US, EU, UK) and use the
      agent/partner-of-record model in smaller or less strategic markets.

      - **Advantages:** Balances control and speed; allows Mosaic Relay to focus resources
      on the most important markets.

      - **Disadvantages:** Complexity in managing multiple models; potential for regulatory
      arbitrage.

      - **Implementation:** Obtain licenses in the US, EU, and UK; use agents in other
      markets; ensure consistent compliance standards across all markets.

      **Path 4: Pure Technology Provider Model**

      - **Structure:** Mosaic Relay structures its services to avoid "payment services"
      or "money transmission" definitions by never taking custody of funds and acting
      solely as a technology layer.

      - **Advantages:** Avoids licensing entirely; fastest time to market.

      - **Disadvantages:** May not be feasible in all jurisdictions; limits the services
      Mosaic Relay can offer; requires careful contractual and operational structuring.

      - **Implementation:** Ensure all funds flow directly between customers, merchants,
      and licensed partners; avoid any custody or control of funds; use clear contractual
      language to define Mosaic Relay''s role as a technology provider.
