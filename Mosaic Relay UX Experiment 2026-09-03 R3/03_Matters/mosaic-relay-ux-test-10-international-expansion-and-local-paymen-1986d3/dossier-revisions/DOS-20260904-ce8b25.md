# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure provider, is planning to expand its services to support merchants in several new countries. This expansion involves enabling local payment methods, automated currency conversion, and cross-border payouts. Counsel is involved to navigate the complex regulatory landscape of each new market, ensuring compliance before a planned pilot in six months. The primary challenge is that the target countries are not yet selected, which gates all specific legal analysis.

## Decision question

Which countries should Mosaic Relay select for its initial international expansion, given that the choice will dictate specific licensing, AML/KYC, consumer protection, and data privacy requirements, and what is the minimum viable compliance framework needed to launch a pilot in six months without exposing the company to undue regulatory risk?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay is not a bank, does not hold deposits, and works through external payment and infrastructure providers.
- The proposed experience uses one global onboarding flow, local payment options at checkout, automated currency conversion, and cross-border payouts to sellers and contractors.
- Product development would begin this quarter, with a pilot in six months and broader release after operational testing.
- The business goal is to help existing customers expand without building separate payment operations for each country.
- Which specific countries are the target for this international expansion? This drives everything downstream — licensing, AML/KYC, privacy, and disclosures are all country-specific. — The target countries are not yet selected. Treat the country list as a launch gate. Do not approve a country until its licensing, funds flow, AML/KYC, privacy, consumer, tax, sanctions, disclosure, and payout requirements are documented.
- In the proposed cross-border flow, who holds customer funds at each step — does Mosaic Relay ever take custody of funds, or do acquiring/local payment providers and payout providers hold and move all funds? — Not yet determined — funds flow is still being designed
- Which Mosaic Relay entity would contract with merchants, sellers, and local payment providers in the new countries — a single global entity, or local/regional entities per country? — Not yet determined — contracting structure is still being designed
- Which payout provider(s) would carry the cross-border payouts to sellers and contractors? — Not yet determined — payout provider not yet selected
- Where will customer and merchant data be located for the new countries — stored in existing Mosaic Relay data centers/regions, or in-country/local data storage? — Not yet determined — data locations not yet decided

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay's existing acquiring/processing partners and local payment providers will carry the licensed money-transmission activity in each new country (agent/partner-of-record model), rather than Mosaic Relay obtaining new licenses itself.
- The 'several new countries' are within Mosaic Relay's existing footprint (North America, UK, EU, selected other markets) unless stated otherwise.
- Cross-border payouts to sellers and contractors are made through external payout providers rather than Mosaic Relay holding funds.

## Issues and workstreams

- Money transmission / licensing exposure in each target country (Mosaic Relay is not a bank and does not hold deposits, but cross-border payouts and local payment acceptance may still trigger licensing or agent/partner-of-record structures)
- Cross-border AML/KYC controls: sanctions screening, KYB/KYC for merchants and recipients, transaction monitoring across jurisdictions
- Consumer protection and funds-flow disclosures: cancellation rights, local disclosures, payout restrictions
- Privacy and data protection: data locations, cross-border data transfers, controller/processor allocation
- Contracting structure: which entity contracts with merchants, sellers, and local payment providers
- Marketing limits and local disclosure requirements
- Country-approval gate: no country is approved until its licensing, funds flow, AML/KYC, privacy, consumer, tax, sanctions, disclosure, and payout requirements are documented

## Open questions

- Which specific countries are targeted for the initial pilot and subsequent rollout? This is the primary gating item for all legal analysis.
- What is the precise funds flow for cross-border transactions? Does Mosaic Relay ever take custody of funds, even momentarily, or is it purely a software layer on top of licensed partners?
- Which legal entity (or entities) will be used to contract with merchants, sellers, and local partners in each new jurisdiction?
- Who are the specific local payment providers and payout partners being considered for each country?
- Where will customer and merchant data be stored and processed for each new country?
- **Money Transmission & Payment Services Licensing:** This is the most significant hurdle. Even if Mosaic Relay does not hold funds, its role in facilitating payments and payouts could classify it as a money transmitter or payment service provider. The analysis will depend on the specific definitions in each country's laws (e.g., PSD2 in the EU, state-level MTLs in the US, Payment Services Act in Singapore). The "agent of the payee" exemption or similar exclusions will be critical but vary by jurisdiction.
- **Cross-Border AML/KYC:** Mosaic Relay will need a robust, risk-based AML program that complies with the standards of each new country. This includes:
- **Sanctions Screening:** Screening all parties (merchants, sellers, payers) against relevant lists (e.g., OFAC, UN, EU, HMT).
- **KYB/KYC:** Verifying the identity of merchants and their beneficial owners, as well as sellers/contractors receiving payouts. Requirements for identity verification documents and processes differ significantly.
- **Transaction Monitoring:** Implementing systems to detect and report suspicious activity according to local rules.
- **Consumer Protection & Disclosures:** Local laws will dictate what information must be provided to payers at checkout. This includes:
- **Disclosures:** Clear information on exchange rates, fees, and the total cost of the transaction.
- **Cancellation/Refund Rights:** Rules governing a consumer's right to cancel a transaction or receive a refund, which can impact settlement and payout timing.
- **Funds-Flow Information:** Disclosures explaining who is processing the payment and holding funds at each step.
- **Privacy & Data Protection:** The transfer of personal data across borders is highly regulated.
- **Data Localization:** Some countries may require certain data to be stored locally.
- **Cross-Border Transfer Mechanisms:** Transferring data from regions like the EU/UK to the US requires a valid legal mechanism (e.g., Standard Contractual Clauses, adequacy decisions).
- **Controller/Processor Roles:** The roles of Mosaic Relay, its merchants, and its vendors must be clearly defined in data processing agreements.
- **Contracting Structure:** The choice of contracting entity (a single US parent vs. local subsidiaries) has major implications for tax, liability, and regulatory oversight. A single-entity model is simpler initially but may not be sustainable or compliant in all jurisdictions.
- **Phased, Partner-Led Approach (Recommended):** Select 2-3 initial countries with well-understood regulatory regimes and strong existing partners. Rely on licensed local partners for all regulated activities (money transmission, payouts). Mosaic Relay would act as a technology layer, structuring its services to fall outside licensing requirements where possible. This minimizes initial regulatory burden and speeds up time-to-market.
- **Hub-and-Spoke Licensing:** Establish a licensed entity in a key region (e.g., an EMI license in Ireland or Lithuania for EU passporting) to serve the broader region. This is a slower, more expensive path but provides greater control and a more scalable long-term solution for a specific bloc.
- **Direct Licensing (Per Country):** Apply for licenses in each target country. This is the most burdensome and slowest path, likely only viable for strategically critical markets where partner-led models are not feasible.
- **Target Country List:** The entire analysis is theoretical until specific countries are named. A country like Brazil has vastly different requirements than Canada or Germany.
- **Funds Flow:** If Mosaic Relay's architecture causes it to take custody or control of funds at any point, it will almost certainly trigger licensing requirements, invalidating the partner-led assumption.
- **Partner Capabilities:** The specific licenses and capabilities of chosen local payment and payout providers will determine what Mosaic Relay can and cannot do in their jurisdiction.
- **Risk Appetite:** The company's tolerance for regulatory risk will influence whether it pursues a conservative, partner-led strategy or a more aggressive, direct-licensing approach.
- **Confirm Target Countries:** The immediate next step is to get a prioritized list of 3-5 target countries from the business team.
- **Map the Funds Flow:** Work with product and engineering to create a detailed funds-flow diagram for a sample transaction in one target country. This is the most critical artifact for the licensing analysis.
- **Engage Local Counsel:** Once 1-2 pilot countries are selected, engage local counsel to verify the licensing analysis, consumer protection rules, and data privacy requirements. Do not rely solely on partner assurances.
- **Vet Partners:** Conduct due diligence on the regulatory status and compliance programs of potential local payment and payout partners.
- **Working assumption:** The analysis assumes Mosaic Relay can operate as a pure technology provider without taking custody of funds. If the funds-flow diagram shows Mosaic Relay touching money, the recommendation shifts from a partner-led model to a licensing-first model, dramatically changing the timeline and cost.
- **Open fork:** The choice of the first pilot country is a major fork. Choosing an EU country leads down a PSD2/GDPR path. Choosing a country in Southeast Asia (e.g., Singapore) leads down a completely different regulatory path with different licensing and data rules.
- **Not examined:** No external authority was retrieved. This analysis is based on general principles of financial regulation. Specific statutes, regulations, and guidance for any particular country have not been reviewed.
- **Working assumption:** It is assumed that existing acquiring and payout partners have the necessary licenses in the (yet-to-be-named) target countries. This must be verified through due diligence. If partners are not licensed, the entire plan is blocked until new partners are found or Mosaic Relay obtains its own licenses.
