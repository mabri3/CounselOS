The target issue for this run is **ISS-7eb9a27f11-46f961836e842b02** — Mosaic Relay's money transmission licensing posture. Here's the path analysis.
## The issue: money transmission licensing posture
**The saved issue:** Operating as an agent under the bank partner's license does not resolve Mosaic Relay's own money transmission licensing posture for initiating ACH payouts, nor its own CIP/AML and sanctions obligations.
**Why the fact matters:** This is the threshold question that determines whether Mosaic Relay can lawfully initiate payouts at all — and it gates every other issue in the matter. If Mosaic Relay is itself a money transmitter (or is deemed to be one), it needs its own state money transmitter licenses and its own FinCEN MSB registration, and it bears its own CIP/AML and sanctions obligations. If it is a pure technology provider with no control over funds, those obligations may shift to the bank partner. The agent-under-the-bank's-license framing is not a legal determination — it's a business arrangement that must be tested against each state's money transmitter act.
**One short contrast:** "We operate as an agent under the bank partner's license" sounds like it resolves the licensing question, but it doesn't. In many states, an "agent of a licensee" exemption has specific conditions — the agent must not receive, hold, or control funds, and the principal must be responsible for the agent's acts. If Mosaic Relay is the party *initiating* the ACH entries and controlling the funds flow, it may not qualify for the exemption and may need its own license. The bank partner "seeing the deck" is not a legal determination of Mosaic Relay's own obligations.
## The applicable test
**Actor:** Mosaic Relay, as the party initiating ACH payouts.
**Jurisdiction:** State-by-state (each state's money transmitter act) plus federal (FinCEN MSB registration).
**The test:** Money transmission licensing is a state-by-state regime. The key questions are: (1) whether initiating ACH payouts falls within the state's definition of "money transmission," and (2) whether Mosaic Relay qualifies for an exemption as an agent of a licensed bank or agent of an exempt person. The critical factual driver is whether Mosaic Relay ever **receives, holds, or controls funds** in the payout flow, or whether it acts purely as a third-party sender instructing the bank partner. If Mosaic Relay controls the funds flow, it likely needs its own licenses.
**Exceptions:** Agent-of-a-licensee exemptions exist in many states but are conditional — they typically require the agent to not hold or control funds and the principal to be responsible. Whether Mosaic Relay qualifies depends on the specific statute in each state where it operates.
## Known vs. unknown facts
**Known:**
- Mosaic Relay initiates the ACH payouts (reported).
- Mosaic Relay operates as an agent under the bank partner's license (reported).
- The bank partner has seen the deck (reported).
**Unknown:**
- Whether Mosaic Relay ever receives, holds, or controls funds in the payout flow (not confirmed).
- Which specific states Mosaic Relay operates in and where sellers are located (not confirmed).
- The specific terms of the agency agreement with the bank partner (not reviewed).
- Whether Mosaic Relay is registered as an MSB with FinCEN (not confirmed).
- Whether the agent-of-a-licensee exemption applies in the relevant states (not confirmed).
## Conditional routes — each as an action the business can take
**Choice 1 — Confirm the funds-flow and agency-agreement facts (recommended first step).**
- **Depends on:** Determining whether Mosaic Relay receives, holds, or controls funds, and reviewing the agency agreement terms.
- **Next consequence:** Establishes whether Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter needing its own licenses, or qualifies for an agent exemption.
- **Risks:** None — this is fact-gathering, not a decision.
- **Trade-off:** Requires internal and legal review before any launch decision.
- **Remaining work:** Review the agency agreement; map the funds flow; identify the states involved.
**Choice 2 — Obtain state money transmitter licenses.**
- **Depends on:** Mosaic Relay being deemed a money transmitter in the relevant states (i.e., it controls funds or doesn't qualify for an exemption).
- **Next consequence:** Mosaic Relay can lawfully initiate payouts in those states; it also becomes a covered financial institution with its own CIP/AML and sanctions obligations.
- **Risks:** Licensing is time-consuming and state-by-state; may not be achievable by Q1. This is an expected cost/constraint, not a legal risk.
- **Trade-off:** Full compliance vs. significant time and cost that may miss Q1.
- **Remaining work:** Identify states, prepare applications, obtain licenses, register as an MSB with FinCEN.
**Choice 3 — Restructure so the bank partner is the party initiating payouts.**
- **Depends on:** The bank partner agreeing to be the ODFI/initiating party, with Mosaic Relay as a pure technology provider that never controls funds.
- **Next consequence:** Mosaic Relay may qualify for an agent exemption and avoid its own licensing; the bank bears primary CIP/AML and sanctions obligations.
- **Risks:** Requires the bank to accept this role contractually; the bank may not agree. The deck review does not constitute this acceptance.
- **Trade-off:** Shifts compliance burden to the bank, but only if the bank agrees and the funds flow is restructured.
- **Remaining work:** Renegotiate the agency agreement; restructure the funds flow; obtain written confirmation from the bank.
**Choice 4 — Launch only in states where an exemption is confirmed.**
- **Depends on:** Identifying states where Mosaic Relay qualifies for an agent-of-a-licensee exemption.
- **Next consequence:** A Q1 launch is possible but only in a reduced set of states.
- **Risks:** Reduced addressable market; requires confirming each state's exemption conditions.
- **Trade-off:** Preserves some Q1 launch vs. reduced market coverage.
- **Remaining work:** State-by-state exemption analysis.
**Choice 5 — Ship as-is and accept the licensing risk (not recommended).**
- **Depends on:** No change to the current agent-under-the-bank's-license arrangement.
- **Next consequence:** Mosaic Relay initiates payouts without confirming its own licensing posture.
- **Risks:** Unlicensed money transmission enforcement risk, fines, and reputational harm. This is an expected consequence if Mosaic Relay is deemed a money transmitter, not merely a possible risk.
- **Trade-off:** No product change vs. the highest licensing exposure.
- **Remaining work:** None — this is the status quo.
## How the choices relate
The key distinction is between **agreeing to a restructure** and **implementing it**. Choosing Choice 3 (restructure so the bank initiates) does not instantly change the operating state — until the agency agreement is renegotiated and the funds flow is actually restructured, the current arrangement remains. So Choice 3 and Choice 5 can coexist in time: the business can commit to the restructure while the current arrangement continues during the transition. Choice 3 only *replaces* Choice 5 once the restructure is implemented. Similarly, Choice 2 (obtain licenses) and Choice 3 (restructure) are not mutually exclusive — Mosaic Relay could obtain licenses in some states while restructuring in others; they can coexist.
## Connections to the other saved issues
This licensing issue is the **gateway** to the matter's other compliance issues, because it determines whether Mosaic Relay is a covered financial institution:
- **$3,000 threshold (ISS-...8c1936ea2c58b600):** This issue **may_resolve** the threshold issue — if Mosaic Relay is a pure technology provider with no control over funds, the CIP obligation may shift to the bank, changing the whole deferred-identity analysis. Conversely, if Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter, it bears its own CIP obligation and the $3,000 threshold is non-compliant.
- **OFAC screening timing (ISS-...0ca150debaa4249f):** **shared_condition** — both depend on Mosaic Relay's status as a covered financial institution. If Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter, it bears its own sanctions obligations; if not, they may shift to the bank.
- **USDC payouts (ISS-...980c2188f70b5315):** **compounds** — the licensing question is more acute for USDC payouts, which may require separate virtual-asset money transmission licenses beyond the fiat ACH analysis. Resolving the fiat licensing posture does not resolve the USDC licensing question.
- **Beneficial ownership (ISS-...22fd89b609272861):** **shared_condition** — if Mosaic Relay is a covered financial institution, the KYB/beneficial-ownership obligations apply to it directly; if not, they may fall on the bank.
- **Progress bar (ISS-...704b6c4c5d335bfe):** **depends_on** — the progress bar's consumer-disclosure concern is secondary to whether Mosaic Relay is a covered financial institution at all; the licensing posture determines the framework within which the disclosure concern is assessed.
## Bottom line
The licensing posture is the **gateway issue** — it determines whether Mosaic Relay is a covered financial institution and therefore whether it bears its own CIP/AML, sanctions, and KYB obligations. The single most useful next step is **Choice 1**: confirm the funds-flow facts and review the agency agreement, because that determines whether Mosaic Relay needs its own licenses (Choice 2), can restructure so the bank initiates (Choice 3), or can launch in a limited set of states (Choice 4). **Choice 5 (ship as-is)** is not recommended.
