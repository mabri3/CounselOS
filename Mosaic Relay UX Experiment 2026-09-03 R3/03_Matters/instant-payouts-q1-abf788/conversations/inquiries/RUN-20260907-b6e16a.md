## The issue: the $3,000 deferred-identity threshold
**The saved issue:** The $3,000 cumulative payout threshold is not a recognized BSA customer-identification threshold. There is no $3,000 BSA CIP trigger. Under BSA/AML, a money transmitter must collect CIP before establishing the account relationship, not after a payout threshold. Deferring identity collection until $3,000 is likely non-compliant.
**Why the fact matters:** This is the foundation of the entire onboarding design. If the threshold is legally unsupportable, every seller who receives payouts below $3,000 is an unidentified customer receiving funds — a window for money laundering, sanctions evasion, and fraud. And you've confirmed the $3,000 figure was purely an engineering/product choice with no legal basis.
**One short contrast:** The $3,000 figure appears to be a confusion with the **$3,000 recordkeeping threshold for funds transfers** (31 CFR § 1020.410(a), the "Travel Rule") — a *per-transaction recordkeeping* obligation. That is not a *customer identification* trigger tied to *cumulative* payouts. CIP applies at account opening, not after a dollar threshold.
## The applicable test
**Actor:** Mosaic Relay, as the party initiating ACH payouts — likely a money services business (MSB)/money transmitter, or an agent of a covered financial institution.
**Jurisdiction:** US federal (BSA/AML, FinCEN CIP rules).
**The test:** Under 31 U.S.C. § 5318(l) and implementing rules (31 CFR § 1022.210 for MSBs; § 1020.220 for banks), a covered financial institution must implement a written CIP that collects the customer's name, DOB (individuals), address, and ID number **before or at the time of opening the account** — not after a transaction threshold is crossed. The "account" for a money transmitter is established when the customer first uses the service to send or receive funds. So the first payout to an unverified seller is the violation point.
**Exceptions:** None apply to CIP timing for money transmitters. The Travel Rule's $3,000 recordkeeping threshold is a separate obligation and creates no CIP exemption. Some risk-based flexibility exists for *how* verification is completed (e.g., phased verification), but not for *whether* identity is collected before the relationship is established.
## Known vs. unknown facts
**Known:**
- Sellers onboard with email and bank account only; full identity is deferred to $3,000 cumulative payouts (reported).
- The $3,000 figure was purely an engineering/product choice, no legal basis confirmed (reported).
- Mosaic Relay initiates the ACH payouts (reported).
- Mosaic Relay operates as an agent under the bank partner's license (reported).
- The bank partner has seen the deck (reported).
**Unknown:**
- Whether Mosaic Relay is registered as an MSB with FinCEN and holds state money transmitter licenses (not confirmed).
- Whether the bank partner has contractually accepted CIP responsibility in the agency agreement (not reviewed).
- Whether any BSA exemption applies (none known, but not affirmatively ruled out).
## Conditional routes — each as an action the business can take
**Choice 1 — Collect CIP at onboarding (recommended).**
- **Depends on:** Mosaic Relay being a covered financial institution (or the bank not assuming CIP), so Mosaic Relay must collect identity before first payout.
- **Next consequence:** Compliant onboarding; identity collected before any payout is released; the unidentified-customer window closes.
- **Risks:** UI friction and engineering effort. This is an expected cost, not a legal risk.
- **Trade-off:** More onboarding friction vs. closing the core AML violation.
- **Remaining work:** Redesign onboarding to collect CIP before first payout; confirm MSB registration and state licensing posture.
**Choice 2 — Rely on the bank partner's CIP.**
- **Depends on:** The bank partner contractually accepting full CIP responsibility through Mosaic Relay's onboarding flow.
- **Next consequence:** Mosaic Relay may rely on the bank's CIP program; onboarding must meet the bank's CIP standards.
- **Risks:** If the bank does not explicitly accept responsibility in writing, the obligation remains with Mosaic Relay. The deck review does not constitute acceptance.
- **Trade-off:** Shifts compliance burden to the bank, but only if the bank agrees and Mosaic Relay's flow meets bank standards.
- **Remaining work:** Obtain written CIP acceptance from the bank; align onboarding to bank CIP standards.
**Choice 3 — Limit pre-verification payouts to a de minimis amount.**
- **Depends on:** Business insisting on some pre-verification payouts; limit to a small amount (e.g., $100 or less) and do not publicize the threshold.
- **Next consequence:** Reduced unidentified-customer exposure, but residual risk remains.
- **Risks:** Not clearly authorized by BSA/AML rules; carries residual enforcement risk. This is a possible risk, not an expected consequence.
- **Trade-off:** Preserves some instant-payout value vs. residual compliance risk.
- **Remaining work:** Document a risk assessment; obtain a legal opinion on the de minimis approach.
**Choice 4 — Ship as-is and remediate later (not recommended).**
- **Depends on:** No change to the deferred-identity design.
- **Next consequence:** Ships with unidentified customers receiving payouts — a core AML violation.
- **Risks:** Enforcement risk, civil money penalties, reputational harm. These are expected consequences of a core AML violation, not merely possible risks.
- **Trade-off:** No product change vs. the highest compliance exposure.
- **Remaining work:** None — this is the status quo.
## How the choices relate
The key distinction is between **agreeing to a redesign** and **implementing it**. Choosing to redesign (Choice 1) does not instantly replace the current design — until the CIP-at-onboarding flow is actually built and deployed, the existing deferred-identity route remains the operating state. So Choice 1 and Choice 4 can coexist in time: the business can commit to the redesign while the current design continues to operate during the build window. Choice 1 only *replaces* Choice 4 once the redesign is implemented. Similarly, Choice 2 (bank CIP) and Choice 1 are not mutually exclusive — the bank could assume CIP while Mosaic Relay still collects identity at onboarding; they can coexist. Choice 3 (de minimis) is a variant that can coexist with either Choice 1 or Choice 2 as an interim measure.
## Connections to the other saved issues
This issue is the **root** of the matter's compliance problems, and it connects to every other saved issue:
- **Licensing posture (ISS-...46f961836e842b02):** Whether Mosaic Relay is a covered financial institution determines whether the CIP obligation falls on it at all. This issue **depends on** the licensing issue — if Mosaic Relay is a pure technology provider with no control over funds, the CIP obligation may shift to the bank, changing the whole analysis. Conversely, resolving the licensing posture may resolve which CIP path is available.
- **OFAC screening timing (ISS-...0ca150debaa4249f):** Both issues share the same underlying condition — Mosaic Relay's status as a covered financial institution. The deferred-identity window and the post-release screening window are **shared_condition** problems: both leave unidentified/unscreened parties receiving funds. Fixing identity collection at onboarding narrows but does not close the OFAC timing issue.
- **USDC payouts (ISS-...980c2188f70b5315):** The deferred-identity gap **compounds** the USDC risk — an unidentified seller receiving USDC to a self-hosted wallet is the highest-risk combination. Collecting CIP at onboarding reduces but does not resolve the separate virtual-asset licensing and sanctions questions.
- **Beneficial ownership (ISS-...22fd89b609272861):** Both are **shared_condition** KYB/AML gaps — neither collects the identity information BSA/AML requires. Fixing CIP timing for individuals does not fix the separate beneficial-ownership gap for business sellers; they are distinct obligations that must each be addressed.
- **Progress bar (ISS-...704b6c4c5d335bfe):** The progress bar is a direct **consequence** of the $3,000 threshold — it advertises the exact amount a seller can receive before verification. If this issue is fixed by collecting CIP at onboarding, the threshold disappears and the progress bar becomes moot (**may_resolve**). Until the redesign is implemented, the bar remains a live issue.
## Bottom line
The $3,000 threshold is not legally supportable and the design must be redesigned to collect identity at onboarding. The realistic paths are **Choice 1 (collect CIP at onboarding)** or **Choice 2 (bank partner contractually assumes CIP)**, with **Choice 3 (de minimis)** as a business compromise. **Choice 4 (ship as-is)** is not recommended. The single most useful next step is confirming Mosaic Relay's MSB status and the bank partner agreement terms, since those determine which path is available — and that same confirmation feeds the licensing, OFAC, and USDC issues.
