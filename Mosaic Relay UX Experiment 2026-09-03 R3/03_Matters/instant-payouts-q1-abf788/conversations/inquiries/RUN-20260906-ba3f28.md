## The issue: the $3,000 deferred-identity threshold
**The saved issue:** The $3,000 cumulative payout threshold is not a recognized BSA customer-identification threshold. There is no $3,000 BSA CIP trigger. Under BSA/AML, a money transmitter must collect CIP before establishing the account relationship, not after a payout threshold. Deferring identity collection until $3,000 is likely non-compliant.
**Why the fact matters:** This is the foundation of the entire onboarding design. If the threshold is legally unsupportable, then every seller who receives payouts below $3,000 is an unidentified customer receiving funds — a window for money laundering, sanctions evasion, and fraud. And you've already confirmed the $3,000 figure was purely an engineering/product choice with no legal basis. So the design rests on a misconception, and the question is what to do about it.
**One short contrast:** The $3,000 figure appears to be a confusion with the **$3,000 recordkeeping threshold for funds transfers** (31 CFR § 1020.410(a), the "Travel Rule") — a *per-transaction recordkeeping* obligation. That is not a *customer identification* trigger tied to *cumulative* payouts. CIP applies at account opening, not after a dollar threshold.
## The test
**Actor:** Mosaic Relay, as the party initiating ACH payouts — likely a money services business (MSB)/money transmitter, or an agent of a covered financial institution.
**Jurisdiction:** US federal (BSA/AML, FinCEN CIP rules).
**The test:** Under 31 U.S.C. § 5318(l) and implementing rules (31 CFR § 1022.210 for MSBs; § 1020.220 for banks), a covered financial institution must implement a written CIP that collects the customer's name, DOB (individuals), address, and ID number **before or at the time of opening the account** — not after a transaction threshold is crossed. The "account" for a money transmitter is established when the customer first uses the service to send or receive funds. So the first payout to an unverified seller is the violation point.
**Exceptions:** None apply to CIP timing for money transmitters. The Travel Rule's $3,000 recordkeeping threshold is a different obligation and does not create a CIP exemption. Some risk-based flexibility exists for *how* verification is completed (e.g., phased verification), but not for *whether* identity is collected before the relationship is established.
## Unknown facts that change the analysis
- **Mosaic Relay's MSB registration status.** If it is not a covered financial institution and is purely a technology provider with no control over funds, the CIP obligation may fall entirely on the bank partner. But the facts state Mosaic Relay is "the one initiating" ACH payouts, which points toward its own obligations.
- **Bank partner's written agreement.** If the bank has contractually accepted full CIP responsibility (including identity collection through Mosaic Relay's onboarding), the analysis shifts. The bank's deck review does **not** constitute this acceptance.
- **Whether any BSA exemption applies.** None are known to apply to CIP timing here.
## Conditional routes
**Path A — Collect CIP at onboarding (recommended).** Redesign onboarding to collect full identity before any payout is released. This is the compliant approach. The UI-friction concern can be addressed through progressive disclosure, third-party identity verification APIs, or risk-based simplified onboarding for low-risk sellers — but identity must be collected before the first payout, not after $3,000.
**Path B — Rely on the bank partner's CIP.** If the bank agrees in writing to take full responsibility for CIP (including collecting identity through Mosaic Relay's flow), Mosaic Relay may be able to rely on the bank's program. This requires the bank to explicitly accept the responsibility and for Mosaic Relay's onboarding to meet the bank's CIP standards. The deck review does not satisfy this.
**Path C — Ship as-is and remediate later (not recommended).** The CIP violation is a core AML requirement, not a technicality. Operating with unidentified customers receiving payouts creates enforcement risk, civil money penalties, and reputational harm.
## Business alternatives
- **Limit pre-verification payouts to a de minimis amount** (e.g., $100 or less) and do not publicly display the threshold. This is a risk-based approach some regulators may tolerate for truly de minimis amounts, but it is not clearly authorized by BSA/AML rules and carries residual risk. Requires a documented risk assessment and legal opinion.
- **Progressive/risk-based onboarding** — collect basic identity at signup, complete enhanced verification within a short window while limiting activity. This preserves some of the "instant" promise while closing the unidentified-customer window.
**Bottom line:** The $3,000 threshold is not legally supportable and the design must be redesigned to collect identity at onboarding. The realistic paths are Path A (collect CIP at onboarding) or Path B (bank partner contractually assumes CIP), with a de minimis or risk-based variant as a business compromise. The single most useful next step is confirming Mosaic Relay's MSB status and the bank partner agreement terms, since those determine which path is available.
