  available_excerpt: "# Research the legal basis for this saved issue: The $3,000\n\
    \n## Question\n\nResearch the legal basis for this saved issue: The $3,000 cumulative\
    \ payout threshold is not a recognized BSA customer-identification threshold;\
    \ there is no $3,000 BSA CIP trigger. Under BSA/AML, a money transmitter must\
    \ collect CIP before establishing the account relationship, not after a payout\
    \ threshold. Deferring identity collection until $3,000 is likely non-compliant..\
    \ Keep useful analysis even if a source cannot be confirmed.\n\n## Working Analysis\n\
    \n**No external authority retrieved**\n\n# Research: Legal Basis for the $3,000\
    \ Threshold Issue\n**No external authority retrieved.**\n---\n## Matter summary\n\
    Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company, is building an Instant Payouts\
    \ feature for marketplace sellers with a Q1 launch target. The onboarding design\
    \ collects only email and bank account at signup, deferring full identity verification\
    \ until a seller crosses $3,000 in cumulative payouts. Engineering selected $3,000\
    \ believing it is \"the BSA threshold.\" Counsel is evaluating whether this deferred-identity\
    \ design is legally supportable or must be redesigned before launch.\n## Decision\
    \ question\nCan Mosaic Relay defer full customer identification until a seller\
    \ reaches $3,000 in cumulative payouts, or does BSA/AML require identity collection\
    \ at onboarding before any payout relationship is established, and if deferred\
    \ collection is non-compliant, can the redesign be completed in time for Q1?\n\
    ---\n## Likely answer\nThe saved issue is correct. **There is no $3,000 BSA customer-identification\
    \ threshold.** The $3,000 figure appears to be a confusion with the $3,000 recordkeeping\
    \ threshold for funds transfers under 31 CFR § 1020.410(a) (the \"Travel Rule\"\
    \ recordkeeping requirement), which requires financial institutions to collect\
    \ and retain certain information about transmittals of funds of $3,000 or more.\
    \ That is a *recordkeeping* obligation tied to individual transactions, not a\
    \ *customer identification* trigger tied to cumulative payouts.\nThe controlling\
    \ framework is the Customer Identification Program (CIP) requirement. Under 31\
    \ U.S.C. § 5318(l) and its implementing regulations (31 CFR § 1020.220 for banks;\
    \ 31 CFR § 1022.210 for money services businesses), a covered financial institution\
    \ must implement a written CIP that includes collecting, at minimum, the customer's\
    \ name, date of birth (for individuals), address, and identification number **before\
    \ or at the time of opening an account** — not after a transaction threshold is\
    \ crossed. For money services businesses (MSBs), which include money transmitters,\
    \ FinCEN's rules at 31 CFR § 1022.210 require the MSB to establish and maintain\
    \ an AML program that includes policies, procedures, and internal controls reasonably\
    \ designed to verify customer identity. FinCEN guidance has consistently interpreted\
    \ this to mean identity verification occurs at the establishment of the customer\
    \ relationship, not after a cumulative payout threshold.\nThe practical consequence:\
    \ if Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter (or is operating as an agent of a money\
    \ transmitter or bank), deferring identity collection until $3,000 in cumulative\
    \ payouts means that every seller who receives payouts below that threshold is\
    \ an unidentified customer receiving funds. This is likely non-compliant with\
    \ BSA/AML requirements and creates a window for money laundering, sanctions evasion,\
    \ and fraud.\n**Conditional alternative:** If Mosaic Relay is not itself a covered\
    \ financial institution and is acting purely as a technology provider with no\
    \ control over funds flow — with the bank partner being the sole covered entity\
    \ responsible for CIP — then the CIP obligation may fall entirely on the bank\
    \ partner. However, the facts state Mosaic Relay is \"the one initiating\" ACH\
    \ payouts [source:SRC-3a9350fde282eecf6295|Start of document], which strongly\
    \ suggests Mosaic Relay has its own obligations. Even if the bank partner bears\
    \ primary CIP responsibility, the bank's own CIP program would likely require\
    \ Mosaic Relay to collect identity information at onboarding as part of the bank's\
    \ agent oversight obligations.\n---\n## Likely rules/issues\n**1. CIP timing —\
    \ identity must be collected at account opening, not after a threshold.**\nThe\
    \ core rule is straightforward: CIP applies when the account relationship is established.\
    \ For a money transmitter, the \"account\" is established when the customer first\
    \ uses the service to send or receive funds. Allowing sellers to receive payouts\
    \ (even small ones) before identity verification means Mosaic Relay is providing\
    \ financial services to unidentified customers. This is the fundamental non-compliance.\n\
    The $3,000 figure has no basis in CIP rules. The closest BSA provision is the\
    \ $3,000 recordkeeping threshold for funds transfers (31 CFR § 1020.410(a)), which\
    \ requires financial institutions to collect and retain information about the\
    \ transmitter and recipient for transfers of $3,000 or more. This is a per-transaction\
    \ recordkeeping rule, not a cumulative payout threshold for identity verification.\
    \ Engineering's belief that $3,000 is \"the BSA threshold\" [source:SRC-3a9350fde282eecf6295|Start\
    \ of document] appears to conflate these two distinct requirements.\n**2. Mosaic\
    \ Relay's regulatory status determines which CIP rule applies.**\nIf Mosaic Relay\
    \ is registered as an MSB with FinCEN (which it likely should be if it is initiating\
    \ ACH payouts), then 31 CFR § 1022.210 applies directly. If Mosaic Relay is operating\
    \ as an agent of the bank partner, the bank's CIP obligations under 31 CFR § 1020.220\
    \ would extend to Mosaic Relay's onboarding process through the agency relationship.\
    \ Either way, identity collection at onboarding is required.\n**3. The $3,000\
    \ threshold creates a structuring vulnerability.**\nBy publicly displaying the\
    \ $3,000 threshold via a progress bar (\"$1,240 of $3,000 until full verification\"\
    ) [source:SRC-3a9350fde282eecf6295|Start of document], Mosaic Relay is advertising\
    \ the exact amount a bad actor can receive before identity verification kicks\
    \ in. This facilitates structuring — deliberately keeping payouts below $3,000\
    \ to avoid identification. This is a separate AML red flag beyond the CIP timing\
    \ issue.\n**4. Interaction with other design elements.**\nThe deferred identity\
    \ collection compounds the risks from nightly batch OFAC screening (sanctions\
    \ screening after funds are released) and W-9-only business onboarding (no beneficial\
    \ ownership). Together, these create a window where unidentified, unscreened parties\
    \ can receive funds. Each element is problematic individually; combined, they\
    \ represent a systemic AML/sanctions compliance failure.\n---\n## Viable paths\n\
    **Path 1: Collect CIP at onboarding (recommended).** Redesign the onboarding flow\
    \ to collect full identity information (name, date of birth, address, identification\
    \ number) before any payout is released. This is the compliant approach. The engineering\
    \ concern about UI friction can be addressed through progressive disclosure, third-party\
    \ identity verification APIs, or risk-based simplified onboarding for low-risk\
    \ sellers — but identity must be collected before the first payout, not after\
    \ $3,000.\n**Path 2: Limit pre-verification payouts to a de minimis amount.**\
    \ If the business insists on allowing some payouts before full verification, limit\
    \ them to a very small amount (e.g., $100 or less) and do not publicly display\
    \ the threshold. This is a risk-based approach that some regulators may tolerate\
    \ for truly de minimis amounts, but it is not clearly authorized by BSA/AML rules\
    \ and carries residual risk. This path requires a documented risk assessment and\
    \ legal opinion.\n**Path 3: Rely on the bank partner's CIP.** If the bank partner\
    \ agrees in writing to take full responsibility for CIP (including collecting\
    \ identity information through Mosaic Relay's onboarding flow), Mosaic Relay may\
    \ be able to rely on the bank's CIP program. However, this requires the bank to\
    \ explicitly accept this responsibility and for Mosaic Relay's onboarding to meet\
    \ the bank's CIP standards. The bank partner's review of the deck does not constitute\
    \ this acceptance [source:SRC-3a9350fde282eecf6295|Start of document].\n**Path\
    \ 4: Ship as-is and remediate later.** Not recommended. The CIP violation is not\
    \ a technicality — it is a core AML requirement. Operating with unidentified customers\
    \ receiving payouts creates enforcement risk, potential civil money penalties,\
    \ and reputational harm.\n---\n## Facts that could change the answer\n- **Mosaic\
    \ Relay's MSB registration status.** If Mosaic Relay is not registered as an MSB\
    \ and is purely a technology provider with no control over funds, the CIP obligation\
    \ may fall entirely on the bank partner. However, the facts state Mosaic Relay\
    \ initiates ACH payouts, which suggests MSB status.\n- **Bank partner's written\
    \ agreement.** If the bank partner has contractually agreed to handle CIP and\
    \ Mosaic Relay's role is limited to providing the technology interface, the analysis\
    \ shifts. This would need to be confirmed in the agency agreement.\n- **Whether\
    \ any BSA exemption applies.** Certain exemptions exist for specific types of\
    \ transactions or customers (e.g., exempt person thresholds for currency transaction\
    \ reporting), but none apply to CIP timing for money transmitters.\n- **State\
    \ money transmitter laws.** Some states may have additional or different identification\
    \ requirements that could affect the analysis.\n---\n## Recommended last-mile\
    \ verification\n1. **Confirm Mosaic Relay's FinCEN MSB registration status** and\
    \ whether it holds state money transmitter licenses for the payout flow.\n2. **Review\
    \ the bank partner agency agreement** to determine whether the bank has contractually\
    \ accepted CIP responsibility and what identity collection standards it requires.\n\
    3. **Obtain a legal opinion** on whether any BSA exemption or safe harbor could\
    \ justify deferred identity collection for de minimis payout amounts.\n4. **Review\
    \ FinCEN guidance and enforcement actions** on CIP timing for money transmitters\
    \ and payment processors to confirm the analysis.\n5. **Assess the engineering\
    \ timeline** for implementing identity collection at onboarding and whether it\
    \ can be completed before Q1.\n---\n## Assumptions\n- Mosaic Relay is a covered\
    \ financial institution (MSB) or is operating as an agent of a covered financial\
    \ institution, making CIP requirements applicable.\n- The bank partner's review\
    \ of the deck does not constitute a legal determination or contractual acceptance\
    \ of CIP responsibility.\n- The $3,000 threshold has no BSA basis and was selected\
    \ based on a misunderstanding of the $3,000 recordkeeping threshold for funds\

### The applicable actor and rule
**Actor:** Mosaic Relay, as the party initiating ACH payouts. Whether it is a covered financial institution (MSB/money transmitter) or an agent of a covered financial institution is the controlling unknown — it determines which CIP rule applies and whether the obligation falls on Mosaic Relay at all.
**Rule (as analyzed, not retrieved):** Under 31 U.S.C. § 5318(l) and its implementing regulations, a covered financial institution must maintain a written Customer Identification Program that collects the customer's name, date of birth (for individuals), address, and identification number **before or at the time of opening the account** — not after a transaction threshold is crossed. For money services businesses, the MSB CIP rule (31 CFR § 1022.210) applies; for banks, 31 CFR § 1020.220. The "account" for a money transmitter is established when the customer first uses the service to send or receive funds, so the first payout to an unverified seller is the violation point.

### Distinguishing business risk reduction from legal permission
This is the critical distinction. **Collecting CIP at onboarding is a risk-reduction measure, not a grant of legal permission.** It does not create a right to operate; it reduces the compliance exposure that arises from the current deferred-identity design. The legal question of *whether Mosaic Relay may initiate payouts at all* (its money transmission licensing posture) is separate and unresolved. Collecting CIP at onboarding reduces the CIP/AML violation but does not resolve the licensing, OFAC-timing, USDC, or beneficial-ownership issues. So the choice is best understood as: **it removes one identified violation, but it does not by itself make the product lawful to ship.**
