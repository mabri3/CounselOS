    ## The issue: the $3,000 deferred-identity threshold

    **The saved issue:** The $3,000 cumulative payout threshold is not a recognized
    BSA customer-identification threshold. There is no $3,000 BSA CIP trigger. Under
    BSA/AML, a money transmitter must collect CIP before establishing the account
    relationship, not after a payout threshold. Deferring identity collection until
    $3,000 is likely non-compliant.

    **Why the fact matters:** This is the foundation of the entire onboarding design.
    If the threshold is legally unsupportable, every seller who receives payouts below
    $3,000 is an unidentified customer receiving funds — a window for money laundering,
    sanctions evasion, and fraud. And you''ve confirmed the $3,000 figure was purely
    an engineering/product choice with no legal basis.

    **One short contrast:** The $3,000 figure appears to be a confusion with the **$3,000
    recordkeeping threshold for funds transfers** (31 CFR § 1020.410(a), the "Travel
    Rule") — a *per-transaction recordkeeping* obligation. That is not a *customer
    identification* trigger tied to *cumulative* payouts. CIP applies at account opening,
    not after a dollar threshold.

    ## The applicable test

    **Actor:** Mosaic Relay, as the party initiating ACH payouts — likely a money
    services business (MSB)/money transmitter, or an agent of a covered financial
    institution.

    **Jurisdiction:** US federal (BSA/AML, FinCEN CIP rules).

    **The test:** Under 31 U.S.C. § 5318(l) and implementing rules (31 CFR § 1022.210
    for MSBs; § 1020.220 for banks), a covered financial institution must implement
    a written CIP that collects the customer''s name, DOB (individuals), address,
    and ID number **before or at the time of opening the account** — not after a transaction
    threshold is crossed. The "account" for a money transmitter is established when
    the customer first uses the service to send or receive funds. So the first payout
    to an unverified seller is the violation point.

    **Exceptions:** None apply to CIP timing for money transmitters. The Travel Rule''s
    $3,000 recordkeeping threshold is a separate obligation and creates no CIP exemption.
    Some risk-based flexibility exists for *how* verification is completed (e.g.,
    phased verification), but not for *whether* identity is collected before the relationship
    is established.

    ## Known vs. unknown facts

    **Known:**

    - Sellers onboard with email and bank account only; full identity is deferred
    to $3,000 cumulative payouts (reported).

    - The $3,000 figure was purely an engineering/product choice, no legal basis confirmed
    (reported).

    - Mosaic Relay initiates the ACH payouts (reported).

    - Mosaic Relay operates as an agent under the bank partner''s license (reported).

    - The bank partner has seen the deck (reported).

    **Unknown:**

    - Whether Mosaic Relay is registered as an MSB with FinCEN and holds state money
    transmitter licenses (not confirmed).

    - Whether the bank partner has contractually accepted CIP responsibility in the
    agency agreement (not reviewed).

    - Whether any BSA exemption applies (none known, but not affirmatively ruled out).

    not assuming CIP), so Mosaic Relay must collect identity before first payout.

    - **Next consequence:** Compliant onboarding; identity collected before any payout
    is released; the unidentified-customer window closes.

    - **Risks:** UI friction and engineering effort. This is an expected cost, not
    a legal risk.

    - **Trade-off:** More onboarding friction vs. closing the core AML violation.

    - **Remaining work:** Redesign onboarding to collect CIP before first payout;

    - **Depends on:** The bank partner contractually accepting full CIP responsibility

    - **Next consequence:** Mosaic Relay may rely on the bank''s CIP program; onboarding
    must meet the bank''s CIP standards.

    - **Risks:** If the bank does not explicitly accept responsibility in writing,

    - **Depends on:** No change to the deferred-identity design.

    - **Trade-off:** No product change vs. the highest compliance exposure.
