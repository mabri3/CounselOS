# What are the state money transmission licensing requirements for a

## Question

What are the state money transmission licensing requirements for a company initiating ACH payouts as an agent of a licensed bank?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company, is launching Instant Payouts for marketplace sellers in Q1. The product team plans to onboard sellers with minimal identity information (email + bank account), initiate ACH payouts through a bank partner, and offer USDC payouts to international sellers in Mexico and Asia. Counsel is involved because the plan raises significant regulatory questions around money transmission licensing, BSA/AML compliance, OFAC screening timing, and KYB requirements. The target launch date is September 5, 2026, and the bank partner has reviewed the deck but not provided legal clearance.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay ship Instant Payouts in Q1 given that it plans to initiate ACH payouts as an agent of a licensed bank, defer full identity collection until sellers cross $3,000 in cumulative payouts, run OFAC screening in nightly batch after payouts are released, and offer USDC payouts to international sellers without collecting beneficial ownership information?
## Open questions
- Which specific states will Mosaic Relay be initiating ACH payouts from, and which states will sellers be located in? Money transmission licensing is state-by-state, and the answer depends on the specific statutes and exemptions in each jurisdiction.
- Does Mosaic Relay ever receive, hold, or control funds in the payout flow, or does it act purely as a third-party sender instructing the bank partner? This determines whether the money transmission trigger is met.
- What are the terms of the agency agreement with the bank partner? Whether Mosaic Relay qualifies for an agent-of-a-licensee or agent-of-a-bank exemption depends on the specific conditions in each state's money transmitter act.
- What is the legal basis for the $3,000 cumulative payout threshold before collecting full identity? This appears to rest on a misconception about BSA requirements and may not be defensible.
- Which countries in Asia will sellers be located in for USDC payouts, and what are the virtual-asset money transmission licensing requirements in those jurisdictions?

## Likely rules/issues
**State money transmission licensing.** Money transmission licensing is a state-by-state regime. Each state has its own money transmitter act with its own definition of "money transmission" and its own exemptions. The key questions are: (1) whether initiating ACH payouts falls within the state's definition of money transmission, and (2) whether Mosaic Relay qualifies for an exemption as an agent of a licensed bank or agent of an exempt person. The supplied public research correctly notes that no state money transmission statute or regulation was provided, so this analysis cannot be completed without identifying the specific states involved and reviewing their statutes.
**BSA/AML customer identification.** The $3,000 cumulative payout threshold is not a recognized BSA customer-identification threshold. Under BSA/AML, a money transmitter must collect customer identification program (CIP) information before establishing the account relationship, not after a payout threshold. Deferring identity collection until $3,000 is likely non-compliant. This is a federal requirement that applies regardless of state licensing status.
**OFAC screening timing.** OFAC screening in nightly batch against the day's payouts means payouts are released before screening completes. This creates a risk of releasing funds to sanctioned parties. OFAC screening must generally occur before funds are released or blocked. The 400ms latency concern is a business problem, not a legal defense.
**USDC payouts to international sellers.** USDC payouts to sellers in Mexico and Asia, including to self-hosted wallets, raise sanctions, AML, and virtual-asset money transmission licensing questions that are distinct from fiat ACH payouts. The fact that Circuit is a licensed exchange does not resolve Mosaic Relay's own obligations.
**Beneficial ownership.** Not collecting beneficial owners for business sellers is a KYB/AML gap. While the UI burden is a legitimate business concern, it does not eliminate the legal requirement.
**Consumer protection and funds-flow disclosures.** Publicly displaying the $3,000 verification threshold via a progress bar may facilitate evasion and raises funds-flow/consumer disclosure concerns.
## Viable paths
**Path 1: Delay launch and fix the compliance gaps.** This is the safest path. It involves: (a) collecting CIP at onboarding, not at $3,000; (b) implementing real-time OFAC screening before payouts are released; (c) collecting beneficial ownership information for business sellers; (d) conducting a state-by-state money transmission licensing analysis and obtaining any necessary licenses or confirming exemptions; and (e) resolving the USDC payout compliance questions. This path likely delays the launch beyond Q1.
**Path 2: Launch with a limited scope.** This involves launching only in states where Mosaic Relay has confirmed it has the necessary licenses or exemptions, and only for sellers who have completed full identity verification. This path allows a Q1 launch but with a significantly reduced addressable market.
**Path 3: Launch as planned and accept the risk.** This is not recommended. The compliance gaps are material and could result in enforcement action, fines, and reputational damage.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Specific states involved.** If Mosaic Relay is only operating in a small number of states with favorable agent-of-a-licensee exemptions, the licensing analysis may be simpler.
- **Flow of funds.** If Mosaic Relay never receives, holds, or controls funds, it may not meet the definition of a money transmitter in some states.
- **Agency agreement terms.** If the agency agreement with the bank partner meets the specific conditions of an exemption in the relevant states, Mosaic Relay may not need its own license.
- **Bank partner's willingness to take on more responsibility.** If the bank partner is willing to take on more of the compliance burden (e.g., by being the party that initiates the ACH entries), Mosaic Relay's obligations may be reduced.
- **Circuit's licensing and compliance posture.** If Circuit has robust compliance controls and is willing to take on more responsibility for the USDC payouts, Mosaic Relay's obligations may be reduced.
## Recommended last-mile verification
- **State money transmission licensing analysis.** Identify the specific states where Mosaic Relay will be initiating ACH payouts and where sellers will be located. Review the money transmitter act in each of those states to determine whether Mosaic Relay needs a license or qualifies for an exemption.
- **BSA/AML compliance review.** Confirm that the CIP collection process meets BSA/AML requirements. This likely means collecting CIP at onboarding, not at $3,000.
- **OFAC screening review.** Confirm that OFAC screening occurs before payouts are released. This may require implementing real-time screening or delaying payouts until screening is complete.
- **KYB review.** Confirm that the beneficial ownership collection process meets KYB/AML requirements.
- **USDC payout review.** Confirm that the USDC payout process complies with sanctions, AML, and virtual-asset money transmission licensing requirements in the relevant jurisdictions.
- **Agency agreement review.** Review the agency agreement with the bank partner to confirm that it meets the conditions of any applicable exemptions.
- **Consumer protection review.** Review the progress bar and other customer-facing disclosures to ensure they are accurate and not misleading.
