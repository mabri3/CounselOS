# What are the virtual-asset money transmission and sanctions requirements for

## Question

What are the virtual-asset money transmission and sanctions requirements for USDC payouts to self-hosted wallets in Mexico?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company, is launching Instant Payouts for marketplace sellers, with Product (Priya) targeting a Q1 ship date. The plan includes allowing international sellers in Mexico to elect payout in USDC through Circuit, a licensed exchange, with sellers providing wallet addresses that may be exchange accounts or self-hosted wallets. Counsel is involved because the current design defers identity collection until $3,000 in cumulative payouts, runs OFAC screening in nightly batch after payouts release, and does not collect beneficial owners for business sellers. The Q1 deadline creates pressure to resolve whether the USDC payout path can proceed without triggering unlicensed money transmission or sanctions violations.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay ship Instant Payouts in Q1 with USDC payouts to self-hosted wallets in Mexico, given that sellers currently onboard with only email and bank account, identity is not collected until $3,000 in cumulative payouts, OFAC screening runs nightly after funds release, and Mosaic Relay operates as an agent under its bank partner's license rather than holding its own money transmitter licenses?
## Open questions
- Does Mosaic Relay's agent relationship with its bank partner extend to virtual-asset payouts, or does initiating USDC transfers to self-hosted wallets require separate money transmitter licenses in the US and Mexico?
- What is the legal status of USDC payouts to self-hosted wallets under Mexican law, and does Mexico require specific licensing or registration for virtual-asset service providers?
- Can OFAC screening be performed pre-release for USDC payouts without undermining the "instant" promise, given that self-hosted wallets lack the account-freeze controls available with exchange accounts?
- Does the $3,000 identity-collection threshold create a window during which sanctioned parties could receive USDC payouts before screening occurs?
- What are the beneficial-ownership and KYB requirements for business sellers receiving USDC payouts, and does the current W-9-only approach satisfy them?
## Likely rules/issues
**US money transmission:** Mosaic Relay's initiation of USDC payouts likely constitutes money transmission under FinCEN guidance, as virtual currency is treated as "value that substitutes for currency." Operating as an agent under a bank partner's license may not cover virtual-asset activities, as most bank partner agreements address fiat rails only. State money transmitter licenses may be required unless an exemption applies.
**Mexico virtual-asset regulation:** Mexico's Fintech Law (Ley Fintech) requires registration with the CNBV for virtual-asset service providers. USDC payouts to Mexican residents may trigger local licensing or registration obligations, particularly if Mosaic Relay is deemed to be operating a virtual-asset exchange or transfer service.
**Sanctions compliance:** OFAC's sanctions apply to US persons and transactions with a US nexus. USDC is a US-dollar-denominated stablecoin issued by Circle, a US company, creating a clear US nexus. Nightly batch screening after payout release violates OFAC's requirement that screening occur before funds are released or blocked. Self-hosted wallets present heightened risk because they lack the account controls of exchange accounts.
**BSA/AML:** The $3,000 threshold for identity collection has no basis in BSA requirements. FinCEN's customer identification program (CIP) rules require identity verification before establishing the account relationship, not after a payout threshold. For money transmitters, the threshold is $1,000 for certain recordkeeping, but CIP applies at account opening.
**Beneficial ownership:** FinCEN's beneficial-ownership rule requires collection of beneficial owners for legal-entity customers. The W-9-only approach does not satisfy this requirement.
## Viable paths
**Path 1: Delay USDC payouts until compliance infrastructure is built.** Postpone the USDC payout option until Mosaic Relay obtains necessary licenses, implements pre-release OFAC screening, and collects full identity and beneficial-ownership information at onboarding. This is the lowest-risk path but may miss the Q1 deadline.
**Path 2: Restrict USDC payouts to exchange accounts only.** Limit USDC payouts to wallets held at licensed exchanges where Circuit or the exchange performs sanctions screening and has account-freeze capabilities. This reduces sanctions risk but does not resolve money transmission licensing or BSA/AML gaps.
**Path 3: Partner with a licensed virtual-asset money transmitter.** Contract with a third-party licensed money transmitter that specializes in virtual-asset payouts to handle USDC transfers. This shifts licensing and compliance burden but requires due diligence and may not be feasible by Q1.
**Path 4: Ship with fiat-only payouts in Q1, add USDC later.** Launch Instant Payouts with ACH-only payouts in Q1, deferring USDC until compliance gaps are resolved. This meets the Q1 deadline for the core product while avoiding the highest-risk feature.
## Facts that could change the answer
- Whether Mosaic Relay's bank partner agreement explicitly covers virtual-asset payouts or requires separate licensing.
- Whether Circuit, as a licensed exchange, assumes any compliance obligations for USDC payouts or merely executes transfers.
- Whether Mexican law provides any exemptions or safe harbors for small-scale or occasional virtual-asset transfers.
- Whether OFAC has issued specific guidance on USDC or self-hosted wallets that modifies general sanctions screening requirements.
- Whether Mosaic Relay's customer base includes any US-sanctioned jurisdictions or parties that would trigger heightened scrutiny.

## What would change this
- **Working assumption:** Mosaic Relay's bank partner agreement does not cover virtual-asset payouts. If it does, the money transmission licensing analysis changes significantly.
- **Open fork:** Whether Mexico requires CNBV registration for USDC payouts. If registration is required, Path 1 or Path 3 becomes necessary; if not, Path 2 or Path 4 may be viable.
- **Not examined:** The specific terms of Mosaic Relay's contract with Circuit, which may allocate compliance responsibilities.
- **Not examined:** Whether any sellers are located in OFAC-sanctioned jurisdictions, which would require immediate blocking rather than deferred screening.
- **Working assumption:** The $3,000 threshold has no BSA basis. If a specific exemption applies, the identity-collection timing may be defensible, but this is unlikely.
