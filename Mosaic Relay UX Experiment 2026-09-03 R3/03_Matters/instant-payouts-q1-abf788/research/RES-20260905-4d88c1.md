# Research: Legal Basis for the $3,000 Threshold Issue
**No external authority retrieved.**
---
## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company, is building an Instant Payouts feature for marketplace sellers with a Q1 launch target. The onboarding design collects only email and bank account at signup, deferring full identity verification until a seller crosses $3,000 in cumulative payouts. Engineering selected $3,000 believing it is "the BSA threshold." Counsel is evaluating whether this deferred-identity design is legally supportable or must be redesigned before launch.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay defer full customer identification until a seller reaches $3,000 in cumulative payouts, or does BSA/AML require identity collection at onboarding before any payout relationship is established, and if deferred collection is non-compliant, can the redesign be completed in time for Q1?
---
## Likely answer
The saved issue is correct. **There is no $3,000 BSA customer-identification threshold.** The $3,000 figure appears to be a confusion with the $3,000 recordkeeping threshold for funds transfers under 31 CFR § 1020.410(a) (the "Travel Rule" recordkeeping requirement), which requires financial institutions to collect and retain certain information about transmittals of funds of $3,000 or more. That is a *recordkeeping* obligation tied to individual transactions, not a *customer identification* trigger tied to cumulative payouts.
The controlling framework is the Customer Identification Program (CIP) requirement. Under 31 U.S.C. § 5318(l) and its implementing regulations (31 CFR § 1020.220 for banks; 31 CFR § 1022.210 for money services businesses), a covered financial institution must implement a written CIP that includes collecting, at minimum, the customer's name, date of birth (for individuals), address, and identification number **before or at the time of opening an account** — not after a transaction threshold is crossed. For money services businesses (MSBs), which include money transmitters, FinCEN's rules at 31 CFR § 1022.210 require the MSB to establish and maintain an AML program that includes policies, procedures, and internal controls reasonably designed to verify customer identity. FinCEN guidance has consistently interpreted this to mean identity verification occurs at the establishment of the customer relationship, not after a cumulative payout threshold.
The practical consequence: if Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter (or is operating as an agent of a money transmitter or bank), deferring identity collection until $3,000 in cumulative payouts means that every seller who receives payouts below that threshold is an unidentified customer receiving funds. This is likely non-compliant with BSA/AML requirements and creates a window for money laundering, sanctions evasion, and fraud.
**Conditional alternative:** If Mosaic Relay is not itself a covered financial institution and is acting purely as a technology provider with no control over funds flow — with the bank partner being the sole covered entity responsible for CIP — then the CIP obligation may fall entirely on the bank partner. However, the facts state Mosaic Relay is "the one initiating" ACH payouts [source:SRC-3a9350fde282eecf6295|Start of document], which strongly suggests Mosaic Relay has its own obligations. Even if the bank partner bears primary CIP responsibility, the bank's own CIP program would likely require Mosaic Relay to collect identity information at onboarding as part of the bank's agent oversight obligations.
---
## Likely rules/issues
**1. CIP timing — identity must be collected at account opening, not after a threshold.**
The core rule is straightforward: CIP applies when the account relationship is established. For a money transmitter, the "account" is established when the customer first uses the service to send or receive funds. Allowing sellers to receive payouts (even small ones) before identity verification means Mosaic Relay is providing financial services to unidentified customers. This is the fundamental non-compliance.
The $3,000 figure has no basis in CIP rules. The closest BSA provision is the $3,000 recordkeeping threshold for funds transfers (31 CFR § 1020.410(a)), which requires financial institutions to collect and retain information about the transmitter and recipient for transfers of $3,000 or more. This is a per-transaction recordkeeping rule, not a cumulative payout threshold for identity verification. Engineering's belief that $3,000 is "the BSA threshold" [source:SRC-3a9350fde282eecf6295|Start of document] appears to conflate these two distinct requirements.
**2. Mosaic Relay's regulatory status determines which CIP rule applies.**
If Mosaic Relay is registered as an MSB with FinCEN (which it likely should be if it is initiating ACH payouts), then 31 CFR § 1022.210 applies directly. If Mosaic Relay is operating as an agent of the bank partner, the bank's CIP obligations under 31 CFR § 1020.220 would extend to Mosaic Relay's onboarding process through the agency relationship. Either way, identity collection at onboarding is required.
**3. The $3,000 threshold creates a structuring vulnerability.**
By publicly displaying the $3,000 threshold via a progress bar ("$1,240 of $3,000 until full verification") [source:SRC-3a9350fde282eecf6295|Start of document], Mosaic Relay is advertising the exact amount a bad actor can receive before identity verification kicks in. This facilitates structuring — deliberately keeping payouts below $3,000 to avoid identification. This is a separate AML red flag beyond the CIP timing issue.
**4. Interaction with other design elements.**
The deferred identity collection compounds the risks from nightly batch OFAC screening (sanctions screening after funds are released) and W-9-only business onboarding (no beneficial ownership). Together, these create a window where unidentified, unscreened parties can receive funds. Each element is problematic individually; combined, they represent a systemic AML/sanctions compliance failure.
---
## Viable paths
**Path 1: Collect CIP at onboarding (recommended).** Redesign the onboarding flow to collect full identity information (name, date of birth, address, identification number) before any payout is released. This is the compliant approach. The engineering concern about UI friction can be addressed through progressive disclosure, third-party identity verification APIs, or risk-based simplified onboarding for low-risk sellers — but identity must be collected before the first payout, not after $3,000.
**Path 2: Limit pre-verification payouts to a de minimis amount.** If the business insists on allowing some payouts before full verification, limit them to a very small amount (e.g., $100 or less) and do not publicly display the threshold. This is a risk-based approach that some regulators may tolerate for truly de minimis amounts, but it is not clearly authorized by BSA/AML rules and carries residual risk. This path requires a documented risk assessment and legal opinion.
**Path 3: Rely on the bank partner's CIP.** If the bank partner agrees in writing to take full responsibility for CIP (including collecting identity information through Mosaic Relay's onboarding flow), Mosaic Relay may be able to rely on the bank's CIP program. However, this requires the bank to explicitly accept this responsibility and for Mosaic Relay's onboarding to meet the bank's CIP standards. The bank partner's review of the deck does not constitute this acceptance [source:SRC-3a9350fde282eecf6295|Start of document].
**Path 4: Ship as-is and remediate later.** Not recommended. The CIP violation is not a technicality — it is a core AML requirement. Operating with unidentified customers receiving payouts creates enforcement risk, potential civil money penalties, and reputational harm.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Mosaic Relay's MSB registration status.** If Mosaic Relay is not registered as an MSB and is purely a technology provider with no control over funds, the CIP obligation may fall entirely on the bank partner. However, the facts state Mosaic Relay initiates ACH payouts, which suggests MSB status.
- **Bank partner's written agreement.** If the bank partner has contractually agreed to handle CIP and Mosaic Relay's role is limited to providing the technology interface, the analysis shifts. This would need to be confirmed in the agency agreement.
- **Whether any BSA exemption applies.** Certain exemptions exist for specific types of transactions or customers (e.g., exempt person thresholds for currency transaction reporting), but none apply to CIP timing for money transmitters.
- **State money transmitter laws.** Some states may have additional or different identification requirements that could affect the analysis.
---
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Confirm Mosaic Relay's FinCEN MSB registration status** and whether it holds state money transmitter licenses for the payout flow.
2. **Review the bank partner agency agreement** to determine whether the bank has contractually accepted CIP responsibility and what identity collection standards it requires.
3. **Obtain a legal opinion** on whether any BSA exemption or safe harbor could justify deferred identity collection for de minimis payout amounts.
4. **Review FinCEN guidance and enforcement actions** on CIP timing for money transmitters and payment processors to confirm the analysis.
5. **Assess the engineering timeline** for implementing identity collection at onboarding and whether it can be completed before Q1.
