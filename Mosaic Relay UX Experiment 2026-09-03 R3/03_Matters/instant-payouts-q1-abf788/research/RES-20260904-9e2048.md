# What are the OFAC and AML obligations for releasing payouts

## Question

What are the OFAC and AML obligations for releasing payouts before sanctions screening completes?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company, is launching Instant Payouts for marketplace sellers. The product team plans to release payouts via the bank partner's ACH rails and through USDC for international sellers, with OFAC screening running nightly in batch after payouts are released. Counsel is involved because the design releases funds before sanctions screening completes, creating potential OFAC and AML exposure. The target launch is Q1 (September 5, 2026).
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay ship Instant Payouts in Q1 with OFAC screening running nightly in batch after payouts are released, or must screening be completed before funds are released to avoid strict liability sanctions exposure and AML program violations?
## Open questions
- What is Mosaic Relay's exact regulatory status for these payouts — is it acting as a money services business (MSB), an agent of the bank partner, or another covered entity under BSA/AML?
- Does the bank partner's review of the deck constitute a legal determination that the batch screening design is acceptable, or is that merely a business acknowledgment?
- What is the specific operational definition of "screening completes" — does it mean the batch job finishes, or that any hits are resolved?
- Are there any payout-specific rules (e.g., card network, ACH network, or state money transmitter rules) that impose timing requirements on sanctions screening separate from OFAC and BSA?
- What is the geographic scope of "international sellers" beyond Mexico and Asia, and do any of those jurisdictions trigger additional sanctions or export control obligations?

## Likely rules/issues
**No external authority retrieved.**
The supplied public source material does not address OFAC, sanctions screening, or AML/BSA obligations. It consists of consumer credit disclosure rules (Regulation Z, Regulation B) that are not triggered by sanctions screening timing. Therefore, the analysis below is generated analysis based on general legal principles, not retrieved authority.
**OFAC strict liability for pre-screening release.** OFAC regulations generally prohibit dealings with sanctioned parties and require blocking of property in which a sanctioned party has an interest. Releasing funds before screening completes creates a risk that funds are released to a sanctioned party, which could constitute a prohibited dealing or failure to block. OFAC liability is generally strict — intent or knowledge is not required for a violation. The nightly batch design means payouts are released before screening occurs, which is the core problem.
**BSA/AML program obligations.** If Mosaic Relay is a covered financial institution (e.g., an MSB), it must maintain an AML program that includes risk-based procedures for sanctions screening. Releasing funds before screening may be inconsistent with a risk-based program, particularly for higher-risk payout types (e.g., international, USDC, self-hosted wallets). The $3,000 threshold for identity collection is not a recognized BSA CIP trigger; CIP must generally be completed before establishing the account relationship.
**Money transmission licensing.** Operating as an agent under the bank partner's license does not resolve Mosaic Relay's own licensing posture. If Mosaic Relay is initiating ACH payouts and controlling the flow of funds, it may need its own money transmitter licenses in applicable states. This is separate from OFAC/AML obligations but intersects with them.
**USDC payouts.** Payouts in USDC to international sellers, including to self-hosted wallets, raise additional sanctions and AML concerns. OFAC has taken enforcement action against virtual currency companies for sanctions violations. The Travel Rule may apply to virtual asset transfers. Self-hosted wallets present heightened risk because there is no intermediary to screen.
**Beneficial ownership.** Not collecting beneficial owners for business sellers is a KYB/AML gap. While the CDD Rule's beneficial ownership requirement applies to covered financial institutions, Mosaic Relay's own risk-based program and bank partner requirements may effectively require it.
**Progress bar disclosure.** Displaying the $3,000 verification threshold may facilitate evasion by allowing bad actors to structure payouts below the threshold. It also creates a misleading impression that $3,000 is a legal compliance threshold.
## Viable paths
**Path 1: Redesign to pre-release screening.** Move OFAC screening to real-time or near-real-time before payout release. This is the lowest-risk path. The 400ms latency concern can be addressed through asynchronous UX (e.g., show "processing" state), pre-screening at onboarding, or risk-based pre-approval for low-risk sellers. This path likely requires engineering changes but preserves the Q1 timeline if started immediately.
**Path 2: Risk-based hybrid with post-release controls.** Release payouts for low-risk sellers (e.g., domestic, established history, low amounts) with pre-screening at onboarding, while holding higher-risk payouts (international, USDC, new sellers, high amounts) for pre-release screening. This requires a documented risk assessment and may still face regulatory scrutiny.
**Path 3: Delay launch to redesign.** Postpone Q1 launch to implement pre-release screening across all payout types. This is the most conservative path but may miss the business window.
**Path 4: Ship as-is with remediation plan.** This is not recommended. Releasing funds before OFAC screening creates strict liability exposure that cannot be remediated after the fact. The bank partner's review of the deck does not constitute legal approval.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Mosaic Relay's regulatory status.** If Mosaic Relay is not a covered financial institution and is acting purely as a technology provider to the bank partner, the OFAC/AML obligations may shift to the bank. However, the facts state Mosaic Relay is "the one initiating" ACH payouts, which suggests it may have its own obligations.
- **Bank partner's contractual requirements.** The bank partner may impose its own screening timing requirements that are stricter than OFAC's minimums.
- **Volume and risk profile.** If the seller base is overwhelmingly low-risk (e.g., domestic, established businesses), a risk-based approach may be more defensible.
- **Circuit's role.** If Circuit is the party releasing USDC and is a licensed exchange with its own OFAC obligations, some risk may shift, but Mosaic Relay's role in initiating the payout still creates exposure.
- **State money transmitter laws.** Some states may have specific timing or screening requirements that differ from federal OFAC rules.
## Recommended last-mile verification
- **Confirm Mosaic Relay's MSB status and state licensing posture.** Determine whether Mosaic Relay is registered as an MSB with FinCEN and holds required state money transmitter licenses for the payout flow.
- **Review the bank partner agreement.** Identify any contractual screening timing requirements, indemnification provisions, and audit rights.
- **Obtain OFAC guidance on batch screening.** Review OFAC's enforcement actions and guidance on sanctions screening timing for payment processors and MSBs.
- **Assess USDC payout flow.** Determine whether Mosaic Relay or Circuit is the party releasing USDC, and whether the Travel Rule applies.
- **Document the risk assessment.** If pursuing a risk-based hybrid approach, document the rationale, controls, and monitoring for any post-release screening.
- **Consult with the bank partner's compliance team.** Obtain written confirmation of their position on the batch screening design, not just business review of the deck.
