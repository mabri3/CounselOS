# What are the UK and EU beneficial-owner verification and KYB/KYC

## Question

What are the UK and EU beneficial-owner verification and KYB/KYC requirements for payment platforms onboarding marketplace sellers?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure provider, is designing a risk-based onboarding flow for a marketplace customer to onboard sellers in the US, UK, and EU. The flow would collect business and ownership data, run KYB/KYC and sanctions checks, and allow low-risk sellers to begin limited card acceptance within minutes while deferring full payouts until enhanced review completes. Counsel is involved because the shortcut must be reconciled with UK and EU beneficial-owner verification and AML obligations before the planned 1–3 month launch. The consequence of misalignment is regulatory exposure for financial-crime control failures and potential inability to operate in target markets.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay enable minutes-level limited card acceptance for general retail sellers in the UK and EU while deferring full beneficial-owner verification and payout activation until enhanced review completes, and what specific UK/EU KYB/KYC controls must be hard-coded into the onboarding flow to avoid AML violations before the 1–3 month launch window closes?
## Open questions
- Which specific EU member states will sellers operate in, and do any require lower beneficial-ownership thresholds or additional local register checks beyond the baseline 25%?
- Will the marketplace or Mosaic Relay be the "obliged entity" for AML purposes in each jurisdiction, or will reliance be placed on third-party introducers, and does that allocation satisfy local regulatory expectations?
- What is the legal basis and retention schedule for personal data under GDPR given the 6-year UK/EU AML retention standard, and are deletion exceptions documented?
- Do any acquiring partners or card networks impose stricter beneficial-owner verification requirements than the statutory baseline for marketplace seller onboarding?
## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.**
Based on model knowledge and matter context, the controlling frameworks are:
**United Kingdom**
- **Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (MLR 2017)**: Requires customer due diligence (CDD) on the seller and any beneficial owner. A beneficial owner is generally any individual owning or controlling more than 25% of shares or voting rights, or exercising control via other means. Verification must be based on documents or information from a reliable, independent source. For marketplaces, the platform operator may be an obliged entity if it enters into a business relationship with sellers.
- **People with Significant Control (PSC) regime**: UK companies must maintain a PSC register. Mosaic Relay should verify seller-provided ownership data against the PSC register where available, but cannot rely solely on self-certification.
- **Risk-based approach**: MLR 2017 permits simplified due diligence (SDD) for lower-risk situations, but SDD is not available where there is a higher risk of money laundering or where the customer is not physically present for identification (relevant for API/onboarding flows). Enhanced due diligence (EDD) is required for higher-risk cases, complex ownership, or high-risk third countries.
**European Union**
- **4th and 5th Anti-Money Laundering Directives (AMLD4/AMLD5)**: Establish a 25% ownership threshold for beneficial owners, plus a control prong. Member states may lower thresholds. Obliged entities must verify identity using documents, data, or information from a reliable and independent source. AMLD5 expanded obliged entities to include certain virtual currency providers and custodian wallet providers, but payment platforms are already covered under AMLD4.
- **Risk-based approach**: SDD is permitted for lower-risk categories, but member states have discretion to restrict SDD. EDD is mandatory for high-risk third countries, politically exposed persons (PEPs), and complex or unusually large transactions.
- **Upcoming changes**: The EU AML Authority (AMLA) and the proposed 6th AML Directive (AMLD6) and EU AML Regulation will harmonize rules further, but the current framework remains member-state transposed law.
**Cross-cutting issues**
- **Money transmission / payment services**: In the UK, the Payment Services Regulations 2017 (PSRs) and in the EU, PSD2 require authorization or registration for payment services. Mosaic Relay’s direct-to-seller funds flow reduces custody risk but does not eliminate the need to determine whether it or the marketplace is providing a "payment service" requiring authorization.
- **Sanctions screening**: UK (OFSI) and EU (Council Regulations) sanctions regimes require screening against consolidated lists. Weekly re-screening is a reasonable baseline, but real-time or near-real-time screening is expected for payout activation.
- **GDPR**: Processing of beneficial-owner and seller personal data requires a lawful basis (likely legal obligation for AML, legitimate interests for fraud prevention). Retention must be justified; 6 years aligns with UK MLR and many EU member-state AML laws, but GDPR’s storage-limitation principle requires documented justification and deletion protocols.
## Viable paths
**Path A: Jurisdiction-gated pilot with hard stops**
Launch first in the US (where the 25% FinCEN CDD standard is confirmed) and defer UK/EU activation until local counsel confirms thresholds and register checks. For UK/EU, require full beneficial-owner verification before any acceptance or payouts. This is the lowest-regulatory-risk path but may miss the 1–3 month window for UK/EU.
**Path B: UK/EU launch with enhanced controls**
Proceed in the UK and selected EU member states (e.g., Ireland, Netherlands, Germany) where local counsel confirms the 25% threshold and PSC/register checks are sufficient. Implement the minutes-level limited approval only after automated sanctions and identity checks pass, but disable payouts until enhanced review completes and beneficial-owner verification is confirmed. This path requires immediate engagement of UK/EU counsel and may still face delays if local requirements differ.
**Path C: Marketplace-led verification with Mosaic Relay oversight**
Rely on the marketplace to perform initial KYB/KYC and beneficial-owner verification, with Mosaic Relay conducting risk-based sampling and audit. This is faster but requires a written agreement meeting UK MLR and EU AMLD "reliance" standards, including audit rights, evidence access, and change notification. Regulators scrutinize reliance arrangements; Mosaic Relay remains liable for failures.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Specific EU member states**: Some countries (e.g., France, Italy) have lower thresholds or additional local register requirements. A country-by-country survey is essential.
- **Entity types**: If sellers include trusts, partnerships, or complex corporate structures, beneficial-owner verification becomes more complex and may require EDD.
- **High-risk categories**: If the marketplace later onboards sellers in gambling, adult, crypto, or other high-risk sectors, SDD is unavailable and EDD is mandatory.
- **Funds flow**: If Mosaic Relay or the marketplace ever holds or controls seller funds (even temporarily), money-transmission or payment-service authorization requirements are triggered.
- **Acquiring partner requirements**: Card networks and acquiring banks may impose stricter standards than statutory minimums, especially for cross-border payouts.
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Engage UK and EU counsel** in each target member state to confirm beneficial-owner thresholds, register-check requirements, and any local deviations from the 25% standard.
2. **Map the obliged-entity allocation** between Mosaic Relay and the marketplace; document who is responsible for CDD, recordkeeping, and reporting in each jurisdiction.
3. **Review acquiring-partner and card-network rules** for marketplace seller onboarding; ensure contractual flow-down of AML and sanctions obligations.
4. **Document GDPR lawful basis and retention schedule**; align the 6-year AML retention with GDPR storage-limitation and deletion protocols.
5. **Test the hard-stop rule** in the onboarding flow: verify that acceptance and payouts are technically blocked when a potential sanctions match or identity failure remains unresolved.
## What would change this
- **Working assumption**: The 25% beneficial-ownership threshold is sufficient for UK and EU operations. If any target member state applies a lower threshold (e.g., 10% for high-risk sectors), the onboarding flow must capture additional owners.
- **Open fork**: Whether Mosaic Relay or the marketplace is the obliged entity for AML purposes. If Mosaic Relay is the obliged entity, it must perform full CDD itself; if the marketplace is, Mosaic Relay can rely on its checks only with a compliant reliance agreement.
- **Not examined**: Country-specific AML laws and guidance for each EU member state where sellers will operate. The answer assumes a harmonized baseline that may not exist in practice.
- **Not examined**: The specific terms of acquiring-partner and card-network agreements, which may impose stricter verification or prohibit certain risk-based shortcuts.
- **Working assumption**: The 6-year retention period is compatible with GDPR. If a member state requires shorter retention or if GDPR authorities challenge the legal basis, the data-retention design must change.
