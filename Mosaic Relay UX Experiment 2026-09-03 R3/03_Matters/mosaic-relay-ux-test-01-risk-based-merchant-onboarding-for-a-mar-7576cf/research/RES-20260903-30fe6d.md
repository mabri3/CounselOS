      ## Matter summary

      Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure provider, is designing a risk-based onboarding
      flow for a new marketplace customer to onboard sellers via API and hosted page.
      The flow would collect extensive seller data (legal name, business details,
      tax information, ownership structure, government ID, bank details) and run KYB/KYC,
      sanctions screening, identity verification, and fraud-risk scoring before enabling
      card acceptance and payouts. The business goal is to reduce onboarding time
      by allowing low-risk sellers to begin limited activity within minutes, with
      enhanced review completed within 1-3 business days when triggered. Counsel is
      involved because this touches money transmission, beneficial ownership verification,
      sanctions compliance, and consumer protection across US, UK, and EU jurisdictions,
      with a target launch in 1-3 months.

      ## Decision question

      What beneficial-ownership verification standards must Mosaic Relay apply to
      legal entity sellers under current US FinCEN Customer Due Diligence (CDD) rules
      and the Corporate Transparency Act (CTA) beneficial-ownership information (BOI)
      reporting requirements, and how do those standards interact with the proposed
      minutes-level limited approval for low-risk sellers?

      ## Open questions

      - Does the marketplace customer''s seller base include entity types (e.g., trusts,
      partnerships, LLCs with layered ownership) that complicate the 25% ownership
      threshold calculation or trigger enhanced due diligence under FinCEN guidance?

      - Will Mosaic Relay rely on the marketplace to collect and verify beneficial-owner
      information, or will Mosaic Relay perform that verification directly, and what
      contractual and operational controls are needed to shift or share that responsibility?

      - How will Mosaic Relay reconcile the 25% US beneficial-ownership threshold
      with UK/EU standards (which may use 25% but apply different verification and
      reporting mechanics) for cross-border sellers?

      - What is the legal consequence if a seller begins limited activity (card acceptance
      or payouts) during the minutes-level approval window and is later found to have
      an undisclosed beneficial owner who is a sanctioned party or otherwise ineligible?

      ---

      ## Likely rules and issues

      **US FinCEN CDD Rule (31 CFR 1010.230)**

      The FinCEN CDD Rule requires covered financial institutions to identify and
      verify the identity of beneficial owners of legal entity customers at account
      opening. A "beneficial owner" is defined as:

      1. **Ownership prong**: Any individual who, directly or indirectly, owns 25%
      or more of the equity interests of the legal entity customer.

      2. **Control prong**: A single individual with significant responsibility to
      control, manage, or direct the legal entity customer (e.g., CEO, CFO, COO, managing
      member, general partner, president, vice president, treasurer, or any other
      individual who regularly performs similar functions).

      **Key obligations:**

      - **Identification**: Collect name, date of birth, address, and identification
      number (SSN for US persons; passport or similar for non-US persons) for each
      beneficial owner.

      - **Verification**: Verify identity using risk-based procedures similar to Customer
      Identification Program (CIP) requirements under 31 USC 5318(l). Verification
      can be documentary (government-issued ID) or non-documentary (database checks,
      credit bureau, etc.).

      - **Timing**: Verification must occur before account opening or within a reasonable
      time thereafter, but the account may be opened if the institution has a reasonable
      belief that it knows the true identity of the beneficial owner.

      - **Recordkeeping**: Maintain records of identifying information and verification
      methods for five years after the account closes.

      **Corporate Transparency Act (CTA) BOI Reporting**

      The CTA (31 USC 5336) requires certain legal entities (corporations, LLCs, and
      similar entities) to report beneficial-ownership information to FinCEN. This
      is a separate obligation from the CDD Rule:

      - **Who must report**: Domestic and foreign entities registered to do business
      in the US, unless exempt (e.g., publicly traded companies, regulated financial
      institutions, tax-exempt entities, large operating companies with 20+ employees
      and $5M+ revenue).

      - **What must be reported**: Name, date of birth, address, and unique identifying
      number (passport, driver''s license, etc.) for each beneficial owner (25% ownership
      or substantial control).

      - **When**: New entities must report within 30 days of formation (as of 2024);
      existing entities had until January 1, 2025.

      - **Access**: FinCEN maintains a non-public database; financial institutions
      can access it with customer consent for CDD compliance.

      **Interaction with Mosaic Relay''s flow:**

      Mosaic Relay is not a bank and does not hold deposits, but it is likely a "covered
      financial institution" under the BSA if it qualifies as a money services business
      (MSB) or money transmitter. If so, the CDD Rule applies to its legal entity
      customers (the marketplace and potentially the sellers, depending on the contractual
      structure).

      **Critical issue**: The CDD Rule applies to "account opening," which for Mosaic
      Relay likely means when it enables payment acceptance or payout services for
      a seller. The proposed minutes-level limited approval creates tension:

      - **Permissible shortcut**: FinCEN guidance allows risk-based verification and
      does not require all beneficial owners to be verified before account opening
      if the institution has a reasonable belief it knows their identities and will
      complete verification within a reasonable time.

      - **Hard limit**: Mosaic Relay cannot enable acceptance or payouts if there
      is an unresolved sanctions match or identity verification failure (confirmed
      in the internal record).

      - **Risk-based approach**: For low-risk sellers (general retail/services, low
      transaction volume, US/UK/EU only), Mosaic Relay could collect beneficial-owner
      information at onboarding, run automated database checks, and enable limited
      activity while completing enhanced verification within 1-3 business days.

      **UK/EU beneficial-ownership standards:**

      - **UK**: The Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (MLR 2017) require identification
      and verification of beneficial owners at 25% ownership or control. The UK also
      has a public register (People with Significant Control, or PSC register) for
      UK companies.

      - **EU**: The 5th Anti-Money Laundering Directive (5AMLD) requires member states
      to maintain beneficial-ownership registers and requires obliged entities to
      identify and verify beneficial owners at 25% ownership or control. The 6th AMLD
      (6AMLD) and proposed EU AML Regulation may harmonize further.

      **Key difference**: UK/EU standards may require more immediate verification
      and public register cross-checks, whereas the US CDD Rule allows more flexibility
      in timing.

      ## Viable paths

      **Path 1: Full beneficial-owner verification before any activity (conservative)**

      - Collect and verify all beneficial owners (25% ownership + control prong) before
      enabling any card acceptance or payouts.

      - Use documentary verification (government ID) and non-documentary methods (database
      checks, credit bureau).

      - Cross-check against sanctions lists and adverse media.

      - **Pros**: Lowest legal risk; clear compliance with CDD Rule and UK/EU standards.

      - **Cons**: Slows onboarding; defeats the business goal of minutes-level approval
      for low-risk sellers.

      **Path 2: Risk-based limited approval with delayed full verification (moderate)**

      - Collect beneficial-owner information at onboarding (name, DOB, address, ID
      number).

      - Run automated sanctions screening and database checks (e.g., LexisNexis, Experian)
      for identity verification.

      - Enable limited activity (e.g., capped transaction volume, no payouts) for
      sellers who pass automated checks and have no red flags.

      - Complete full verification (documentary ID, enhanced due diligence) within
      1-3 business days before enabling full activity or payouts.

      - **Pros**: Balances speed and compliance; aligns with FinCEN risk-based approach;
      meets business goal.

      - **Cons**: Requires robust automated checks and clear escalation triggers;
      risk of enabling activity for a seller with an undisclosed sanctioned beneficial
      owner.

      **Path 3: Marketplace-led verification with Mosaic Relay oversight (delegated)**

      - Contractually require the marketplace to collect and verify beneficial-owner
      information per Mosaic Relay''s standards.

      - Mosaic Relay performs sanctions screening and spot-checks or audits marketplace
      verification.

      - Enable activity based on marketplace attestation plus Mosaic Relay''s automated
      screening.

      - **Pros**: Fastest onboarding; shifts operational burden to marketplace.

      - **Cons**: Mosaic Relay remains legally responsible for CDD compliance; requires
      strong contractual indemnities and audit rights; may not satisfy UK/EU standards
      if marketplace verification is inadequate.

      - **Entity types in seller base**: If the marketplace onboards trusts, partnerships,
      or LLCs with complex ownership structures, the 25% threshold calculation becomes
      more difficult and may require enhanced due diligence or legal review.

      - **Cross-border sellers**: If sellers are organized in or have beneficial owners
      in high-risk jurisdictions (e.g., FATF grey/blacklist countries), enhanced due
      diligence and possibly senior management approval are required.

      - **Transaction volume and velocity**: If a seller''s transaction volume or
      velocity exceeds expectations during the limited-approval window, Mosaic Relay
      may need to suspend activity pending full verification.

      - **Marketplace''s regulatory status**: If the marketplace is itself a regulated
      financial institution (e.g., a licensed money transmitter or bank), Mosaic Relay
      may be able to rely on the marketplace''s CDD under certain conditions, but
      this requires careful legal analysis and contractual documentation.

      - **State money transmission laws**: If Mosaic Relay''s activities trigger state
      money transmission licensing requirements (which vary by state), additional
      beneficial-ownership or control-person disclosure requirements may apply.
