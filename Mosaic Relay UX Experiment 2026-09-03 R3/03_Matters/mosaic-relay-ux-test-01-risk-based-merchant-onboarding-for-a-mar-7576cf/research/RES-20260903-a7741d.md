# What are the money transmission licensing requirements in US states

## Question

What are the money transmission licensing requirements in US states and the UK/EU for platforms that route funds directly to sellers without holding them?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure provider, is designing a risk-based onboarding flow for a marketplace customer to onboard sellers in the US, UK, and EU. The proposed flow would allow low-risk sellers to begin limited card acceptance within minutes, with enhanced review completed within one to three business days when needed. Mosaic Relay is not a bank and does not hold deposits, but the flow involves cross-border card acceptance and payouts with expected monthly volume up to $3M. Counsel is involved because the money transmission licensing requirements for platforms that route funds directly to sellers without holding them are unresolved and could materially affect the launch timeline and compliance posture.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay lawfully enable limited card acceptance and seller payouts for a marketplace customer in the US, UK, and EU without holding funds, and what licensing, verification, and control conditions must be satisfied before launch in 1–3 months?
## Open questions
- Which specific US states will the marketplace and its sellers operate in, and do any of those states require money transmitter licenses for platforms that route funds directly to sellers without holding them?
- What are the UK and EU definitions of regulated payment services and acquiring, and do any exclusions apply to Mosaic Relay's direct-to-seller model?
- Does the marketplace or Mosaic Relay ever take possession or control of buyer funds, even momentarily, during settlement?
- What are the acquiring partner's licensing and compliance requirements, and how do they allocate responsibility for money transmission and sanctions screening?
- What are the specific seller categories, geographic coverage by country, and cross-border activity patterns that could trigger additional licensing or regulatory obligations?
## Likely rules/issues
- **Money transmission licensing**: US state money transmitter statutes vary, but many define "money transmission" broadly to include receiving money for transmission or transmitting money. The direct-to-seller model may reduce exposure, but some states may still require licenses if the platform or its partner ever takes possession or control of funds. The UK Payment Services Regulations and EU PSD2 define regulated payment services and acquiring, with potential exclusions for commercial agents or technical service providers.
- **Beneficial-owner verification**: The US FinCEN CDD standard requires verification of beneficial owners at 25% ownership, but UK and EU rules may have different thresholds or definitions. The marketplace-led verification model must meet written standards, audit rights, and oversight requirements.
- **Sanctions screening**: US, UK, and EU sanctions regimes require screening of sellers, beneficial owners, and payout recipients. Weekly re-screening and a mechanism to act on new hits are essential.
- **Consumer protection**: Regulation E and EFTA § 913 govern consumer error resolution and unauthorized transfers, but do not directly address licensing. However, they may affect the platform's obligations if consumers are involved in transactions.
## Viable paths
1. **Controlled pilot with jurisdiction-gating**: Launch in a limited number of US states and EU countries with clear licensing guidance, and expand only after verifying compliance. This path requires a 50-state and country-by-country survey, which has not been completed.
2. **Partner-led licensing**: Rely on the acquiring partner's licenses and compliance infrastructure, with Mosaic Relay acting as a technical service provider. This path requires clear contractual allocation of responsibility and verification of the partner's licenses.
3. **Marketplace-led verification**: Allow the marketplace to conduct KYB/KYC and sanctions screening, with Mosaic Relay providing oversight and audit rights. This path requires written standards, access to evidence, and change notification.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Possession or control of funds**: If Mosaic Relay or the marketplace ever takes possession or control of buyer funds, even momentarily, the licensing analysis could change significantly.
- **Seller categories and geographic coverage**: High-risk categories or operations in high-risk jurisdictions could trigger additional licensing or regulatory obligations.
- **Cross-border activity**: Cross-border card acceptance and payouts could trigger additional licensing or regulatory obligations in the US, UK, and EU.
- **Acquiring partner requirements**: The acquiring partner's licensing and compliance requirements could affect the allocation of responsibility and the viability of the direct-to-seller model.
## Recommended last-mile verification
- **50-state and country-by-country survey**: Complete a survey of US state money transmitter statutes and UK/EU payment services regulations to identify licensing requirements and exclusions.
- **Acquiring partner due diligence**: Verify the acquiring partner's licenses and compliance infrastructure, and confirm the allocation of responsibility for money transmission and sanctions screening.
- **Contract review**: Review the marketplace and seller contracts to ensure clear allocation of responsibility and compliance with licensing and regulatory requirements.
- **Sanctions screening validation**: Validate the sanctions screening process, including fuzzy-match handling, disposition evidence, escalation ownership, blocking, rescreening, and release authority.
