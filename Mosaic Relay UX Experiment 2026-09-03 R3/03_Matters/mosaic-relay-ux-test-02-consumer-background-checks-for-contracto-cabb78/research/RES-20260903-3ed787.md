# FCRA requirements for users of consumer reports in employment/contractor screeni

## Question

FCRA requirements for users of consumer reports in employment/contractor screening and adverse action

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay wants to offer a platform customer a feature that screens contractors before they can receive payouts, using a background-screening vendor to obtain reports and returning an eligibility result ("eligible," "manual review," or "not eligible") to the customer. The platform customer makes the final eligibility decision. Screening would occur at onboarding, annually, and after certain fraud or dispute events, and may use criminal-record, identity, sanctions, and address information. Counsel is involved because the feature likely implicates the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) and related consumer-protection obligations, and the allocation of compliance duties between Mosaic Relay, the customer, and the vendor is unresolved.
## Decision question
May Mosaic Relay lawfully provide a contractor-screening feature that returns eligibility results to a platform customer for payout decisions, and if so, what FCRA disclosures, consents, adverse-action procedures, and information-use restrictions must be built into the product before launch?
## Open questions
- Is the background-screening vendor a consumer reporting agency (CRA) subject to FCRA, or a non-FCRA data provider? This determines whether the full FCRA framework applies.
- Will the screening reports include criminal-record information, and if so, which state ban-the-box and criminal-record-use laws apply to the platform customer's eligibility decisions?
- What is the intended data flow: does Mosaic Relay receive the full report and return only a status code, or does the customer receive report contents? This affects who is a "user" of consumer reports and who bears adverse-action duties.
- What retention and disposal periods will apply to contractor identifying information and screening results?
- Will any screening information be used to restrict, delay, or condition payouts (as opposed to gating onboarding), and if so, what additional consumer-protection or funds-flow disclosures are required?

## Likely rules and issues
### FCRA applicability
The FCRA governs "consumer reports" — communications from a consumer reporting agency bearing on a consumer's character, general reputation, personal characteristics, or mode of living that are used or expected to be used for employment purposes or other permissible purposes. Background checks on contractors for payout eligibility almost certainly qualify as consumer reports when obtained from a CRA.
**Key threshold question:** Is the vendor a CRA? If the vendor assembles or evaluates consumer information for fees and regularly furnishes consumer reports to third parties, it is a CRA subject to FCRA. Most commercial background-screening vendors are CRAs. If the vendor is not a CRA (e.g., a pure data aggregator that does not assemble or evaluate), FCRA may not apply, but this is uncommon for the type of screening described.
**Permissible purpose:** Employment purposes is a permissible purpose under FCRA § 604(a)(3)(B). Screening contractors for payout eligibility likely falls within "employment purposes" as defined by FCRA § 603(h), which covers "evaluating a consumer for employment, promotion, reassignment or retention as an employee." The FTC and courts have generally treated independent contractors as covered when the screening is for work eligibility.
### User obligations (Mosaic Relay and/or the platform customer)
Whoever is the "user" of the consumer report bears FCRA obligations:
1. **Certification to the CRA** (§ 604(f)): The user must certify to the CRA that it has a permissible purpose, will comply with FCRA, and will not use the information in violation of equal employment opportunity laws.
2. **Disclosure and authorization** (§ 604(b)(2)): Before procuring a consumer report for employment purposes, the user must:
- Provide a clear and conspicuous disclosure in a document consisting solely of the disclosure that a consumer report may be obtained for employment purposes
- Obtain the consumer's written authorization
3. **Adverse action procedures** (§ 604(b)(3) and § 615(a)): If the user takes adverse action based in whole or in part on the consumer report, the user must:
- **Before** taking adverse action: provide the consumer with a copy of the report and the CFPB's "Summary of Your Rights Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act"
- **After** taking adverse action: provide notice of the adverse action, the name/address/phone of the CRA that furnished the report, a statement that the CRA did not make the decision and cannot explain it, notice of the consumer's right to obtain a free copy of the report from the CRA within 60 days, and notice of the right to dispute accuracy
**Who is the user?** The facts state the platform customer makes the final eligibility decision. If Mosaic Relay obtains the report and returns only a status code, Mosaic Relay is likely the user. If the customer receives report contents or makes decisions based on report data, the customer is also a user. Both may be users. The product design should clarify this allocation.
### Sanctions screening vs. consumer reports
Sanctions screening (e.g., OFAC list matching) is generally **not** a consumer report under FCRA if it is based solely on matching a name against a government list without assembling or evaluating other consumer information. However, if sanctions screening is bundled into a background report from a CRA, it becomes part of the consumer report. Identity verification and address verification may or may not be consumer reports depending on the data sources and whether the vendor assembles/evaluates the information.
### State law overlay
Even with US-only scope, state laws add requirements:
- **Ban-the-box / fair chance laws:** Many states and localities restrict when criminal history can be considered and require individualized assessment before adverse action. These apply to the entity making the eligibility decision (the platform customer).
- **State consumer reporting laws:** Some states (e.g., California, New York) have their own consumer reporting statutes with additional disclosure, consent, or adverse-action requirements.
- **State criminal record use restrictions:** Some states limit the use of arrests not resulting in conviction, expunged records, or records older than a certain period.
### Payout restrictions
Using screening results to restrict, delay, or condition payouts (as opposed to gating initial onboarding) raises additional issues:
- **Consumer protection / unfairness:** If a contractor has already performed work and payout is withheld based on screening, this could be challenged as unfair or deceptive if not clearly disclosed upfront.
- **Funds-flow disclosures:** Mosaic Relay's role as a payment infrastructure provider means payout timing and conditions should be clearly disclosed in the platform's terms with contractors.
- **Adverse action timing:** If a payout restriction is an adverse action based on a consumer report, the full FCRA adverse-action process must be followed before the restriction takes effect.

## Viable paths
### Path 1: Mosaic Relay as the user, returning only status codes
Mosaic Relay obtains the report from the CRA, applies decision rules, and returns only "eligible," "manual review," or "not eligible" to the customer. The customer never sees report contents.
**Compliance allocation:**
- Mosaic Relay is the user and bears FCRA obligations: certification, disclosure/authorization, adverse action.
- The customer is not a user (receives only a status code, not report contents).
- Mosaic Relay must build the full FCRA workflow into the product.
**Advantages:** Cleaner allocation; customer has no FCRA exposure; consistent with the business goal of not exposing detailed reports to platform staff.
**Challenges:** Mosaic Relay must handle adverse action directly with contractors, which may be operationally complex. The customer may want more visibility into why a contractor was flagged.
### Path 2: Customer as the user, Mosaic Relay as a conduit
The customer obtains the report directly from the CRA (or through Mosaic Relay as a technical conduit), makes the eligibility decision, and bears all FCRA obligations.
**Compliance allocation:**
- The customer is the user and bears all FCRA obligations.
- Mosaic Relay is a service provider / conduit and has limited FCRA exposure (but should still contractually require the customer to comply).
**Advantages:** Mosaic Relay's compliance burden is lower; the customer controls the decision and the adverse-action process.
**Challenges:** The customer may not want to handle FCRA compliance; Mosaic Relay may still be seen as a user if it receives or processes report contents; the business goal of shielding platform staff from detailed reports is harder to achieve.
### Path 3: Hybrid — Mosaic Relay as user for screening, customer as user for decision
Mosaic Relay obtains the report, applies initial decision rules, and returns a status code. For "manual review" cases, the customer receives limited report contents (e.g., criminal/identity only) and makes the final decision.
**Compliance allocation:**
- Mosaic Relay is a user for the initial screening.
- The customer is a user for manual review cases where it receives report contents.
- Both must comply with FCRA for their respective uses.
**Advantages:** Balances the business goal (most cases return only status codes) with customer flexibility (manual review gets more detail).
**Challenges:** More complex compliance allocation; both parties need FCRA workflows; the "limited report" concept needs careful definition to avoid scope creep.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Vendor status:** If the vendor is not a CRA, FCRA may not apply, and the analysis shifts to state law and contract.
- **Report contents:** If the report includes only sanctions and identity verification (no criminal records, no credit), it may not be a consumer report, or may be subject to fewer restrictions.
- **Decision-maker:** If Mosaic Relay (not the customer) makes the final eligibility decision, Mosaic Relay is clearly the user and bears all FCRA duties.
- **Payout timing:** If screening occurs only before any work is performed (pure onboarding gate), the consumer-protection and funds-flow issues are simpler. If screening occurs after work is performed and affects payout for completed work, the unfairness and disclosure issues are more acute.
- **State scope:** If contractors are concentrated in states with aggressive ban-the-box or consumer reporting laws (e.g., California, New York, Illinois), the state law overlay becomes more significant.
