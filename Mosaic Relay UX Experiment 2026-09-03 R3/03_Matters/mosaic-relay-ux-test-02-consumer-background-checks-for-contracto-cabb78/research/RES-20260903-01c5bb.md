# Whether sanctions and identity screening fall outside FCRA consumer-report oblig

## Question

Whether sanctions and identity screening fall outside FCRA consumer-report obligations

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure provider, wants to offer platform customers a feature that screens contractors before they can receive payouts. The feature would collect contractor consent and identifying information, obtain background reports from a vendor, and return eligibility results to the platform. Counsel is involved because the feature implicates federal FCRA obligations, state consumer-reporting laws, and questions about who bears compliance duties when screening affects payment access. The platform customer has been confirmed as the final decision-maker, and the service would operate US-only with screening at onboarding, annually, and after certain fraud events.
## Decision question
Whether Mosaic Relay may treat sanctions screening and identity verification as separate from FCRA consumer-report obligations when those checks are bundled with or used alongside criminal background reports that determine contractor payout eligibility, and if separation is possible, what product architecture and contractual terms are required to maintain that distinction without creating co-user FCRA liability for Mosaic Relay.
## Open questions
- Does the background-screening vendor operate as a consumer reporting agency (CRA) under FCRA, or does it provide only non-FCRA data services such as sanctions list matching or identity verification?
- Will sanctions and identity data be included in the same report as criminal-record information, or delivered through separate API calls or report sections?
- Does Mosaic Relay procure the background report from the vendor on behalf of the platform customer, or does the platform contract directly with the vendor?
- Will Mosaic Relay apply decision rules or scoring to the screening results, or only transmit vendor-supplied data to the platform?
- Are contractors located in states with consumer-reporting laws that impose duties beyond federal FCRA, such as California's Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act or New York's Fair Credit Reporting Act?

## Likely rules and issues
**FCRA scope and definitions.** The Fair Credit Reporting Act applies when a "consumer reporting agency" furnishes a "consumer report" to a "user" for a "permissible purpose." A consumer report is any communication of information bearing on a consumer's character, general reputation, personal characteristics, or mode of living that is used or expected to be used for employment, credit, insurance, or other authorized purposes. Employment purposes include evaluating an individual for employment, promotion, reassignment, or retention as an employee or contractor.
**Sanctions screening.** Sanctions screening against government watchlists (e.g., OFAC SDN list) is generally not considered a consumer report under FCRA if the screening involves only matching a name and identifying information against a public government list, without assembling or evaluating other information about the individual's character or reputation. However, if the vendor combines sanctions data with other information (criminal records, address history, identity verification) and provides a composite score or recommendation, the combined product may be treated as a consumer report.
**Identity verification.** Identity verification services that confirm whether a name, Social Security number, date of birth, and address match records held by credit bureaus or government databases may fall outside FCRA if the service only returns a match/no-match result and does not include evaluative information. However, if the identity verification includes fraud scores, synthetic-identity indicators, or other assessments of risk or character, it may be treated as a consumer report.
**Bundled reports.** If the vendor provides a single report that includes criminal records, sanctions screening, and identity verification, the entire report is likely a consumer report under FCRA, even if some components could be provided separately without triggering FCRA. The "user" of the report (here, the platform customer) must then comply with FCRA's permissible-purpose, disclosure, authorization, and adverse-action requirements for the entire report.
**Mosaic Relay's role.** If Mosaic Relay procures the report from the vendor on behalf of the platform, applies decision rules or scoring, or makes eligibility recommendations, Mosaic Relay may be treated as a co-user of the consumer report and may bear FCRA obligations. If Mosaic Relay only transmits data between the vendor and the platform without accessing or using the report content, it may avoid user status, but this depends on the exact data flow and contractual terms.
**State law overlays.** Several states have consumer-reporting laws that impose duties beyond federal FCRA. California's Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act (ICRAA) applies to background checks for employment purposes and requires specific notices and consent. New York's Fair Credit Reporting Act and Article 23-A of the Correction Law impose additional requirements for criminal-record checks. Some states also have "ban the box" laws that limit when criminal-history information may be requested or considered.
**Adverse action.** If the platform customer takes adverse action (denying or restricting payouts) based in whole or in part on a consumer report, FCRA requires a two-step notice process: (1) a pre-adverse-action notice that includes a copy of the report and the CFPB's Summary of Rights, and (2) a final adverse-action notice that includes the CRA's name and contact information, a statement that the CRA did not make the decision, notice of the right to obtain a free copy of the report within 60 days, and notice of the right to dispute the report's accuracy.

## Viable paths
**Path 1: Separate sanctions and identity checks from the consumer report.** Structure the product so that sanctions screening and identity verification are performed through separate API calls or vendor services that do not include criminal-record or other evaluative information. The platform customer would receive: (a) a sanctions match/no-match result, (b) an identity verification match/no-match result, and (c) a separate consumer report containing criminal records and other background information. The platform would apply its own decision rules to each component. This path requires the vendor to offer unbundled services and requires Mosaic Relay's product to keep the data flows separate. If successful, only the criminal-background component would be treated as a consumer report, and FCRA duties would apply only to that component.
**Path 2: Treat the entire screening product as a consumer report.** Accept that the bundled report (criminal records, sanctions, identity, address history) is a consumer report and build the full FCRA compliance workflow: permissible-purpose certification, standalone disclosure and written authorization, pre-adverse-action notice with report and Summary of Rights, final adverse-action notice, and dispute handling. This path is simpler to implement but imposes FCRA duties on all screening components, including sanctions and identity checks that might otherwise fall outside FCRA.
**Path 3: Mosaic Relay as a service provider, not a user.** Structure the data flow and contracts so that the platform customer contracts directly with the background-screening vendor, procures the report, and applies all decision rules. Mosaic Relay would provide only the technical infrastructure (API connections, data transmission, result display) without accessing report content or making eligibility recommendations. This path minimizes Mosaic Relay's FCRA exposure but requires careful contract drafting and product design to ensure Mosaic Relay does not become a co-user.
**Path 4: Hybrid approach with contractual allocation.** If Mosaic Relay must access report content or apply decision rules, allocate FCRA duties by contract: the platform customer certifies permissible purpose and provides disclosure/authorization, and Mosaic Relay agrees to handle the pre-adverse and adverse-action notice workflow on the platform's behalf. This path requires the vendor to agree to the arrangement and requires clear contractual allocation of duties and indemnities.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Vendor status.** If the background-screening vendor is not a CRA (e.g., it only provides access to public records or government lists without assembling or evaluating information), FCRA may not apply at all. Confirm the vendor's status and whether it certifies compliance with FCRA.
- **Report contents.** If the report includes only sanctions and identity data, without criminal records or other evaluative information, it may fall outside FCRA. Confirm the exact data elements included in the report.
- **Decision-maker.** The platform customer has been confirmed as the decision-maker, but if Mosaic Relay applies scoring, decision rules, or eligibility recommendations, Mosaic Relay may be a co-user. Confirm whether Mosaic Relay's product includes any automated decision logic.
- **State scope.** If contractors are located in California, New York, or other states with consumer-reporting laws beyond federal FCRA, additional notice, consent, and adverse-action duties may apply. Confirm the states where contractors are located.
- **Payout restrictions.** If the platform restricts or delays payment for work already completed (rather than gating future work), state wage-and-hour laws and consumer-protection laws may impose additional duties. Confirm whether screening affects only future work or also completed work.
