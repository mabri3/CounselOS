# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor Pay in an asset purchase targeted to close Nov 15 (~10 weeks out). The plan involves migrating 240,000 consumer and 12,000 business accounts into Mosaic’s app on day 1, relying on Harbor’s prior KYC (for which underlying documents are missing for pre-2023 users), assuming Harbor’s 38 state MTLs to create a combined 44-state footprint, and launching a $50 welcome bonus campaign. Harbor has ~40 open alerts and unreviewed SARs, and its transaction-monitoring SaaS contract terminates at close.

## Decision question

Does any part of the Harbor Pay acquisition migration need legal review before the Nov 15 close?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor Pay; close targeted Nov 15 (~10 weeks out).
- Migration moves 240k consumer users and 12k business accounts into Mosaic Relay's app on day 1 post-close, with one unified account and balance and full access to Mosaic's product set.
- Plan is no re-verification; Harbor users were already KYC'd by Harbor.
- Harbor holds money transmitter licenses (MTLs) in 38 states; Mosaic Relay holds 41; combined footprint is 44 states, opening four new markets.

- Harbor's transaction monitoring is a third-party SaaS whose contract terminates at close; plan is to repoint Harbor's transaction feed at Mosaic's engine on day 1.
- Marketing wants a 'Welcome' campaign with a $50 bonus on first transfer, launching close+1.
- Diligence flagged ~40 open alerts and 'a handful' of SARs from the last year at Harbor; nobody has read them yet.

### Latest research-based proposal — not yet accepted

**No external authority retrieved**

**Practical answer**

**Do not treat Harbor’s 38 money transmitter licenses as transferable assets.** For closing purposes, assume that Mosaic cannot use them unless each relevant state regulator confirms a lawful path.

An asset purchase usually leaves the license with the licensed seller. It does not automatically move the license to the buyer. The buyer generally needs its own authority before it conducts regulated money transmission. A contract that says Mosaic “assumes” the licenses does not bind a regulator.

This makes the proposed four-state expansion a closing issue. It may also affect Harbor customers in states where both companies already hold licenses because the operating entity, customer agreement, custody of funds, or licensed activity can change.

**Notice and approval analysis**

The team should not limit its review to provisions called “license transfer.” The relevant rule may instead appear under:

- transfer of substantially all assets;
- change in control or ownership;
- merger or corporate reorganization;
- change in legal entity or licensed name;
- change in business plan;
- change in responsible individuals;
- change in locations or agents;
- assignment of customer obligations;
- surrender, suspension, or termination of a license; or
- acquisition of outstanding payment obligations.

A pure asset sale may not meet a state’s technical definition of “change of control.” That does **not** mean no filing is required. The seller’s disposition of the business and the buyer’s start of regulated activity can create separate requirements.

Some filings may be notices after signing or before closing. Others may require approval before the transaction, before the buyer begins business, or before the seller surrenders its license. The applicable timing must be verified state by state.

**Interim operation**

Mosaic should not assume it can operate while a new-license or approval request is pending. A filed application usually should not be treated as operating authority without a verified rule or written regulator position.

A possible transition is for Harbor to remain the licensed provider for a limited period. This works only if it is legally and operationally real:

- Harbor remains responsible to customers.
- Harbor retains control required of the licensee.
- Customer terms identify the correct provider.
- Customer funds and outstanding obligations remain properly covered.
- Required bonds, permissible investments, reports, records, and examinations continue.
- Mosaic’s role is permitted under applicable law.
- The arrangement does not amount to Mosaic using Harbor’s license as a rented license.
- The transaction documents keep Harbor able to perform these duties.
- Relevant regulators approve or accept the structure when required.

The present plan does not appear to support that model. It moves users into Mosaic’s app with one Mosaic account, one balance, and access to Mosaic’s products on day one. Those facts point toward Mosaic becoming the operating provider. If so, Harbor’s continuing license may not solve Mosaic’s authorization gap.

**Transition requirements**

The licensing plan should cover more than the four new states. It should address:

1. **Outstanding obligations:** Identify which entity owes stored balances, transfers, refunds, and claims at each transition point.
2. **Customer contracts:** Confirm whether agreements can be assigned and whether notice or consent is required.
3. **Funds and safeguarding:** Map the movement of customer funds and the related permissible-investment coverage.
4. **Surety bonds:** Keep required bonds active until each regulator permits release.
5. **Reports and examinations:** Assign responsibility for pre-close and post-close reports, complaints, examinations, and record production.
6. **Books and records:** Preserve Harbor’s required records and regulator access after the vendor and corporate transition.
7. **Agents and delegates:** Determine whether any registered agents, authorized delegates, or locations need termination or new appointments.
8. **Surrender:** Do not surrender Harbor’s licenses until outstanding obligations and regulator conditions are satisfied.
9. **Customer communications:** Ensure notices state the correct provider, effective date, rights, fees, and complaint contact.
10. **Product scope:** Confirm that Mosaic’s existing licenses cover all products that migrated users will receive, not only Harbor’s former service.

**Recommended closing position**

Treat authorization for each affected state as a **launch condition**, not as a post-close clean-up item.

For each state, require one of these outcomes before Mosaic serves migrated customers:

1. Mosaic already has a license that covers the planned activity and product set.
2. Mosaic obtains the required license or approval.
3. The regulator confirms a lawful interim structure.
4. The affected customers remain with Harbor under a supportable run-off model.
5. Regulated functionality is blocked until authority exists.

The four Harbor-only states should not be presented as part of Mosaic’s available footprint until one of these paths is documented. If the state review cannot finish before close, the most defensible fallback is a state-based migration hold. Mosaic can close the asset acquisition without activating regulated service in those states.

This result does not materially change the saved working position. It gives that position a clearer operational rule: **no regulator-confirmed authority, no day-one regulated operation by Mosaic in the affected state.**

### Earlier saved position — 2026-09-09T20:57:53+00:00

**Working position**

The Harbor Pay migration needs legal and compliance review before the planned close. The leading concern is that an asset purchase generally requires Mosaic Relay to confirm its own authority to operate in each state; the current plan to use or assume Harbor's licenses is not established as legally effective.

The second concern is BSA/AML readiness. Mosaic Relay has not confirmed whether its AML/CIP program permits reliance on Harbor's prior KYC, and underlying records are missing for some pre-2023 users. Harbor's open alerts and SAR history also remain unread. These gaps make a day-one migration without re-verification or account restrictions difficult to support.

Transaction monitoring continuity is also material because Harbor's vendor ends at close. The repointed feed should be tested and validated before migrated users receive full access. The $50 bonus is lower priority but still needs review once its eligibility, funding, terms, and disclosures are known.

**Plausible alternatives**

A narrower launch could exclude the four new markets, restrict users whose KYC evidence is incomplete, or delay full product access until alert review and monitoring validation finish. A full day-one launch remains possible only if state licensing, AML/CIP reliance, alert handling, and monitoring continuity are confirmed.

Reported facts include the asset-purchase structure, the current plan to rely on Harbor's licenses, the lack of a compliance answer on KYC reliance, and the unread alerts and SAR history. The analysis assumes both entities conduct regulated money-transmission activity and that Mosaic Relay will become the operating provider immediately after close. It does not assume that Harbor's licenses transfer or that Harbor's KYC is sufficient.
