# Harbor 2 — Mosaic Relay / Harbor Pay acquisition migration

Matter MAT-20260909-d89ad8 · prepared from the captured records through Sept 10, 2026 · draft for counsel review

## Current position

**Yes. Four parts of the migration need legal review before the Nov 15 close, and each of the four gates day-1 migration. A fifth item gates the close+1 campaign launch.** The plan as described is not a data migration. It moves 252,000 customer relationships (240k consumer, 12k business) into Mosaic Relay's app, transfers an unreviewed BSA/AML book, and turns Harbor's license footprint into an assumed, rather than verified, four-state expansion.

The recommended course is to keep the Nov 15 close date and run the four gates in parallel, while limiting day-1 onboarding to what Mosaic can lawfully serve:

| Gate | What it blocks | Milestone |
|---|---|---|
| Licensing in Harbor-only states | Onboarding customers there; the bonus campaign there | Day 1 / close+1 |
| KYC reliance for the pre-2023 cohort | The "no re-verification" plan for those users | Day 1 |
| Review of the ~40 open alerts and SAR history | Deal protections; account exclusions from the migration book | Before close |
| Monitoring cutover validation | The day-1 repoint with no coverage gap | Close / day 1 |
| Bonus terms and disclosures | The $50 "Welcome" campaign | Close+1 |

**Why this serves the objective.** Each gate protects something the deal is buying — the license footprint and the customer book — and none of them requires delaying the close if work starts now. The riskiest premise in the plan is the one described as "a nice win": the four new markets. A combined 44-state footprint is only a win if Mosaic can lawfully serve those customers after an **asset purchase**, in which Harbor's licenses stay with Harbor and do not travel with the assets. Closing Nov 15 into an unlicensed launch in even a few states, or migrating users whose identity records cannot be produced, creates exposure that is difficult to unwind after day 1.

**What can proceed now.** Data mapping and migration engineering; customer notice and campaign drafting; monitoring scenario tuning and test runs; the business workstreams.

**What should wait or change.** Onboarding in states where Mosaic lacks a license or approval; the bonus launch in those states; treating the alert/SAR review as post-close cleanup; and presenting the plan as "no re-verification" until compliance confirms that reliance is permitted. SAR content should not travel through the ordinary migration data flows.

**Working view vs. this draft.** The matter already records a working approach: treat legal and compliance readiness as a pre-close gate; do not assume Harbor's licenses transfer; consider a narrower launch if licensing, KYC records, alert review, or monitoring tests are incomplete (assistant summary, [source:MSG-20260910-2f255e] — analysis, not a decision). This draft aligns with that approach and specifies which item gates which milestone. The material read contains no recorded decision on the migration; producing this dossier records none and adopts no alternative.

### Money transmission licensing: whether Mosaic Relay can operate under the combined 44-state footprint and whether Harbor's MTLs transfer/survive the acquisition

**Current answer** — The premise that Mosaic will "use/assume Harbor's licenses" for the four new markets (lawyer's intake answer, [source:MSG-20260909-2e549e]) is the least supportable part of the plan. Money transmission licenses are held by the licensee; on an asset purchase they stay with Harbor, and Mosaic needs its own authority — or a state-specific successor/transfer mechanism where one exists — before it transmits for, or holds balances of, those customers. Provisional view: proceed with day-1 migration in states where Mosaic already holds an MTL, subject to the other gates; hold the Harbor-only states for licensing or approvals. Even in Mosaic-licensed states, confirm that each license covers the specific activity for the migrated book (balance holding, business-account features) and what filings or notices accompany Harbor's exit.

**Rule and support** — State money transmitter statutes require a license to engage in money transmission; operating without one can be a federal crime under 18 U.S.C. § 1960 as well as a state enforcement matter (**Needs checking** — verify the states, the activity, and any exemption). Licenses are generally not transferable in an asset sale; states typically require new applications, approvals, or advance notice, and many have adopted parts of the Money Transmission Modernization Act (**Needs checking**, state by state). A purchase agreement that says licenses will "transfer" is a contractual expectation, not the legal permission itself.

**Application** — On the reported counts (Harbor 38, Mosaic 41, union 44), 35 states overlap, 3 states are Harbor-only, and 6 are Mosaic-only. The plan's "four new markets" does not match that arithmetic, so the licensing scope cannot be sized until both schedules are produced (see Record conflicts). The facts file still carries an older assumption that the deal structure was unconfirmed; the later explicit answer — asset purchase — controls, which makes non-transfer the weak point rather than a bank-style change-of-control filing. Decisive facts: the state-by-state license lists; which states allow successor operation and on what timeline; and whether Mosaic's product set for migrated accounts stays inside its license permissions.

**Next step** — Pull both license schedules and build a state matrix with a day-1 go/no-go column; start applications or approvals immediately, because state processing times are the binding constraint against the Nov 15 runway. Interim plan: two-wave migration (Mosaic-licensed states on day 1; Harbor-only states after clearance).

### BSA/AML and KYC: whether relying on Harbor's prior KYC (with missing underlying documents for pre-2023 users) satisfies Mosaic Relay's independent CIP/AML obligations

**Current answer** — Mosaic cannot treat Harbor's pass/fail flags as satisfying Mosaic's own obligations unless its AML/CIP program permits reliance and the conditions of that reliance are documented. The lawyer's intake answer is that this is unknown and compliance must confirm — that confirmation is the gating item. Provisional view: reliance may be arguable for cohorts whose verification records exist and can be transferred; for users onboarded before 2023, whose underlying documents were lost when the vendor shut down in 2024, pass/fail flags alone are unlikely to evidence verification. Plan to re-verify or step-up verify that cohort, or to limit account features until verification is complete. Mosaic should assume it bears independent responsibility for each migrated customer, whatever reliance its program allows.

**Rule and support** — AML program requirement, 31 U.S.C. § 5318(h); MSB AML program rule, 31 CFR 1022.210 (**Needs checking**). The bank CIP rule, 31 CFR 1020.220, contains a reliance provision that requires the other institution to be subject to the BSA and regulated by a federal functional regulator, and requires the relying institution to obtain and retain the required customer information; whether a federal equivalent or a state-MTL requirement governs nonbank money transmitters here must be verified (**Needs checking**). Guidance on BSA/AML obligations when an institution acquires customer accounts (**Needs checking** — applicability to nonbanks).

**Application** — The 252k users split into cohorts by documentation: records for post-2023 onboarding are presumably retrievable; pre-2023 records are not. Decisive conditions: the text of Mosaic's own AML/CIP policy, what the applicable state and federal expectations require for reliance, and the size of the missing-document cohort (not stated in the records read — obtain the count). Related checks worth folding into the same workstream: sanctions-screening evidence for both cohorts, and whether beneficial ownership information must be collected or refreshed for the 12k business accounts (the federal beneficial ownership requirement for legal entity customers, 31 CFR 1010.230, has a defined coverage list — **Needs checking** whether it or a state requirement reaches this entity). If reliance is not permitted, the options are (a) verify before day 1, (b) migrate with account limits until verified, or (c) exclude that cohort from day 1; each choice flows into the migration schedule and customer messaging.

**Next step** — Obtain the AML/CIP policy text and the cohort count from compliance, and have compliance with outside AML counsel state in writing whether reliance is permitted and on what conditions. That written answer determines the pre-2023 cohort's day-1 treatment. Fallback if it is not available before the migration: migrate with feature limits and a scheduled verification campaign.

### SAR and alert handling: obligations to review, file, and manage Harbor's ~40 open alerts and SARs

**Current answer** — This is the item most likely to change the deal or the migration list, and it should be reviewed before close through a controlled channel. Read all ~40 open alerts and the SAR history; determine whether any implicate accounts in the migrated book or reflect ongoing activity that continues past close. Two handling constraints apply: SARs and the fact of filing are confidential, so SAR content should not be pushed through ordinary migration data flows or shared beyond need-to-know personnel; and SARs filed by Harbor do not simply transfer with the assets — the acquisition documents should allocate pre-close exposure and post-close filing, investigation, and cooperation duties. Provisional view: nothing in the record yet suggests a violation — unread alerts are a diligence problem; legal risk crystallizes only if required reviews or filings were missed, or continue unaddressed after close.

**Rule and support** — SAR filing duty, 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(1); prohibition on disclosure, 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(2); MSB SAR rule, 31 CFR 1022.320 (**Needs checking**). Guidance on how BSA/AML and SAR obligations apply when institutions acquire accounts (**Needs checking**).

**Application** — As of the intake answer, nothing had been reviewed and the alerts remained in diligence ([source:MSG-20260909-2e549e]). Two practical consequences: once read, the alert population becomes an account-level input to the migration list — accounts with unresolved suspicion may need exclusion, heightened monitoring, or exit; and the review must be structured (clean team / BSA counsel) so SAR information is not improperly disclosed to the integration team.

**Next step** — Stand up the review now. Deliverable: a privileged alert/SAR memo with account-level flags and a filing-gap assessment. Fallback if access is delayed: negotiate a specific indemnity and cooperation covenant, and keep unresolved accounts out of the day-1 book.

### Monitoring vendor transition

**Current answer** — The cutover needs validation before close, not just a repoint plan. The reported design — Harbor's third-party monitoring SaaS contract terminates at close; the feed is repointed to Mosaic's engine on day 1 ([source:FACT-20260909-073838]/78d5cd) — creates two risks: a coverage gap if the dates do not align, and under-detection if Mosaic's scenarios are not tuned for Harbor's customer base and typologies on day 1. Provisional view: require a documented, tested cutover with a parallel-run period, and confirm whether the vendor contract provides post-termination access or transition assistance; if not, plan a short extension or interim manual monitoring.

**Rule and support** — AML program internal controls, 31 U.S.C. § 5318(h) and 31 CFR 1022.210 (**Needs checking**); the vendor contract's termination, data-return, and transition terms (private contract — read the actual clauses; contract terms are support of their own kind, not public law).

**Application** — The record does not say whether "day 1" is close day or the day after; if the contract ends at close and the repoint occurs the next day, the record does not say who monitors in between (see Record conflicts). Second, tuning: Mosaic's engine must detect on Harbor's patterns; a same-day repoint without scenario testing can under-detect during the first weeks after close — exactly when post-acquisition monitoring matters most.

**Next step** — Obtain the vendor contract's termination/data clauses and the cutover runbook; require test evidence and a named go/no-go owner before close. Fallback: extend Harbor's vendor through a validated cutover, or dual-run both systems briefly.

### Welcome bonus campaign ($50 on first transfer, launching close+1)

**Current answer** — The campaign can likely launch, but not on unreviewed terms and not in states where the entity cannot lawfully operate. Review should cover the offer mechanics (eligibility, what counts as "first transfer," caps, timing, funding); how the bonus posts (a balance credit inside a money-transmission product is itself regulated activity and interacts with the unified-balance design); the accuracy of advertising claims and required disclosures; and state-by-state availability. Provisional view: this is primarily a marketing and disclosure problem, not a deal gating item — but it must be tied to the licensing go/no-go list, so a limited-state launch may be necessary even if the close date holds.

**Rule and support** — State UDAP ("little FTC") acts; CFPB UDAAP authority over covered persons, Dodd-Frank Act § 1031, 12 U.S.C. § 5531 (**Needs checking** — applicability to this entity's activities). State money-transmission advertising and recordkeeping restrictions (**Needs checking**). If any relevant flows are transfers to foreign countries, EFTA/Regulation E remittance-transfer disclosure and error-resolution duties, 12 CFR part 1005 subpart B (**Needs checking**). Tax reporting for the bonus (**Needs checking**).

**Application** — "First transfer" plus "full access to our product set" means the bonus can be earned through a range of product flows, each with its own disclosure profile. The decisive inputs are the actual campaign terms and the set of states where Mosaic can operate on close+1 — both should be routed to consumer-finance review before launch.

**Next step** — Obtain the campaign brief and terms now; run the review alongside the licensing matrix; build a per-state availability check into the launch plan. Fallback: launch only in cleared states and hold the rest for a second wave.

### Privacy and data protection of the migrated records *(scope area flagged in the conversation; not confirmed against the saved issue list)*

**Current answer** — The transfer of 252k consumer and small-business records needs a data-mapping and lawful-basis review before day 1. Likely questions: what notices or consents govern moving Harbor's customer data into Mosaic; what the applicable state privacy statutes require for pre-close diligence access and post-close processing (notice at collection, opt-outs, limits on onward disclosure); and what security and breach-notification exposure follows from records with incomplete documentation. Provisional view: the transfer itself can normally be structured — several regimes contain merger/asset-sale provisions (**Needs checking**) — but notices and data maps should be updated before migration, and Harbor's customer terms may require notice of the transfer.

**Rule and support** — GLBA privacy and safeguards obligations, 15 U.S.C. §§ 6801–6809; Reg P (12 CFR part 1016 / 16 CFR part 313, depending on the enforcing regulator); FTC Safeguards Rule, 16 CFR part 314 (**Needs checking** for entity coverage and any sale-transfer exception). State comprehensive privacy laws, e.g., California CCPA/CPRA (**Needs checking** for each customer state).

**Application** — The higher-risk element is the combination: incomplete KYC documentation, a defunct vendor, and 240k consumer records changing systems. That combination raises recordkeeping-integrity and security questions that intersect with the KYC issue (can Mosaic evidence verification?) and the account-transfer issue below (what the migration looks like to the customer).

**Next step** — Data inventory and privacy counsel review; fold required notice updates into the pre-close communications workstream.
