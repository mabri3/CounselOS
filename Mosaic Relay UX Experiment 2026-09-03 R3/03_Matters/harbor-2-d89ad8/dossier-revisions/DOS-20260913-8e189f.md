# Harbor 2 — Harbor Pay acquisition migration (Mosaic Relay)

Matter MAT-20260909-d89ad8 · close targeted Nov 15 (reported ~10 weeks out from the Sept 9 request) · day-1 migration of 240k consumer and 12k business accounts

## Current position

**Yes — several parts need legal review before the Nov 15 close, and two of them can change what is migrated on day 1.** The migration is not "just a data migration," and the plan already contains two unverified permissions: that Mosaic can operate under Harbor's licenses in the four new markets after an asset purchase, and that Harbor's KYC can carry over without re-verification. Neither has been checked (intake answers, [source:MSG-20260909-2e549e]).

The recommended course now:

- **Proceed** with migration build, data mapping, and the pre-close collection of Harbor's license schedules, customer agreements, privacy policy, monitoring-vendor contract, and alert/SAR files.
- **Resolve two gating questions first.** (1) **Licensing:** obtain a state-by-state picture of the combined footprint and each state's requirements for moving customers in an asset purchase; plan the day-1 footprint around the states where Mosaic already holds licenses and hold the new-market states until each is cleared. (2) **KYC:** get compliance's written answer on the AML/CIP program, and plan for a verification-gated transition for any cohort whose records cannot support reliance (pre-2023 customers in particular). These two items decide whether day-1 "one unified account, full access" can be delivered to all 252k accounts or to a subset.
- **Start the alert/SAR review now** through a counsel-controlled, SAR-confidentiality-compliant channel. It is both a diligence completion item and a potential deal-terms item; findings could affect which accounts or cohorts move cleanly on day 1.
- **Test the monitoring repoint before cutover** (dual-running, defined acceptance criteria) and secure the SaaS data and export rights before the contract terminates at close.
- **Wait on the $50 "Welcome" campaign** until its terms are reviewed and the launch states match the licensing map. This item can run in parallel and is the most severable from the close.
- **Add one workstream the plan does not yet show:** the migration of customer data, account agreements and balances (assignment/consent, privacy, custody of funds in transit).

What this draft is and is not: the recorded business plan is Dana's full day-1 migration; this is the planning baseline. A saved working note ([source:MSG-20260910-2f255e], path SCN-490c71c86d9c658974321c1e) frames a legal-and-compliance readiness gate as its "current approach" and raises a narrower launch if readiness is incomplete. **No recorded decision adopting a gate — or rejecting one — was found in the material read**, and this draft's gating recommendation is a recommendation, not a recorded decision. ### Money transmitter licensing for the four new markets

**Current answer** — Review before close, and the plan to "use/assume Harbor's licenses" is the weakest premise in the migration. In an asset purchase, Harbor's money transmitter licenses generally stay with Harbor; Mosaic needs its own authority in each new state, or a state-specific approval or interim mechanism, and approvals are commonly measured in months, not the ~10 weeks to close (state-by-state, **Needs checking**). Provisional: plan day 1 for the states where Mosaic is already licensed; do not onboard the new-market states until each is cleared. Also resolve the count: the request reports 38 + 41 = 44 combined and "opens four new markets," but 44 − 41 = **three** states new to Mosaic (six new to Harbor; overlap implied is 35). The scope of the licensing work depends on the real list.

**Rule and support** — State money transmission statutes condition transmitting — and in many states advertising or offering — on holding a license, and commonly require notice or approval for a change of control, acquisition, or transfer of a licensee's business; licenses are generally non-transferable in an asset sale (each of the 44 states' provisions — **Needs checking**). Research question: for each state in the union, what does an asset purchase of a licensed transmitter's customer base require, on what timeline, and can Mosaic lawfully serve those customers on day 1 without its own license? Sources: request ([source:MSG-20260909-982278]); intake answer ([source:MSG-20260909-2e549e]); initial issue map ([source:MSG-20260909-efcbdd]).

**Application** — The structure is reported as an asset purchase (intake, 2026-09-09; [source:FACT-20260909-ed9f06]), which supersedes the older facts.md assumption that the structure was unconfirmed — that is a changed input and it drives this issue. On day 1, migrated customers get Mosaic's app, Mosaic's account, and Mosaic's product set, so Mosaic's authority in each customer's state is the operative question; the four new markets are exactly where that authority is not established. The "44 — nice win" framing converts a licensing project (applications, background checks, surety bonds, minimum net worth, possible examinations) into assumed day-1 capability; it is a benefit only if cleared in time. If licensing is a closing condition for those states, the Nov 15 close itself can slip (the purchase agreement was not in the material read). Where Mosaic holds its 41 licenses, this issue narrows, but the KYC, alerts and data issues below still apply. Launching the "Welcome" campaign into an unlicensed state may itself be regulated advertising (state-by-state — **Needs checking**).

**Next step** — Build the 44-state matrix (entity, license, permitted activities, transfer/change-of-control/notice requirements, realistic timelines) and resolve the 3-vs-4 discrepancy from both license schedules. That matrix feeds the decision on the day-1 footprint. Fallback if it is not ready before the migration build freeze: plan on the assumption that each new-market state requires its own clearance and exclude them from day 1 — a planning assumption, not a legal conclusion.

### Reliance on Harbor's KYC and the pre-2023 customers (BSA/AML)

**Current answer** — Legal and compliance review before close. The "no re-verification" plan has not been checked against Mosaic's AML/CIP program (intake answer: "Not sure / need to check with compliance"), and for customers onboarded before 2023 the only surviving artifact is a pass/fail result — the underlying documents are gone with the vendor that shut down in 2024. Two questions: (a) may Mosaic rely on Harbor's prior verification, and do the legal conditions hold; (b) if not, what does Mosaic do about the affected cohort? Provisional: plan for verification-gated access for any cohort lacking adequate records — refresh identity information at first login or first transaction, or limit account functionality until refreshed. Do not promise those accounts "full access, one balance" on day 1 until this resolves.

**Rule and support** — For banks, the federal CIP reliance provision (31 C.F.R. § 1020.220(a)(6) — **Needs checking**) permits relying on another institution only where conditions are met, including that the relied-upon institution is subject to a BSA AML program rule and federally regulated, that the reliance appears in the bank's written CIP, and that a contract provides for it with an annual certification. Money services businesses have no parallel standalone federal CIP rule; their identity-verification expectations arise through the risk-based AML program requirement (31 C.F.R. § 1022.210 — **Needs checking**) and state money transmitter recordkeeping rules (state-by-state — **Needs checking**). BSA records are generally retained five years (31 C.F.R. § 1010.430 — **Needs checking**). Research question: whether Mosaic's program and the applicable state rules permit reliance here, and what satisfies Mosaic's own customer-record obligations for the pre-2023 cohort. Note also a record distinction: the missing documents prove a record gap, not that Harbor's verification was deficient when performed — that is a diligence and reps point, while Mosaic's forward obligation is the migration question.

**Application** — Because the compliance answer does not exist yet, the migration currently assumes a permission nobody has established. Reliance is factually harder here for two reasons: the underlying documents for pre-2023 users are missing (so Harbor's file cannot be re-evidenced), and the vendor no longer exists (so there is no operating vendor to certify performance). The choice changes the day-1 build: (i) refresh verification at first login (product flow plus operations); (ii) gated access — account functions limited until verification completes — which reduces onboarding friction but is a risk control, not a legal safe harbor, unless Mosaic's own program provides for it; (iii) documented reliance, if the program and law permit it and its conditions can be satisfied in substance. If either company operates through a bank partner, the bank CIP rule may govern instead of the MSB framework — no bank-partner structure is reported, so this remains open.

**Next step** — Compliance owns the program-policy answer; legal owns the reliance conditions and state recordkeeping requirements. Deliverable: written confirmation of what Mosaic's program permits, plus a memo on reliance and records. Fallback if that is not available before the build freeze: build the gated option — access can be expanded later; unverified full access cannot be undone. (Working recommendation, not a recorded decision.)

### Unread alerts, SAR history and monitoring continuity

**Current answer** — Review before close through a counsel-controlled channel; this is the item most capable of changing the deal posture. Three questions: (a) how the ~40 open alerts and the SARs — and SAR supporting material — may be accessed, shared and used in diligence, given SAR confidentiality (31 C.F.R. § 1022.320(e) for money services businesses / § 1020.320(e) for banks — **Needs checking**); (b) what the files show — unresolved suspicious activity tied to accounts about to migrate can affect whether additional filings are needed, whether some cohorts should be handled differently on day 1, and the reps/indemnity posture; (c) whether monitoring can transfer with no coverage gap at the close, since Harbor's SaaS contract terminates then.

**Rule and support** — The SAR rules prohibit disclosing a SAR and its existence, subject to limited exceptions; whether and how SARs or alert files may be shown to Mosaic (or its counsel) in an acquisition diligence process must be checked against the rule text before any file moves — **Needs checking**. Program and retention leads: 31 C.F.R. § 1022.210 (risk-based monitoring and program) and § 1010.430 (retention) — **Needs checking**. Research question: the precise permitted-disclosure route for SAR and alert material between Harbor and Mosaic pre-close, and the scope of records that must survive the SaaS termination. The "highest decision-changing item" ranking is earlier analysis ([source:MSG-20260909-6caeae]), not verified support.

**Application** — "~40 open alerts and 'a handful' of SARs from the last year… nobody has read them" is simultaneously (i) a diligence gap — Mosaic cannot assess what it is buying; (ii) an integration issue — those alerts concern customers who become Mosaic's on day 1, with Mosaic holding the forward filing and monitoring obligations; and (iii) possibly a filing issue — alert dispositions can produce SARs, and an unreviewed backlog can cross the closing line. Sequencing matters: establish the confidentiality-compliant review mechanism before pulling files, not after. Monitoring continuity is a separable hard gate: the feed repoint must be tested with dual-running and defined pass criteria, and the vendor's data (and export rights under a terminating contract) must be secured before termination — a coverage gap at close is a program problem, not just an IT problem. If the transition cannot be validated in time, the options are a short contract extension or documented manual coverage for the gap; that choice belongs at the migration freeze.

**Next step** — Open the secure review now, with the goal of completing it before close because findings can affect deal terms and day-1 cohorts. Deliverable: dispositions of the open alerts, a counsel-reviewed SAR inventory, and a findings memo. Fallback if incomplete at closing: specific indemnity/escrow plus a post-close remediation plan — a deal-terms choice for counsel and the deal team, not a legal conclusion. For monitoring: obtain the termination/data-return terms and a test plan; fallback is extension or manual coverage.

### The $50 welcome bonus and launch marketing

**Current answer** — Review before launch (close+1); unlikely to change the close date, so it can run in parallel. Three checks: the campaign terms (eligibility, funding source, conditions, states); the licensing interaction — do not market or operate the campaign in states where Mosaic is not yet licensed; and the treatment of the $50 once credited — if it enters the account balance, whose money it is and what safeguarding rules apply (state-by-state — **Needs checking**).

**Rule and support** — State unfair/deceptive-practices and advertising rules govern the claim and its conditions; some money transmitter statutes regulate advertising/offering — **Needs checking** per launch state. If the bonus is paid through a bank-partner deposit program, bonus/rate advertising rules may apply (e.g., 12 C.F.R. Part 1030 — **Needs checking**; no bank partner is reported, so applicability is unestablished). On tax: a promotional credit may be treated as a premium/prize; $50 is likely below the $600 federal 1099-MISC reporting threshold, but state treatment varies — **Needs checking**. Research question: what disclosures, state limitations, and safeguarding treatment the final terms require.

**Application** — The terms do not exist in the records yet (the initial map noted "review the $50 bonus when its terms are available"), so this is currently a placeholder with three concrete interfaces: the licensing map (launch states), the migration mechanics (a "first transfer" bonus conditions value on a transaction, and bonus-driven small transfers will show up in monitoring — the engine test should account for that pattern), and the balance treatment (a credited bonus likely becomes customer funds subject to the applicable safeguarding regime). If the campaign is limited to licensed states, it remains severable from day-1 migration; if marketing insists on all 44 states at launch, it collides with the licensing gate.

**Next step** — Deliverable: the campaign terms plus a short review memo keyed to the licensing matrix. Fallback: delay the bonus and keep the migration — the campaign is the one workstream that can slip without moving the close or the account migration.

### Newly identified in this draft: customer data, account agreements and balances at migration

(This issue is not one of the four in the saved issue map read; it is identified from the request's own facts — 240k + 12k accounts, "one unified account, one balance" — and its source is flagged so it is not confused with the saved list.)

**Current answer** — Needs legal review before close; "really just a data migration" fails precisely here. Moving the accounts requires checking, per document type: (a) whether Harbor's customer agreements can be assigned to or assumed by Mosaic, or whether customers must consent; (b) how terms and privacy policies may be changed (notice, continued use, or opt-in), including the privacy policy's change-of-control clause; (c) what data can move, on what basis, and what notices or opt-outs apply; and (d) what happens to balances at cutover — who holds the funds, under which bank/processor/custody contracts, and on what timing. The answer to "silent migration vs. notice vs. consent" comes from those documents; the operative terms are the assignment/novation clause, the amendment/notice clause, the privacy clause, and any change-of-control or consent provision in the custody/processor contracts. Whether consent is needed changes the comms plan and possibly the day-1 cohort.

**Rule and support** — The customer agreement, privacy policy and vendor/bank contracts govern assignability and notice — these are private agreements, read them directly. Public-law overlays to check: GLBA privacy/Reg P notice and reuse rules if the entities are "financial institutions" for GLBA purposes (money transmitters — **Needs checking**); state privacy statutes' treatment of data transferred as part of a merger or acquisition, and any notice/opt-out requirements (state-by-state — **Needs checking**); state safeguarding rules for customer funds in transit (— **Needs checking**). Research question: which document clauses permit migration without customer consent, and which require notice or consent, for each account type.

**Application** — Two facts raise the stakes: the 12k business accounts may sit on negotiated or different terms than the consumer population, and the pre-2023 data gaps (above) mean the transferred data set is incomplete on its face — the migration inherits that gap and should not be scoped as a clean copy. Any bank or processor holding Harbor's balances may have its own consent or change-of-control provisions that gate when funds can move; those contracts were not in the material read. The possible outcomes have very different schedules: silent migration (fastest), notice-based migration with opt-outs (medium), consent-gated migration for some agreements (slowest — must be scoped now because it changes the day-1 cohort and the communications build).

**Next step** — Collect Harbor's consumer agreement, business agreement, privacy policy and the custody/bank/processor contracts into one review. Deliverable: a gap memo listing, per document, the operative clause and required action (none / notice / consent). Result needed: the migration comms and any consent flow. Fallback if incomplete: build the notice flow for the standard consumer set rather than promising a silent migration; hold accounts whose governing terms remain unchecked.
