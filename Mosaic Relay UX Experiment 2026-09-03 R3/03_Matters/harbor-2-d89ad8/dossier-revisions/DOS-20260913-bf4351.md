# Harbor 2 — Harbor Pay acquisition migration (Mosaic Relay)

*Prepared from matter records dated 9–10 September 2026 (MAT-20260909-d89ad8). Close target: 15 November 2026. Basis and coverage limitations: see **Record coverage** at the end.*

## Current position

**Short answer.** Yes. The migration needs legal review before 15 November, and two items should gate day-1 execution unless they are cleared first: the licensing basis for serving customers in the new-market states, and the KYC reliance plan for the pre-2023 cohort. Two items should complete before the deal protections are finalized: a controlled read of Harbor's ~40 open alerts and SAR files, and validation of the transaction-monitoring cutover. Two can run closer to launch: the customer notice/privacy check, and the $50 bonus terms review before the close+1 campaign.

**Recommended course now (this draft).** Keep the full day-1 migration of the 240k consumer users and 12k business accounts as the planning baseline in the states where Mosaic is already licensed, and make it conditional on the KYC and monitoring gates clearing. Sequence the new-market customers into a later wave unless state-by-state licensing is confirmed before the migration freeze. Migrate the pre-2023 cohort only with a documented reliance determination or with restricted account access. Read the alerts and SAR files before finalizing representations and indemnities. Validate monitoring with a written no-gap sign-off, with a fallback to extend Harbor's monitoring vendor briefly if the repoint slips. This course protects both the close date and the four-market upside; the alternative — migrating everything on day 1 and sorting out legal after — risks serving customers without a valid authorization in the target states and inheriting AML exposure that surfaces a year later.

**What can proceed.** The legal/compliance work itself (licensing analysis, alert review, KYC determination, cutover testing), in-footprint migration preparation, and campaign preparation.

**What should wait or change.** Day-1 onboarding in the new-market states; unrestricted day-1 access for the pre-2023 cohort; final AML deal protections until the alert review lands; and customer communications until the notice/privacy check clears. The bonus can launch at close+1 only if its terms are cleared.

**Premise tested.** The request frames this as "really just a data migration" (Dana, PM Growth & Integration, 9 Sept). The record and this draft treat it as a change of who provides the service and who runs the AML controls for 252,000 accounts across a license footprint being assembled through an asset purchase. Two supporting premises also need correction: an asset purchase generally does not deliver Harbor's licenses, and the stated counts (Harbor 38 states, Mosaic 41, combined 44, "four new markets") do not reconcile — the arithmetic yields three new states, not four (**Record conflicts**). Do not rely on the market-upside number until the license schedules are checked.

**Draft vs. working view vs. decision.** The saved working note of 10 September already treats legal and compliance readiness as a pre-close gate, warns not to assume Harbor's licenses transfer, and flags a narrower launch as a consideration. That note is fallible analysis, not a recorded decision; this draft is consistent with it and adds specific per-issue gates. The only recorded choices are the 9 September intake answers (structure reported as an asset purchase; compliance to check the KYC reliance question; plan to use Harbor's licenses; alerts and SARs unreviewed). ## Issues

### 1. State licensing for the new markets and the fate of Harbor's MTLs in an asset purchase

**Current answer.** Not cleared. "Use/assume Harbor's licenses" is an unchecked plan for a lawful basis to serve those customers. In an asset purchase, licenses generally stay with the licensed Harbor entity; they do not travel with the assets by default, and states commonly require notice, approval, or a new authorization when a licensee's business is acquired or when a buyer takes over the activity. Whether Mosaic can serve the new-market customers under Harbor's licenses through a permitted delegate/agent arrangement, or needs its own license in each state, is a state-by-state question with filing lead times. Until answered, day-1 onboarding of customers in states where only Harbor is licensed should be treated as conditional.

**Rule and support.** The relevant frameworks are state money transmission licensing statutes and the CSBS Money Transmission Modernization Act in adopting states; the license-transfer/change-of-control approval provisions in those statutes; and authorized-delegate provisions that let a licensee serve customers through a delegate under conditions. All of these are unverified leads — **needs checking**; the readable records state that no external authority had been retrieved. Reported facts: structure is an asset purchase; 38/41/44 license counts [original request [source:MSG-20260909-982278]; intake answer [source:MSG-20260909-2e549e]]. Research question (state once): for each new-market state, does the post-close arrangement require Mosaic's own license or an approved arrangement, and what is the realistic timeline?

**Application.** The plan itself — "one unified account, one balance, full access to our product set" — indicates Mosaic will be the provider to these customers, which is why the delegate/own-license question is live, not hypothetical. The new markets are exactly where Mosaic's 41-state footprint ends. Conditions that change the answer: whether Harbor's licensed entity survives post-close and keeps its licenses; whether each state permits delegate service for this activity; and whether Mosaic's existing licenses actually cover each product it will give migrated users (products vs. licenses is unverified). Competing reading: if the Harbor licensed entity remained the account provider and Mosaic supplied only technology, the analysis would shift — but nothing in the readable record says that, and the unified-account description reads the other way. The "one balance" element also raises a funds-side question: state MTL regimes impose safeguarding obligations on the licensee holding customer funds, so moving balances between providers belongs in the same state-by-state check (unverified lead — needs checking).

**Next step.** Obtain both license schedules and a state-by-state memo before deciding day-1 scope for the new markets; default to a later wave for any state not cleared (this draft's recommendation, not a recorded decision). The state matrix should also fix the three-versus-four count.

**Status.** Remains: information (license schedules; new-state list), research (state transfer/delegate rules), decision (day-1 scope for new markets).

### 2. BSA/AML — relying on Harbor's KYC and the pre-2023 records gap

**Current answer.** The no-re-verification plan is not established. Two questions: (a) does Mosaic's own CIP/AML program permit reliance on another institution's prior verification, and (b) do the reliance conditions hold when the underlying identification records for pre-2023 users are gone (Harbor's vendor shut down in 2024)? Expect that the pre-2023 cohort cannot be carried through on the current record; the workable paths are re-verification or restricted access until verified. If reliance is permitted and the pass/fail results contain the identification data the rule requires, the rest of the population may be migratable without re-verification, on compliance's documented determination.

**Rule and support.** Federal BSA CIP and AML program rules for money services businesses (31 CFR 1022.220; 1022.210 — unverified leads, **needs checking**) and Mosaic's own program/policy. The reliance provisions in the federal CIP rules (for example, the bank CIP reliance provision at 31 CFR 1020.220(a)(6)(i)) typically condition reliance on obtaining the customer identification information from the other institution; whether Mosaic's rule or state-law equivalents provide an equivalent mechanism, and on what conditions, is an open question — **needs checking**. Entity-customer due diligence/beneficial ownership (31 CFR 1010.230) for the 12k business accounts — scope for Mosaic's institution type **needs checking**. Research question (state once): under the rule applicable to Mosaic and its own policy, what must Mosaic hold to rely on Harbor's verification, and can those conditions be met for each cohort?

**Application.** The pre-2023 problem is documentary: relying on Harbor's check when Harbor cannot produce the underlying records may fail the reliance condition regardless of program design, and the 2024 vendor shutdown removes any rescue path. Cohort size is unknown — if small, a pre-close re-verification campaign may resolve the issue; if large, restricted access is the realistic remedy. The business accounts add a separate records question (whether entity/beneficial-ownership information must be refreshed at migration — **needs checking**). Competing interpretation: if the pass/fail results include full identifying data and verification method, the documentary gap is narrower than it appears — confirm the actual fields (see **Assumptions used**).

**Next step.** Compliance to answer the policy/reliance question and size the pre-2023 cohort; counsel to confirm the rule conditions; then choose re-verification versus staged limits. Interim course: do not promise unrestricted day-1 access to the pre-2023 cohort before this clears.

**Status.** Remains: information (compliance position; cohort size; data fields), analysis (reliance conditions), decision (re-verify vs. limits), implementation (cohort controls).

### 3. Harbor's ~40 open alerts and the SAR history

**Current answer.** Read them before close. The first analysis in the record ranked this the single most decision-changing item, and this draft agrees: unresolved alerts and unreviewed SAR files are the classic source of inherited AML exposure and of specific deal protections (representations, indemnities, holdbacks), and some alerts may indicate continuing conduct that should not be migrated without action. Two clarifications drive the work: whether "SARs nobody has read" means filed reports whose supporting case files were never reviewed, or drafted narratives that were never filed (a filing gap). The first is a documentation problem; the second is a potential violation. Also, SAR confidentiality constrains how the review is run: access must be controlled and documented, and SAR information should not be broadly circulated in diligence.

**Rule and support.** Suspicious activity reporting obligations (31 CFR 1022.320 — unverified lead, **needs checking**); SAR confidentiality provisions (FinCEN's SAR confidentiality rule — pin the citation before relying; **needs checking**); AML program effectiveness for monitoring and case management (31 CFR 1022.210 — unverified lead). On successor exposure in an asset purchase, the readable records contain no analysis — treat that as a research gap, not as a clean bill.

**Application.** Reported facts: ~40 open alerts and "a handful" of SARs from the last year, unreviewed, still in diligence [[source:FACT-20260909-adf2cb]; [source:MSG-20260909-2e549e]]. The answer branches: (i) if most alerts are low-risk closures, the effect is cleanup plus a reps/indemnity backstop; (ii) if any alert reflects conduct tied to accounts being migrated, carve those accounts out of day 1 pending resolution. The filed-versus-unfiled distinction above changes both exposure and what must happen before close.

**Next step.** Commission the controlled review now. Deliverable: findings memo that flags any ongoing conduct, states whether anything remains unfiled or needs follow-up, and feeds specific deal protections. If it cannot finish before the deal documents are final, paper the unreviewed exposure into the reps/indemnity package and revisit migration scope for flagged accounts. (This is one of the two next steps already recorded on 10 September; the other is licensing.)

**Status.** Remains: information (the files themselves), analysis (successor exposure; deal terms), decision (protections; account carve-outs), implementation (post-close remediation if needed).

### 4. Transaction-monitoring continuity at close

**Current answer.** The repoint plan needs validation before day 1 and must survive two failure modes: the SaaS contract terminates at close, so a slipped repoint means no monitoring at all; and the new engine must see the full feed for Harbor's customer and product mix with tuning, testing, and an audit trail. A monitoring gap across a 252k-account migration is a program deficiency and creates missed-alert risk whose consequences appear later — feeding directly into the Issue 3 problem one year out. Also confirm the contract's data-export and transition-assistance rights; the historical alert and case data needed for Issue 3, and any post-close access, depend on them.

**Rule and support.** AML program requirement that monitoring be reasonably designed to detect and report suspicious activity (31 CFR 1022.210 — unverified lead, **needs checking**); sanctions-screening continuity (OFAC obligations — **needs checking**); and the vendor contract itself, a private agreement whose termination, extension, transition, and data-export terms control. The vendor contract was not in the readable record — a material gap for this issue.

**Application.** Reported facts: third-party SaaS contract terminates at close; plan is a day-1 repoint to Mosaic's engine [[source:FACT-20260909-073838]; [source:FACT-20260909-78d5cd]]. Conditions that change the answer: whether the contract allows a short extension (the natural fallback); who monitors Harbor's accounts between signing and close and immediately after; whether engine scenarios cover the Harbor product mix, including the business accounts; and whether historical data can be exported to seed cases and tuning.

**Next step.** Compliance and engineering to produce a cutover runbook with test evidence, a no-gap attestation, and confirmed vendor rights. Target sign-off before the migration freeze. Fallbacks: short vendor extension, or documented manual monitoring for the gap — never no monitoring.

**Status.** Remains: information (contract rights; vendor cooperation), analysis (validation results), decision (repoint go/no-go; fallback choice), implementation (cutover).

## Assumptions used

- **KYC data content.** This draft assumes the pass/fail results include usable identifying data (name, date of birth, ID number, verification method, provider records). If they are bare flags, reliance likely fails for the whole migrated population, not just pre-2023 — the largest swing in this dossier (drives Issue 2 and the size of the remedy).
- **Post-close provider.** This draft assumes Mosaic will provide the accounts and services post-close, consistent with "move into our app," "one unified account, one balance," and "full access to our product set." If instead the Harbor licensed entity remains the provider and Mosaic supplies only technology, Issue 1's licensing analysis and part of Issue 2 change (drives Issues 1, 2, and 6).
- **Cohort size.** This draft assumes the pre-2023 no-documents cohort is large enough to matter to the 15 November timetable. If it is small, a pre-close re-verification campaign may fully resolve Issue 2 (drives Issue 2's remedy and timing).
- **New-market authorization premise.** This draft assumes at least some new-market states require Mosaic's own authorization or an approved delegate arrangement before it serves those customers. If delegate service is freely available in all of them, the recommendation shrinks toward paperwork and filings (drives Issue 1's timing recommendation).
