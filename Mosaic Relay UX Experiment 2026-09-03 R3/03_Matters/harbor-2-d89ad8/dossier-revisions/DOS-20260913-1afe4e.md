# Harbor 2 — Harbor Pay acquisition migration

## Current position

Yes — several parts of the migration need legal review before the Nov 15 close, and two of them can change the launch plan itself. Dana frames the program as "really just a data migration," but the plan combines four legal decision points: serving the four new markets under Harbor's licenses, moving 240k consumer users and 12k business accounts without re-verification (including users whose pre-2023 KYC records are missing), inheriting ~40 unread alerts and a handful of SARs along with the customer accounts, and replacing the monitoring system the day the vendor contract ends. A fifth item, the $50 welcome bonus launching close+1, is a more contained review.

Recommended course now: keep building the migration, but make the licensing question for the four new markets and the alerts/SAR review pre-close gates, and make the KYC reliance answer a day-1 gate. Concretely:

1. Start state-by-state licensing analysis now. The recorded structure is an asset purchase; in that structure Harbor's money transmitter licenses (MTLs) generally stay with Harbor's licensed entity rather than transferring with the assets, so the intake plan to "use/assume Harbor's licenses" needs testing before the close and before any day-1 launch in those four markets.
2. Get compliance's written answer on whether Mosaic Relay's program permits reliance on Harbor's prior KYC, with a plan for the pre-2023 cohort.
3. Obtain and review the open alerts and SAR history under appropriate confidentiality controls, and check how the results feed the deal's representations, indemnities, and closing conditions.
4. Require a tested monitoring cutover — or a short-term bridge with the vendor — before the SaaS contract terminates at close.
5. Hold the bonus campaign until its terms are reviewed. This review can run in parallel with the four items above.

What can proceed now: migration engineering, data mapping, and communications planning, provided the build can hold back a state or a user cohort if a gate fails. What should wait or change: any commitment that the four new markets go live on day 1; the no-re-verification plan for pre-2023 users until compliance confirms reliance; the bonus launch until terms are reviewed; and the working assumption that Harbor's licenses come with the assets.

Decisive conditions: the actual list of the four new states and each state's rule for license transfer or change-of-control approval; compliance's reliance answer; the content of the alerts and SARs; the cutover test result; the bonus terms. Nov 15 is a business target from the request, not a legal deadline; the licensing and alert items are the ones that can affect the deal or its terms before close.

This draft's recommendation differs from the recorded plan in two places: the plan assumes use of Harbor's licenses in the four new markets and no re-verification; this draft treats both as unverified and likely to require a change (approvals and a narrower launch, or re-verification/limits for the pre-2023 cohort). Earlier assistant analysis (Sept 9–10 records) reached a similar gate-based view and raised a narrower launch; that is prior analysis, not a recorded decision. No recorded decision changes the plan's scope.

### Issue — Money transmission licensing for the four new markets

**Current answer** — Needs review now, and it is the item most likely to change the launch plan. In an asset purchase, Harbor's MTLs ordinarily remain with Harbor's licensed entity; adding the two license counts together does not give Mosaic Relay a right to serve the four new states at close. Interim view: plan for the possibility that the four new markets cannot go live on day 1 without state approvals (new licenses, transfer or change-of-control approvals, or retaining the licensed entity). If a state's process is quick or the deal keeps the licensed entity, day-1 service in that state may survive. Serving the migrated users in Mosaic's existing 41 states depends on Mosaic's own licenses covering that activity.

**Rule and support** — State money transmission statutes govern who may transmit money in each state; licenses are issued to specific entities, and transfers or changes of control require regulator approval, with materially different mechanics and timing by state (state regimes are administered through NMLS; New York's is administered by NYDFS). **Needs checking** — no authority was retrieved or verified in this matter's captured research. The precise research question for counsel: for each of the four (or actual) states, can Mosaic Relay serve the migrated customers at close under (a) a transferred license, (b) a change-of-control approval of the Harbor entity, or (c) a new Mosaic license, and by when.

**Application** — Recorded facts: asset purchase (intake answer); Harbor holds 38 MTLs; Mosaic holds 41; the request says the combined footprint of 44 "opens four new markets"; plan is to use/assume Harbor's licenses. Two conditions decide the outcome: the real state list, and each state's process and timing. If the four states require approvals that cannot be completed by Nov 15 or before launch, the realistic options are to exclude those states from day-1 migration, serve them only after approval, or restructure to retain the licensed entity — each a business choice with timing and cost effects. Note: the license arithmetic in the request does not reconcile (see Record conflicts), so even the number of exposed states needs confirmation.

**Next step** — Commission a state-by-state licensing memo covering the actual new states (process, timing, interim plan); use it to fix day-1 market scope. Interim: keep those markets behind a gate rather than assuming day-1 launch.

### Issue — Reliance on Harbor's KYC and missing pre-2023 records

**Current answer** — Review needed before day-1 migration. The no-re-verification plan rests on an unconfirmed compliance answer ("need to check with compliance"). Likely outcomes: if Mosaic's program permits reliance and the retained results can support it, migration without re-verification may be defensible for users with adequate records; for pre-2023 users whose underlying documents are missing, expect either re-verification, account limits, or a documented risk-based exception before full access. Which outcome applies depends on compliance's written answer and on the MSB/state requirements that apply to Mosaic — neither is recorded.

**Rule and support** — The Bank Secrecy Act framework and FinCEN's money services business rules (BSA, 31 U.S.C. § 5311 et seq.; MSB AML-program rule, 31 C.F.R. § 1023.210) impose risk-based program and recordkeeping obligations; the bank customer identification program rule (31 C.F.R. § 1020.220) contains reliance provisions that permit reliance on another financial institution's verification only under conditions. State MTL regimes add their own verification and recordkeeping conditions. **Needs checking** — none retrieved. Research question: which rule or state condition governs Mosaic's reliance on Harbor's checks, and what record must exist to support it for each cohort.

**Application** — Recorded facts: Harbor's KYC vendor shut down in 2024; pass/fail results exist for everyone; underlying documents are missing for users onboarded before 2023; the reliance question is unanswered. The weakest group is the pre-2023 cohort, where reliance would rest on results without the records the verification rules contemplate. Two outcomes: (a) compliance confirms reliance with a defined remediation (re-verify or limit the pre-2023 cohort), or (b) reliance is not permitted or not supportable, and part of the 240k must be re-verified or limited before full access. The migration should be built so a cohort can be held back without stopping the whole program.

**Next step** — Obtain compliance's written reliance answer and a remediation plan sized to the pre-2023 cohort before day-1 migration; build hold-back capability in the interim.

### Issue — Unread open alerts and SAR history

**Current answer** — Review before close. This is the item most capable of affecting the deal rather than only the migration: what the alerts and SARs show can change valuation, indemnities, closing conditions, and Mosaic's first days of servicing decisions. It should be handled with SAR confidentiality constraints in mind, not circulated as ordinary diligence material, because SARs are confidential and their disclosure and use are restricted.

**Rule and support** — BSA suspicious activity reporting and confidentiality obligations: 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g); FinCEN SAR rule for money services businesses, 31 C.F.R. § 1023.320. **Needs checking** — not retrieved. Successor and continuity effects depend on the asset-purchase structure: the filer for past conduct is generally Harbor, while Mosaic's own reporting obligations attach to the customer relationships it acquires and to suspicious activity it identifies after close, including activity sitting in the inherited alerts. Research question: what diligence access to confidential SAR material is permitted, and which obligations continue or transfer.

**Application** — Recorded facts: ~40 open alerts and a handful of SARs from the last year; none reviewed; still in diligence; asset purchase. The decisive condition is content, which is unknown. If the alerts or SARs reflect unresolved activity tied to accounts moving to Mosaic, servicing those accounts on day 1 raises immediate decisions (restrict, close, file, or monitor) and possible notice and indemnity questions. If the review shows nothing material, the item closes.

**Next step** — Structured review by AML counsel under confidentiality controls; feed results into deal terms and day-1 servicing rules. Interim: no conclusion either way.

### Issue — Transaction monitoring repoint at close

**Current answer** — Review and validate before close. The plan replaces the monitoring system on the day the SaaS contract ends. That timing creates a gap risk that matters legally because monitoring is part of the AML program Mosaic must operate for the acquired portfolio from day 1; the practical control is a tested cutover (or a short bridge) with documented results.

**Rule and support** — BSA/AML program requirements include ongoing monitoring and reporting (MSB AML-program rule, 31 C.F.R. § 1023.210). **Needs checking** — not retrieved. Separately, Harbor's monitoring SaaS contract terminates at close; its transition, cooperation, and data terms are contractual questions to check in the vendor agreement, not public law.

**Application** — Recorded facts: third-party SaaS monitoring terminates at close; plan is to repoint Harbor's feed to Mosaic's engine on day 1; no validation results are recorded. If the cutover slips or data is dropped, a monitoring gap opens at close for customers Mosaic is already serving — an AML program control failure. Options: a parallel run before close, a short-term bridge extension, or documented manual monitoring until the new engine is validated.

**Next step** — Cutover test plan with acceptance criteria and recorded results before close; review the vendor contract for transition assistance. Interim: no day-1 launch on an untested repoint.

### Issue — $50 welcome bonus campaign

**Current answer** — Review before launch (close+1); lower gating risk than the other items, but do not launch un-reviewed. The answer depends on terms not yet in the record: how eligibility and "first transfer" are defined, when and how the $50 is paid and whether it is withdrawable, expiry, advertising claims, and tax characterization.

**Rule and support** — Consumer advertising and promotion law applies to the campaign: FTC Act § 5 (deceptive acts or practices) and state advertising/promotion statutes, some of which add specific requirements. **Needs checking** — none retrieved. Research question: what terms and claims must be confirmed, and whether any launch state has specific promotion rules; tax treatment is a separate check.

**Application** — Recorded facts: $50 bonus on first transfer, launching close+1, terms not yet provided. Standard, clear terms would be a limited review; ambiguous terms (for example, an undefined "first transfer" or conditions that make the bonus hard to obtain) would require rework of the offer and copy. The campaign also presupposes the migration runs; if day-1 scope narrows for licensing or KYC reasons, the campaign scope narrows with it, and it should not be used to pull users into states that are not cleared.

**Next step** — Obtain the campaign terms and copy and run the review in parallel with the gating items; if terms are not ready, delay the campaign rather than the close.
