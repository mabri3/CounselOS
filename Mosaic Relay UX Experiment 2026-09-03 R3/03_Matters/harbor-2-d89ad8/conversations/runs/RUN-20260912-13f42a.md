    ## Current position


    Mosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor Pay in an asset purchase targeted to close Nov
    15 (~10 weeks out). The migration plan moves 240,000 consumer and 12,000 business
    accounts into Mosaic''s app on day 1, relying on Harbor''s prior KYC (with missing
    underlying documents for pre-2023 users), assuming Harbor''s 38 state MTLs to
    create a combined 44-state footprint, and launching a $50 welcome bonus campaign.
    Harbor has ~40 open alerts and unreviewed SARs, and its transaction-monitoring
    SaaS contract terminates at close.


    **The migration needs legal and compliance review before close.** The leading
    concern is that an asset purchase generally requires Mosaic Relay to confirm its
    own authority to operate in each state; the plan to use or assume Harbor''s licenses
    is not established as legally effective. The most important unresolved decision
    is whether to hold the regulated launch in the four Harbor-only states until Mosaic
    has documented authority, and whether the ~40 open alerts and SARs reveal BSA/AML
    exposure that must be addressed before close.


    ## Decision question


    Does any part of the Harbor Pay acquisition migration need legal review before
    the Nov 15 close?


    ## Issues

    ### Money transmission licensing: whether Mosaic Relay can operate under the combined
    44-state footprint and whether Harbor''s MTLs transfer/survive the acquisition


    **Current answer**


    Do not rely on transferability without an applicable state rule, approval, or
    written regulator position. The answer must be determined state by state and must
    cover more than technical change-of-control rules. This path is possible only
    if Harbor remains the real provider and the relevant states permit the arrangement.
    The unified Mosaic account design may conflict with that position. The result
    depends on the contracts and actual funds flow. Custody alone should not be treated
    as a licensing exemption or transfer mechanism.


    **Rule and support**


    State money transmitter licenses are entity-specific. An asset purchase does not
    automatically transfer Harbor''s licenses to Mosaic. The CSBS Money Transmission
    Modernization Act is a model act, not proof of the law adopted in any specific
    state, and no exact operative provision about asset sales, license transfer, interim
    operation, or custody was available for review. [source:SRC-37ee676da5b354ba8538]
    (retrieved; passage not reviewed). The New York DFS licensing page confirms that
    money transmitter licensing is a state regulatory process but does not address
    transferability in an acquisition. [source:SRC-71b20ecf65825d6522c4] (retrieved;
    passage not reviewed). The OCC interpretive letter on National Bank Act preemption
    is not applicable here — Mosaic Relay is not a national bank. [source:SRC-e6c1e4e85e004ccbe413]
    (retrieved; passage not reviewed).


    **Application**


    The acquisition is confirmed as an asset purchase (FACT-20260909-ed9f06). Mosaic
    plans to use or assume Harbor''s licenses for the four new markets (FACT-20260909-f0fb01,
    FACT-20260909-771ee1). Because an asset purchase does not change Harbor''s ownership,
    a technical "change of control" rule may not even apply — but that does not resolve
    the issue. Harbor may still need a notice or surrender approval, and Mosaic may
    separately need authority before it starts regulated activity. The planned unified
    Mosaic account (one account, one balance, full product access) suggests Mosaic,
    not Harbor, will be the actual provider post-close, which makes it harder to show
    Harbor remains the true licensed provider under any interim structure. Partner-bank
    custody must be reviewed, but a bank account alone does not establish Mosaic''s
    authority.


    **Next step**


    Identify the four Harbor-only states and complete one matrix covering license
    authority, transaction filings, interim operation, bank custody, outstanding obligations,
    and license surrender. Until Mosaic has documented authority in a state, hold
    the regulated launch there.

    ### BSA/AML and KYC: whether relying on Harbor''s prior KYC (with missing underlying
    documents for pre-2023 users) satisfies Mosaic Relay''s independent CIP/AML obligations


    **Current answer**


    Not yet established. Mosaic Relay has not confirmed whether its AML/CIP program
    permits reliance on Harbor''s prior KYC, and underlying records are missing for
    some pre-2023 users. These gaps make a day-one migration without re-verification
    or account restrictions difficult to support.


    **Rule and support**


    No research update has been saved for this issue. The analysis relies on the reported
    facts and the general principle that a money services business must maintain its
    own BSA/AML program, including customer identification and recordkeeping obligations.
    No external authority has been retrieved to support a specific reliance standard.


    **Application**


    The plan is no re-verification; Harbor users were already KYC''d by Harbor (FACT-20260909-9a0c61,
    FACT-20260909-f2c49f). Harbor''s KYC ran through a vendor that shut down in 2024;
    Mosaic has pass/fail results for everyone but not the underlying documents for
    users onboarded before 2023 (FACT-20260909-aea8ec, FACT-20260909-13d4c1). Mosaic
    Relay''s compliance has not confirmed whether its AML/CIP program permits reliance
    on Harbor''s prior KYC (FACT-20260909-b5e71e, FACT-20260909-620c62). Whether the
    missing underlying documents create a compliance gap depends on how Mosaic plans
    to satisfy its own CIP/recordkeeping obligations for these users.


    **Next step**


    Compliance must confirm whether Mosaic Relay''s AML/CIP program permits reliance
    on Harbor''s prior KYC without underlying documents, and if not, what remediation
    (re-verification, account restrictions, or document recovery) is required before
    migrated users receive full access.

    ### SAR and alert handling: obligations to review, file, and manage Harbor''s

    **Current answer**


    The open alerts and SAR history have not been reviewed and remain in diligence.

    exposure that must be addressed before close and could affect the deal itself.


    **Rule and support**


    No research update has been saved for this issue. No external authority has been
    retrieved on successor liability for SARs and alerts in an asset acquisition.


    **Application**


    Diligence flagged ~40 open alerts and "a handful" of SARs from the last year at
    Harbor; nobody has read them yet (FACT-20260909-adf2cb, FACT-20260909-9f7bde,
    FACT-20260909-849b00, FACT-20260909-070aec). The nature, age, and whether any
    are unresolved or reportable remain unknown. In an asset purchase, the question
    of which entity inherits the obligation to review, file, and manage these items
    depends on the deal structure and the allocation of regulatory obligations — which
    has not been determined.


    **Next step**


    Review the ~40 open alerts and the handful of SARs before close. Determine whether
    any are unresolved or reportable, and confirm which entity bears the obligation
    to complete, file, or manage them post-close.

    ### Monitoring vendor transition: continuity of transaction monitoring when the
    SaaS contract terminates at close

    close. The repointed feed should be tested and validated before migrated users
    receive full access.


    **Rule and support**


    No research update has been saved for this issue. No external authority has been
    retrieved on monitoring continuity obligations in an acquisition.


    **Application**


    Harbor''s transaction monitoring is a third-party SaaS whose contract terminates
    at close; the plan is to repoint Harbor''s transaction feed at Mosaic''s engine

    can be completed and validated before close without a monitoring gap (ASM-20260909-1a519f,
    open). A day-one repoint without prior testing and validation creates a risk of

    **Next step**

    Confirm there is no monitoring gap between the SaaS termination at close and the
    day-1 repoint.
