    <summary>Detailed analysis</summary>


    **Practical answer**


    **Do not treat Harbor’s money transmitter licenses as assets that move to Mosaic
    in the asset purchase.** Use this closing rule:


    > **No confirmed authority means no regulated launch by Mosaic in that state.**


    The asset purchase can still close. However, Mosaic should not activate regulated
    services in the four Harbor-only states unless one of these conditions is met:


    1. Mosaic obtains its own license.

    2. The state approves a transfer or other license change.

    3. The regulator confirms a lawful interim structure.

    4. Harbor remains the real licensed provider under a valid transition plan.

    5. Mosaic blocks regulated functions in that state.


    The planned unified Mosaic account is important. It suggests that Mosaic, not
    Harbor, will become the service provider. If that is correct, keeping Harbor’s
    legal entity and licenses alive might not give Mosaic authority to operate.


    **No verified operative authority retrieved.** The collection found the CSBS Model
    Money Transmission Modernization Act and a New York regulator licensing page.
    However, no exact provision about an asset sale, license transfer, interim operation,
    or custody was available for review. Also, a model act is not proof of the law
    adopted in a specific state. The state-specific conclusions below remain conditional.


    **Work needed before launch**


    | Work | Why | Proposed owner | Needed by | Evidence to proceed | Fallback |

    |---|---|---|---|---|---|

    | Identify the four Harbor-only states | These states have the clearest authority
    gap | Licensing team | Now | Confirmed license list and covered activities | Block
    regulated functions in those states |

    | Build a matrix for every affected state | Transfer, notice, approval, and surrender
    rules can differ | State regulatory counsel | Before the closing conditions are
    final | Applicable rule, filing deadline, regulator response, and license status
    | Delay migration in the affected state |

    | Confirm the post-close service provider | The licensed entity must match the
    actual operating model | Product and deal counsel | Before customer notices and
    account design are final | Contracts, user interface, funds-flow diagram, and
    operating roles | Keep Harbor as the actual provider where permitted |

    | Review notices and approvals | An asset sale can create separate seller and
    buyer requirements | Licensing team | Before each state deadline | Filing receipts
    and required approvals or non-objections | Make approval a state launch condition
    |

    | Confirm interim operating authority | A pending application should not be assumed
    to permit operation | State regulatory counsel | Before close | Applicable rule
    or written regulator confirmation | Keep Harbor customers segregated or restrict
    transactions |

    | Review partner-bank arrangements | Bank custody does not, by itself, establish
    Mosaic’s licensing position | Payments counsel and bank relationship owner | Before
    balances move | Bank agreements, account titles, control rights, reconciliation
    design, and bank consent | Keep funds and obligations in the existing structure
    |

    | Prepare license run-off and surrender plans | Harbor may retain duties for balances,
    claims, records, and reports | Harbor compliance and deal counsel | Before surrender
    or operational shutdown | Regulator-approved plan and continued bonds and financial
    coverage | Keep Harbor licensed and operational longer |


    **State notice and approval**


    Do not search only for a rule called “license transfer.” Review each state for:


    - sale of substantially all assets;

    - change of control;

    - change in business plan;

    - change in licensed activities;

    - assignment of outstanding payment obligations;

    - change in responsible persons, locations, or agents;

    - license surrender or termination;

    - customer agreement assignment; and

    - movement of stored balances or customer funds.


    An asset purchase might fall outside a technical “change of control” rule because
    Harbor’s ownership does not change. That does not resolve the issue. Harbor might
    still need a notice or surrender approval. Mosaic might separately need authority
    before it starts the regulated activity.


    The state matrix should distinguish:


    - notice only;

    - approval before closing;

    - approval before customer migration;

    - new license required;

    - operation permitted while an application is pending;

    - regulator-approved transition available; and

    - no supported interim path.


    **Interim operation**


    Harbor could remain the licensed provider for a transition period. However, the
    structure must be real, not only contractual language.


    A supportable structure would normally need to show that:


    - Harbor remains responsible to customers.

    - Customer terms name Harbor as the provider.

    - Harbor controls the regulated activity as required.

    - Harbor’s licenses, bonds, and financial coverage remain active.

    - Customer funds and outstanding obligations remain covered.

    - Harbor can complete reports, refunds, complaints, and examinations.

    - Required records remain available.

    - Mosaic’s operational role is permitted.

    - The partner bank accepts the structure.

    - Each relevant regulator permits or accepts the structure.


    The present plan creates tension with this approach. A single Mosaic account,
    one combined balance, and full access to Mosaic products make it harder to show
    that Harbor remains the true provider.


    **Partner-bank custody**


    A partner bank account is not automatically a substitute for a money transmitter
    license. The key questions concern the complete activity and obligation flow.


    For each customer balance and transfer, document:


    1. Who receives the customer’s instruction?

    2. Who accepts the obligation to transmit or return the funds?

    3. In whose name is the bank account held?

    4. Is it a custodial or “for benefit of” account?

    5. Who can direct withdrawals?

    6. Who owns the ledger and resolves errors?

    7. Who bears loss if a transfer fails?

    8. Which entity owes the customer a refund?

    9. Which entity’s license, bond, and permissible investments cover the obligation?

    10. Does the bank agreement permit assignment, migration, or a change in the program
    manager?

    11. Is bank consent required before closing or migration?

    12. What happens to funds if Harbor, Mosaic, or the bank relationship ends?


    A bank-led model might provide a different licensing analysis if the bank is the
    actual provider and Mosaic performs only permitted services. A bank account that
    merely holds funds for Mosaic or Harbor is different. The legal documents, customer
    terms, control rights, and actual operations must all support the claimed model.


    Also review:


    - account segregation;

    - daily reconciliation;

    - shortfalls and reserve funding;

    - bank setoff rights;

    - liens and security interests;

    - continuity if the account is frozen;

    - treatment of pending transfers;

    - return and chargeback duties;

    - insolvency treatment;

    - customer disclosures; and

    - whether any deposit-insurance statement is accurate for the actual account structure.


    **Recommended closing position**


    Keep the acquisition closing separate from the regulated-service launch.


    For the four Harbor-only states, use a state launch hold unless Mosaic has documented
    authority. For overlapping states, confirm that Mosaic’s existing licenses cover
    the complete post-close product set and funds flow.


    Do not surrender Harbor’s licenses or release its bonds until the applicable regulators
    permit it and all outstanding obligations have been assigned, completed, or placed
    into an approved run-off structure.


    This investigation does not change the current recommendation. It adds one material
    point: **partner-bank custody is part of the licensing analysis, but custody alone
    does not cure an authority gap.**

  Do not search only for a rule called “license transfer.” Review each state for:


  - sale of substantially all assets;

  - change of control;

  - change in licensed activities;

  - assignment of outstanding payment obligations;

  **Interim operation**


  Harbor could remain the licensed provider for a transition period. However, the
  structure must be real, not only contractual language.


  A supportable structure would normally need to show that:


  - Harbor remains responsible to customers.

  - Customer terms name Harbor as the provider.

  - Harbor controls the regulated activity as required.

  - Harbor’s licenses, bonds, and financial coverage remain active.

  - Customer funds and outstanding obligations remain covered.

  - Harbor can complete reports, refunds, complaints, and examinations.

  - Required records remain available.

  - Mosaic’s operational role is permitted.

  - The partner bank accepts the structure.

  - Each relevant regulator permits or accepts the structure.


  The present plan creates tension with this approach. A single Mosaic account, one
  combined balance, and full access to Mosaic products make it harder to show that
  Harbor remains the true provider.


  **Partner-bank custody**


  A partner bank account is not automatically a substitute for a money transmitter
  license. The key questions concern the complete activity and obligation flow.


  For each customer balance and transfer, document:


  1. Who receives the customer’s instruction?

  2. Who accepts the obligation to transmit or return the funds?

  3. In whose name is the bank account held?

  4. Is it a custodial or “for benefit of” account?

  5. Who can direct withdrawals?

  6. Who owns the ledger and resolves errors?

  7. Who bears loss if a transfer fails?

  8. Which entity owes the customer a refund?

  9. Which entity’s license, bond, and permissible investments cover the obligation?

  10. Does the bank agreement permit assignment, migration, or a change in the program
  manager?

  11. Is bank consent required before closing or migration?

  12. What happens to funds if Harbor, Mosaic, or the bank relationship ends?

  actual provider and Mosaic performs only permitted services. A bank account that
  merely holds funds for Mosaic or Harbor is different. The legal documents, customer
  terms, control rights, and actual operations must all support the claimed model.

  - account segregation;

  - bank setoff rights;

  - treatment of pending transfers;

  - insolvency treatment;

  - customer disclosures; and

  - whether any deposit-insurance statement is accurate for the actual account structure.
