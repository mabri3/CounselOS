  ## Notice and approval analysis


  The team should not limit its review to provisions called “license transfer.” The
  relevant rule may instead appear under:


  - transfer of substantially all assets;

  - change in control or ownership;

  - merger or corporate reorganization;

  - change in legal entity or licensed name;

  - change in business plan;

  - change in responsible individuals;

  - change in locations or agents;

  - assignment of customer obligations;

  - surrender, suspension, or termination of a license; or

  - acquisition of outstanding payment obligations.


  A pure asset sale may not meet a state’s technical definition of “change of control.”
  That does **not** mean no filing is required. The seller’s disposition of the business
  and the buyer’s start of regulated activity can create separate requirements.


  Some filings may be notices after signing or before closing. Others may require
  approval before the transaction, before the buyer begins business, or before the
  seller surrenders its license. The applicable timing must be verified state by state.


  ## Interim operation


  Mosaic should not assume it can operate while a new-license or approval request
  is pending. A filed application usually should not be treated as operating authority
  without a verified rule or written regulator position.


  A possible transition is for Harbor to remain the licensed provider for a limited
  period. This works only if it is legally and operationally real:


  - Harbor remains responsible to customers.

  - Harbor retains control required of the licensee.

  - Customer terms identify the correct provider.

  - Customer funds and outstanding obligations remain properly covered.

  - Required bonds, permissible investments, reports, records, and examinations continue.

  - Mosaic’s role is permitted under applicable law.

  - The arrangement does not amount to Mosaic using Harbor’s license as a rented license.

  - The transaction documents keep Harbor able to perform these duties.

  - Relevant regulators approve or accept the structure when required.


  The present plan does not appear to support that model. It moves users into Mosaic’s
  app with one Mosaic account, one balance, and access to Mosaic’s products on day
  one. Those facts point toward Mosaic becoming the operating provider. If so, Harbor’s
  continuing license may not solve Mosaic’s authorization gap.


  ## Transition requirements


  The licensing plan should cover more than the four new states. It should address:


  1. **Outstanding obligations:** Identify which entity owes stored balances, transfers,
  refunds, and claims at each transition point.

  2. **Customer contracts:** Confirm whether agreements can be assigned and whether
  notice or consent is required.

  3. **Funds and safeguarding:** Map the movement of customer funds and the related
  permissible-investment coverage.

  4. **Surety bonds:** Keep required bonds active until each regulator permits release.

  5. **Reports and examinations:** Assign responsibility for pre-close and post-close
  reports, complaints, examinations, and record production.

  6. **Books and records:** Preserve Harbor’s required records and regulator access
  after the vendor and corporate transition.

  7. **Agents and delegates:** Determine whether any registered agents, authorized
  delegates, or locations need termination or new appointments.

  8. **Surrender:** Do not surrender Harbor’s licenses until outstanding obligations
  and regulator conditions are satisfied.

  9. **Customer communications:** Ensure notices state the correct provider, effective
  date, rights, fees, and complaint contact.

  10. **Product scope:** Confirm that Mosaic’s existing licenses cover all products
  that migrated users will receive, not only Harbor’s former service.


  ## Recommended closing position


  Treat authorization for each affected state as a **launch condition**, not as a
  post-close clean-up item.


  For each state, require one of these outcomes before Mosaic serves migrated customers:


  1. Mosaic already has a license that covers the planned activity and product set.

  2. Mosaic obtains the required license or approval.

  3. The regulator confirms a lawful interim structure.

  4. The affected customers remain with Harbor under a supportable run-off model.

  5. Regulated functionality is blocked until authority exists.


  The four Harbor-only states should not be presented as part of Mosaic’s available
  footprint until one of these paths is documented. If the state review cannot finish
  before close, the most defensible fallback is a state-based migration hold. Mosaic
  can close the asset acquisition without activating regulated service in those states.


  This result does not materially change the saved working position. It gives that
  position a clearer operational rule: **no regulator-confirmed authority, no day-one
  regulated operation by Mosaic in the affected state.**

  ## Practical answer


  **Do not treat Harbor’s 38 money transmitter licenses as transferable assets.**
  For closing purposes, assume that Mosaic cannot use them unless each relevant state
  regulator confirms a lawful path.


  An asset purchase usually leaves the license with the licensed seller. It does not
  automatically move the license to the buyer. The buyer generally needs its own authority
  before it conducts regulated money transmission. A contract that says Mosaic “assumes”
  the licenses does not bind a regulator.

For each state, require one of these outcomes before Mosaic serves migrated customers:

1. Mosaic already has a license that covers the planned activity and product set.
2. Mosaic obtains the required license or approval.
3. The regulator confirms a lawful interim structure.
4. The affected customers remain with Harbor under a supportable run-off model.
5. Regulated functionality is blocked until authority exists.

The four Harbor-only states should not be presented as part of Mosaic’s available footprint until one of these paths is documented. If the state review cannot finish before close, the most defensible fallback is a state-based migration hold. Mosaic can close the asset acquisition without activating regulated service in those states.

This result does not materially change the saved working position. It gives that position a clearer operational rule: **no regulator-confirmed authority, no day-one regulated operation by Mosaic in the affected state.**
