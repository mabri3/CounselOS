Section 6.02 Notice and Information Requirements for a Change of Key Individuals. 
(a) 
A licensee adding or replacing any key individual shall: 
(1) 
Provide notice in a manner prescribed by the [Commissioner] within 
15 days after the effective date of the key individual's appointment; and 
(2) 
Provide information as required by [Section 5.04] within 45 days of 
the effective date. 
(b) 
Within 90 days of the date on which the notice provided pursuant to [Section 
6.02(a)] was determined to be complete, the [Commissioner] may issue a notice of disapproval of 
a key individual if the competence, experience, character, or integrity of the individual would not 
be in the best interests of the public or the customers of the licensee to permit the individual to be 
a key individual of such licensee. 
(c) 
A notice of disapproval shall contain a statement of the basis for disapproval 
and shall be sent to the licensee and the disapproved individual. A licensee may appeal a notice of 
disapproval [pursuant to state administrative law procedures] [within thirty (30) days]18 after 
receipt of such notice of disapproval. 
(d) 
If the notice provided pursuant to [Section 6.02(a)] is not disapproved 
within 90 days after the date on which the notice was determined to be complete, the key individual 
is deemed approved. 
(e) 
If a multistate licensing process includes a key individual notice review and 
disapproval process pursuant to [Section 6.02] and the licensee avails itself or is otherwise subject 
to the multistate licensing process: 
(1) 
the [Commissioner] is authorized and encouraged to accept the 
determination of another state  if the investigating state has sufficient staffing, expertise, 
and minimum standards for the purpose of [Section 6.02]; or 
(2) 
if [state] is a lead investigative state, the [Commissioner] is 
authorized and encouraged to investigate the applicant pursuant to [Section 6.02(b)] and 
the timeframes established by agreement through the multistate licensing process. 
 
 
18 Commentary: Administrative procedures for challenging a departmental decision may be derived from other 
aspects of state law. In such a case, this Act should be adjusted to reflect the relevant administrative process.
