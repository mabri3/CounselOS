      ### Money transmission licensing for the new markets after an asset purchase


      **Current answer** — Not cleared. An asset purchase transfers assets; it does
      not by itself transfer the regulator''s permission to transmit money. Mosaic''s
      plan to "use/assume Harbor''s licenses" in the new markets is a business assumption,
      not a legal position, and must be confirmed state by state. Assume the new markets
      cannot be served on day 1 until each state confirms whether Harbor''s license
      can be used, must be transferred by approval, or must be replaced by a new Mosaic
      application. Also correct the state arithmetic (below).


      **Rule and support** — State money transmission acts license the entity that
      receives and transmits funds; states have their own change-of-control, transfer,
      notification and new-application rules, and many require prior approval. The
      CSBS Money Transmission Modernization Act is a useful lead for how states handle
      change-of-control approval, permissible investments and safeguarding — **Needs
      checking** against the specific states. This premise (licenses do not follow
      an asset sale automatically) is a general statement, not verified against any
      state statute in the records read.


      **Application** — Recorded facts: asset purchase; Harbor holds MTLs in 38 states;
      Mosaic in 41; the plan assumes a combined 44 and "four new markets." The numbers
      do not add up as stated: 38 + 41 with 44 combined implies 35 overlapping states
      and only **three** Harbor-only states. Build the work plan from the actual state
      lists, not the "four" figure. Two items sit inside this issue: (1) moving customer
      balances into "one unified account, one balance" may trigger each state''s safeguarding
      and permissible-investment requirements for the migrated balances — **Needs
      checking**; (2) "full access to our product set" means migrated users may access
      products (lending, cards, crypto or others — the records do not say which) that
      carry their own state approvals. Activity-specific approvals are unresolved.


      **Next step** — Produce a state-by-state matrix (license held / transfer allowed
      / approval or new application needed / regulator timeline) from both license
      lists, and a product-by-product mapping for the migrated product set. Fallback
      if clearance cannot be completed: launch day 1 only in states Mosaic is already
      licensed for and defer the new states until confirmed.


      ### KYC reliance and the missing pre-2023 records


      **Current answer** — Open; this is the plan''s weakest legal premise. Whether
      Mosaic may rely on Harbor''s prior KYC without re-verification depends on Mosaic''s
      own AML/CIP program and the rules applicable to Mosaic''s regulatory status
      — compliance has not confirmed it ("Not sure / need to check with compliance").
      The pre-2023 cohort is the exposure: pass/fail results exist, but the underlying
      documents do not, and the vendor that held them shut down in 2024. The likely
      landing zone is cohort-based: reliance (if permitted) for users with adequate
      records, and re-verification or account limits for the pre-2023 cohort until
      cured.


      **Rule and support** — Federal BSA law requires money services businesses to
      maintain risk-based AML programs: 31 U.S.C. § 5318(h) and 31 CFR 1023.210 —
      **Needs checking** as applied to Mosaic. The federal customer identification
      rule and its reliance mechanism are bank rules: 31 CFR 1020.220 — **Needs checking**;
      if Mosaic is not a bank, that mechanism does not itself authorize the plan,
      and Mosaic''s program documents and state requirements govern. Record retention
      is generally five years: 31 CFR 1010.430 — **Needs checking**.


      **Application** — Because the migration opens a new unified Mosaic account for
      each customer, Mosaic''s own program obligations attach at that moment regardless
      of what Harbor did. Reliance regimes typically require the relying institution
      to obtain the records or confirm the other institution will provide them; here
      the record-holder is gone, so reliance may be practically unavailable for pre-2023
      users even where legally permitted. A blanket "no re-verification" default fits
      the post-2023 cohort better than the pre-2023 one.


      **Next step** — Obtain compliance''s written position: does the program permit
      reliance, on what conditions, and what satisfies recordkeeping for pre-2023
      users? Fallback: re-verify the pre-2023 cohort before full access, or migrate
      them with limited functionality pending verification.

      ### Monitoring repoint and continuity at close


      **Current answer** — Legally, the program must maintain continuous, effective
      monitoring; operationally, the plan has a hard edge because Harbor''s monitoring
      SaaS contract ends at close and the feed is repointed to Mosaic''s engine on
      day 1. If the repoint is not validated before close, the gap becomes an AML
      program deficiency at exactly the moment 252,000 accounts change systems.


      **Rule and support** — Monitoring is part of the required AML program''s internal
      controls: 31 U.S.C. § 5318(h); 31 CFR 1023.210 — **Needs checking**; suspicious
      activity reporting obligations continue regardless of systems. The governing
      contractual question — whether the SaaS agreement ends by expiry, non-renewal,
      or change-of-control termination — determines whether an extension is even available
      as a fallback; the records state only that it "terminates at close."


      **Application** — A day-1 cutover means the new engine starts without the legacy

      close is the control that shows the new system catches what the old one did,
      and alert/SAR records must survive the vendor transition. The fallback options
      are a short vendor extension or interim manual monitoring — either must be decided
      before the contract ends, because it ends at close.


      **Next step** — Read the monitoring contract (termination mechanism), then run
      and document a parallel test before close. Fallback: negotiate a short extension
      or stand up interim manual monitoring for the cutover window.
