## Harbor 2 — preparation note (close targeted Nov 15)

**Controlling question:** does any part of the Harbor Pay acquisition migration need legal review before the Nov 15 close? The newest turn ("Generate the Dossier") and the surrounding bank-held-funds comparisons are supporting work; they do not change that question. The record contains **no recorded decisions** at all, all five registered issues are `open`, and one intake question — how the $50 bonus is structured — was left open with no answer.

**Lawyer reframe.** Dana's framing is "it's really just a data migration." The reframe is that the migration *is* the regulated event: on day 1 Mosaic Relay onboards 252,000 accounts into its own licensed product set with no re-verification, repoints monitoring, and launches a cash offer — under an asset-purchase structure in which nothing about Harbor's licenses or compliance record transfers by default. The operative question is therefore not "can we inherit Harbor's status," but **"what must Mosaic obtain, file, decide or preserve in its own name before Nov 15, and which parts of the stated deal upside are not actually available at close."**

**Three premises the record lets us test**

1. *"Just a data migration."* Fails. The day-1 cutover is the moment CIP, licensing, monitoring and disclosure obligations attach to 252k accounts.
2. *"44 states is a nice win — opens four new markets."* Fails on the arithmetic and on the law. Harbor 38 + Mosaic 41 with a 44-state union yields **three** new states, not four, and the states are never named; and the plan to "use/assume" Harbor's licenses rests on an assumption the record still marks open ([source:ASM-20260909-29b823]), against the saved position that transferability should not be relied on without a state rule, approval or written regulator position.
3. *"No re-verification — they were already KYC'd."* Unresolved. Whether Mosaic's own AML/CIP program permits reliance is unknown, and the pre-2023 records cannot be recovered (vendor shut down in 2024; only pass/fail survives).

**Different route the evidence supports.** Run the remaining ~9 weeks as a *gated launch*, not a documentation exercise: read and preserve the alert/SAR backlog; decide KYC reliance per cohort and schedule remediation; prove or drop the Harbor-only states; validate the monitoring cutover with a parallel run; paper notice/consent, new customer terms and closing conditions. If a gate fails, migrate only in the overlapping states on day 1 and onboard KYC-deficient accounts with limits — a scoped migration rather than the full one.

**Material issues.** Five registered: money transmission licensing (44-state footprint); BSA/AML and KYC reliance for pre-2023 users; SAR and alert handling (~40 open alerts, unreviewed); monitoring vendor transition at close; $50 welcome bonus. Three that are missing and material: **migration data handling and customer notice** (the piece the request actually calls "just a data migration," never analyzed); **pre-close papering** (closing conditions, interim operation, allocation of the alert/SAR and KYC-record risk — no draft or terms appear anywhere); and **post-close access to Harbor's alert/case history** (the records live in the SaaS system whose contract ends at close).

**Three business priorities** — ranked by effect on the business question, time-fixedness and irreversibility, not by difficulty:

1. **Read and preserve the alert/SAR backlog before anything else is scheduled.** It is the only item that can change the deal itself; it is entirely unread, so no one can yet say whether a filing is late or an account must be excluded; and the records needed to answer it may become inaccessible at close.
2. **Decide KYC reliance for the pre-2023 cohort — rely, re-verify, or restrict access.** It determines whether "no re-verification" is available at all for the largest population; the underlying documents cannot be recreated; and re-verifying or limiting a cohort is a multi-week program that has to be decided before close, not after.
3. **Prove the Harbor-only states can be served at close, or pull them from day-1 scope.** It tests the premise the deal is being sold on, state timelines are the longest external dependency and cannot be compressed, and serving a state without authority is a violation later paperwork cannot undo.

**Research these first:** the SAR/alert backlog ([source:ISS-c9d7c39f63-69af3f282d21ef38]), KYC reliance ([source:ISS-c9d7c39f63-ca95e529feeb1045]), and the licensing position for the Harbor-only states ([source:ISS-c9d7c39f63-94e42e2b38090045]) — in that order. If the team needs the earliest single go/no-go decision, licensing converts fastest — but it should still follow the backlog read, because a live SAR problem can outrank market access.
