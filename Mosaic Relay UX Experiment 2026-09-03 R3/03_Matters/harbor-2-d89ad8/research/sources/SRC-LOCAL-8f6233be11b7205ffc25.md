## Preparation note

**Result:** Legal and compliance review is required before the Nov 15 close. The migration is not only a technical data transfer.

**Lawyer reframe:** Determine which pre-close and day-one actions could change the deal risk, exceed Mosaic’s legal authority, weaken anti-money-laundering controls, or harm customers. Then separate safe preparation from actions that require legal clearance.

The current plan relies on unconfirmed assumptions. Harbor’s licenses might not transfer in an asset purchase. Mosaic might not be able to rely on Harbor’s identity checks. About 40 alerts and several suspicious activity reports remain unreviewed. Harbor’s monitoring service will end at close, but the replacement has not been shown to work.

The safer route is a staged launch. Mosaic can complete reversible technical work before close. It should delay affected accounts, new markets, or full product access if a legal or compliance gate remains open.

### Material issues

- Unresolved alerts and suspicious activity history.
- State money-transmission authority after the asset purchase.
- Reliance on identity checks that lack underlying records.
- Continuous transaction monitoring at cutover.
- Purchase-agreement limits and allocation of compliance liabilities.
- Customer terms, privacy notices, data transfers, and the $50 bonus.

### Three business priorities

1. **Protect the acquisition decision.** Review unresolved compliance matters before Mosaic accepts risk that is difficult to reverse.
2. **Confirm lawful day-one service.** Verify that Mosaic can serve each account and market after an asset purchase.
3. **Preserve a safe fallback.** Keep monitoring active and permit a staged launch if any required control is not ready.

### First three issues to research

1. **Open alerts and suspicious activity history.** The findings could change deal terms, closing conditions, remediation, or account eligibility.
2. **State licensing.** Regulatory approval could take time and could prevent launch in the four new markets.
3. **Identity-verification reliance.** Missing source records affect 240,000 consumers and 12,000 business accounts and could require re-verification or access limits.
