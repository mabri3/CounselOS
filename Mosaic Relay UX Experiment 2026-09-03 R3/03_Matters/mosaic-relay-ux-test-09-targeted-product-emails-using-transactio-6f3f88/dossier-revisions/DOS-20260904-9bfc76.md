# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company, wants to send targeted product emails to merchants and platform administrators using service data (transaction volume, dispute rates, payout behavior, onboarding status, and fraud-risk indicators). The business goal is to increase feature adoption and reduce operational problems through weekly and event-triggered messages. Counsel is involved because the targeting model uses risk/fraud scores, no opt-out or preference controls exist yet, and the legal basis for using service data for marketing is undetermined. The matter is in research status with normal priority and no fixed launch date.

## Decision question

May Mosaic Relay use service data — including fraud-risk scores — to send targeted marketing emails to business contacts at merchants and platforms, and if so, under what legal basis (consent vs. legitimate interest), with what profiling, unsubscribe, suppression, and retention controls, given that recipients' jurisdictions are undetermined and no opt-out mechanism currently exists?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay wants to send targeted emails to merchants and platform administrators based on transaction volume, dispute rates, payout behavior, onboarding status, and fraud-risk indicators.
- Example use cases: recommending dispute tools to merchants with rising chargebacks; promoting faster onboarding to businesses that have not completed verification.
- Business goal is to increase feature adoption and reduce avoidable operational problems without sending irrelevant messages.
- Messages would be sent weekly, with event-triggered emails after a transaction or account review.
- Mosaic Relay collects payment, onboarding, identity, risk, and operations data to provide its services.

## Issues and workstreams

- Use of service data for marketing vs. the purpose for which it was collected (purpose limitation)
- Consent vs. legitimate-interest legal basis across jurisdictions
- Profiling and sensitive-data restrictions (GDPR Art. 22, special categories) given risk/fraud-score targeting
- Message classification: transactional/service vs. marketing (CAN-SPAM, PECR, GDPR)
- Allocation of responsibility between Mosaic Relay and its platform customers
- Suppression, unsubscribe, and preference-management controls (none exist today)
- Retention of targeting data and suppression lists (no policy yet)

## Open questions

- **Which jurisdictions will recipients be in?** This determines whether GDPR/UK GDPR and PECR apply, or only US CAN-SPAM, or both — and the consent/opt-out rules differ materially between them.
- **What do Mosaic Relay's customer contracts say about using platform-customer data for Mosaic's own marketing?** If contracts restrict secondary use, Mosaic may need its own consent or a contract amendment regardless of what privacy law permits.
- **Do the fraud-risk scores used for targeting reveal or proxy special-category data (e.g., health, ethnicity, financial hardship)?** If so, GDPR Art. 9 restrictions and Art. 22 automated-decision rules are triggered more directly.
- **Are any recipients sole traders or individual partnerships (PECR "individual subscribers") rather than corporate subscribers?** PECR's B2B exemption applies only to corporate subscribers; sole traders get the same protections as consumers.
- **What retention period will apply to targeting data and suppression lists?** No policy exists; suppression lists must be retained indefinitely (or as long as the person remains opted out) to be effective, which creates a tension with data-minimization obligations.
