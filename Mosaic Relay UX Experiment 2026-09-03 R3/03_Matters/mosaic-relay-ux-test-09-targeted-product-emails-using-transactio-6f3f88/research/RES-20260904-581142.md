# US CAN-SPAM requirements for commercial email to business contacts, including

## Question

US CAN-SPAM requirements for commercial email to business contacts, including unsubscribe obligations, and how they interact with GDPR/PECR for a US-headquartered company sending to business contacts in the US, UK, and EU.

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a US-headquartered payment infrastructure company, plans to send weekly and event-triggered targeted marketing emails to merchants and platform administrators using service data (transaction volume, dispute rates, payout behavior, onboarding status, and fraud-risk indicators). Counsel is involved because the recipients are business contacts in the US, UK, and EU, and the company currently has no opt-out controls, no consent framework, and no retention policy for this use. The immediate consequence is that Mosaic Relay cannot lawfully launch until it builds suppression and preference controls and confirms which legal regime governs each recipient segment.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay send targeted marketing emails to business contacts in the US, UK, and EU using service data and fraud-risk scores, and if so, what consent, unsubscribe, and suppression controls must be built before launch to comply with CAN-SPAM, PECR, and GDPR?
## Open questions
- **Recipient jurisdiction mix:** Which specific countries will recipients be in? This determines whether PECR’s corporate-subscriber exemption applies (UK) or whether GDPR consent is required (EU member states with stricter ePrivacy rules).
- **Corporate vs. sole trader status:** Are any recipients sole traders or unincorporated partnerships? PECR treats these as individual subscribers requiring consent or soft opt-in, which intake indicates is currently unavailable.
- **Special-category data audit:** Do the fraud-risk scores or targeting inputs constitute or proxy special-category data under GDPR Art. 9? If yes, legitimate interest cannot support the marketing use.
- **Customer contract restrictions:** Do Mosaic Relay’s platform terms or customer agreements prohibit secondary use of merchant/admin data for Mosaic’s own marketing? If restricted, contract amendments are needed before launch.
- **Event-triggered email classification:** Will the event-triggered emails (sent after a transaction or account review) be classified as transactional/service messages or marketing? Misclassification creates CAN-SPAM and PECR risk.
---
## Likely rules and issues
### US CAN-SPAM Act
- **Applicability:** Applies to all commercial email sent to US recipients, including B2B. No consent is required, but messages must include a clear unsubscribe mechanism, a valid physical postal address, and accurate header information.
- **Unsubscribe obligations:** Opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days. The sender cannot sell or transfer the email address of someone who has opted out (except to a third party helping with compliance).
- **Content requirements:** The message must be identified as an advertisement (unless the recipient has given prior affirmative consent), and the subject line must not be deceptive.
- **Interaction with GDPR/PECR:** CAN-SPAM is the floor for US recipients. For UK/EU recipients, Mosaic Relay must comply with the stricter of CAN-SPAM or the local regime.
### UK PECR (Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations)
- **Corporate subscribers:** Marketing emails to corporate subscribers (limited companies, LLPs, etc.) do not require prior consent, but must include a valid unsubscribe mechanism and identify the sender.
- **Individual subscribers:** Sole traders and unincorporated partnerships are treated as individuals. Marketing emails require consent or soft opt-in (opt-out offered at collection and in every message). Intake indicates no opt-out was offered at collection, so soft opt-in is likely unavailable.
- **GDPR overlay:** Even where PECR allows marketing without consent, UK GDPR still requires a lawful basis for processing the personal data used for targeting. Legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) is the likely basis, but requires a documented legitimate interest assessment (LIA).
### EU GDPR and ePrivacy Directive
- **Consent requirement:** Most EU member states require prior consent for marketing emails to individuals, including business contacts. Some member states (e.g., Germany) have stricter rules than the UK.
- **Legitimate interest:** GDPR Art. 6(1)(f) can support the processing of personal data for direct marketing, but Recital 47 notes that direct marketing may be a legitimate interest only if it does not override the recipient’s rights. An LIA is required.
- **Right to object:** GDPR Art. 21(2) gives recipients an absolute right to object to direct marketing. Once an objection is received, no further marketing emails may be sent.
- **Special-category data:** If the targeting model uses or proxies special-category data (e.g., health, race, political opinions), Art. 9 prohibits processing unless an exception applies. Fraud-risk scores are not inherently special-category data, but if they reveal or infer sensitive attributes, legitimate interest alone cannot support the use.
### Message classification
- **Transactional vs. marketing:** Event-triggered emails sent after a transaction or account review may be classified as transactional if their primary purpose is to facilitate a service the recipient requested. However, if the primary purpose is to promote a product or feature, they are marketing and subject to CAN-SPAM/PECR/GDPR marketing rules.
- **Primary-purpose test:** The content, tone, and call-to-action determine classification. A message that says "Your dispute rate is rising" is likely transactional; a message that says "Try our new dispute tool" is likely marketing.
### Allocation of responsibility
- **Mosaic Relay as controller:** Mosaic Relay is the data controller for its own marketing emails. It cannot rely on its platform customers’ consent or legal basis unless the customer contract explicitly authorizes secondary use for Mosaic’s marketing.
- **Customer instructions:** If the platform customer is the controller and Mosaic Relay is the processor, Mosaic Relay must act only on documented instructions. Marketing emails sent on Mosaic’s own initiative are outside the processor role and require Mosaic to establish its own legal basis.
### Suppression and preference management
- **Suppression list:** A suppression list must be maintained to ensure opted-out individuals are not re-contacted. The list should be checked before every send.
- **Preference center:** A preference center allowing recipients to choose topics and frequency is a best practice and may be required under some EU member state laws.
- **Retention:** Suppression list entries should be retained indefinitely (or until the individual re-consents). Targeting-segment data should have a defined refresh/purge cadence.

## Viable paths
### Path A — Legitimate interest + corporate-subscriber exemption (primary path)
- **Scope:** US and UK corporate subscribers only.
- **Legal basis:** GDPR/UK GDPR Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interest, documented in an LIA.
- **Email rules:** CAN-SPAM for US recipients; PECR corporate-subscriber exemption for UK recipients.
- **Controls:** Build unsubscribe, suppression, and preference controls before launch. Disclose the marketing use in the privacy notice.
- **Risk:** Low for US/UK corporate subscribers. Does not cover EU recipients or sole traders.
### Path B — Soft opt-in (fallback for sole traders)
- **Scope:** UK sole traders and unincorporated partnerships.
- **Legal basis:** PECR soft opt-in (opt-out offered at collection and in every message).
- **Current status:** Intake indicates no opt-out was offered at collection, so soft opt-in is likely unavailable. These recipients should be excluded or moved to Path C.
- **Risk:** High if soft opt-in is not properly established.
### Path C — Consent (target state for EU and sole traders)
- **Scope:** EU recipients and any UK sole traders not covered by Path B.
- **Legal basis:** GDPR Art. 6(1)(a) consent, obtained via a preference center or account settings.
- **Controls:** Consent must be as easy to withdraw as to give. A record of consent must be maintained.
- **Risk:** Low if consent is properly obtained and documented. Requires a consent-gathering mechanism before launch.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Jurisdiction mix:** If all recipients are in the US, CAN-SPAM alone governs and no consent is needed. If any recipients are in the EU, consent is likely required.
- **Sole trader status:** If all recipients are incorporated businesses, PECR’s corporate-subscriber exemption applies. If any are sole traders, consent or soft opt-in is required.
- **Special-category data:** If the targeting model uses or proxies special-category data, legitimate interest cannot support the marketing use and consent is required.
- **Customer contract restrictions:** If customer contracts prohibit secondary use of merchant/admin data for Mosaic’s marketing, Mosaic cannot proceed without a contract amendment.
- **Event-triggered email classification:** If event-triggered emails are classified as transactional, they are exempt from CAN-SPAM’s content requirements but still subject to GDPR/PECR if they use personal data for targeting.

## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Confirm recipient jurisdiction mix** by segmenting the recipient list by country and subscriber type (corporate vs. individual).
2. **Audit the targeting model inputs** to confirm whether fraud-risk scores or any input fields constitute or proxy special-category data under GDPR Art. 9.
3. **Review customer contracts and platform terms** for restrictions on secondary use of merchant/admin data for Mosaic’s own marketing.
4. **Classify event-triggered emails** using the primary-purpose test to determine whether they are transactional or marketing.
5. **Document a legitimate interest assessment (LIA)** if relying on Art. 6(1)(f) for GDPR/UK GDPR.
6. **Build suppression, unsubscribe, and preference controls** before launch, and define retention periods for targeting-segment data and suppression-list entries.

## What would change this
- **Working assumption:** Recipients are business contacts (merchants/platform admins), not consumers. If any recipients are consumers, the analysis changes significantly — consumer protection laws (e.g., GDPR, CCPA) impose stricter consent and transparency requirements.
- **Working assumption:** Mosaic Relay is the data controller for its own marketing emails. If Mosaic Relay is acting as a processor on behalf of its platform customers, the customers’ instructions and legal basis govern, and Mosaic Relay cannot send marketing emails on its own initiative.
- **Open fork:** Whether the targeting model uses or proxies special-category data. If it does, legitimate interest cannot support the marketing use and consent is required. If it does not, legitimate interest is a viable basis for corporate subscribers.
- **Open fork:** Whether customer contracts restrict secondary use of merchant/admin data. If restricted, Mosaic Relay must obtain a contract amendment or the platform’s agreement before proceeding. If not restricted, Mosaic Relay can proceed on its own legal basis.
- **Not examined:** The specific content of the event-triggered emails. The classification of these emails as transactional or marketing depends on their content, tone, and call-to-action, which were not provided.
- **Not examined:** The specific EU member states where recipients are located. Some member states (e.g., Germany) have stricter ePrivacy rules than the UK, and the analysis may need to be tailored to the strictest applicable regime.
