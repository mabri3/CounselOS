      ## Matter summary

      Mosaic Relay, a US-headquartered payment infrastructure company, plans to send
      weekly and event-triggered targeted marketing emails to merchants and platform
      administrators using service data (transaction volume, dispute rates, payout
      behavior, onboarding status, and fraud-risk indicators). Counsel is involved
      because the recipients are business contacts in the US, UK, and EU, and the
      company currently lacks consent, opt-out controls, and a retention policy. The
      targeting model uses risk/fraud scores, raising GDPR Art. 9 special-category
      and Art. 22 automated-decision concerns. Launch timing is flexible, but suppression
      and preference controls must be built before any send.

      ## Decision question

      Can Mosaic Relay lawfully use fraud-risk scores and other service data to target
      marketing emails to business contacts in the US, UK, and EU, and if so, under
      what legal basis, with what suppression and preference controls, and subject
      to what restrictions on profiling and special-category data?

      ## Open questions

      - Which jurisdictions will recipients be in? This determines whether GDPR/UK
      GDPR, PECR, CAN-SPAM, or a mix applies, and whether consent is required.

      - Are any recipients sole traders or unincorporated partnerships? If so, PECR
      soft opt-in or consent may be required instead of the corporate-subscriber exemption.

      - Do the fraud-risk scores or their inputs constitute or proxy special-category
      data under GDPR Art. 9? If yes, legitimate interest alone cannot support the
      marketing use.

      - Do customer contracts or platform terms restrict Mosaic Relay''s use of merchant/admin
      data for its own marketing? If restricted, a contract amendment or platform
      agreement is needed.

      - What retention periods will apply to targeting-segment data and suppression
      lists? No policy exists yet.

      ## Likely rules and issues

      **No external authority retrieved.** The supplied public source material is
      exclusively US federal consumer-financial law (FCRA, ECOA/Regulation B, TILA/Regulation
      Z, RESPA, Regulation V) and does not address GDPR, PECR, or CAN-SPAM. The analysis
      below is model-only analysis based on the matter facts and general legal knowledge.

      ### 1. Permitted use of service data for marketing

      Mosaic Relay collected payment, onboarding, identity, risk, and operations data
      to provide its services. Using that data for marketing is a secondary purpose.
      Under GDPR/UK GDPR Art. 5(1)(b), further processing must be compatible with
      the original purpose. Marketing to existing business contacts about related
      products is generally compatible, but this must be documented and disclosed
      in the privacy notice.

      Under US law, CAN-SPAM does not prohibit using service data for marketing, but
      it requires accurate header information, non-deceptive subject lines, identification
      as an ad, a valid physical postal address, and a working opt-out mechanism honored
      within 10 business days.

      ### 2. Consent and unsubscribe requirements

      **UK/EU (PECR + GDPR/UK GDPR):**

      - For corporate subscribers (employees of incorporated businesses), PECR allows
      marketing emails without prior consent, but GDPR/UK GDPR still requires a lawful
      basis — typically legitimate interest under Art. 6(1)(f), documented in a legitimate
      interest assessment (LIA).

      - For sole traders and unincorporated partnerships, PECR requires either consent
      or soft opt-in. Soft opt-in requires that an opt-out was offered at collection
      and in every message. Intake indicates no opt-out was offered at collection,
      so soft opt-in is likely unavailable.

      - GDPR Art. 21(2) gives an absolute right to object to direct marketing, including
      profiling to the extent it relates to direct marketing. Once a recipient objects,
      no balancing test applies — processing must stop.

      **US (CAN-SPAM):**

      - No consent required for B2B emails, but a clear and conspicuous opt-out mechanism
      is mandatory, and opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days.

      ### 3. Profiling and sensitive-data restrictions

      **GDPR Art. 22:** The right not to be subject to solely automated decisions
      with legal or similarly significant effect. Marketing targeting based on fraud-risk
      scores is unlikely to produce legal or similarly significant effects on the
      data subject (the merchant/admin), so Art. 22 is likely not triggered. However,
      if the targeting leads to account restrictions, payout delays, or other significant
      consequences, Art. 22 could apply.

      **GDPR Art. 9:** Special-category data includes racial/ethnic origin, political
      opinions, religious beliefs, trade-union membership, genetic/biometric data,
      health data, and sex life/orientation data. Fraud-risk scores are not inherently
      special-category data, but they can proxy for it if the inputs or the score
      itself reveal sensitive attributes (e.g., if the score correlates strongly with
      ethnicity or health conditions). If the score proxies special-category data,
      legitimate interest alone cannot support the processing — explicit consent or
      another Art. 9(2) exception is required.

      **US analogue:** ECOA/Regulation B § 1002.6(a)(2) prohibits facially neutral
      practices that function as proxies for protected characteristics with intent
      to advantage or disadvantage. This is a narrower, intent-based test with no
      direct GDPR counterpart, but it flags the same concern.

      ### 4. Message classification

      **Transactional vs. marketing:** Under CAN-SPAM, a message is transactional
      if its primary purpose is to facilitate, complete, or confirm a commercial transaction
      the recipient has already agreed to. Under PECR/GDPR, a message is a service
      message if it is necessary for the performance of a contract or is requested
      by the recipient. Event-triggered emails after a transaction or account review
      could be classified as transactional if they primarily provide information about
      the transaction or account status. However, if the primary purpose is to promote
      a product or feature (e.g., recommending dispute tools), the message is marketing
      and subject to full marketing rules.

      **Primary-purpose test:** Apply honestly. Do not relabel marketing as transactional
      to avoid consent/opt-out requirements.

      ### 5. Customer vs. Mosaic Relay responsibility

      Mosaic Relay is the data controller for its own marketing emails. Its platform
      customers are separate controllers for their own data. If Mosaic uses customer
      data (merchant/admin data) for its own marketing, it must ensure its customer
      contracts permit this secondary use. If the contracts restrict secondary use,
      Mosaic must obtain a contract amendment or the platform''s agreement.

      ### 6. Retention

      No retention policy exists. Required:

      - **Targeting-segment data:** Define a refresh/purge cadence (e.g., 12–24 months).

      - **Suppression-list entries:** Retain indefinitely or until re-consent, to
      ensure opted-out individuals are not re-contacted.

      - **Engagement data (opens, clicks):** Define retention for marketing analytics
      (e.g., 12 months).

      ### 7. Required controls for suppression and preference management

      - **Working unsubscribe mechanism** in every marketing email.

      - **Suppression list** maintained so opted-out individuals are not re-contacted.

      - **Preference center** allowing topic/frequency choice.

      - **Identification** of Mosaic Relay as sender in every message.

      - **Privacy-notice disclosure** of the marketing use and lawful basis.
