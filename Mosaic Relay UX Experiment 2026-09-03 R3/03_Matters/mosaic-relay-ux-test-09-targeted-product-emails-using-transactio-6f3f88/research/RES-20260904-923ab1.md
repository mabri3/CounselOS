# US CAN-SPAM requirements for commercial email to business contacts, including

## Question

US CAN-SPAM requirements for commercial email to business contacts, including unsubscribe obligations

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company, wants to send targeted marketing emails to merchants and platform administrators using service data (transaction volume, dispute rates, payout behavior, onboarding status, and fraud-risk indicators). The business goal is to increase feature adoption and reduce operational problems. Counsel is involved because this is a privacy review of using service data for marketing purposes, and the company currently has no opt-out controls, no retention policy, and no confirmed legal basis for the marketing use. The matter is in research status with a working recommendation already drafted.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay send weekly and event-triggered marketing emails to business contacts (merchants and platform administrators) using transaction and risk data collected for service delivery, and if so, what consent, unsubscribe, suppression, and retention controls must be built before launch to comply with US CAN-SPAM and other applicable regimes?
## Open questions
- **Recipient jurisdiction mix** — Not yet confirmed. If recipients include UK/EU contacts, GDPR/PECR rules (consent or soft opt-in for sole traders, legitimate interest for corporate subscribers) apply in addition to CAN-SPAM, and the strictest regime controls.
- **Targeting model inputs** — The model uses risk/fraud scores. Whether those scores or their inputs constitute or proxy special-category data under GDPR Art. 9 has not been audited. If they do, legitimate interest alone cannot support the marketing use.
- **Customer contract terms** — Whether Mosaic Relay's platform agreements restrict secondary use of merchant/admin data for Mosaic's own marketing is unknown. If restricted, a contract amendment or platform agreement is needed.
- **Recipient subscriber type** — Whether recipients are corporate subscribers (incorporated businesses) or include sole traders/unincorporated partnerships. This determines whether PECR's corporate-subscriber exemption applies or whether consent/soft opt-in is required.
- **Retention periods** — No retention policy exists for targeting-segment data, suppression lists, or engagement data. Retention periods must be defined before launch.

## Likely rules and issues
### US CAN-SPAM Act (15 U.S.C. § 7701 et seq.)
CAN-SPAM applies to "commercial electronic mail messages" — any email whose primary purpose is commercial advertisement or promotion of a commercial product or service. The targeted product emails described here (recommending dispute tools, promoting faster onboarding) are commercial messages under CAN-SPAM, even though they are sent to existing customers and even though they relate to the recipient's own account activity.
**Key CAN-SPAM requirements for commercial email to business contacts:**
1. **No false or misleading header information.** The "From," "To," "Reply-To," and routing information must be accurate and identify Mosaic Relay as the sender.
2. **No deceptive subject lines.** The subject line must accurately reflect the content of the message.
3. **Identification as an advertisement.** The message must be identified as an advertisement or solicitation. The FTC has clarified that this can be done in the body of the message and does not require "ADV:" in the subject line.
4. **Valid physical postal address.** The message must include Mosaic Relay's valid physical postal address (street address, PO box, or private mailbox).
5. **Clear and conspicuous opt-out mechanism.** The message must provide a clear and conspicuous explanation of how to opt out of receiving future commercial email, and a functioning return email address or other Internet-based mechanism (such as a link) that the recipient can use to opt out. The opt-out mechanism must remain functional for at least 30 days after the email is sent.
6. **Honor opt-out requests promptly.** Opt-out requests must be honored within **10 business days**. Mosaic Relay cannot charge a fee, require the recipient to provide any information beyond an email address, or require any step beyond sending a reply email or visiting a single web page to opt out.
7. **No sale or transfer of opted-out addresses.** Once a recipient opts out, Mosaic Relay cannot sell or transfer their email address (except to a third party helping with CAN-SPAM compliance).
8. **Monitor third-party compliance.** If Mosaic Relay uses an email service provider or marketing vendor, Mosaic Relay remains responsible for CAN-SPAM compliance and cannot contract away that responsibility.
**Business-to-business email is not exempt.** CAN-SPAM applies equally to B2B and B2C commercial email. There is no general B2B exemption under CAN-SPAM (unlike some other regimes, such as PECR's corporate-subscriber exemption).
**Penalties.** CAN-SPAM violations can result in civil penalties of up to $50,120 per violation (adjusted for inflation as of 2024), and aggravated violations can lead to additional penalties. State attorneys general and the FTC enforce CAN-SPAM.
### Message classification: transactional vs. commercial
CAN-SPAM distinguishes between "transactional or relationship messages" (which are exempt from most CAN-SPAM requirements) and "commercial messages" (which are subject to all requirements). A transactional or relationship message is one whose primary purpose is to facilitate, complete, or confirm a commercial transaction that the recipient has previously agreed to, or to provide information about an existing account or relationship.
The targeted product emails described here — recommending dispute tools, promoting faster onboarding — are **commercial messages**, not transactional messages, because their primary purpose is to promote Mosaic Relay's products and features, not to facilitate or confirm a transaction the recipient initiated. Even though they are triggered by account activity (rising chargebacks, incomplete verification), the primary purpose is promotional.
**Event-triggered emails** (sent after a transaction or account review) must be classified by their primary purpose. If the primary purpose is to promote a product or feature, they are commercial messages subject to CAN-SPAM, even if they also contain account-related information. The FTC uses a "primary purpose" test that considers the subject line, the content, and the placement of commercial vs. transactional content.
### Interaction with other regimes
If recipients include UK or EU contacts, GDPR/UK GDPR and PECR apply in addition to CAN-SPAM. The working recommendation already addresses those regimes (legitimate interest for corporate subscribers, consent or soft opt-in for sole traders). CAN-SPAM is the baseline for US recipients, but the strictest applicable regime controls for each recipient.

## Viable paths
### Path A — Proceed with CAN-SPAM compliance for US recipients (primary path for US-only campaign)
If the campaign is limited to US recipients, Mosaic Relay may proceed under CAN-SPAM by:
1. **Building a working unsubscribe mechanism** in every email (a link or reply-to address that remains functional for 30 days).
2. **Honoring opt-outs within 10 business days** and maintaining a suppression list so opted-out recipients are not re-contacted.
3. **Including Mosaic Relay's valid physical postal address** in every email.
4. **Identifying the message as an advertisement** (can be done in the body).
5. **Using accurate header information and non-deceptive subject lines.**
6. **Monitoring any third-party email service providers** for compliance.
CAN-SPAM does not require prior consent for commercial email to business contacts, so Mosaic Relay can send the emails without opt-in consent, as long as the above requirements are met.
### Path B — Multi-jurisdictional compliance (if UK/EU recipients are included)
If recipients include UK or EU contacts, Mosaic Relay must comply with GDPR/UK GDPR and PECR in addition to CAN-SPAM. The working recommendation already outlines this path:
- **Legitimate interest** (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)) for corporate subscribers, documented in a legitimate interest assessment (LIA).
- **PECR corporate-subscriber exemption** for incorporated businesses (no prior consent needed).
- **Consent or soft opt-in** for sole traders (but soft opt-in is likely unavailable because no opt-out was offered at collection).
- **GDPR Art. 21(2) right to object** — recipients have an absolute right to object to direct marketing, and Mosaic Relay must honor objections immediately.
### Path C — Consent-based approach (target state)
Obtain explicit opt-in consent via a preference center or account settings for all recipients. This is the most conservative approach and future-proofs the program against regulatory changes, but it is not required by CAN-SPAM for US business contacts.

## Facts that could change the answer
1. **Recipient jurisdiction mix.** If recipients are US-only, CAN-SPAM is the primary regime and no prior consent is required. If UK/EU recipients are included, GDPR/PECR apply and consent or legitimate interest is required. This is the single most important missing fact.
2. **Recipient subscriber type.** If all recipients are corporate subscribers (incorporated businesses), PECR's corporate-subscriber exemption applies and no prior consent is needed under UK law. If any recipients are sole traders, consent or soft opt-in is required, and soft opt-in is likely unavailable.
3. **Targeting model inputs.** If the fraud-risk scores or other inputs constitute or proxy special-category data under GDPR Art. 9, legitimate interest alone cannot support the marketing use, and explicit consent would be required. This could block the campaign for UK/EU recipients.
4. **Customer contract terms.** If Mosaic Relay's platform agreements restrict secondary use of merchant/admin data for Mosaic's own marketing, Mosaic Relay cannot proceed without a contract amendment or the platform's agreement, regardless of CAN-SPAM compliance.
5. **Message content and primary purpose.** If the event-triggered emails are primarily transactional (e.g., "Your dispute rate has increased; here is how to resolve it") rather than primarily promotional (e.g., "Try our new dispute resolution tool"), they may be classified as transactional messages exempt from most CAN-SPAM requirements. The classification depends on the actual content and subject lines.

## Recommended last-mile verification
Before launch, counsel should:
1. **Confirm the recipient jurisdiction mix** with the product and marketing teams. Segment the recipient list by jurisdiction and apply the strictest applicable regime to each segment.
2. **Audit the targeting model inputs** to determine whether fraud-risk scores or any input fields constitute or proxy special-category data under GDPR Art. 9. If they do, exclude UK/EU recipients or obtain explicit consent.
3. **Review customer contracts and platform terms** for restrictions on secondary use of merchant/admin data for Mosaic's own marketing. If restricted, obtain a contract amendment or the platform's agreement.
4. **Segment the recipient list by subscriber type** (corporate vs. sole trader). Exclude or separately handle sole traders until their status is confirmed.
5. **Draft and test the unsubscribe mechanism** to ensure it is clear, conspicuous, and functional for at least 30 days after each email is sent.
6. **Build the suppression list** and test the process for honoring opt-outs within 10 business days.
7. **Draft the privacy notice disclosure** of the marketing use and lawful basis.
8. **Define retention periods** for targeting-segment data, suppression lists, and engagement data.
9. **Document a legitimate interest assessment (LIA)** if relying on GDPR Art. 6(1)(f) for UK/EU recipients.
10. **Classify each email template** as transactional or commercial using the FTC's primary-purpose test, and apply CAN-SPAM requirements to all commercial messages.
