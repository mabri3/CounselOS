# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

AML monitoring gap from failed rules-engine migration; suspected but unconfirmed illicit activity during the gap; Series C data room disclosure and remediation obligations.

## Decision question

Does the 5.5-month monitoring gap from the failed rules-engine migration (structuring detection, rapid funds movement, dormant-account reactivation) need to be disclosed in the Series C data room, and what are the AML/regulatory, bank-partner, and seller-communication obligations around Marcus's proposed remediation plan?

## Material facts

- On March 14, during cutover to a new rules engine, three of nine monitoring rules were not ported: structuring-pattern detection, rapid movement of funds (in-and-out within 24h), and dormant-account reactivation.
- The monitoring gap ran from March 14 to September 2, approximately 5.5 months, covering roughly 2.1M transactions and ~48k accounts.
- The gap was not caught until a seller escalation last week (early September 2026).
- Marcus's plan: ship the fix tonight (already in review), backfill the three rules across the gap period this weekend, and auto-close anything the fraud model already scored as low risk.
- Marcus proposes holding off telling the bank partner until clean numbers are available, approximately 3 weeks.
- Marcus wants to email affected sellers apologizing for 'a delay in reviewing some transactions.'
- A postmortem was written in the engineering Confluence space and a timeline posted in #eng-general.
- Series C diligence opens in 6 weeks; CFO is asking whether this goes in the data room.
