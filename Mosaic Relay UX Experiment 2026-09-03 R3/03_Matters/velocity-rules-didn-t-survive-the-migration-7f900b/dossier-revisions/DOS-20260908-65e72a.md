# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Does the 5.5-month monitoring gap from the failed rules-engine migration (structuring detection, rapid funds movement, dormant-account reactivation) need to be disclosed in the Series C data room, and what are the AML/regulatory, bank-partner, and seller-communication obligations around Marcus's proposed remediation plan?

## Decision question

Does the 5.5-month monitoring gap from the failed rules-engine migration (structuring detection, rapid funds movement, dormant-account reactivation) need to be disclosed in the Series C data room, and what are the AML/regulatory, bank-partner, and seller-communication obligations around Marcus's proposed remediation plan?

## Material facts

- On March 14, during cutover to a new rules engine, three of nine monitoring rules were not ported: structuring-pattern detection, rapid movement of funds (in-and-out within 24h), and dormant-account reactivation.
- The monitoring gap ran from March 14 to September 2, approximately 5.5 months, covering roughly 2.1M transactions and ~48k accounts.
- The gap was not caught until a seller escalation last week (early September 2026).
- Marcus's plan: ship the fix tonight (already in review), backfill the three rules across the gap period this weekend, and auto-close anything the fraud model already scored as low risk.
- Marcus proposes holding off telling the bank partner until clean numbers are available, approximately 3 weeks.
- Marcus wants to email affected sellers apologizing for 'a delay in reviewing some transactions.'
- A postmortem was written in the engineering Confluence space and a timeline posted in #eng-general.
- Series C diligence opens in 6 weeks; CFO is asking whether this goes in the data room.
- Did any actual fraud, structuring, or sanctions activity occur during the gap period that went undetected (or is it still unknown)? — Suspected but not confirmed

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay is subject to BSA/AML obligations as a money services business / payment facilitator (not a bank) and has contractual AML obligations to its acquiring bank partner.
- The three dark rules are AML/transaction-monitoring controls whose absence created a compliance gap, not merely an operational one.
- The bank partner has contractual or regulatory expectations around timely notification of monitoring failures.

## Issues and workstreams

- BSA/AML program adequacy: whether the 5.5-month monitoring gap constitutes a failure of the AML monitoring program and what remediation/backfill obligations follow
- Regulatory notification: whether the gap triggers any reporting/notification obligation to regulators or the bank partner
- Data room / diligence disclosure: whether the incident and remediation must be disclosed in the Series C data room
- Seller communications: whether the proposed apology email to affected sellers raises misrepresentation or consumer-protection concerns
- Auto-close of low-risk alerts: whether auto-closing backfilled alerts that the fraud model scored low-risk is defensible given the monitoring gap

## Open questions

- Whether the bank partner agreement or any regulatory framework imposes a specific notification deadline for monitoring failures
- Whether any regulator or the bank partner has already been notified
- Whether the fraud model's low-risk scores during the gap are reliable enough to support auto-closing backfilled alerts
- Whether Mosaic Relay has a documented AML program and who owns it
- Whether the seller escalation that surfaced the gap involved actual fraud or loss
