# What are the US BSA/AML program-adequacy expectations for a payment

## Question

What are the US BSA/AML program-adequacy expectations for a payment facilitator's transaction-monitoring controls?

**No external authority retrieved.**
## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment facilitator, experienced a 5.5-month gap in three critical AML transaction-monitoring rules (structuring detection, rapid funds movement, dormant-account reactivation) following a March 14 rules-engine migration. The gap affected ~2.1M transactions across ~48k accounts and was not detected until a seller escalation in early September. Marcus (PM) proposes shipping the fix immediately, backfilling the rules this weekend, auto-closing low-risk alerts, delaying bank-partner notification for ~3 weeks, and sending a vague apology to sellers. Series C diligence opens in 6 weeks, and the CFO is asking whether this goes in the data room.
## Decision question
Should Mosaic Relay disclose the 5.5-month AML monitoring gap in the Series C data room, and what immediate changes are required to Marcus's remediation plan (auto-close logic, bank notification timing, seller communications) to satisfy BSA/AML program-adequacy expectations and avoid regulatory or contractual breach?
## Likely answer
**Yes, this must be disclosed in the data room, and Marcus's plan requires material modification before execution.** A 5.5-month failure of three core transaction-monitoring controls is a reportable event under standard bank-partner agreements and creates potential BSA/AML program deficiencies that sophisticated investors will expect to see documented. The proposed 3-week delay in bank notification likely violates contractual prompt-notice requirements, and auto-closing backfilled alerts without human review creates unacceptable regulatory risk given that the fraud model itself may have been compromised by the monitoring gap.
**Conditional alternative:** If the bank-partner agreement is verified to have no notification requirement (unlikely) and if the fraud model's low-risk scoring is independently validated as unaffected by the monitoring gap, the auto-close and delayed-notification paths might be defensible. However, the data-room disclosure obligation remains regardless.
## Likely rules and issues
**BSA/AML Program Adequacy (31 U.S.C. § 5318(h); 31 C.F.R. § 1022.210):**
Payment facilitators operating as money services businesses must maintain a written AML program with internal controls reasonably designed to assure compliance. A 5.5-month failure of structuring detection, rapid-movement monitoring, and dormant-account reactivation controls creates a presumptive program deficiency. The three dark rules map directly to core BSA red flags (31 C.F.R. § 1022.210(b)(5) requires monitoring for structuring and suspicious activity).
**Bank-Partner Contractual Obligations:**
Acquiring banks typically impose strict AML monitoring and prompt-notification requirements on payment facilitators. A 3-week delay in notification likely breaches "prompt" or "immediate" notice clauses. The bank may also have independent regulatory obligations (e.g., OCC, Fed, or state banking requirements) that require it to report the facilitator's failure.
**SAR/CTR Backfiling Risk:**
If the backfill reveals structuring or suspicious activity that should have been reported during the gap, Mosaic Relay faces potential civil money penalties for late SAR filings (31 U.S.C. § 5321). The auto-close plan compounds this risk by potentially suppressing alerts that require human review.
**Securities Disclosure (Series C):**
Material weaknesses in AML controls are disclosable events in private placements. Sophisticated investors (and their counsel) will treat a 5.5-month monitoring gap as a material regulatory risk factor. Failure to disclose could support securities-fraud claims under Rule 10b-5 if the gap later becomes public or triggers enforcement.
## Viable paths
**Path 1: Immediate full disclosure and conservative remediation (Recommended)**
- Notify bank partner within 24-48 hours with preliminary scope, not "clean numbers"
- Engage outside AML counsel to oversee backfill and determine SAR backfiling obligations
- Do not auto-close any backfilled alerts; triage all through human review (even if outsourced)
- Prepare detailed data-room disclosure including root-cause analysis, remediation timeline, and regulatory-risk assessment
- Delay seller communications until legal review; do not characterize as "delay in reviewing transactions"
**Path 2: Modified Marcus plan (Higher risk)**
- Ship fix immediately, but delay backfill until counsel reviews auto-close methodology
- Notify bank partner within 1 week with preliminary findings
- Auto-close only alerts where fraud model scoring is independently validated as unaffected by the monitoring gap
- Disclose in data room with heavy qualification
**Path 3: Status quo (Not recommended)**
- Follow Marcus's plan as proposed
- High risk of bank-partner breach, regulatory enforcement, and securities-disclosure liability
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Bank-partner agreement terms:** If the agreement lacks prompt-notice requirements or explicitly allows delayed notification for "operational issues," Path 2 becomes more viable.
- **Fraud model independence:** If the fraud model operates on entirely separate data feeds and rules from the three dark monitoring rules, auto-close may be defensible for low-risk alerts.
- **Actual illicit activity:** If the backfill reveals no structuring or suspicious activity (unlikely given ~2.1M transactions), the regulatory severity decreases, but the program-adequacy failure remains.
- **Prior regulatory exams:** If Mosaic Relay recently passed a BSA/AML exam with no findings, the gap may be characterized as an isolated operational failure rather than a systemic program deficiency.
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Pull the bank-partner agreement** and identify all notification, audit, and AML-monitoring clauses. Look for "prompt," "immediate," or "within X days" requirements.
2. **Engage outside AML counsel** to review the backfill methodology and determine whether any SARs must be backfiled. Do not rely on internal fraud-model scoring alone.
3. **Document the fraud model's independence** from the three dark rules. If the model uses any of the same data feeds or logic, auto-close is not defensible.
4. **Prepare a data-room disclosure memo** that frames the gap as a one-time operational failure with immediate remediation, not a systemic program weakness. Include the root-cause analysis and the "lessons learned" from the postmortem.
5. **Review the seller-communication draft** with counsel. "Delay in reviewing transactions" is misleading; the accurate characterization is "temporary suspension of certain monitoring controls."
## Remaining gaps

- **Fraud model architecture:** Whether the model's low-risk scoring is truly independent of the three dark rules.
- **SAR backfiling scope:** Whether any transactions during the gap period triggered SAR filing obligations.
- **Regulatory exam history:** Whether Mosaic Relay has been examined for BSA/AML compliance in the past 2 years.
