# What are the bank-partner notification obligations for a monitoring failure

## Question

What are the bank-partner notification obligations for a monitoring failure under US AML frameworks?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

**No external authority retrieved.**
## Likely answer
Under US AML frameworks, Mosaic Relay likely has **no direct statutory duty to notify its bank partner of a monitoring failure**—the Bank Secrecy Act does not require MSBs to report program deficiencies to counterparties. However, the **bank partner agreement almost certainly imposes contractual notification obligations** (typically 24–72 hours for material control failures), and the 5.5-month gap likely qualifies as a reportable event under standard sponsor-bank MSB agreements. Marcus's plan to wait 3 weeks for "clean numbers" likely breaches that contractual duty and creates regulatory risk if the bank discovers the gap independently.
**Conditional alternative:** If the bank agreement is silent on monitoring failures (uncommon but possible), Mosaic Relay still faces practical pressure to disclose promptly because the bank will likely discover the gap during routine audits or when backfilled alerts generate SAR filings, and delayed disclosure damages the relationship more than the underlying failure.
---
## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment facilitator operating as an MSB under BSA/AML obligations, experienced a 5.5-month monitoring gap (March 14–September 2) when three transaction-monitoring rules failed to port during a rules-engine migration. The gap affected ~2.1M transactions across ~48k accounts and went undetected until a seller escalation. Marcus (PM, Risk Platform) proposes shipping a fix, backfilling alerts, auto-closing low-risk items, delaying bank notification for 3 weeks, and sending a vague apology to sellers. Counsel is involved because the CFO needs to know whether this goes in the Series C data room (opening in 6 weeks) and whether the remediation plan creates legal exposure.

Should Mosaic Relay notify its bank partner of the 5.5-month AML monitoring gap now (before backfill completes), wait for Marcus's proposed 3-week timeline, or follow a different notification schedule—and does the answer change if the bank agreement lacks an explicit monitoring-failure clause?

## Likely rules and issues
### 1. BSA/AML program requirements (31 U.S.C. § 5318(h); 31 C.F.R. § 1022.210)
MSBs must maintain a written AML program with internal controls, including transaction monitoring. A 5.5-month gap in three core monitoring rules (structuring detection, rapid funds movement, dormant-account reactivation) is a **material program deficiency**. FinCEN and bank regulators expect MSBs to detect and remediate control failures promptly. The gap itself is not automatically a violation, but failure to remediate and document the response can support a finding that the program was not "reasonably designed" to prevent money laundering.
**Key issue:** Whether Mosaic Relay's overall AML program remained adequate during the gap (e.g., did other controls compensate?) and whether the remediation plan is defensible.
### 2. SAR filing obligations (31 C.F.R. § 1022.320)
MSBs must file SARs for transactions they "know, suspect, or have reason to suspect" involve illicit activity. The monitoring gap means Mosaic Relay may have missed structuring or other suspicious patterns. Backfilling alerts is the right step, but **auto-closing low-risk alerts without human review is risky**—the fraud model's scoring during a monitoring gap may not satisfy the "reason to suspect" standard for AML purposes.
**Key issue:** Whether any backfilled alerts require SAR filings, and whether the auto-close logic is defensible if regulators later question why alerts were closed without review.
### 3. Bank partner notification (contractual, not statutory)
Sponsor banks typically impose strict notification requirements on MSB partners for material control failures, often within 24–72 hours of discovery. The agreement likely requires notice of:
- Material deficiencies in the AML program
- Events that could affect the bank's own BSA/AML compliance
- Regulatory inquiries or enforcement risk
Marcus's plan to wait 3 weeks for "clean numbers" likely violates this duty. Banks view delayed notification as a breach of trust and may terminate the relationship or report the MSB to FinCEN.
**Key issue:** The exact notification deadline and scope in the bank agreement (currently unknown—needs immediate review).
### 4. Series C data room disclosure
Material AML program deficiencies and potential regulatory exposure are **standard data room items**. Investors will ask about compliance controls, and a 5.5-month monitoring gap affecting 2.1M transactions is material. Failure to disclose could support a securities fraud claim if the gap later results in enforcement or bank termination.
**Key issue:** How to frame the disclosure (remediated control gap vs. ongoing risk) and whether to include the postmortem and remediation plan.
### 5. Seller communication
Marcus's proposed apology for "a delay in reviewing some transactions" is **too vague and potentially misleading**. If sellers were affected by the monitoring gap (e.g., delayed payouts, account holds), the communication should be accurate but not alarmist. If no sellers were actually harmed, the apology may create unnecessary concern.
**Key issue:** Whether any sellers were actually harmed and what the communication should say.

## Viable paths
### Path A: Notify the bank partner now (recommended)
**Action:** Notify the bank partner within 24–48 hours of discovering the gap, even before backfill completes. Provide a high-level summary of the gap, the remediation plan, and a commitment to follow up with detailed findings.
**Pros:**
- Likely complies with contractual notification duty
- Preserves trust with the bank
- Reduces risk of the bank discovering the gap independently and terminating the relationship
- Positions Mosaic Relay as proactive and transparent
**Cons:**
- Requires disclosure before all facts are known
- May trigger bank scrutiny or audit
**When to use:** If the bank agreement imposes a notification deadline (likely) or if the relationship is strategically important.
### Path B: Wait for "clean numbers" (Marcus's plan)
**Action:** Delay notification for ~3 weeks until backfill and analysis are complete.
**Pros:**
- Allows Mosaic Relay to present a complete picture
- Avoids alarming the bank with incomplete information
**Cons:**
- Likely breaches contractual notification duty
- Risks the bank discovering the gap independently (e.g., through SAR filings or audit)
- Damages trust if the bank feels Mosaic Relay withheld information
- May violate the bank's own BSA/AML obligations if the bank is required to monitor its MSB partners
**When to use:** Only if the bank agreement explicitly allows delayed notification or if there is no notification clause (unlikely).
### Path C: Phased notification
**Action:** Notify the bank immediately of the gap and remediation plan, then provide a detailed follow-up report after backfill completes.
**Pros:**
- Balances transparency with completeness
- Likely satisfies contractual duty while allowing time for analysis
**Cons:**
- Requires two communications
- May still trigger bank scrutiny
**When to use:** If the bank agreement requires prompt notification but Mosaic Relay wants to provide a complete analysis.

## Facts that could change the answer
1. **Bank agreement notification clause:** If the agreement imposes a 24–72 hour deadline, Marcus's 3-week delay is a clear breach. If the agreement is silent, Mosaic Relay has more flexibility but still faces practical pressure.
2. **Actual fraud or structuring during the gap:** If backfill reveals actual illicit activity, Mosaic Relay may need to file SARs and notify FinCEN, which increases the urgency of bank notification.
3. **Bank's own BSA/AML obligations:** If the bank is required to monitor its MSB partners and report deficiencies to FinCEN, delayed notification could expose the bank to regulatory risk, which will damage the relationship.
4. **Series C diligence timeline:** If diligence opens in 6 weeks, Mosaic Relay needs to resolve the bank notification and remediation before then to avoid a data room surprise.
