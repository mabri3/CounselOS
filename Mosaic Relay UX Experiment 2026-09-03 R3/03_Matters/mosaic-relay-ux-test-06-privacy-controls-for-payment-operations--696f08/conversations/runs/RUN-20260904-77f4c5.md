    # Mosaic Relay — Privacy Controls for Payment Operations Data

    ## Recommendation (working recommendation, not a recorded decision)

    Do not launch the unified dashboard until Mosaic Relay documents a **data map**,
    a **role-based access matrix**, **export controls**, and a **controller/processor
    allocation per data flow and vendor**. Default to the least data needed for reconciliation
    or investigation; mask identity, KYC/KYB, fraud, dispute, support, and payout
    fields unless the role and purpose require them. Log searches, views, exports,
    and administrative changes. Require a reason, narrow date range, and approval
    for bulk exports, with rate limits and alerts.

    ## Controller and processor roles

    Treat the **customer platform as controller** for its own end-user data where
    it determines the purposes and means of processing. Treat **Mosaic Relay as processor**
    for customer-directed dashboard processing, subject to a data processing agreement
    and documented instructions. Mosaic Relay may be an **independent controller**
    for its own fraud prevention, security, legal, and compliance duties only to the
    extent required by applicable law. Confirm the allocation per data flow and per
    vendor (identity, fraud, acquiring, processing, cloud).

    ## Privacy notices

    Update customer-facing and individual-facing notices to cover: what is collected,
    sources, purposes, data categories, recipients and vendors, retention, transfers,
    profiling, individual rights, and complaint routes. Do not use dashboard data
    for customer marketing unless a separate purpose, lawful basis, notice, and permission
    support it.

    ## Lawful bases

    Select and document a lawful basis per purpose and jurisdiction:

    - **Contract** — requested payment services.

    - **Legal obligation** — AML, sanctions, accounting, and dispute duties.

    - **Legitimate interests** — security and fraud prevention, after a necessity
    and balancing review.

    - **Consent** should not be the default for core service processing.

    Separate sensitive identity/KYC data from general operations views, define field-level
    access, and prohibit unrestricted person-wide search.

    ## Data minimization, access, and export permissions

    Use purpose- and role-limited views. Mask sensitive fields by default. Require
    reason-coded, time-limited access for investigations. Restrict exports to selected
    fields and date ranges, require approval for bulk export, log every search/view/export,
    alert on unusual volume, and apply retention and deletion to exported copies.

    ## Automated decision requirements

    Inventory fraud scores and risk rules. Where output has **legal or similarly significant
    effects**, provide required notice and meaningful information, human review, challenge
    routes, and safeguards. Do not let a score alone deny service or freeze funds
    without documented review and escalation.

    ## Retention and deletion

    Set purpose-specific retention schedules by record type. Keep legal, fraud, accounting,
    and support holds separate; suspend deletion only for a documented exception and
    release it when the reason ends. Define deletion, correction, and access workflows
    across Mosaic Relay and its vendors.

    ## Cross-border transfers

    Confirm countries, hosting locations, sub-processors, transfer mechanisms, and
    supplementary safeguards before EU/UK data moves cross-border.

    ## Restrictions on secondary use

    Do not permit customer marketing reuse of dashboard data without a separate purpose,
    notice, and lawful basis.

    ---

    ## What would change this

    - **Working assumption — jurisdictions unknown.** The analysis is jurisdiction-agnostic.
    If the dashboard is US-only, GDPR/UK GDPR, Art. 22, and cross-border transfer
    rules drop out (state privacy laws, FCRA adverse-action, and state AI laws become
    the leads). If UK/EU, those regimes apply in full. The answer changes materially
    either way.

    - **Working assumption — customer is controller / Mosaic Relay is processor.**
    If a customer platform does not actually determine purposes and means (e.g., Mosaic
    Relay''s own compliance duties dominate a flow), the allocation shifts and Mosaic
    Relay becomes independent controller for that flow.

    - **Open fork — automated decisions.** If fraud scores can deny service or freeze
    funds without human review, Art. 22 / FCRA-style safeguards are required; if scores
    are advisory only, they are not. This is unresolved.

    - **Open fork — secondary use.** Whether customers may use dashboard data for
    their own marketing determines whether a separate purpose, notice, and lawful
    basis are needed. Unresolved.

    - **Not examined — vendor contracts.** The identity, fraud, acquiring, processing,
    and cloud vendor agreements were not supplied or reviewed; controller/processor
    allocation, sub-processor lists, and transfer mechanisms within them are unverified.

    - **Not examined — retention schedules and deletion exceptions.** No retention
    schedule or deletion-hold policy was supplied; the specific schedules and exceptions
    are unverified.

    and controls above are **generated analysis** based on those facts. No external

  # Mosaic Relay — Privacy Controls for Payment Operations Data

  ## Recommendation (working recommendation, not a recorded decision)

  Do not launch the unified dashboard until Mosaic Relay documents a **data map**,
  a **role-based access matrix**, **export controls**, and a **controller/processor
  allocation per data flow and vendor**. Default to the least data needed for reconciliation
  or investigation; mask identity, KYC/KYB, fraud, dispute, support, and payout fields
  unless the role and purpose require them. Log searches, views, exports, and administrative
  changes. Require a reason, narrow date range, and approval for bulk exports, with
  rate limits and alerts.

  ## Controller and processor roles

  Treat the **customer platform as controller** for its own end-user data where it
  determines the purposes and means of processing. Treat **Mosaic Relay as processor**
  for customer-directed dashboard processing, subject to a data processing agreement
  and documented instructions. Mosaic Relay may be an **independent controller** for
  its own fraud prevention, security, legal, and compliance duties only to the extent
  required by applicable law. Confirm the allocation per data flow and per vendor
  (identity, fraud, acquiring, processing, cloud).

  ## Privacy notices

  Update customer-facing and individual-facing notices to cover: what is collected,
  sources, purposes, data categories, recipients and vendors, retention, transfers,
  profiling, individual rights, and complaint routes. Do not use dashboard data for
  customer marketing unless a separate purpose, lawful basis, notice, and permission
  support it.

  ## Lawful bases

  Select and document a lawful basis per purpose and jurisdiction:

  - **Contract** — requested payment services.

  - **Legal obligation** — AML, sanctions, accounting, and dispute duties.

  - **Legitimate interests** — security and fraud prevention, after a necessity and
  balancing review.

  - **Consent** should not be the default for core service processing.

  Separate sensitive identity/KYC data from general operations views, define field-level
  access, and prohibit unrestricted person-wide search.

  ## Data minimization, access, and export permissions

  Use purpose- and role-limited views. Mask sensitive fields by default. Require reason-coded,
  time-limited access for investigations. Restrict exports to selected fields and
  date ranges, require approval for bulk export, log every search/view/export, alert
  on unusual volume, and apply retention and deletion to exported copies.

  ## Automated decision requirements

  Inventory fraud scores and risk rules. Where output has **legal or similarly significant
  effects**, provide required notice and meaningful information, human review, challenge
  routes, and safeguards. Do not let a score alone deny service or freeze funds without
  documented review and escalation.

  ## Retention and deletion

  Set purpose-specific retention schedules by record type. Keep legal, fraud, accounting,
  and support holds separate; suspend deletion only for a documented exception and
  release it when the reason ends. Define deletion, correction, and access workflows
  across Mosaic Relay and its vendors.

  ## Cross-border transfers

  Confirm countries, hosting locations, sub-processors, transfer mechanisms, and supplementary
  safeguards before EU/UK data moves cross-border.

  ## Restrictions on secondary use

  Do not permit customer marketing reuse of dashboard data without a separate purpose,
  notice, and lawful basis.
