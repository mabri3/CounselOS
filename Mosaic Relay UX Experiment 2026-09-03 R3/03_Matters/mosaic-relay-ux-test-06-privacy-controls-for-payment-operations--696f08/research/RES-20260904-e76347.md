# Which jurisdictions will the dashboard launch in, and where do

## Question

Which jurisdictions will the dashboard launch in, and where do the underlying data subjects reside?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure provider, wants to build a unified operations dashboard that combines transaction details, identity-verification results, KYB/KYC data, fraud scores, dispute records, support notes, and payout information. Authorized customer users would search by person, business, payment, or recipient and export selected records for reconciliation and investigations. Counsel is involved because the design may expose more personal information than each user needs, and the company must determine controller/processor roles, lawful bases, and cross-border transfer rules before launch. The dashboard would display data throughout the customer relationship and retain it for legal, fraud, accounting, and support purposes.
## Decision question
Which specific countries will the dashboard launch in, and where do the underlying data subjects (payers, recipients, merchants, sellers) reside, given that this determines whether GDPR/UK GDPR applies, what transfer mechanisms are required for cross-border data flows, and whether Mosaic Relay acts as a processor or joint controller with its customer platforms?
## Open questions
- **Countries of users and data subjects**: The request identifies this as a missing fact. This determines whether GDPR, UK GDPR, or other regimes apply, and drives the Article 22 (automated decision-making) and transfer analysis.
- **Role-based access rules**: Who within customer organizations (administrators vs. operations users) and Mosaic Relay (support vs. compliance) can access which data categories, and under what approval workflows?
- **Export controls**: What technical and contractual restrictions will limit bulk exports, and how will Mosaic Relay prevent customers from using exported data for unauthorized secondary purposes like marketing?
- **Vendor contracts and data flows**: Do existing contracts with identity, fraud, acquiring, processing, and cloud vendors permit the data combination and cross-border transfers contemplated by the dashboard?
- **Retention schedules and deletion exceptions**: What are the specific retention periods for each data category (transaction, KYC, fraud, dispute, support, payout), and what legal holds or fraud-prevention exceptions apply?
---
## Likely rules and issues
Based on the matter facts and general privacy law principles applicable to payment infrastructure:
**Controller/Processor Allocation**: Mosaic Relay likely acts as a **processor** when providing the dashboard tool to customer platforms (who are controllers of their end-user data), but may be an **independent controller** for its own compliance obligations (BSA/AML, sanctions screening, fraud prevention). This dual role requires careful contractual allocation and transparency.
**Lawful Bases**:
- **Contractual necessity** (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) for transaction processing and payout functions
- **Legal obligation** (Art. 6(1)(c)) for KYB/KYC, sanctions screening, and AML compliance
- **Legitimate interests** (Art. 6(1)(f)) for fraud monitoring and security, balanced against data subject rights
- **Consent** likely required for any secondary marketing use by customers
**Data Minimization**: The unified dashboard creates tension with GDPR Article 5(1)(c) (data minimization) and Article 25 (data protection by design). Combining identity, fraud, dispute, and payout data in one interface risks exceeding what individual users need for their specific tasks.
**Automated Decision-Making**: If fraud scores or risk logic drive decisions with legal or similarly significant effects (e.g., blocking payouts, terminating accounts), GDPR Article 22 requires:
- Transparency about the logic involved
- Human review mechanisms
- Right to challenge the decision
**Cross-Border Transfers**: If data subjects are in the EU/UK and data is accessed from or stored in the US or other non-adequate jurisdictions, Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) or other transfer mechanisms are required, plus supplementary safeguards given the sensitive nature of financial and identity data.
**Retention and Deletion**: Different data categories have different retention requirements:
- Transaction records: typically 5-7 years for accounting/tax
- KYC/KYB data: varies by jurisdiction (often 5 years post-relationship for AML)
- Fraud scores: may need shorter retention to avoid indefinite profiling
- Support notes: business-driven, but subject to data subject rights
**Secondary Use Restrictions**: Customer platforms may want to use dashboard data for their own marketing or analytics. This requires:
- Separate lawful basis (likely consent)
- Clear notice to data subjects
- Contractual restrictions in Mosaic Relay's terms of service
- Technical controls to prevent unauthorized exports

## Viable paths
**Path 1: Phased launch with jurisdiction-specific controls**
- Launch first in the US with state-law compliance (CCPA/CPRA, state money transmission laws)
- Delay EU/UK launch until transfer mechanisms, Article 22 safeguards, and DPIA are complete
- Implement role-based access controls and export restrictions from day one
- **Trade-off**: Slower time-to-market in EU/UK, but lower regulatory risk
**Path 2: Global launch with maximum safeguards**
- Build GDPR/UK GDPR compliance into the core product (data minimization, purpose limitation, Article 22 safeguards)
- Use SCCs for all cross-border transfers, with supplementary encryption and access controls
- Conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) before launch
- **Trade-off**: Higher upfront engineering and legal cost, but faster global scalability
**Path 3: Customer-hosted or regional data segregation**
- Offer customers the option to host the dashboard in their own environment or in specific regions (EU data stays in EU)
- Mosaic Relay acts purely as a software provider, reducing its controller/processor obligations
- **Trade-off**: Higher infrastructure cost and complexity, but strongest data sovereignty posture

## Facts that could change the answer
- **If data subjects are primarily in the US**: GDPR/UK GDPR analysis becomes moot; focus shifts to state privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA, VCDPA, etc.) and sectoral rules (GLBA if Mosaic Relay is deemed a financial institution).
- **If customers are banks or regulated financial institutions**: Mosaic Relay may be a service provider under GLBA, requiring different contractual and safeguard obligations.
- **If fraud scores are used solely for internal risk management and not shared with customers**: Article 22 analysis may be simplified, but transparency and security obligations remain.
- **If customers are prohibited from exporting data**: Data minimization and secondary use risks are significantly reduced, simplifying compliance.
- **If Mosaic Relay already has SCCs and a DPIA in place for existing services**: The incremental compliance burden for the dashboard may be lower than assumed.

## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Confirm launch jurisdictions and data subject locations**: Obtain a definitive list from Product and Sales. This is the single most important missing fact.
2. **Review existing vendor contracts**: Verify that identity, fraud, acquiring, processing, and cloud vendors permit the data combination and cross-border transfers contemplated by the dashboard. Look for data processing addendums (DPAs) and SCCs.
3. **Conduct a data mapping exercise**: Document each data element, its source, its purpose, who accesses it, where it's stored, and how long it's retained. This is a prerequisite for GDPR Article 30 (records of processing) and for identifying minimization opportunities.
4. **Draft or update privacy notices**: Ensure that Mosaic Relay's privacy notice and its customers' notices accurately describe the dashboard's data flows, purposes, and data subject rights.
5. **Implement technical controls**: Role-based access, field-level masking, export logging, and automated alerts for unusual access patterns should be built before launch, not retrofitted.
6. **Prepare a DPIA**: If EU/UK data subjects are involved, a DPIA is likely required under GDPR Article 35 given the systematic monitoring and large-scale processing of sensitive data.
