# State money transmitter and consumer-protection notice requirements for merchant

## Question

State money transmitter and consumer-protection notice requirements for merchant account suspension/termination

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure provider (not a bank), needs a standardized process to deactivate merchants whose activity triggers sanctions, fraud, chargeback, consumer-harm, or money-laundering concerns. The monitoring team proposes a flow that temporarily restricts new payments or payouts, preserves records, contacts the merchant when legally appropriate, and ends with either restored access or termination. Counsel is involved because the process must distinguish between mandatory sanctions blocking (which prohibits fund release without authorization) and discretionary risk-based restrictions (which require contractual and consumer-protection compliance), while a restriction may need to occur within minutes of an alert.
## Decision question
Should Mosaic Relay implement a dual-track deactivation process that immediately blocks funds and accounts for confirmed sanctions matches while using reversible, time-boxed restrictions for discretionary fraud or AML concerns, and what specific state money-transmitter and consumer-protection notice requirements must the process include to avoid wrongful closure claims and regulatory penalties when restricting merchant access to pending funds?
## Open questions
- What specific suspension, termination, notice, cure, and appeal rights are granted in Mosaic Relay’s existing merchant agreements, and do they vary by state or merchant type?
- Which specific states does Mosaic Relay operate in as a money transmitter (or through partner banks), and what are the exact notice timing requirements (e.g., 30 days vs. immediate) for account suspension in those jurisdictions?
- Does Mosaic Relay currently hold pending merchant funds in its own accounts or are funds held directly by acquiring partners, and what contractual rights exist to freeze or return those specific funds?
- Are there current law-enforcement requests or active SAR filings that would trigger tipping-off prohibitions and restrict Mosaic Relay’s ability to notify merchants of the reason for deactivation?
- What consumer-protection disclosures (e.g., Reg E error resolution rights, state escheatment laws) apply to end-user buyers when a merchant is deactivated mid-transaction?

## Likely rules and issues
**State Money Transmitter Laws (MTLs):**
Most states regulate entities that "receive money for transmission" or "hold funds incident to transmission." If Mosaic Relay holds merchant funds pending payout (even briefly), it likely needs money transmitter licenses in states where it operates. Key issues:
- **License requirement:** Operating without a license can result in civil penalties, cease-and-desist orders, and criminal liability.
- **Permissible investments:** States require that customer funds be held in specific permissible investments (cash, CDs, etc.) and segregated from operational funds.
- **Notice requirements:** Many states require specific advance notice (often 30 days) before suspending or terminating a customer’s access to funds, unless fraud or illegal activity is suspected. Some states require notice to the state regulator within a specific timeframe (e.g., 24–72 hours) when freezing accounts due to suspected money laundering.
- **Surety bonds:** Licensees must maintain bonds that may be forfeited if funds are wrongfully withheld.
**Consumer Protection Laws:**
- **Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA) / Regulation E:** If Mosaic Relay’s merchants are consumers (sole proprietors) or if the platform holds consumer funds, Reg E error resolution and timing requirements may apply to disputed transactions.
- **State Unfair and Deceptive Acts and Practices (UDAP):** Wrongful deactivation or failure to provide required notice can trigger UDAP claims, including statutory damages and attorney’s fees.
- **State escheatment laws:** Unclaimed funds from deactivated accounts must be handled according to state abandoned property laws, not retained indefinitely.
**Contract Law:**
The merchant agreement governs the right to suspend or terminate. Without clear contractual authority, Mosaic Relay risks breach of contract claims, conversion claims for withheld funds, and tortious interference claims if the deactivation disrupts the merchant’s relationships with its own customers.
**SAR Confidentiality (FinCEN):**
If a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) is filed, Mosaic Relay cannot disclose the existence of the SAR or the investigation to the merchant (tipping-off prohibition under 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(2)). This creates a tension with state notice requirements that demand explanation for account closure.
**OFAC Sanctions:**
Confirmed sanctions matches require immediate blocking (not just restriction) and reporting to OFAC within 10 days. Unlike discretionary risk-based closures, sanctions blocks cannot be lifted without OFAC authorization, and funds cannot be returned to the merchant.
## Viable paths
**Path A: Dual-Track Process (Recommended)**
1. **Sanctions Track (Mandatory):** Immediate block of all property and interests in property. No notice to merchant until after OFAC reporting. No fund release without OFAC license. Documented coordination with acquiring partner.
2. **Discretionary Risk Track (Fraud/AML/Chargeback):**
- Immediate temporary restriction on new payments/payouts (reversible).
- Time-boxed investigation (e.g., 5–10 business days).
- Notice to merchant within 24 hours of restriction (unless SAR confidentiality prohibits specific disclosure).
- Final decision: restore, restrict permanently, or terminate with contractually required notice (typically 30 days for convenience, immediate for cause).
- Funds handling: Hold pending investigation, then release per contract or state law, or refund consumers.
**Path B: Uniform Immediate Termination**
Terminate all high-risk merchants immediately upon alert. *Risk:* High wrongful-closure exposure, potential UDAP claims, and breach of contract if agreements require cure periods or notice.
**Path C: Graduated Response**
Warning → Restriction → Termination. *Risk:* Too slow for sanctions or active fraud; may allow dissipation of funds or continued consumer harm.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Licensing status:** If Mosaic Relay operates as an agent of a licensed money transmitter or bank (rather than holding its own licenses), the notice and funds-handling obligations shift to the partner institution.
- **Contract terms:** If merchant agreements already include immediate termination rights for "suspected illegal activity" without notice, the state law analysis changes (though some states void such waivers).
- **Fund custody:** If Mosaic Relay never takes custody of funds (pure API/software layer), money transmitter laws may not apply, but card network rules and processor agreements still govern fund holds.
- **SAR status:** If a SAR has been filed on a specific merchant, the "contact the merchant" step must be eliminated or heavily sanitized to avoid tipping-off violations.
- **State-specific mini-FTC acts:** Some states (e.g., California, New York) have specific statutes governing account freezes by payment processors that impose stricter notice requirements than general money transmitter laws.
## Recommended last-mile verification
Before finalizing the process, verify:
1. **State-by-state survey:** Confirm Mosaic Relay’s licensing footprint and identify the 3–5 strictest states for notice requirements (typically NY, CA, TX, FL, WA) to use as the compliance baseline.
2. **Merchant agreement audit:** Confirm that suspension/termination clauses explicitly cover "suspected fraud, money laundering, or sanctions risk" and specify whether notice is required before or after restriction.
3. **OFAC procedures:** Confirm the 10-day reporting timeline for blocked property and the specific "reject" vs. "block" procedures for the acquiring partner.
4. **SAR protocol:** Establish a "no-notice" flag in the case management system that automatically suppresses merchant communications when a SAR is filed or law enforcement requests confidentiality.
5. **Card network rules:** Verify Visa/Mastercard requirements for merchant termination (e.g., MATCH system listing) and chargeback handling during account restriction.
