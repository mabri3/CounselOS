# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Deactivation of a high-risk merchant: legal basis and process for temporary restriction and termination, notices, funds handling, SAR tipping-off, sanctions obligations, and supporting records.

## Decision question

Advise on the legal basis and process for Mosaic Relay to temporarily restrict and/or terminate a merchant whose activity raises sanctions, fraud, chargeback, consumer-harm, or money-laundering concerns — covering required notices, handling of pending funds and refunds, suspicious-activity confidentiality (SAR tipping-off), sanctions obligations, and the records needed to support the decision. Goal: act quickly while reducing wrongful closures and inconsistent treatment across customers.

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay's monitoring team wants a standard process to deactivate merchants whose activity raises sanctions, fraud, chargeback, consumer-harm, or money-laundering concerns.
- The proposed flow would place a temporary restriction on new payments or payouts, preserve relevant records, contact the merchant when legally and operationally appropriate, and either restore access or terminate the relationship.
- The business goal is to act quickly while reducing wrongful closures and avoiding inconsistent treatment across customers.
- A restriction may need to occur within minutes of an alert, while investigation and final disposition may take several days.
- Mosaic Relay may process payments and payouts for customers but is not a bank and does not hold customer deposits.
- Actors include Mosaic Relay, the merchant, the merchant's sellers and buyers, acquiring partners, processors, identity and fraud vendors, compliance staff, legal staff, and customer support.
- What do Mosaic Relay's merchant agreements say about suspension and termination rights for sanctions, fraud, chargeback, consumer-harm, or AML concerns? — Not yet reviewed / not sure
- Which jurisdictions must the deactivation process cover? — Not sure yet

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay's merchant agreements contain suspension and termination rights for risk, fraud, sanctions, and AML concerns (to be confirmed).
- Mosaic Relay operates as a payment facilitator under partner licenses rather than a licensed money transmitter (consistent with prior matters).
- Deactivation may be triggered by either a confirmed sanctions match (mandatory blocking) or discretionary risk signals (fraud/chargeback/AML).

## Issues and workstreams

- Legal basis for temporary restriction and termination (contractual rights vs. regulatory mandate)
- Required notices to the merchant and any appeal rights
- Handling of pending funds, payouts in flight, and consumer orders in progress
- Suspicious-activity confidentiality and SAR tipping-off constraints on contacting the merchant
- Sanctions (OFAC/other) blocking obligations vs. discretionary fraud/chargeback deactivation
- Records needed to support and defend the deactivation decision
- Avoiding wrongful closures and inconsistent treatment across customers

## Open questions

- Whether the trigger is a confirmed sanctions match vs. discretionary risk signals
- Amount and status of funds currently pending (merchant balances, payouts in flight, consumer orders in progress)
- Whether any law-enforcement request or SAR filing is involved (tipping-off constraints)
- Notice and appeal rights owed to the merchant under contract and law
- What kind of trigger is the deactivation process primarily designed to handle?
