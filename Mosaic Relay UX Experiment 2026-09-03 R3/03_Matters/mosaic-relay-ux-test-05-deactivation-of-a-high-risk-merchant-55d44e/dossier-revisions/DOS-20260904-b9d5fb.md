# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company, is designing a standard process to deactivate merchants whose activity raises sanctions, fraud, chargeback, consumer-harm, or money-laundering concerns. The proposed flow would temporarily restrict new payments or payouts, preserve records, contact the merchant when legally and operationally appropriate, and either restore access or terminate the relationship. Counsel is involved because the process must move within minutes of an alert while avoiding wrongful closures, inconsistent treatment, and violations of suspicious-activity confidentiality or sanctions obligations. The specific research question here is how SAR tipping-off and suspicious-activity confidentiality rules constrain what Mosaic Relay can tell a merchant when restricting their account.

## Decision question

When Mosaic Relay restricts or terminates a merchant based on suspected money laundering or other suspicious activity that may require a SAR filing, what can the monitoring and support teams lawfully disclose to the merchant about the reason for the restriction, and what internal controls must exist to prevent tipping-off violations while still providing enough notice to satisfy contractual and consumer-protection obligations?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay's monitoring team wants a standard process to deactivate merchants whose activity raises sanctions, fraud, chargeback, consumer-harm, or money-laundering concerns.
- The proposed flow would place a temporary restriction on new payments or payouts, preserve relevant records, contact the merchant when legally and operationally appropriate, and either restore access or terminate the relationship.
- The business goal is to act quickly while reducing wrongful closures and avoiding inconsistent treatment across customers.
- A restriction may need to occur within minutes of an alert, while investigation and final disposition may take several days.
- Mosaic Relay may process payments and payouts for customers but is not a bank and does not hold customer deposits.
- Actors include Mosaic Relay, the merchant, the merchant's sellers and buyers, acquiring partners, processors, identity and fraud vendors, compliance staff, legal staff, and customer support.

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay's merchant agreements contain suspension and termination rights for risk, fraud, sanctions, and AML concerns (to be confirmed).
- Mosaic Relay operates as a payment facilitator under partner licenses rather than a licensed money transmitter (consistent with prior matters).
- Deactivation may be triggered by either a confirmed sanctions match (mandatory blocking) or discretionary risk signals (fraud/chargeback/AML).

## Issues and workstreams

- Legal basis for temporary restriction and termination (contractual rights vs. regulatory mandate)
- Required notices to the merchant and any appeal rights
- Handling of pending funds, payouts in flight, and consumer orders in progress
- Suspicious-activity confidentiality and SAR tipping-off constraints on contacting the merchant
- Sanctions (OFAC/other) blocking obligations vs. discretionary fraud/chargeback deactivation
- Records needed to support and defend the deactivation decision
- Avoiding wrongful closures and inconsistent treatment across customers

## Open questions

- Do Mosaic Relay's merchant agreements include contractual suspension and termination rights that allow restriction without disclosing the specific compliance reason? (Not yet reviewed.)
- Is Mosaic Relay operating as a payment facilitator under partner licenses or as a licensed money transmitter with its own BSA/AML obligations? This affects which entity bears the SAR filing and tipping-off liability.
- Which jurisdictions must the deactivation process cover? UK and EU tipping-off rules differ materially from US rules.
- Does Mosaic Relay file SARs directly, or does it rely on its acquiring bank or processor partners to file? This determines who holds the confidentiality obligation and who can be liable for disclosure.
- What is the current practice for handling pending funds and consumer orders in progress when a restriction is imposed?
