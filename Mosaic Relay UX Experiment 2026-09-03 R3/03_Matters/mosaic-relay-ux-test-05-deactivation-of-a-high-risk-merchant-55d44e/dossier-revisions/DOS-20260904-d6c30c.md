# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company, is designing a standard process to deactivate merchants whose activity raises sanctions, fraud, chargeback, consumer-harm, or money-laundering concerns. The proposed flow would temporarily restrict new payments or payouts, preserve records, contact the merchant when legally and operationally appropriate, and either restore access or terminate the relationship. Counsel is involved because the process must move within minutes of an alert while avoiding wrongful closures, inconsistent treatment, and violations of suspicious-activity confidentiality or sanctions obligations. The specific research question here is how SAR tipping-off and suspicious-activity confidentiality rules constrain what Mosaic Relay can tell a merchant when restricting their account.

## Decision question

When Mosaic Relay restricts or terminates a merchant based on suspected money laundering or other suspicious activity that may require a SAR filing, what can the monitoring and support teams lawfully disclose to the merchant about the reason for the restriction, and what internal controls must exist to prevent tipping-off violations while still providing enough notice to satisfy contractual and consumer-protection obligations?

## Material facts

- The proposed flow would place a temporary restriction on new payments or payouts, preserve relevant records, contact the merchant when legally and operationally appropriate, and either restore access or terminate the relationship.
- The business goal is to act quickly while reducing wrongful closures and avoiding inconsistent treatment across customers.

## Options or working recommendation

# Working recommendation

This recommendation is for counsel review only. It is not a durable business decision.

## Recommended dual-track process

1. Add a mandatory sanctions gate to every high-risk deactivation. If a match is confirmed, block relevant property immediately, preserve evidence, coordinate with the acquiring partner, and do not return or release funds without the required authorization.
2. For discretionary fraud, chargeback, consumer-harm, or AML signals, use a reversible, time-boxed restriction on new payments and/or payouts. Record the trigger, evidence, owner, scope, and review deadline.
3. Investigate with compliance and legal review. Separate confirmed facts from model or vendor leads. Apply consistent criteria and escalate material exceptions.
4. Decide to restore, extend with a documented reason, or terminate under the merchant agreement. Keep sanctions handling separate from ordinary appeal or cure rights.
5. Manage pending funds and consumer orders through a documented path for holds, refunds, chargebacks, reserves, and lawful return. Coordinate with processors before changing settlement instructions.
6. Send merchant and consumer notices when contract and law require, but suppress SAR-related details and any communication that could cause tipping-off or interfere with law enforcement.
7. Preserve the complete audit trail: alert and screening result, evidence, timestamps, decision-maker, notice text, partner communications, funds treatment, filings, appeal, and final outcome.

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay is a US payment facilitator operating under partner-bank arrangements, not a bank or directly licensed money transmitter.
- The process must support both confirmed sanctions matches and discretionary risk signals.
- A restriction may be required within minutes, with investigation and disposition over several days.
