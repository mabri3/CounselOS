# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Marketing review of fraud-prevention and compliance claims for global/multiple markets, pre-launch next quarter.

## Decision question

Advise which marketing claims about fraud prevention and compliance are misleading or impermissible, what substantiation and qualifiers are required, how to describe third-party dependencies, how to approve case studies, and whether compliance-related claims create contractual or regulatory risk.

## Material facts

- Marketing wants to advertise onboarding, fraud monitoring, sanctions screening, and transaction-risk products as helping customers 'stop fraud,' 'ensure compliant payments,' and 'prevent money laundering.'
- Sales wants customer-specific case studies stating Mosaic Relay reduced losses by a particular percentage.
- Campaigns would run on the website, in sales presentations, email, webinars, and partner materials beginning next quarter.
- Mosaic Relay provides infrastructure and hosted tools and relies on acquiring banks, processors, identity and fraud vendors, card networks, and cloud providers.
- Mosaic Relay cannot guarantee perfect results.
- Business goal is to increase adoption among marketplaces and platforms while giving prospects confidence in Mosaic Relay's controls.
- What evidence or data exists to substantiate the proposed claims, and are the case-study loss-reduction percentages real and measured? — Not sure what evidence exists
- Have the referenced customers given permission for the case studies, and what would they disclose? — Not sure of the status
- When does the business need the legal answer? — Before a planned launch
- Who is the intended audience and in which jurisdictions will the campaigns run? (select all that apply) — Global/multiple markets
- Who owns approval of marketing claims and case studies before they go live — No owner decided yet

## Options or working recommendation

# Working recommendation

## Recommendation
1. Do not publish “stop fraud,” “ensure compliant payments,” or “prevent money laundering” as absolute claims. Replace them with supported capability language such as “helps reduce fraud losses,” “supports compliant payment operations,” and “helps detect and report suspicious activity.”
2. Do not publish quantified customer loss-reduction percentages until Product/Analytics verifies the method, baseline, period, sample, and result; the customer approves the exact figure and channels in writing; and typicality and material-connection disclosures are clear.
3. Use a plain qualifier: “Results vary by customer, configuration, and transaction mix. Mosaic Relay does not guarantee prevention of all fraud or money laundering.”
4. Describe acquiring banks, processors, identity/fraud vendors, card networks, and cloud providers as dependencies where material. Align the wording with the customer contract and service limits.
5. Set a named approval owner before launch. Recommended workflow: Product/Analytics evidence check → Sales customer consent → Legal claim and disclosure review → Marketing release approval → periodic re-check.
6. Map exact countries, audience, channels, and product scope before launch. Confirm current US, UK/EU, and other local advertising, endorsement, AML, sanctions, and financial-promotion requirements.

## Known facts
- Evidence and customer permission are currently unknown.
- Campaigns are planned before next quarter in global/multiple markets.
- No approval owner is defined.
- Mosaic Relay cannot guarantee perfect results.

## Assumptions and missing facts
This recommendation assumes mainly B2B use and uses general US/UK/EU principles as an unverified lens. Confirm target countries, consumer exposure, data methods, contracts, vendor representations, and customer permissions.

## Unverified leads
FTC deception and endorsement principles, UK/EU unfair-commercial-practice rules, financial-promotion rules, and AML/sanctions implications require current jurisdiction-specific verification. No durable decision is recorded.
