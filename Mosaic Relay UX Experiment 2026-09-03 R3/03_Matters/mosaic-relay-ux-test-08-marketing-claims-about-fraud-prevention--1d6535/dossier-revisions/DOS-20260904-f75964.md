# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Advise Mosaic Relay's marketing and sales teams on which fraud-prevention and compliance-related advertising claims ("stop fraud," "ensure compliant payments," "prevent money laundering," and customer case studies with specific loss-reduction percentages) are misleading or impermissible, what substantiation and qualifiers are required, how to describe third-party dependencies, how to approve customer case studies, and whether any compliance-related claim creates contractual or regulatory risk. Campaigns run on website, sales presentations, email, webinars, and partner materials beginning next quarter.

## Decision question

Advise Mosaic Relay's marketing and sales teams on which fraud-prevention and compliance-related advertising claims ("stop fraud," "ensure compliant payments," "prevent money laundering," and customer case studies with specific loss-reduction percentages) are misleading or impermissible, what substantiation and qualifiers are required, how to describe third-party dependencies, how to approve customer case studies, and whether any compliance-related claim creates contractual or regulatory risk. Campaigns run on website, sales presentations, email, webinars, and partner materials beginning next quarter.

## Material facts

- Marketing wants to advertise onboarding, fraud monitoring, sanctions screening, and transaction-risk products as helping customers 'stop fraud,' 'ensure compliant payments,' and 'prevent money laundering.'
- Sales wants customer-specific case studies stating Mosaic Relay reduced losses by a particular percentage.
- Campaigns would run on the website, in sales presentations, email, webinars, and partner materials beginning next quarter.
- Mosaic Relay provides infrastructure and hosted tools and relies on acquiring banks, processors, identity and fraud vendors, card networks, and cloud providers.
- Mosaic Relay cannot guarantee perfect results.
- Business goal is to increase adoption among marketplaces and platforms while giving prospects confidence in Mosaic Relay's controls.
- What evidence or data exists to substantiate the proposed claims, and are the case-study loss-reduction percentages real and measured? — Not sure what evidence exists
- Have the referenced customers given permission for the case studies, and what would they disclose? — Not sure of the status
- When does the business need the legal answer? — Before a planned launch
- Who is the intended audience and in which jurisdictions will the campaigns run? (select all that apply) — Global/multiple markets

## Assumptions

- US FTC framework is the primary lens given US-headquartered company, though EU/UK consumer and financial promotion rules may also apply
- The case-study loss-reduction percentages are not yet substantiated by data
- Marketing claims would be directed primarily at business customers (marketplaces/platforms) rather than consumers

## Issues and workstreams

- Deceptive advertising risk under FTC Act Section 5 for absolute claims like 'stop fraud' and 'prevent money laundering'
- Substantiation requirements for quantified loss-reduction claims in case studies
- FTC endorsement/testimonial rules for customer case studies (disclosure of material connections, typicality)
- Third-party dependency disclosure accuracy
- Regulatory risk from compliance-related claims (AML/sanctions claims implying guaranteed compliance)
- Contractual risk if marketing claims exceed what the product/contract delivers

## Open questions

- Who owns approval of marketing claims and case studies before they go live
