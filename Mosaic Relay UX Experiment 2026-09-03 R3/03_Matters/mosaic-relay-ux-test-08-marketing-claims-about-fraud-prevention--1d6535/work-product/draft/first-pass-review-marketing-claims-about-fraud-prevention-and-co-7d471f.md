# First-Pass Review — Marketing Claims About Fraud Prevention and Compliance

## 1. Claim-by-claim assessment

### 1.1 "Stop fraud"
**Assessment: Misleading / impermissible as an unqualified absolute claim.**
- "Stop fraud" is an absolute, outcome-guarantee claim. It implies Mosaic Relay's products eliminate fraud entirely. That is a claim the company cannot substantiate and that contradicts the known fact that Mosaic Relay cannot guarantee perfect results.
- Under US FTC deception standards (FTC Act § 5), a claim is deceptive if it is likely to mislead a reasonable consumer acting reasonably and is material. An absolute performance claim carries the highest substantiation burden — the advertiser must possess competent and reliable evidence before the claim runs.
- Because the claim is directed at business customers (marketplaces/platforms) but also appears in public web/email/webinar materials, it can reach both sophisticated and less-sophisticated readers. Absolute language is risky regardless of audience.
- **Recommended alternative:** "helps reduce fraud losses," "detects and blocks fraudulent transactions," "reduces fraud exposure." These are capability claims, not guarantees, and are substantiable with product/performance data.

### 1.2 "Ensure compliant payments"
**Assessment: Misleading / impermissible as an unqualified claim.**
- "Ensure compliant payments" implies Mosaic Relay guarantees regulatory compliance of its customers' payment operations. Compliance is a function of the customer's own conduct, the underlying acquiring banks/processors, and evolving regulation — none of which Mosaic Relay controls or guarantees.
- This claim risks being read as a guarantee of AML/sanctions/regulatory compliance, which the company cannot deliver and which could create both regulatory and contractual exposure (see § 5).
- **Recommended alternative:** "supports compliant payment operations," "helps you meet onboarding and screening obligations," "built to support AML and sanctions screening." These describe the tool's role without guaranteeing outcomes.

### 1.3 "Prevent money laundering"
**Assessment: Misleading / impermissible as an unqualified claim.**
- "Prevent money laundering" is the highest-risk claim. It implies Mosaic Relay's screening and monitoring tools stop money laundering entirely. No screening or monitoring system can guarantee this, and the company explicitly cannot guarantee perfect results.
- This claim also carries heightened regulatory sensitivity because it touches AML/BSA and sanctions obligations. An absolute prevention claim could be read as an overstatement of the effectiveness of the company's controls and could draw regulatory scrutiny, particularly in the UK/EU where financial promotion and AML messaging are closely watched.
- **Recommended alternative:** "helps detect and report suspicious activity," "supports your AML and sanctions screening program," "aids in identifying potential money-laundering risk." These are capability/support claims.

### 1.4 Quantified customer case studies (loss-reduction percentages)
**Assessment: Impermissible to run as proposed until substantiated and permissioned.**
- Specific loss-reduction percentages are quantified performance claims. They require competent and reliable evidence — real, measured, statistically sound data — before publication.
- Two independent gaps block these today: (a) no known substantiation data exists (recorded fact), and (b) customer permission and disclosure scope are unconfirmed (recorded fact).
- Under FTC endorsement/testimonial rules, a case study that states a specific result must reflect the typical experience of customers, or the advertiser must clearly and conspicuously disclose the generally expected performance in the circumstances. A single customer's result presented as typical without such disclosure is deceptive.
- **Recommended alternative:** Do not run quantified percentages until (a) the data is verified and measured, (b) the customer has signed off on the specific figures and disclosure, and (c) the claim is framed as that customer's experience with a typicality qualifier or clear disclosure.

## 2. Substantiation and qualifier requirements

### Substantiation
- **Absolute claims** ("stop," "ensure," "prevent") require the highest level of competent and reliable evidence. Given the recorded fact that no substantiation data is known to exist, these claims should not run in their current form.
- **Quantified claims** (case-study percentages) require real, measured data that is statistically sound and representative. The specific figure must be traceable to actual customer results.
- **Capability claims** ("detects," "reduces," "supports," "helps") still require reasonable substantiation — product performance data, testing, or documented feature behavior — but carry a lower burden than absolute or quantified claims.

### Qualifiers
- Any claim that survives should carry a qualifier that (a) describes the tool's role rather than a guarantee, and (b) discloses material limitations.
- Suggested qualifier language: "Results vary by customer, configuration, and transaction mix. Mosaic Relay does not guarantee the prevention of all fraud or money laundering." 
- For case studies: "Individual results may vary. [Customer]'s results reflect its specific configuration and are not typical of all customers."

### Timing
- Substantiation must exist **before** the claim runs, not after. The recorded timing is "before a planned launch" (next quarter), so substantiation must be gathered and verified in advance.

## 3. Third-party dependency language

- Mosaic Relay relies on acquiring banks, processors, identity and fraud vendors, card networks, and cloud providers. Marketing must not imply these are Mosaic Relay's own capabilities or that Mosaic Relay controls their performance.
- **Recommended approach:** Describe the products as "powered by" or "supported by" third-party providers where accuracy requires it, and avoid implying sole ownership of capabilities that depend on vendors.
- **Risk:** If marketing implies Mosaic Relay alone delivers a result that actually depends on a vendor, and the vendor underperforms, the claim is both deceptive and potentially a breach of the customer contract (see § 5).
- **Recommended language:** "Mosaic Relay's screening and monitoring tools are supported by third-party identity, fraud, and sanctions data providers." This is accurate and manages expectations.

### Customer permissions

No case study should run until the referenced customer has signed off in writing on: (a) the specific figures, (b) the disclosure of their name and metrics, and (c) the context in which the case study will appear.

5. **Post-launch monitoring** — Re-verify claims periodically and update if the underlying data or customer relationship changes.

## 5. Contractual and regulatory risk across global markets

### Contractual risk
- If marketing claims promise outcomes ("stop fraud," "ensure compliant payments," "prevent money laundering") that exceed what the product and the customer contract deliver, the claims can create:
  - **Warranty exposure** — customers could argue the marketing created an express warranty the product fails to meet.
  - **Misrepresentation claims** — customers could claim they relied on the marketing in contracting.
  - **Vendor/partner risk** — if Mosaic Relay overstates capabilities that depend on third-party vendors, and a vendor underperforms, Mosaic Relay bears the customer-facing risk.
- **Mitigation:** Align marketing claims with the actual contractual scope of services and disclaimers. Do not let marketing promise more than the contract delivers.

### Regulatory risk
- **US:** FTC Act § 5 deception and substantiation standards; FTC endorsement/testimonial guides for case studies.
- **UK/EU:** Consumer protection from unfair trading (e.g., UK Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008; EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive) and financial promotion rules. Absolute compliance/prevention claims are sensitive in financial-services-adjacent messaging.
- **AML/sanctions sensitivity:** Claims about "preventing money laundering" touch AML/BSA and sanctions messaging. Overstating the effectiveness of screening controls can draw regulatory scrutiny and undermine the credibility of the compliance program.
- **Global/multiple markets:** Because campaigns run across global markets, the strictest applicable standard in any target jurisdiction will govern. Absolute and quantified claims are the most likely to fail in the strictest regime.
