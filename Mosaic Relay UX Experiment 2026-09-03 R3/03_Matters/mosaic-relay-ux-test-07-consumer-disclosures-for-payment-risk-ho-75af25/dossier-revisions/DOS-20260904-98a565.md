# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure provider, plans to implement automated risk holds that delay merchant payouts when transaction scoring detects unusual activity, high dispute risk, or incomplete verification. Merchants will see a "review required" status and must submit documents through a secure portal to resume payouts. Counsel is involved because the feature implicates state automated-decision laws, consumer protection statutes, and funds-handling regulations, particularly given that holds can last from hours to weeks and may affect funds belonging to underlying sellers or consumers. The company operates on a US-only basis for this analysis and does not hold deposits or operate as a bank.

## Decision question

Should Mosaic Relay launch automated payout holds that delay merchant funds for up to several weeks based solely on algorithmic risk scoring, and if so, what specific disclosure language, human review triggers, and maximum duration limits must be implemented to comply with state automated-decision laws and avoid unfair or deceptive practice claims before the feature goes live?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay proposes a risk-control feature that temporarily delays a merchant payout when transaction-risk scoring identifies unusual activity, high dispute risk, or incomplete verification.
- The merchant would see a status such as 'review required', receive a general explanation, and submit documents through a secure portal.
- Holds could begin automatically within minutes of a transaction and last from several hours to multiple weeks depending on review results.
- Mosaic Relay provides payout controls and risk scoring but does not hold deposits or operate as a bank.
- The business goal is to prevent losses while giving legitimate merchants a clear path to resume payouts.

## Issues and workstreams

- Whether held funds belong to the merchant or to underlying sellers/consumers, which determines whether this is a merchant-contract/regulatory matter or a consumer-protection matter
- Whether Mosaic Relay has contractual authority to delay payouts and what the merchant agreement currently says about holds
- Fairness and transparency obligations for automated risk decisions under US law (state automated-decision laws, FTC unfair/deceptive practices)
- Interest/fee treatment and what happens if a merchant cannot provide documents
- Whether the hold is triggered automatically by the risk-scoring model without human review

## Open questions

- Does the current merchant agreement grant Mosaic Relay contractual authority to delay payouts for risk review, and does it specify notice, timing, or appeal rights?
- Do the held funds belong to the merchant directly, or to underlying sellers/consumers who are paid through the merchant (which would trigger consumer protection laws rather than just commercial contract law)?
- Will holds be triggered automatically by the risk-scoring model without human review, or will a human analyst confirm each hold before it is placed?
- During the hold period, will interest or fees accrue to the merchant, and what specific outcome occurs if a merchant cannot provide the requested verification documents?
- Which specific states' automated-decision laws (e.g., California, Colorado, Illinois) apply based on where Mosaic Relay's merchants are located, and do any of those laws provide exemptions for fraud prevention or financial services?
