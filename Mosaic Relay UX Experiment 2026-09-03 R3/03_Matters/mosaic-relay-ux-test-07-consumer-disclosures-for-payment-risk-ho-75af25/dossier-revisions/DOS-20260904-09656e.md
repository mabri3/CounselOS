# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company, proposes a risk-control feature that automatically delays merchant payouts when transaction-risk scoring flags unusual activity, high dispute risk, or incomplete verification. Merchants would see a "review required" status, receive a general explanation, and submit documents through a secure portal. Counsel is involved because the FTC Act's prohibition on unfair or deceptive acts or practices may constrain how Mosaic Relay designs, discloses, and executes these automated holds. Holds could begin within minutes and last from hours to multiple weeks, creating significant cash-flow consequences for merchants.

## Decision question

Should Mosaic Relay launch an automated payout-hold feature that delays merchant funds for hours to weeks based on algorithmic risk scoring, and if so, what specific disclosure language, timing limits, and human-review triggers must be built into the product to avoid FTC unfair or deceptive practices liability?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay proposes a risk-control feature that temporarily delays a merchant payout when transaction-risk scoring identifies unusual activity, high dispute risk, or incomplete verification.
- The merchant would see a status such as 'review required', receive a general explanation, and submit documents through a secure portal.
- Holds could begin automatically within minutes of a transaction and last from several hours to multiple weeks depending on review results.
- Mosaic Relay provides payout controls and risk scoring but does not hold deposits or operate as a bank.
- The business goal is to prevent losses while giving legitimate merchants a clear path to resume payouts.

- Mosaic Relay's direct contractual counterparty is the merchant/platform, not the end consumer; consumer-protection exposure likely flows through the merchant's own obligations unless Mosaic Relay holds funds belonging to consumers.
- Analysis proceeds on a US-only jurisdiction basis; EU/UK obligations are out of scope for the primary analysis.

## Issues and workstreams

- Whether held funds belong to the merchant or to underlying sellers/consumers, which determines whether this is a merchant-contract/regulatory matter or a consumer-protection matter
- Whether Mosaic Relay has contractual authority to delay payouts and what the merchant agreement currently says about holds
- Fairness and transparency obligations for automated risk decisions under US law (state automated-decision laws, FTC unfair/deceptive practices)
- Interest/fee treatment and what happens if a merchant cannot provide documents
- Whether the hold is triggered automatically by the risk-scoring model without human review

## Open questions

- Does the current merchant agreement grant Mosaic Relay contractual authority to delay payouts for risk review, and does it specify notice, timing, or appeal rights?
- Whose funds are being held—the merchant's own settlement funds, or funds belonging to underlying sellers or consumers paid through the merchant?
- Is the hold triggered automatically by the risk-scoring model without human review, or does a human analyst confirm before funds are delayed?
- During the hold, do interest or fees accrue to the merchant, and what happens if a merchant cannot provide the requested verification documents?
- What specific criteria does the risk-scoring model use to trigger a hold, and are those criteria disclosed to merchants in advance?
