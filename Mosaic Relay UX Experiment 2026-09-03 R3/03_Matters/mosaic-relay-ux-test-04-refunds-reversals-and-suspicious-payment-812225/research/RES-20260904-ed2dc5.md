# ACH and local payment return/reversal procedures and timeframes

## Question

ACH and local payment return/reversal procedures and timeframes

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure provider, is developing a self-service refund tool for merchants that would process full or partial refunds across card, ACH, and local payment methods. The tool would route funds back to original payers where possible and include manual operations overrides for closed merchant accounts. Counsel is involved because operations has identified potential misuse cases where merchants could exploit refunds to move funds between unrelated cards or recipients, creating AML and regulatory exposure. The tool lacks a defined launch date, and critical operational parameters—including refund windows by payment method and transaction-monitoring coverage—remain undefined.
## Decision question
Should Mosaic Relay launch a merchant self-service refund tool that processes full and partial refunds across card, ACH, and local payment methods with manual operations overrides for closed accounts, and if so, what specific controls, monitoring rules, and disclosure requirements must be implemented to prevent misuse for money laundering while complying with varying refund windows and network rules across jurisdictions?
## Open questions
- Which specific jurisdictions will the tool launch in, given that refund windows, consumer protection rules, and AML obligations vary significantly across North America, the UK, and EU markets?
- What are the exact refund and return windows for each payment method (card networks, ACH, local payment schemes) per acquiring partner and processor contracts?
- Does Mosaic Relay's existing transaction-monitoring system currently cover refunds and reversals, or does this represent a new monitoring surface requiring separate rules and alerts?
- What specific limits, approval workflows, and audit trails apply to manual operations overrides, particularly for closed merchant accounts?
- How should refunds be treated when requested after a payout has occurred or after a merchant account has been closed?

Based on the matter facts and general regulatory frameworks applicable to payment infrastructure providers:
### ACH and local payment return/reversal procedures
**ACH Network (US):**
- ACH transactions operate under Nacha rules with specific return timeframes: unauthorized debit returns generally within 60 days of settlement, and credit returns within 2 banking days for most reasons
- Reversals (as opposed to returns) are permitted only for specific error conditions: duplicate entries, incorrect amount, or incorrect account number, and must be initiated within 5 banking days
- Unlike card refunds, ACH "refunds" are typically new credit transactions rather than reversals of the original debit, creating different reconciliation and monitoring requirements
- RDFI (Receiving Depository Financial Institution) return rights create uncertainty windows that extend beyond settlement
**Local payment methods (UK/EU):**
- SEPA Credit Transfers: recalls possible within 10 working days for unauthorized transactions, with beneficiary bank having 15 working days to respond
- SEPA Direct Debits: 8-week refund right for consumers on authorized transactions, 13 months for unauthorized
- UK Faster Payments: generally irrevocable once settled, requiring new payment for refunds rather than reversal
- Each local scheme (iDEAL, Sofort, etc.) has distinct reversal/return procedures and timeframes
### AML and financial-crime risks
**Refund-as-layering risk:**
- The identified misuse pattern—using refunds to move funds between unrelated cards or recipients—constitutes classic layering in money laundering typologies
- Refund transactions may not trigger the same monitoring rules as original payments, creating a surveillance gap
- Partial refunds and refunds to different funding sources than the original payment are higher-risk indicators
**Regulatory exposure:**
- BSA/AML obligations require monitoring of refund patterns, not just original transactions
- FinCEN guidance on money services businesses applies to refund processing as money transmission
- EU AMLD5/6 requirements for transaction monitoring extend to refund flows
### Consumer protection and disclosure obligations
**US:**
- Regulation E (EFTA) error resolution rights interact with refund timing
- State money transmitter laws may impose specific refund handling requirements
- Card network rules mandate refund processing timeframes (typically 3-5 business days for card refunds)
**EU/UK:**
- PSD2 refund rights for unauthorized transactions
- Consumer Rights Act 2015 (UK) and Consumer Rights Directive (EU) refund entitlements
- GDPR considerations for refund data retention and processing
### Recordkeeping requirements
- BSA recordkeeping: 5-year retention for transactions over $3,000, including refund records
- Card network rules: refund records must link to original transaction IDs
- Nacha: ACH return and reversal records must include specific reason codes and timestamps
- Local schemes: varying retention requirements (typically 5-7 years in EU)

## Viable paths
### Path A: Phased launch with enhanced controls (Recommended)
**Structure:**
1. **Phase 1:** Card refunds only, original-source-only, no manual overrides
2. **Phase 2:** Add ACH with separate monitoring rules, limited overrides with dual approval
3. **Phase 3:** Local payment methods after jurisdiction-specific legal review
**Controls:**
- Real-time refund monitoring rules distinct from payment monitoring
- Velocity limits on refunds per merchant, per payer, per funding source
- Mandatory delay (24-48 hours) for refunds to non-original sources
- Enhanced due diligence for merchants with high refund volumes or ratios
**Pros:** Limits initial AML exposure, allows monitoring rule tuning, matches regulatory expectations for risk-based approach
**Cons:** Slower time-to-market, may not meet all merchant needs immediately
### Path B: Full launch with restrictive defaults
**Structure:**
- All payment methods at launch
- Original-source refunds only (no exceptions)
- Manual overrides require: dual approval, documented business justification, compliance review for amounts over threshold
- Real-time AML screening on all refund transactions
**Controls:**
- Hard block on refunds to unrelated cards/recipients
- Automated alerts for refund patterns indicating potential layering
- Monthly compliance review of override usage
**Pros:** Faster merchant value delivery, simpler initial rule set
**Cons:** Higher residual AML risk, potential for override abuse, may require retroactive controls
### Path C: Delayed launch pending monitoring enhancement
**Structure:**
- Delay launch until transaction-monitoring system confirmed to cover refunds/reversals
- Implement comprehensive refund-specific monitoring rules before go-live
- Pilot with limited merchant cohort
**Pros:** Lowest AML risk, clean compliance posture
**Cons:** Significant business delay, competitive disadvantage, may not address urgent merchant needs

## Facts that could change the answer
1. **Launch jurisdictions:** If limited to US-only initially, ACH and card rules dominate; EU/UK launch triggers PSD2, GDPR, and local scheme complexity
2. **Existing monitoring coverage:** If current transaction-monitoring already covers refunds with specific rules, Path B becomes more viable; if not, Path C may be necessary
3. **Acquiring partner contracts:** Specific refund window commitments and liability allocations in processor agreements could mandate certain timeframes or procedures
4. **Merchant risk profile:** If target merchants are primarily low-risk (established businesses with low refund rates), controls can be calibrated differently than for high-risk verticals
5. **Override volume expectations:** If manual overrides are expected to be rare (<1% of refunds), dual-approval workflow is manageable; if frequent, automated risk scoring becomes essential
6. **Historical refund fraud:** If Mosaic Relay has experienced refund-related fraud or AML incidents, enhanced controls become mandatory rather than optional

## Recommended last-mile verification
Before finalizing the recommendation, counsel should verify:
1. **Contract review:** Obtain and review all acquiring partner, processor, and card network agreements for specific refund window commitments, liability provisions, and prohibited practices
2. **Monitoring system audit:** Confirm with compliance and engineering whether existing transaction-monitoring rules cover refund transactions, and if so, with what specific parameters
3. **Jurisdictional analysis:** For each target launch jurisdiction, confirm specific refund window requirements, consumer protection obligations, and AML reporting thresholds
4. **Nacha rule verification:** Confirm current ACH return and reversal timeframes and reason codes, as Nacha rules are updated periodically
5. **Card network bulletins:** Review current Visa, Mastercard, and other network rules regarding refund processing timeframes and prohibited refund practices
6. **State money transmitter analysis:** For US launch, confirm whether refund processing triggers any state-specific licensing or compliance requirements
7. **GDPR/data protection impact:** Assess whether refund data retention and processing require DPIA or specific consent mechanisms
