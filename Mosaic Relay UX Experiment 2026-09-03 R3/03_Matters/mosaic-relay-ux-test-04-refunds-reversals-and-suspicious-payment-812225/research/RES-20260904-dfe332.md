# AML structuring/layering indicators involving refunds and reversals

## Question

AML structuring/layering indicators involving refunds and reversals

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure provider, is building a self-service refund tool for merchants that would process full or partial refunds across card, ACH, and local payment methods, routing funds back to original payers where possible. Operations has flagged that merchants could exploit this tool to move funds between unrelated cards or recipients, creating potential money laundering exposure. Counsel is involved because the tool implicates AML/financial-crime controls, card network rules, ACH return procedures, consumer protection laws, and recordkeeping obligations. The tool has no firm launch date, but the matter is high priority and high risk.
## Decision question
Should Mosaic Relay launch the merchant self-service refund tool in its current form, launch it in phases with specific controls (original-source-only refunds, enhanced monitoring, restricted manual overrides), or delay launch until transaction-monitoring coverage for refunds/reversals is confirmed and acquiring-partner agreements are reviewed — given that operations has identified potential misuse for moving funds between unrelated parties?
## Open questions
- Does Mosaic Relay's existing transaction-monitoring system currently cover refunds and reversals, or is this a new monitoring surface that must be built before launch?
- What are the specific refund windows and permitted destination rules for each payment method (card networks, ACH, local methods) under Mosaic Relay's acquiring-partner and processor agreements?
- What is Mosaic Relay's legal entity structure and licensing status (money transmitter licenses, agent-of-licensee arrangements), and how does the funds-flow model work — does Mosaic Relay touch funds or only instruct partners?
- Which jurisdictions will the tool be available in at launch, and do any of those jurisdictions impose additional refund, consumer-protection, or AML obligations beyond US federal requirements?
- What limits and approval requirements apply to manual operations overrides for closed accounts, and are those limits documented and enforced?

## Likely rules and issues
### AML and financial-crime obligations
**Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) and FinCEN regulations.** If Mosaic Relay is a money services business (MSB) — which is likely given its payment routing and payout services — it is subject to BSA requirements including:
- **Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) filing**: 31 CFR § 1022.320 requires MSBs to file SARs for transactions of $2,000 or more when the MSB knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect the transaction involves funds derived from illegal activity, is designed to evade BSA requirements, or has no business or apparent lawful purpose. Refunds used to move funds between unrelated cards or recipients could trigger SAR obligations.
- **Anti-money laundering program**: 31 CFR § 1022.210 requires MSBs to develop, implement, and maintain an effective written AML program reasonably designed to prevent the MSB from being used to facilitate money laundering or terrorist financing. The program must include policies, procedures, and internal controls; a designated compliance officer; ongoing employee training; and independent testing.
- **Recordkeeping**: 31 CFR § 1022.410 requires MSBs to maintain records of certain transactions, including transaction amounts, dates, and parties involved. Refund transactions must be recorded with sufficient detail to reconstruct the transaction and identify the parties.
**Structuring and layering indicators.** Operations has identified that merchants may use refunds to move funds between unrelated cards or recipients. This is a classic layering technique in money laundering:
- **Structuring**: Breaking large transactions into smaller amounts to evade reporting thresholds (e.g., multiple refunds just below $2,000 or $10,000).
- **Layering**: Moving funds through multiple accounts or payment methods to obscure the source or destination (e.g., refunding to a different card than the original payment source, or refunding to a third party).
**Red flags specific to refunds and reversals:**
- Refund to a destination other than the original funding source (different card, different account, different person).
- Refund amount significantly different from original payment amount without clear business justification.
- Multiple refunds to the same destination from different original payers.
- Refunds requested immediately after payment, especially if the original payment was flagged as high-risk.
- Refunds requested long after the original payment, especially if the merchant account is closed or dormant.
- Refunds that appear to be round-tripping (payment followed by refund to a different destination, then another payment from that destination).
- Refunds to high-risk jurisdictions or sanctioned parties.
- Refunds that exceed the original payment amount (over-refunding).
### Card network rules
**Visa and Mastercard refund and reversal rules.** Card networks distinguish between:
- **Reversals**: Canceling a transaction before it settles (same-day or within a short window). Reversals are typically processed as authorization reversals or voids.
- **Refunds**: Returning funds after settlement. Refunds are processed as credit transactions and are subject to network rules on timing, amount, and destination.
**Key network rules:**
- **Original-source requirement**: Visa and Mastercard generally require refunds to be processed to the same card used for the original purchase. Refunds to a different card or account are typically prohibited or require special handling.
- **Refund windows**: Card networks allow refunds for a limited period after the original transaction (typically 120–180 days, but this varies by network and merchant category). After the refund window closes, the merchant may need to process a new credit transaction, which is treated differently.
- **Partial refunds**: Networks allow partial refunds, but the total refund amount cannot exceed the original transaction amount.
- **Refund timing**: Networks require refunds to be processed promptly (typically within 3–5 business days) and may impose fees or penalties for delayed refunds.
**Chargeback and dispute implications.** If a merchant processes a refund to a different destination and the original payer disputes the charge, the merchant may be liable for the chargeback and the refund, creating double liability.
### ACH and local payment method rules
**NACHA rules for ACH reversals and returns.** The National Automated Clearing House Association (NACHA) Operating Rules govern ACH transactions:
- **Reversals**: NACHA allows originators to reverse ACH entries under limited circumstances (e.g., duplicate entry, erroneous entry, or entry not authorized). Reversals must be initiated within 5 banking days of settlement and must be for the full amount of the original entry.
- **Returns**: Receiving depository financial institutions (RDFIs) can return ACH entries for various reasons (insufficient funds, account closed, unauthorized, etc.). Returns must be initiated within specific timeframes (typically 2 banking days for most return reasons, 60 days for unauthorized entries).
- **Refunds**: NACHA does not have a formal "refund" process like card networks. Instead, the originator must initiate a new ACH credit entry to return funds to the receiver. This new entry is subject to the same authorization and risk rules as any other ACH credit.
**Local payment methods.** Local payment methods (e.g., SEPA in the EU, Faster Payments in the UK, Interac in Canada) have their own rules on refunds, reversals, and returns. These rules vary by jurisdiction and payment scheme. Mosaic Relay must confirm the specific rules for each local payment method it supports.
### Consumer protection and disclosure obligations
**Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA) and Regulation E.** If Mosaic Relay's refund tool involves electronic fund transfers from consumer accounts, EFTA and Regulation E may apply:
- **Error resolution**: 12 CFR § 1005.11 requires financial institutions to investigate and resolve errors (including unauthorized or incorrect transfers) within specific timeframes. If a consumer disputes a refund or claims a refund was not received, Mosaic Relay or its partners may be required to investigate and resolve the dispute.
- **Disclosures**: 12 CFR § 1005.7 requires financial institutions to provide initial disclosures of terms and conditions, including error resolution procedures. Mosaic Relay should ensure that merchants and payers receive clear disclosures about refund policies, timing, and procedures.
**Truth in Lending Act (TILA) and Regulation Z.** If the original payment was a credit card transaction, TILA and Regulation Z may apply:
- **Billing error resolution**: 12 CFR § 1026.13 requires card issuers to investigate and resolve billing errors (including failure to credit a refund) within specific timeframes.
- **Prompt crediting of refunds**: 12 CFR § 1026.12(d) requires card issuers to credit refunds to the cardholder's account within 3 business days of receiving the refund from the merchant.
**State money transmitter laws.** If Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter, state money transmitter laws may impose additional requirements on refunds, including:
- **Permissible investments**: Some states require money transmitters to hold permissible investments (e.g., cash, government securities) equal to outstanding payment obligations. Refunds may reduce outstanding obligations and affect permissible investment calculations.
- **Net worth requirements**: Some states impose net worth requirements on money transmitters. Refunds may affect net worth calculations.
- **Reporting**: Some states require money transmitters to file periodic reports on transaction volumes, including refunds.
### Recordkeeping and audit trail
**BSA recordkeeping.** As noted above, 31 CFR § 1022.410 requires MSBs to maintain records of certain transactions. For refunds, Mosaic Relay should maintain:
- Original transaction details (amount, date, parties, payment method).
- Refund transaction details (amount, date, destination, reason).
- Linkage between original transaction and refund.
- Approval and override records (who approved, when, why).
- Monitoring alerts and investigation records.
**Card network recordkeeping.** Visa and Mastercard require merchants and acquirers to maintain records of transactions and refunds for a specified period (typically 13 months to 7 years, depending on the network and jurisdiction). These records must be sufficient to respond to chargebacks, disputes, and audits.
**ACH recordkeeping.** NACHA rules require originators and RDFIs to maintain records of ACH entries, including reversals and returns, for a specified period (typically 6 years).
### Controls against misuse
**Original-source-only refunds.** The strongest control against misuse is to require refunds to be processed to the original funding source (same card, same account, same person). This prevents merchants from using refunds to move funds to unrelated parties.
**Transaction monitoring.** Mosaic Relay should implement transaction-monitoring rules to detect suspicious refund patterns, including:
- Refunds to a destination other than the original funding source.
- Refunds that exceed the original payment amount.
- Multiple refunds to the same destination from different original payers.
- Refunds requested immediately after payment or long after payment.
- Refunds to high-risk jurisdictions or sanctioned parties.
- Refunds that appear to be round-tripping.
**Manual override controls.** Manual operations overrides should be subject to:
- **Two-person approval**: Require two authorized individuals to approve any override.
- **Logging and audit trail**: Log all overrides, including who approved, when, why, and the destination of the refund.
- **Limits**: Impose dollar limits on overrides (e.g., no overrides above $10,000 without senior management approval).
- **Post-transaction review**: Review all overrides after the fact to detect patterns of misuse.
**Merchant onboarding and monitoring.** Mosaic Relay should:
- **Enhanced due diligence (EDD)**: Conduct EDD on merchants that request refunds to destinations other than the original funding source.
- **Ongoing monitoring**: Monitor merchant refund patterns for suspicious activity.
- **Termination**: Terminate merchants that engage in suspicious refund activity.
### When to delay, reject, or investigate a refund
**Delay a refund when:**
- The refund triggers a monitoring alert and requires investigation.
- The refund is to a destination other than the original funding source and requires EDD.
- The refund is to a high-risk jurisdiction or sanctioned party and requires OFAC screening.
- The refund is requested long after the original payment and requires verification of the original transaction.
**Reject a refund when:**
- The refund is to a destination other than the original funding source and the merchant cannot provide a legitimate business justification.
- The refund exceeds the original payment amount.
- The refund is to a sanctioned party or prohibited jurisdiction.
- The refund is requested after the refund window has closed and the merchant cannot process a new credit transaction.
**Investigate a refund when:**
- The refund triggers a monitoring alert.
- The refund is part of a pattern of suspicious activity.
- The refund is requested by a merchant with a history of suspicious activity.
- The refund is requested by a merchant that is under investigation or has been terminated.

## Viable paths
### Path 1: Launch in phases with strict controls
**Phase 1: Card refunds only, original-source-only, no destination-changing overrides.**
- Launch the tool for card refunds only.
- Require refunds to be processed to the original card.
- Prohibit destination-changing overrides.
- Implement transaction-monitoring rules for card refunds.
- Confirm that existing transaction-monitoring covers card refunds.
**Phase 2: ACH refunds, original-source-only, limited destination-changing overrides.**
- Expand the tool to ACH refunds.
- Require refunds to be processed to the original account.
- Allow destination-changing overrides only with two-person approval and EDD.
- Implement transaction-monitoring rules for ACH refunds.
**Phase 3: Local payment methods, original-source-only, limited destination-changing overrides.**
- Expand the tool to local payment methods.
- Require refunds to be processed to the original funding source.
- Allow destination-changing overrides only with two-person approval and EDD.
- Implement transaction-monitoring rules for local payment methods.
**Launch gates:**
- Confirm that transaction-monitoring covers refunds/reversals (or build that coverage).
- Review acquiring-partner/processor agreements to confirm refund windows and destination rules.
- Confirm Mosaic Relay's legal entity/licensing status and funds-flow model.
- Confirm per-partner refund windows.
- Draft merchant and payer disclosures.
**Pros:**
- Reduces risk by launching in phases and implementing strict controls.
- Allows Mosaic Relay to test the tool and refine controls before expanding to higher-risk payment methods.
- Aligns with the working recommendation in the matter file.
**Cons:**
- Slower time to market.
- May not meet the business goal of shortening refund processing time if controls are too restrictive.
### Path 2: Launch with original-source-only refunds and no destination-changing overrides
- Launch the tool for all payment methods (card, ACH, local).
- Require refunds to be processed to the original funding source.
- Prohibit destination-changing overrides.
- Implement transaction-monitoring rules for all payment methods.
**Pros:**
- Faster time to market than Path 1.
- Reduces risk by prohibiting destination-changing overrides.
**Cons:**
- May not meet the business goal if merchants need destination-changing overrides for legitimate reasons (e.g., original card is closed).
- May not be feasible if acquiring-partner/processor agreements require destination-changing overrides for certain payment methods.
### Path 3: Delay launch until transaction-monitoring coverage is confirmed and acquiring-partner agreements are reviewed
- Delay launch until Mosaic Relay confirms that transaction-monitoring covers refunds/reversals.
- Review acquiring-partner/processor agreements to confirm refund windows and destination rules.
- Confirm Mosaic Relay's legal entity/licensing status and funds-flow model.
- Draft merchant and payer disclosures.
**Pros:**
- Reduces risk by ensuring that all controls are in place before launch.
- Allows Mosaic Relay to address any gaps in transaction-monitoring or acquiring-partner agreements.
**Cons:**
- Slowest time to market.
- May not meet the business goal if the delay is too long.
