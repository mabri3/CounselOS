    ## Matter summary

    Mosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor Pay in an asset purchase, with close targeted
    for November 15. The plan is to migrate 240,000 consumer users and 12,000 business
    accounts into Mosaic''s app on day one post-close, without re-verifying KYC, while
    assuming Harbor''s money transmitter licenses in four states where Mosaic lacks
    its own. Harbor''s transaction monitoring vendor contract terminates at close,
    and diligence has flagged approximately 40 open alerts and a handful of SARs that
    no one has reviewed. Marketing wants to launch a $50 first-transfer welcome bonus
    the day after close.

    ## Decision question

    Which legal workstreams—money transmission licensing, BSA/AML continuity, SAR/alert
    handling, monitoring transition, and bonus compliance—must be resolved before
    the November 15 close to avoid regulatory exposure, and which can be addressed
    post-close with interim controls?

    ## Likely answer

    Yes, multiple parts of this migration need legal review before close. The highest-risk
    items are: (1) the assumption that Harbor''s MTLs can simply be "used" by Mosaic
    in four states, which is almost certainly incorrect under state money transmission
    law; (2) the plan to migrate users without re-verification when Mosaic lacks underlying
    KYC documents for pre-2023 users, which creates a BSA/AML compliance gap unless
    Mosaic''s own CIP program permits reliance and the gap is addressed; (3) the unread
    open alerts and SARs, which represent unknown BSA/AML liability that must be triaged
    before close; and (4) the monitoring vendor transition, which must be validated

    ## 1. State money transmission licensing

    **The core problem:** Money transmitter licenses are generally not transferable
    or assignable. State money transmission statutes typically require the licensed
    entity to notify the regulator of a change of control or to apply for a new license
    when the licensed entity is acquired or its assets are transferred. In an asset
    purchase, the buyer does not automatically acquire the seller''s licenses.

    **What this means for the four new markets:** Mosaic cannot simply "use" Harbor''s
    licenses in the four states where it lacks its own license. If Harbor''s licenses
    are held in Harbor''s name and Harbor''s corporate entity will not survive the
    asset purchase as a licensed subsidiary, those licenses will likely lapse or be
    revoked. Mosaic would need to apply for new licenses in those states, which typically
    takes 3–6 months or longer. Operating without a license in a state where money
    transmission is regulated can result in cease-and-desist orders, fines, and criminal
    penalties.

    **What this means for the other 40 states:** Even in states where both Mosaic
    and Harbor hold licenses, the acquisition may trigger change-of-control or notice
    requirements. Many states require advance notice (often 30–90 days) before a change
    of control of a licensed entity. Failure to provide notice can result in license
    suspension or revocation.

    holds MTLs in 41 states, but it is not confirmed whether Mosaic operates as a

              ## 1. State money transmission licensing

              **The core problem:** Money transmitter licenses are generally not transferable
              or assignable. State money transmission statutes typically require the
              licensed entity to notify the regulator of a change of control or to
              apply for a new license when the licensed entity is acquired or its
              assets are transferred. In an asset purchase, the buyer does not automatically
              acquire the seller''s licenses.

              **What this means for the four new markets:** Mosaic cannot simply "use"
              Harbor''s licenses in the four states where it lacks its own license.
              If Harbor''s licenses are held in Harbor''s name and Harbor''s corporate
              entity will not survive the asset purchase as a licensed subsidiary,
              those licenses will likely lapse or be revoked. Mosaic would need to
              apply for new licenses in those states, which typically takes 3–6 months
              or longer. Operating without a license in a state where money transmission
              is regulated can result in cease-and-desist orders, fines, and criminal
              penalties.

              **What this means for the other 40 states:** Even in states where both
              Mosaic and Harbor hold licenses, the acquisition may trigger change-of-control
              or notice requirements. Many states require advance notice (often 30–90
              days) before a change of control of a licensed entity. Failure to provide
              notice can result in license suspension or revocation.

              **Uncertainty about Mosaic''s regulated status:** The request states
              that Mosaic holds MTLs in 41 states, but it is not confirmed whether
              Mosaic operates as a licensed money transmitter or under a partner-bank
              model. If Mosaic operates under a bank partnership, the licensing analysis
              may differ—the bank''s licenses may cover the activity, but the bank''s
              consent and regulatory approvals may be required.

              **Viable paths:**

              - **Path A: Delay close in the four new states.** Mosaic applies for
              new licenses in the four states where it lacks coverage and delays onboarding
              customers in those states until licenses are granted. This is the safest
              path but delays the "nice win" of opening four new markets.

              - **Path B: Structure the deal to preserve Harbor''s licenses.** If
              Harbor''s corporate entity survives as a subsidiary of Mosaic and retains
              its licenses, Mosaic may be able to operate under Harbor''s licenses
              in the four new states, subject to change-of-control notice and approval.
              This requires restructuring the deal as a stock purchase or a hybrid
              structure.

              - **Path C: Partner-bank reliance.** If Mosaic operates under a partner-bank
              model, confirm whether the bank''s licenses cover the four new states
              and whether the bank consents to the expansion.

              ## 2. BSA/AML and KYC/CIP obligations

              **The core problem:** Under the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA), money transmitters
              are required to maintain a Customer Identification Program (CIP) that
              includes verifying the identity of customers and maintaining records
              of the information used to verify identity. When Mosaic migrates Harbor''s
              users without re-verification, it is relying on Harbor''s prior KYC.
              However, Mosaic lacks the underlying KYC documents for users onboarded
              before 2023 because Harbor''s KYC vendor shut down in 2024.

              **What this means:** Mosaic''s own CIP program must permit reliance
              on another institution''s prior KYC, and Mosaic must have a reasonable
              basis for believing that Harbor''s KYC was adequate. The pass/fail results
              alone may not be sufficient—Mosaic may need the underlying documents
              to demonstrate that Harbor''s KYC met regulatory standards. The absence
              of underlying documents for pre-2023 users creates a compliance gap.

              **FinCEN guidance:** FinCEN has issued guidance on reliance on another
              financial institution''s CIP, but that guidance generally requires a
              written agreement and a reasonable basis for reliance. The absence of
              underlying documents may undermine the reasonableness of reliance.
