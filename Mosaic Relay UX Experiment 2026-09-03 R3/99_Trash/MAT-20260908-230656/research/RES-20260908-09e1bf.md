**General pattern (unverified):** Most state money-transmitter licensing regimes treat a change of control of a licensed entity as a regulated event. The typical structure is:
1. **Prior approval required.** Many states require the licensee to obtain the regulator's written approval before a change of control becomes effective. The definition of "control" commonly turns on ownership or voting-power thresholds (often 10% or 25%), and may include presumptions of control and treatment of indirect acquisitions through holding companies.
2. **Application and review period.** Where prior approval is required, the licensee typically must file an application (sometimes through NMLS) with supporting documentation about the acquiring party, its financial condition, and its compliance program. Statutory review windows vary; some states have deemed-approval mechanisms if the regulator does not act within a specified period, while others have no such backstop.
3. **Post-closing notice.** A smaller number of states require only post-closing notice, but this is less common for money transmitter licenses than for some other license types.
