    SARs and open alerts create immediate BSA/AML successor-liability exposure, and
    the change-of-control approval timelines in California, New York, and Florida
    likely exceed your 10-week runway, making the current Nov 15 date untenable without
    regulator engagement or a structure change.

    ---

    ## Matter summary

    Mosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor Pay in a deal targeted to close Nov 15 (~10 weeks).
    Day-1 post-close, 240k consumer and 12k business accounts migrate to Mosaic Relay’s
    app with unified balances and no re-verification. Diligence flagged ~40 open alerts
    and “a handful” of SARs at Harbor that no one has read yet. The combined MTL footprint
    opens four new markets: CA, IA, NY, and FL.

    ## Decision question

    Can Mosaic Relay close the Harbor Pay acquisition on Nov 15 and migrate 252k accounts
    on day 1 without (a) reviewing Harbor’s open SARs and alerts, (b) obtaining change-of-control
    approvals in CA, NY, and FL, and (c) inheriting undisclosed BSA/AML liability,
    or must the close be delayed or restructured to accommodate state approval timelines
    and SAR due diligence?

    ---

    ## Likely rules and issues

    ### 1. FinCEN SAR and recordkeeping obligations survive the acquisition

    **SAR filing duties transfer to the successor.** As a money services business
    (MSB), Harbor is required to file SARs for suspicious transactions of $2,000 or
    more [source:SRC-2eae7ecf5a9ce404b656]. When Mosaic Relay acquires Harbor’s assets
    and customer base, it inherits the obligation to continue monitoring those accounts
    and file SARs on suspicious activity detected post-close. More critically, **the
    ~40 open alerts and historical SARs represent unfinished compliance work** that
    becomes Mosaic Relay’s problem on day 1.

    **Record retention is non-negotiable.** FinCEN requires MSBs to retain SARs and
    supporting documentation for five years [source:SRC-13a1c9d0c4dfc56d5c54]. The
    fact that Harbor’s pre-2023 KYC documents are lost (vendor shutdown) creates a
    recordkeeping gap. While FinCEN’s October 2025 FAQs clarify that institutions
    are not required to file SARs solely based on transaction amount [source:SRC-05ef668fbe36b4969a2a],
    they do not excuse missing underlying documentation for existing customers.

    **Confidentiality and safe harbor.** SARs are confidential, but federal law provides
    complete civil-liability protection for good-faith reporting [source:SRC-af9157724c1139e824db].
    This protects Mosaic Relay if it files SARs on inherited suspicious activity,
    but it does not protect against regulatory action for failing to review known
    alerts.

    ### 2. State change-of-control requirements vs. the 10-week timeline

    The four new markets (CA, IA, NY, FL) each have distinct change-of-control regimes
    that likely conflict with a Nov 15 close:

    | State | Requirement | Typical Timeline | Feasibility in 10 Weeks |

    |-------|-------------|------------------|-------------------------|

    | **California** | Prior approval required for change of control of a money transmitter
    (Cal. Fin. Code § 2100 et seq.). DFPI must approve the acquisition before close.
    | 60–90 days for standard review; complex deals can take 4–6 months. | **At risk.**
    Even if filed immediately, approval may not issue by Nov 15. |

    | **New York** | Prior approval required for change of control under 23 NYCRR
    Part 200 (BitLicense regime) and/or MTL statutes. NYDFS conducts thorough background
    checks. | 90–120 days minimum; often 6+ months for money transmitters. | **Not
    feasible.** NYDFS rarely approves MTL changes in under 3 months. |

    | **Florida** | Prior approval required for change of control of a money transmitter
    (Fla. Stat. § 560.204). OFR must approve the new control person. | 30–60 days
    for complete applications. | **Tight but possible** if application is filed immediately
    and Harbor’s compliance history is clean. |

    | **Iowa** | Notice-based or abbreviated approval for change of control (Iowa
    Code § 533C.204). Generally less burdensome than CA/NY. | 30 days or less. | **Likely
    feasible** within 10 weeks. |

    **Critical path:** New York and California are the long poles. Without pre-close
    approval from NYDFS and DFPI, Mosaic Relay cannot legally operate in those states
    post-close. If Harbor currently serves NY or CA residents, closing without approval
    risks unlicensed activity.

    ### 3. The “no re-verification” plan creates CIP/recordkeeping risk

    Mosaic Relay’s plan to skip re-verification relies on Harbor’s legacy KYC. However,
    FinCEN’s Customer Identification Program (CIP) rules require institutions to retain
    identity-verification documents. The absence of pre-2023 underlying documents
    (only pass/fail results) means Mosaic Relay may be unable to demonstrate compliance
    with CIP recordkeeping requirements for a portion of the 240k consumer accounts.
    This is a remediable gap, but it requires a risk-based decision on whether to
    re-verify high-risk accounts or accept the documentation deficiency.

    ---

    ## Viable paths

    **Path A: Delay close to Q1 2026 (recommended)**

    - File change-of-control applications in CA, NY, and FL immediately.

    - Use the extended diligence period to review the ~40 open alerts and SARs.

    - Remediate or re-verify high-risk pre-2023 accounts.

    - **Trade-off:** Delays revenue synergy and market entry by 3–4 months.

    **Path B: Close on Nov 15 with regulatory carve-outs**

    - Close the corporate transaction but **do not migrate NY or CA customers** until
    state approvals issue.

    - Operate Harbor as a separate subsidiary in NY/CA under its existing licenses
    until approvals are obtained.

    - Review SARs/alerts pre-close and escrow funds for potential BSA/AML fines.

    - **Trade-off:** Complex operational split; delays “one unified account” value
    proposition; may still trigger “doing business” issues if NY/CA customers are
    migrated prematurely.

    **Path C: Close on Nov 15 as planned (high risk)**

    - Proceed without state approvals or SAR review.

    - **Consequence:** Likely enforcement action in NY and CA for unlicensed money
    transmission; potential FinCEN penalties for failing to monitor inherited accounts;
    successor liability for Harbor’s unresolved SARs.

    ---

    ## Facts that could change the answer

    - **Whether Harbor currently operates in NY or CA.** If Harbor lacks NY/CA licenses
    (and thus no NY/CA customers), the change-of-control burden drops significantly.

    - **The content of the ~40 open alerts.** If they involve potential structuring
    or money laundering by migrated customers, Mosaic Relay may need to file back
    SARs or freeze accounts, delaying migration.

    - **Whether the deal is structured as an asset purchase vs. stock purchase.**
    Asset purchases may allow Mosaic Relay to leave behind certain liabilities, but
    state MTL laws often treat any transfer of control as requiring approval regardless
    of structure.

    ---

    ## Recommended last-mile verification

    1. **Immediately confirm Harbor’s license status in NY and CA** and whether any
    NY/CA residents are among the 240k consumers.

    2. **Engage NYDFS and DFPI informally** this week to gauge realistic approval
    timelines for a change-of-control application.

    3. **Review the ~40 open alerts and SARs before signing** (or at least before
    close) to quantify potential BSA/AML exposure.

    4. **Verify whether Harbor’s monitoring SaaS contract allows assignment** or if
    Mosaic Relay must build a parallel monitoring environment pre-close to avoid a
    gap in transaction surveillance.

    ---

    ## Assumptions

    - Mosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor’s assets and customer accounts (not just stock),
    making it the successor MSB for BSA purposes.

    - Harbor currently holds active MTLs in CA, IA, NY, and FL, and serves customers
    in those states.

    - The “four new markets” are states where Mosaic Relay currently lacks licenses
    but Harbor has them.

    ## Remaining gaps

    - **State-specific statutory citations** for CA, NY, FL, and IA change-of-control
    requirements were not retrieved in this search; the timelines above are based
    on general regulatory practice.

    - **FinCEN’s specific guidance on successor liability for SARs** in M&A contexts
    (as opposed to ongoing reporting duties) was not directly retrieved; the analysis
    relies on general MSB SAR obligations.'

# What are the current FinCEN rules and guidance on suspicious

## Question

What are the current FinCEN rules and guidance on suspicious activity reporting (SAR) and record retention obligations for a money services business after an acquisition or change of control, including whether a successor institution inherits the predecessor's SAR and recordkeeping duties? Also, what are the current money-transmitter change-of-control notice or prior-approval requirements in California, Iowa, New York, and Florida, and how do those approval timelines compare to a ~10-week close?

## Working Analysis

**Short answer:** Yes — this needs legal review before close. The unreviewed SARs and open alerts create immediate BSA/AML successor-liability exposure, and the change-of-control approval timelines in California, New York, and Florida likely exceed your 10-week runway, making the current Nov 15 date untenable without regulator engagement or a structure change.
---
## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor Pay in a deal targeted to close Nov 15 (~10 weeks). Day-1 post-close, 240k consumer and 12k business accounts migrate to Mosaic Relay’s app with unified balances and no re-verification. Diligence flagged ~40 open alerts and “a handful” of SARs at Harbor that no one has read yet. The combined MTL footprint opens four new markets: CA, IA, NY, and FL.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay close the Harbor Pay acquisition on Nov 15 and migrate 252k accounts on day 1 without (a) reviewing Harbor’s open SARs and alerts, (b) obtaining change-of-control approvals in CA, NY, and FL, and (c) inheriting undisclosed BSA/AML liability, or must the close be delayed or restructured to accommodate state approval timelines and SAR due diligence?
---
## Likely rules and issues
### 1. FinCEN SAR and recordkeeping obligations survive the acquisition
**SAR filing duties transfer to the successor.** As a money services business (MSB), Harbor is required to file SARs for suspicious transactions of $2,000 or more [source:SRC-2eae7ecf5a9ce404b656]. When Mosaic Relay acquires Harbor’s assets and customer base, it inherits the obligation to continue monitoring those accounts and file SARs on suspicious activity detected post-close. More critically, **the ~40 open alerts and historical SARs represent unfinished compliance work** that becomes Mosaic Relay’s problem on day 1.
**Record retention is non-negotiable.** FinCEN requires MSBs to retain SARs and supporting documentation for five years [source:SRC-13a1c9d0c4dfc56d5c54]. The fact that Harbor’s pre-2023 KYC documents are lost (vendor shutdown) creates a recordkeeping gap. While FinCEN’s October 2025 FAQs clarify that institutions are not required to file SARs solely based on transaction amount [source:SRC-05ef668fbe36b4969a2a], they do not excuse missing underlying documentation for existing customers.
**Confidentiality and safe harbor.** SARs are confidential, but federal law provides complete civil-liability protection for good-faith reporting [source:SRC-af9157724c1139e824db]. This protects Mosaic Relay if it files SARs on inherited suspicious activity, but it does not protect against regulatory action for failing to review known alerts.
### 2. State change-of-control requirements vs. the 10-week timeline
The four new markets (CA, IA, NY, FL) each have distinct change-of-control regimes that likely conflict with a Nov 15 close:
| State | Requirement | Typical Timeline | Feasibility in 10 Weeks |
|-------|-------------|------------------|-------------------------|
| **California** | Prior approval required for change of control of a money transmitter (Cal. Fin. Code § 2100 et seq.). DFPI must approve the acquisition before close. | 60–90 days for standard review; complex deals can take 4–6 months. | **At risk.** Even if filed immediately, approval may not issue by Nov 15. |
| **New York** | Prior approval required for change of control under 23 NYCRR Part 200 (BitLicense regime) and/or MTL statutes. NYDFS conducts thorough background checks. | 90–120 days minimum; often 6+ months for money transmitters. | **Not feasible.** NYDFS rarely approves MTL changes in under 3 months. |
| **Florida** | Prior approval required for change of control of a money transmitter (Fla. Stat. § 560.204). OFR must approve the new control person. | 30–60 days for complete applications. | **Tight but possible** if application is filed immediately and Harbor’s compliance history is clean. |
| **Iowa** | Notice-based or abbreviated approval for change of control (Iowa Code § 533C.204). Generally less burdensome than CA/NY. | 30 days or less. | **Likely feasible** within 10 weeks. |
**Critical path:** New York and California are the long poles. Without pre-close approval from NYDFS and DFPI, Mosaic Relay cannot legally operate in those states post-close. If Harbor currently serves NY or CA residents, closing without approval risks unlicensed activity.
### 3. The “no re-verification” plan creates CIP/recordkeeping risk
Mosaic Relay’s plan to skip re-verification relies on Harbor’s legacy KYC. However, FinCEN’s Customer Identification Program (CIP) rules require institutions to retain identity-verification documents. The absence of pre-2023 underlying documents (only pass/fail results) means Mosaic Relay may be unable to demonstrate compliance with CIP recordkeeping requirements for a portion of the 240k consumer accounts. This is a remediable gap, but it requires a risk-based decision on whether to re-verify high-risk accounts or accept the documentation deficiency.
---
## Viable paths
**Path A: Delay close to Q1 2026 (recommended)**
- File change-of-control applications in CA, NY, and FL immediately.
- Use the extended diligence period to review the ~40 open alerts and SARs.
- Remediate or re-verify high-risk pre-2023 accounts.
- **Trade-off:** Delays revenue synergy and market entry by 3–4 months.
**Path B: Close on Nov 15 with regulatory carve-outs**
- Close the corporate transaction but **do not migrate NY or CA customers** until state approvals issue.
- Operate Harbor as a separate subsidiary in NY/CA under its existing licenses until approvals are obtained.
- Review SARs/alerts pre-close and escrow funds for potential BSA/AML fines.
- **Trade-off:** Complex operational split; delays “one unified account” value proposition; may still trigger “doing business” issues if NY/CA customers are migrated prematurely.
**Path C: Close on Nov 15 as planned (high risk)**
- Proceed without state approvals or SAR review.
- **Consequence:** Likely enforcement action in NY and CA for unlicensed money transmission; potential FinCEN penalties for failing to monitor inherited accounts; successor liability for Harbor’s unresolved SARs.
---
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Whether Harbor currently operates in NY or CA.** If Harbor lacks NY/CA licenses (and thus no NY/CA customers), the change-of-control burden drops significantly.
- **The content of the ~40 open alerts.** If they involve potential structuring or money laundering by migrated customers, Mosaic Relay may need to file back SARs or freeze accounts, delaying migration.
- **Whether the deal is structured as an asset purchase vs. stock purchase.** Asset purchases may allow Mosaic Relay to leave behind certain liabilities, but state MTL laws often treat any transfer of control as requiring approval regardless of structure.
---
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Immediately confirm Harbor’s license status in NY and CA** and whether any NY/CA residents are among the 240k consumers.
2. **Engage NYDFS and DFPI informally** this week to gauge realistic approval timelines for a change-of-control application.
3. **Review the ~40 open alerts and SARs before signing** (or at least before close) to quantify potential BSA/AML exposure.
4. **Verify whether Harbor’s monitoring SaaS contract allows assignment** or if Mosaic Relay must build a parallel monitoring environment pre-close to avoid a gap in transaction surveillance.
---
## Assumptions
- Mosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor’s assets and customer accounts (not just stock), making it the successor MSB for BSA purposes.
- Harbor currently holds active MTLs in CA, IA, NY, and FL, and serves customers in those states.
- The “four new markets” are states where Mosaic Relay currently lacks licenses but Harbor has them.
## Remaining gaps
- **State-specific statutory citations** for CA, NY, FL, and IA change-of-control requirements were not retrieved in this search; the timelines above are based on general regulatory practice.
- **FinCEN’s specific guidance on successor liability for SARs** in M&A contexts (as opposed to ongoing reporting duties) was not directly retrieved; the analysis relies on general MSB SAR obligations.
