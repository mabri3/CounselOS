# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor Pay in a deal targeted to close Nov 15 (~10 weeks). Day-1 post-close, 240k consumer and 12k business accounts migrate to Mosaic Relay’s app with unified balances and no re-verification. Diligence flagged ~40 open alerts and “a handful” of SARs at Harbor that no one has read yet. The combined MTL footprint opens four new markets: CA, IA, NY, and FL.

## Decision question

Does any part of the Harbor Pay acquisition and day-1 migration need legal review before the Nov 15 close, or is it "really just a data migration"?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor Pay; close targeted Nov 15 (~10 weeks out).
- Day-1 post-close migration moves 240k consumer users and 12k business accounts into Mosaic Relay's app with one unified account and one balance.
- Plan is no re-verification; Harbor users were already KYC'd by Harbor.
- Harbor holds money transmitter licenses (MTLs) in 38 states; Mosaic Relay holds 41; combined 44, opening four new markets.
- Harbor's KYC ran through a vendor that shut down in 2024; Mosaic Relay has pass/fail results for everyone but not underlying documents for users onboarded before 2023.
- Harbor's transaction monitoring is a third-party SaaS whose contract terminates at close; plan is to repoint Harbor's transaction feed at Mosaic Relay's engine on day 1.
- Marketing wants a 'Welcome' campaign with a $50 bonus on first transfer, launching close+1.
- Diligence flagged ~40 open alerts and 'a handful' of SARs from the last year at Harbor; none have been read yet.
- Have the ~40 open alerts and the SARs from the last year been reviewed yet, and what do they show? — The ~40 open alerts and the SARs from the last year have not been reviewed yet.
- The four new markets opened by the combined MTL footprint are CA, IA, NY, and FL.

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter subject to state MTL requirements and BSA/AML obligations in the states where it operates.
- The acquired Harbor accounts and balances transfer to Mosaic Relay as the licensed entity.

## Issues and workstreams

- BSA/AML exposure from unreviewed ~40 open alerts and SARs
- KYC recordkeeping gap for pre-2023 onboarded users
- MTL footprint and state change-of-control notice/approval in CA, IA, NY, FL
- Day-1 transaction monitoring continuity after Harbor SaaS contract terminates
- $50 Welcome bonus characterization and consumer-protection disclosures
- Consumer-protection disclosures for unified-account migration

## Open questions

- Whether the pre-2023 underlying KYC documents can be reconstructed
- Whether the $50 bonus is offered to all migrated users and tied to any condition
- Whether Harbor's and Mosaic Relay's MTL footprints overlap in any state

## Options or working recommendation

# Working recommendation — Harbor Pay acquisition and day-1 migration

## Leading theory

This is not "really just a data migration." The Harbor Pay acquisition and day-1 migration require legal review before the Nov 15 close, and at least one item is decision-changing on its own. The leading view is that the deal can close, but not on the current Nov 15 plan without addressing two gating items: (1) the unreviewed ~40 open alerts and SARs, and (2) the change-of-control approval path in CA, NY, and FL.

## Still-plausible alternatives

- **Delay close until the necessary review and approvals are complete.** File change-of-control applications in CA, NY, FL immediately, use the extended diligence window to review SARs/alerts, and remediate or re-verify high-risk pre-2023 accounts. Trade-off: delays revenue synergy and market entry; the duration needs confirmation.
- **Close on Nov 15 with regulatory carve-outs.** Close the corporate transaction but do not migrate NY or CA customers until state approvals issue; operate Harbor as a separate subsidiary in NY/CA under its existing licenses; review SARs/alerts pre-close and escrow funds for potential BSA/AML fines. Trade-off: complex operational split and delays the "one unified account" value proposition.
- **Close on Nov 15 as planned (high risk, not recommended).** Proceed without state approvals or SAR review. Likely enforcement action in NY and CA for unlicensed money transmission, potential FinCEN penalties for failing to monitor inherited accounts, and successor liability for Harbor's unresolved SARs.

## Material facts and assumptions

- Mosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor Pay; close targeted Nov 15 (~10 weeks out). Day-1 migration moves 240k consumer and 12k business accounts with one unified account and balance, no re-verification.
- Harbor holds MTLs in 38 states; Mosaic Relay holds 41; combined 44, opening four new markets: CA, IA, NY, FL.
- Harbor's KYC ran through a vendor that shut down in 2024; only pass/fail results exist for pre-2023 onboarded users (no underlying documents).
- Harbor's transaction monitoring is a third-party SaaS whose contract terminates at close; plan is to repoint the feed at Mosaic Relay's engine on day 1.
- Marketing wants a $50 first-transfer "Welcome" bonus launching close+1.
- Diligence flagged ~40 open alerts and "a handful" of SARs from the last year; none have been reviewed yet (reported fact).
- Assumption: Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter subject to state MTL and BSA/AML obligations in the states where it operates.
- Assumption: The acquired Harbor accounts and balances transfer to Mosaic Relay as the licensed entity.
- Assumption (unverified): Harbor currently holds active MTLs in and serves customers in CA, IA, NY, and FL.

## What could change the recommendation

- **Content of the ~40 open alerts and SARs** — the single most decision-changing item. If they involve structuring or money laundering by migrated customers, Mosaic Relay may need to file back SARs or freeze accounts, delaying migration.
- **Transaction structure** — stock purchase/merger vs. asset purchase determines whether change-of-control approval attaches to Harbor's licenses or raises separate licensing questions for Mosaic Relay in the four new states.
- **Percentage of voting equity/power changing hands** — whether it meets each state's control threshold.
- **Whether Harbor actually operates in NY or CA** — if not, the change-of-control burden drops significantly.
- **State approval timelines** — the CA/NY/FL timelines are unverified working hypotheses; NY and CA commonly exceed 10 weeks.
