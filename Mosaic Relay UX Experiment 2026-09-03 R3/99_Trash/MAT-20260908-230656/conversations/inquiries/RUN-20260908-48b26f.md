**Conclusion** — No, this is not "really just a data migration." Several parts of the Harbor Pay acquisition and day-1 migration need legal review before the Nov 15 close, and at least one item (the unreviewed SARs/alerts) is decision-changing on its own.

**Rule** — As a money services business, Mosaic Relay inherits BSA/AML obligations for the acquired accounts, including continuing transaction monitoring and SAR filing on suspicious activity, and must retain SARs and supporting documentation for five years [source:SRC-2eae7ecf5a9ce404b656][source:SRC-13a1c9d0c4dfc56d5c54]. State money-transmitter regimes in the four new markets (CA, IA, NY, FL) generally treat a change of control of a licensed entity as a regulated event requiring notice or prior approval. The state-specific statutes were not retrieved, so the approval timelines are unverified working hypotheses.

**Analysis** — The single most consequential item is the ~40 open alerts and "a handful" of SARs that no one has read. These are unfinished compliance work that becomes Mosaic Relay's problem on day 1, and they can create BSA/AML exposure, regulator notice obligations, and even affect whether the deal can close as planned. That alone refutes the "just a data migration" framing. Beyond that:

- **Change of control in CA, NY, FL** — If the deal transfers control of Harbor as the licensed entity, prior approval is likely required, and NY and CA timelines commonly exceed 10 weeks. Closing without approval risks unlicensed activity in those states. This is the biggest schedule risk to Nov 15.
- **KYC recordkeeping gap** — Pre-2023 users have only pass/fail results, no underlying documents. That is a CIP/recordkeeping gap needing a risk-based decision on re-verification of high-risk accounts.
- **Monitoring continuity** — Repointing Harbor's feed at Mosaic Relay's engine on day 1 creates a surveillance gap risk if not built and tested pre-close.
- **$50 Welcome bonus** — Needs characterization (inducement/consumer-protection disclosure) review, but this is lower priority.

**Conclusion / next step** — The honest answer to Dana is: this is not just a data migration; it needs legal review before close, and the critical path is (1) review the SARs/alerts now, and (2) confirm the transaction structure and Harbor's license status in CA/NY/FL to determine whether change-of-control approvals can realistically be obtained by Nov 15. The most decision-changing unknown is the content of those unreviewed SARs and alerts.

**Assumptions used** — Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter subject to state MTL and BSA/AML obligations; the acquired accounts and balances transfer to Mosaic Relay as the licensed entity; and Harbor currently holds active MTLs in and serves customers in CA, IA, NY, and FL. The state approval timelines are unverified and need confirmation against the actual statutes and regulators.
