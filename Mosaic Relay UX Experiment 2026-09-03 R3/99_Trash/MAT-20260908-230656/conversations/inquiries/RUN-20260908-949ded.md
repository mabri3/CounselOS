  available_excerpt: "# What are the current FinCEN rules and guidance on suspicious\n\
    \n## Question\n\nWhat are the current FinCEN rules and guidance on suspicious\
    \ activity reporting (SAR) and record retention obligations for a money services\
    \ business after an acquisition or change of control, including whether a successor\
    \ institution inherits the predecessor's SAR and recordkeeping duties? Also, what\
    \ are the current money-transmitter change-of-control notice or prior-approval\
    \ requirements in California, Iowa, New York, and Florida, and how do those approval\
    \ timelines compare to a ~10-week close?\n\n## Working Analysis\n\n**Short answer:**\
    \ Yes — this needs legal review before close. The unreviewed SARs and open alerts\
    \ create immediate BSA/AML successor-liability exposure, and the change-of-control\
    \ approval timelines in California, New York, and Florida likely exceed your 10-week\
    \ runway, making the current Nov 15 date untenable without regulator engagement\
    \ or a structure change.\n---\n## Matter summary\nMosaic Relay is acquiring Harbor\
    \ Pay in a deal targeted to close Nov 15 (~10 weeks). Day-1 post-close, 240k consumer\
    \ and 12k business accounts migrate to Mosaic Relay’s app with unified balances\
    \ and no re-verification. Diligence flagged ~40 open alerts and “a handful” of\
    \ SARs at Harbor that no one has read yet. The combined MTL footprint opens four\
    \ new markets: CA, IA, NY, and FL.\n## Decision question\nCan Mosaic Relay close\
    \ the Harbor Pay acquisition on Nov 15 and migrate 252k accounts on day 1 without\
    \ (a) reviewing Harbor’s open SARs and alerts, (b) obtaining change-of-control\
    \ approvals in CA, NY, and FL, and (c) inheriting undisclosed BSA/AML liability,\
    \ or must the close be delayed or restructured to accommodate state approval timelines\
    \ and SAR due diligence?\n---\n## Likely rules and issues\n### 1. FinCEN SAR and\
    \ recordkeeping obligations survive the acquisition\n**SAR filing duties transfer\
    \ to the successor.** As a money services business (MSB), Harbor is required to\
    \ file SARs for suspicious transactions of $2,000 or more [source:SRC-2eae7ecf5a9ce404b656].\
    \ When Mosaic Relay acquires Harbor’s assets and customer base, it inherits the\
    \ obligation to continue monitoring those accounts and file SARs on suspicious\
    \ activity detected post-close. More critically, **the ~40 open alerts and historical\
    \ SARs represent unfinished compliance work** that becomes Mosaic Relay’s problem\
    \ on day 1.\n**Record retention is non-negotiable.** FinCEN requires MSBs to retain\
    \ SARs and supporting documentation for five years [source:SRC-13a1c9d0c4dfc56d5c54].\
    \ The fact that Harbor’s pre-2023 KYC documents are lost (vendor shutdown) creates\
    \ a recordkeeping gap. While FinCEN’s October 2025 FAQs clarify that institutions\
    \ are not required to file SARs solely based on transaction amount [source:SRC-05ef668fbe36b4969a2a],\
    \ they do not excuse missing underlying documentation for existing customers.\n\
    **Confidentiality and safe harbor.** SARs are confidential, but federal law provides\
    \ complete civil-liability protection for good-faith reporting [source:SRC-af9157724c1139e824db].\
    \ This protects Mosaic Relay if it files SARs on inherited suspicious activity,\
    \ but it does not protect against regulatory action for failing to review known\
    \ alerts.\n### 2. State change-of-control requirements vs. the 10-week timeline\n\
    The four new markets (CA, IA, NY, FL) each have distinct change-of-control regimes\
    \ that likely conflict with a Nov 15 close:\n| State | Requirement | Typical Timeline\
    \ | Feasibility in 10 Weeks |\n|-------|-------------|------------------|-------------------------|\n\
    | **California** | Prior approval required for change of control of a money transmitter\
    \ (Cal. Fin. Code § 2100 et seq.). DFPI must approve the acquisition before close.\
    \ | 60–90 days for standard review; complex deals can take 4–6 months. | **At\
    \ risk.** Even if filed immediately, approval may not issue by Nov 15. |\n| **New\
    \ York** | Prior approval required for change of control under 23 NYCRR Part 200\
    \ (BitLicense regime) and/or MTL statutes. NYDFS conducts thorough background\
    \ checks. | 90–120 days minimum; often 6+ months for money transmitters. | **Not\
    \ feasible.** NYDFS rarely approves MTL changes in under 3 months. |\n| **Florida**\
    \ | Prior approval required for change of control of a money transmitter (Fla.\
    \ Stat. § 560.204). OFR must approve the new control person. | 30–60 days for\
    \ complete applications. | **Tight but possible** if application is filed immediately\
    \ and Harbor’s compliance history is clean. |\n| **Iowa** | Notice-based or abbreviated\
    \ approval for change of control (Iowa Code § 533C.204). Generally less burdensome\
    \ than CA/NY. | 30 days or less. | **Likely feasible** within 10 weeks. |\n**Critical\
    \ path:** New York and California are the long poles. Without pre-close approval\
    \ from NYDFS and DFPI, Mosaic Relay cannot legally operate in those states post-close.\
    \ If Harbor currently serves NY or CA residents, closing without approval risks\
    \ unlicensed activity.\n### 3. The “no re-verification” plan creates CIP/recordkeeping\
    \ risk\nMosaic Relay’s plan to skip re-verification relies on Harbor’s legacy\
    \ KYC. However, FinCEN’s Customer Identification Program (CIP) rules require institutions\
    \ to retain identity-verification documents. The absence of pre-2023 underlying\
    \ documents (only pass/fail results) means Mosaic Relay may be unable to demonstrate\
    \ compliance with CIP recordkeeping requirements for a portion of the 240k consumer\
    \ accounts. This is a remediable gap, but it requires a risk-based decision on\
    \ whether to re-verify high-risk accounts or accept the documentation deficiency.\n\
    ---\n## Viable paths\n**Path A: Delay close to Q1 2026 (recommended)**\n- File\
    \ change-of-control applications in CA, NY, and FL immediately.\n- Use the extended\
    \ diligence period to review the ~40 open alerts and SARs.\n- Remediate or re-verify\
    \ high-risk pre-2023 accounts.\n- **Trade-off:** Delays revenue synergy and market\
    \ entry by 3–4 months.\n**Path B: Close on Nov 15 with regulatory carve-outs**\n\
    - Close the corporate transaction but **do not migrate NY or CA customers** until\
    \ state approvals issue.\n- Operate Harbor as a separate subsidiary in NY/CA under\
    \ its existing licenses until approvals are obtained.\n- Review SARs/alerts pre-close\
    \ and escrow funds for potential BSA/AML fines.\n- **Trade-off:** Complex operational\
    \ split; delays “one unified account” value proposition; may still trigger “doing\
    \ business” issues if NY/CA customers are migrated prematurely.\n**Path C: Close\
    \ on Nov 15 as planned (high risk)**\n- Proceed without state approvals or SAR\
    \ review.\n- **Consequence:** Likely enforcement action in NY and CA for unlicensed\
    \ money transmission; potential FinCEN penalties for failing to monitor inherited\
    \ accounts; successor liability for Harbor’s unresolved SARs.\n---\n## Facts that\
    \ could change the answer\n- **Whether Harbor currently operates in NY or CA.**\
    \ If Harbor lacks NY/CA licenses (and thus no NY/CA customers), the change-of-control\
    \ burden drops significantly.\n- **The content of the ~40 open alerts.** If they\
    \ involve potential structuring or money laundering by migrated customers, Mosaic\
    \ Relay may need to file back SARs or freeze accounts, delaying migration.\n-\
    \ **Whether the deal is structured as an asset purchase vs. stock purchase.**\
    \ Asset purchases may allow Mosaic Relay to leave behind certain liabilities,\
    \ but state MTL laws often treat any transfer of control as requiring approval\
    \ regardless of structure.\n---\n## Recommended last-mile verification\n1. **Immediately\
    \ confirm Harbor’s license status in NY and CA** and whether any NY/CA residents\

The fresh research run completed and retrieved external authority this time. Here is the IRAC based on that new research.

**Issue** — Whether Mosaic Relay can close the Harbor Pay acquisition on Nov 15 and migrate 252k accounts on day 1 without first resolving Harbor's unreviewed ~40 open alerts and past-year SARs, and without obtaining state change-of-control approvals in the four new markets (CA, IA, NY, FL) — or whether the close must be delayed or restructured.

**Rule** — As a money services business, Harbor is required to file SARs on suspicious transactions of $2,000 or more, and FinCEN requires MSBs to retain SARs and supporting documentation for five years [source:SRC-2eae7ecf5a9ce404b656][source:SRC-13a1c9d0c4dfc56d5c54]. FinCEN's October 2025 SAR FAQs clarify that institutions are not required to file SARs solely based on transaction amount [source:SRC-05ef668fbe36b4969a2a]. Federal law provides complete civil-liability protection for good-faith SAR reporting [source:SRC-af9157724c1139e824db]. On the state side, California (Cal. Fin. Code § 2100 et seq.), New York (23 NYCRR Part 200 / MTL statutes), and Florida (Fla. Stat. § 560.204) require prior approval for a change of control of a money transmitter; Iowa (Iowa Code § 533C.204) uses a lighter notice-based or abbreviated approval.

**Analysis** — The unreviewed alerts and SARs are the single most decision-changing item: they represent unfinished compliance work that becomes Mosaic Relay's problem on day 1, and the research concludes they create immediate BSA/AML successor-liability exposure. The state change-of-control timelines are the second critical constraint. The research assesses New York as not feasible within 10 weeks (90–120 days minimum, often 6+ months), California as at risk (60–90 days standard, 4–6 months for complex deals), Florida as tight but possible (30–60 days), and Iowa as likely feasible (30 days or less). Without pre-close approval from NYDFS and DFPI, Mosaic Relay cannot legally operate in NY or CA post-close, and closing without it risks unlicensed money transmission. The "no re-verification" plan also creates a CIP recordkeeping gap for pre-2023 accounts where only pass/fail results exist.

**Conclusion** — This is not "just a data migration." The research supports that the Nov 15 close as planned is high-risk: it requires either delaying to obtain state approvals and review the SARs/alerts, or restructuring (e.g., closing the corporate transaction but carving out NY/CA customers until approvals issue, and reviewing SARs pre-close with escrow for potential fines). The two facts most likely to change the answer are whether Harbor actually operates in NY/CA and the content of the ~40 open alerts.

Two honesty caveats on the support: the retrieved sources are marked "retrieved" but carry **no available excerpts**, so I have not verified their text — treat the statutory citations and timelines as **Needs checking**. And the research itself flags that the state-specific statutory citations and FinCEN's specific successor-liability guidance were not directly retrieved; the state timelines rest on general regulatory practice.
