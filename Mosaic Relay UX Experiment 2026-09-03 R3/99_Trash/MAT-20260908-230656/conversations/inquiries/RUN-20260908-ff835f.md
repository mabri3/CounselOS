Here's the quick answer to the core question — **does any part of the Harbor Pay acquisition and day-1 migration need legal review before the Nov 15 close?**

**Yes — this is not "really just a data migration."** At least four distinct legal workstreams need attention before close, and one of them (the unreviewed alerts/SARs) is genuinely deal-relevant.

**1. Unreviewed ~40 open alerts and SARs (highest priority).** A money transmitter must maintain an effective AML program and act on alerts; unreviewed alerts and SARs are a live gap in that program at close. Because none have been read, the content is unknown — it could range from benign to deal-affecting. This is the single most decision-changing unknown. **No external authority retrieved** for the specific acquisition-inheritance question, so treat the AML frame as conditional, not source-backed.

**2. KYC recordkeeping gap for pre-2023 users.** Pass/fail is all that exists for pre-2023 onboarded users — the underlying documents are gone (vendor shut down in 2024). BSA customer-identification and record-retention rules generally require retaining underlying identifying information and documents. A pass/fail result alone may not satisfy that retention obligation, creating a remediation question before or after close. **No external authority retrieved.**

**3. MTL footprint and change-of-control notice in CA, IA, NY, FL.** The combined footprint opens four new markets, but that only works if Mosaic Relay holds a valid MTL in each and gets required change-of-control notice/approval for the acquisition. CA and NY are high-scrutiny states. The "combined 44" math assumes zero overlap — unverified. This is a hard-deadline item against the ~10-week close. **No external authority retrieved.**

**4. Day-1 transaction monitoring continuity.** Harbor's monitoring SaaS contract terminates at close; the plan is to repoint Harbor's feed at Mosaic Relay's engine on day 1. Any gap in monitoring coverage is a BSA/AML program failure risk. Whether the repoint is tested and validated before close is unknown.

**5. $50 Welcome bonus and consumer-protection disclosures.** Whether the bonus is offered to all migrated users and tied to a condition affects whether it could be a prohibited inducement or trigger disclosure requirements. This is the one intake question still open. The unified-account migration also raises funds-availability, fee, and account-agreement disclosure questions.

**Key unknowns:**
- Content of the ~40 open alerts and SARs (unreviewed)
- Whether the $50 bonus is universal or conditional
- Whether the MTL footprints actually have zero overlap
- Whether the transaction is structured as stock purchase, merger, or asset purchase (affects change-of-control trigger)
- Whether the day-1 monitoring repoint is tested before close

**Bottom line:** The alerts/SARs review is the item most likely to change whether the deal can close as planned, and the state change-of-control filings are the most time-sensitive. Both are set up as work items and research runs. The legal research runs so far returned no public sources, so the analysis above is conditional and needs verification against actual statutes and regulator guidance before reliance.
