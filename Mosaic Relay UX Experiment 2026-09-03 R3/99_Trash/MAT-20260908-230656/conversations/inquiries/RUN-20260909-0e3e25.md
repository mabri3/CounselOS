My recommendation is that this is **not** "really just a data migration" — it needs legal review before the Nov 15 close, and the deal as currently planned carries material, avoidable risk.

**What I'd actually do, in priority order:**

1. **Review the ~40 open alerts and SARs now, before signing.** This is the single most decision-changing item. Unreviewed, they create BSA/AML successor exposure and can affect whether the deal closes as planned. This should gate the close, not run in parallel.

2. **Confirm the transaction structure and Harbor's license status in CA, NY, FL.** If the deal transfers control of Harbor as the licensed entity, prior approval is likely required, and NY/CA timelines commonly exceed 10 weeks. That makes Nov 15 untenable without regulator engagement or a carve-out. This is the biggest schedule risk.

3. **Make a risk-based call on the pre-2023 KYC gap.** Pass/fail-only records for pre-2023 users is a CIP/recordkeeping gap. Decide whether to re-verify high-risk accounts or accept the deficiency with documented rationale.

4. **De-risk the day-1 monitoring handoff.** Repointing Harbor's feed at Mosaic Relay's engine on day 1 needs to be built and tested pre-close to avoid a surveillance gap.

5. **Characterize the $50 bonus** for inducement/consumer-protection disclosure — lower priority, but confirm before launch.

**Bottom line:** I would not recommend closing Nov 15 as planned without first reviewing the SARs/alerts and confirming the change-of-control path in CA/NY/FL. The most likely workable path is either delaying close or closing the corporate transaction while carving out NY/CA customers until approvals issue.

The biggest open fact that could change this: **what's actually in those SARs and alerts.** That's where I'd start.
