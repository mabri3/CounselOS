The KYC-document question was already answered earlier in this conversation ("Pass/fail is all that exists"), so it's not actually left open — that's saved. The one genuinely open intake item is the $50 Welcome bonus characterization. I'll analyze with the current facts, leave the bonus and the alerts/SARs content as unknowns, and give the issue map and assumptions.

## Initial issue map

**1. Unreviewed ~40 open alerts and SARs (BSA/AML exposure).** This is the highest-stakes item. Under the Bank Secrecy Act and AML program rules, a money transmitter must maintain an effective AML program, file SARs, and act on alerts. Inheriting unreviewed alerts and SARs means Mosaic Relay takes on whatever exposure they represent at close, and there may be regulator notice obligations. Because none have been read, the content is unknown — this could range from benign to deal-affecting. This is the single most decision-changing unknown.

**2. KYC recordkeeping gap for pre-2023 users.** Pass/fail is all that exists for pre-2023 onboarded users. BSA customer identification and record-retention rules generally require retaining underlying identifying information and documents (commonly five years after account closure). A pass/fail result without underlying documents may not satisfy that retention obligation, creating a remediation question before or after close.

**3. MTL footprint and change-of-control notice in CA, IA, NY, FL.** The combined footprint opens four new markets. Each of those states requires Mosaic Relay to hold a valid MTL there and may require change-of-control notice or approval for the acquisition. CA and NY are high-scrutiny money-transmission states. Whether the "combined 44" math is accurate (no overlap) is unverified.

**4. Day-1 transaction monitoring continuity.** Harbor's monitoring SaaS contract terminates at close; the plan is to repoint Harbor's feed at Mosaic Relay's engine on day 1. Any gap in monitoring coverage is a BSA/AML program failure risk. Whether the repoint is tested and validated before close is unknown.

**5. $50 Welcome bonus characterization.** Whether the bonus is offered to all migrated users and tied to a condition affects whether it could be a prohibited inducement or trigger consumer-protection disclosure requirements. This is the one intake question still open.

**6. Consumer-protection disclosures for unified-account migration.** Moving 240k consumer + 12k business accounts into one unified account/balance with full product access raises funds-availability, fee, and account-agreement disclosure questions under state money-transmission and federal consumer-protection law.

## Assumptions used

- **Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter subject to state MTL and BSA/AML obligations** in the states where it operates. This underpins issues 1, 3, and 4. If Mosaic Relay instead operates through a bank partner or under a different licensing model, the state change-of-control and AML analysis shifts.
- **The acquired Harbor accounts and balances transfer to Mosaic Relay as the licensed entity.** This underpins the MTL footprint analysis and the consumer-protection disclosure issue. If accounts transfer under a different structure (e.g., a bank partner holds them), the analysis changes.

Both assumptions are open and material. The content of the alerts/SARs and the bonus terms remain unknown and are left as gaps.

The most consequential next step is reviewing the ~40 open alerts and SARs — that's the item most likely to change whether the deal can close as planned. Want me to start a research run on the BSA/AML obligations around inheriting unreviewed alerts/SARs in an acquisition, or set up a work item to drive that review?
