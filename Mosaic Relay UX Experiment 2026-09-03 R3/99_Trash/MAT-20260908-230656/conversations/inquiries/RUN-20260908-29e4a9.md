The claim that Harbor users were "already KYC'd" means Harbor ran identity verification on them when they onboarded, and the result was a pass/fail determination. In practice, that typically means Harbor collected identifying information (name, address, date of birth, and for many accounts an ID document) and checked it against a verification source, then made a yes/no decision on whether the person was who they claimed to be.

The important nuance for this matter is what "KYC'd" does **not** mean here. Per the facts, Harbor's KYC ran through a vendor that shut down in 2024, and for users onboarded before 2023, **only the pass/fail result survives — the underlying documents are gone**. So "already KYC'd" is a claim about the *outcome* of verification, not about the *evidence* backing it.

That distinction matters for two reasons:

**Issue** — Whether the "already KYC'd" claim is enough to support the no-re-verification migration plan.

**Rule** — Under the BSA, a money services business must maintain a Customer Identification Program (CIP) and retain the records of identity verification. The relevant recordkeeping requirement is that the institution keep the identifying information and verification records it relied on. FinCEN's MSB SAR rule (31 C.F.R. § 1022.320) and the CIP rules require retention of these records. These are **Needs checking** — I have not retrieved or verified their current text in this matter.

**Analysis** — "Already KYC'd" describes a completed verification event, but the underlying documents for pre-2023 users no longer exist. So the pass/fail result is all that remains. That creates a recordkeeping gap: if Mosaic Relay relies on Harbor's verification without re-verifying, it inherits accounts where it cannot produce the underlying identity documents if asked. That is a remediable gap, but it is a real one — it is not solved by the fact that a verification once happened.

**Conclusion** — "Already KYC'd" means Harbor verified these users and got a pass/fail result, but for pre-2023 users the supporting documents are lost. The claim supports the *decision* that was made, but not the *evidence* behind it. The practical question is whether Mosaic Relay re-verifies high-risk pre-2023 accounts or accepts the documentation deficiency as a risk-based decision.

The one fact that would sharpen this: whether the pass/fail records include enough identifying information (name, DOB, address, ID type/number) to satisfy CIP recordkeeping even without the original documents. Do you know what the pass/fail records actually contain?
