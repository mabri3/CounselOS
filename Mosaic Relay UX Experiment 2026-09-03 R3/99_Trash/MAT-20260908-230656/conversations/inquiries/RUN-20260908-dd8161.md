Here is my suggestion, focused on the pre-2023 accounts where only pass/fail KYC results survive.

**Conclusion** — Don't re-verify all 240k accounts; that's disproportionate and unnecessary. Instead, take a risk-based approach: keep the pass/fail result as the baseline for the migrated population, but re-verify the subset that actually matters, and fix the recordkeeping gap for the rest.

**Rule** — The BSA requires a money services business to maintain a Customer Identification Program and retain the records of identity verification it relied on. FinCEN's CIP and MSB recordkeeping rules (31 C.F.R. § 1022.320 and the CIP rule) are the relevant framework. These are **Needs checking** — I have not retrieved or verified their current text in this matter.

**Analysis** — The problem is not that verification never happened; it's that the underlying documents for pre-2023 users are gone, so only pass/fail survives. A full re-verification of 240k accounts would be costly, slow, and would undermine the "one unified account, day-1" value proposition. But accepting the gap blindly leaves Mosaic Relay unable to produce identity records if a regulator asks, and it inherits the riskiest accounts (the ~40 open alerts and SARs) without fresh scrutiny. The proportionate path is to segment: (1) re-verify the high-risk slice — accounts tied to the open alerts/SARs, high-value or high-velocity accounts, and any flagged during diligence; (2) for the rest, preserve the pass/fail result as the record and document a risk-based decision to accept the documentation deficiency; and (3) confirm what the pass/fail records actually contain, because if they include name, DOB, address, and ID type/number, the recordkeeping gap is much smaller than if they're just a binary flag.

**Conclusion / next step** — My suggestion: re-verify the high-risk subset now (before close), keep pass/fail as the baseline for the rest, and document the risk-based acceptance of the pre-2023 documentation gap. The single most useful fact to confirm is what data the pass/fail records actually hold — that determines whether this is a small gap or a large one. Do you know what those records contain?
