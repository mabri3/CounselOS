Here's my orientation. First, a short factual summary of what I understand the request to be.
**What I understand you're asking:** You're acquiring Harbor Pay (close targeted Nov 15, ~10 weeks out) and moving 240k consumer + 12k business accounts into Mosaic Relay's app on day 1 post-close — one unified account, one balance, no re-verification. You're asking whether any of this needs legal review before close, or whether it's "really just a data migration."
**My short answer: this is not just a data migration.** Several items carry real legal and regulatory weight that should be reviewed before close. The most decision-changing ones:
1. **Unreviewed alerts and SARs.** ~40 open alerts and "a handful" of SARs from the last year, and nobody has read them. This is the single biggest item. Under BSA/AML obligations, you're acquiring a book of business with unresolved financial-crime signals. If any of those SARs or alerts reflect ongoing suspicious activity, you could be inheriting exposure — and there may be regulator notice obligations tied to the acquisition. This needs review before close, not after.
2. **No re-verification + missing underlying KYC documents.** You're taking on 252k accounts based on Harbor's pass/fail KYC results, but the underlying documents for pre-2023 onboardings are gone (vendor shut down in 2024). That's a recordkeeping gap that regulators may expect you to remediate, and it affects whether you can rely on those KYC determinations at all.
3. **MTL licensing.** The "combined 44, opens four new markets" framing needs verification — which four states, and do the footprints overlap? State money transmitter regulators often require notice/approval for a change of control or for assuming another licensee's business. This is a pre-close item.
4. **Monitoring continuity.** Repointing Harbor's transaction feed at your engine on day 1 means your monitoring must be live and validated before accounts move — and the ~40 open alerts need to be dispositioned, not just handed over.
5. **The $50 "Welcome" bonus.** Depending on how it's structured, this can raise state money-transmission and consumer-protection questions (e.g., prohibited inducements or disclosure requirements). Worth a quick check before marketing locks it in.
6. **Consumer protection / funds-flow disclosures** for the unified-account migration itself.
