**Issue** — The main legal issue is whether Mosaic Relay can inherit Harbor Pay's customer base and transaction-monitoring posture on day 1 without first resolving the unreviewed ~40 open alerts and the past year's SARs, and whether that inherited exposure creates BSA/AML liability or regulator notice obligations that could affect the Nov 15 close.

**Rule** — A money transmitter is subject to the Bank Secrecy Act and its implementing AML rules, including a duty to maintain an effective anti-money-laundering program and to file Suspicious Activity Reports on transactions that meet the reporting threshold or otherwise warrant filing. The obligation to file a SAR is not extinguished by an acquisition; the acquiring institution inherits the duty to review and, where required, report suspicious activity that occurred before or around the change. Relevant authority: the BSA (31 U.S.C. § 5311 et seq.) and the FinCEN SAR rule for money services businesses (31 C.F.R. § 1022.320) — **Needs checking** (I have not retrieved or verified the current text). The general principle that a successor institution assumes the predecessor's SAR and recordkeeping obligations is well established in BSA practice but I have not verified a controlling citation.

**Analysis** — The facts are the most decision-changing item in this matter: ~40 open alerts and a handful of SARs from the last year have not been read. Unreviewed alerts mean Mosaic Relay cannot know whether any of them should have been filed as SARs, and unreviewed SARs mean it cannot assess whether it is inheriting a filing gap or a pattern that regulators would expect to see disclosed. Because the plan is to repoint Harbor's transaction feed into Mosaic Relay's engine on day 1, the monitoring continuity issue is real but secondary — the deeper problem is that the inherited data has not been evaluated. This is not "just a data migration": the migration transfers not only accounts and balances but also the compliance history and any unreviewed suspicious-activity exposure attached to them.

**Conclusion** — The main issue is BSA/AML exposure from the unreviewed alerts and SARs, and it is the one item most likely to affect whether the deal can close as planned or whether a regulator must be notified. The practical next step is to review the ~40 alerts and the SARs before close, determine whether any require filing or disclosure, and confirm whether the transaction structure triggers any change-of-control notice to state or federal regulators.
