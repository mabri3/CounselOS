# Harborline Financial — Fictional Product Counsel Requests

## 01 — Expanding Instant Account Opening for Small Businesses

**Business goal:** Harborline Financial wants to increase funded small-business accounts by letting an owner complete business-account onboarding in under five minutes, with fewer manual reviews. **Proposed flow:** The owner enters legal entity data, selects a banking-partner account type, provides beneficial-owner information, links a mobile number, and receives provisional account access while automated checks run. A “pending verification” status would limit certain transfers until review is complete. **Actors:** the senior product manager, Harborline onboarding engineering, the banking partner, the KYC/KYB vendor, business applicants, beneficial owners, and operations reviewers. **Timing:** Product wants a controlled pilot in six weeks before the seasonal increase in marketplace sellers.

**Known facts:** Harborline offers consumer and business bank accounts through a banking partner and also provides substantial payment functionality. The current business flow requires a manual review for many sole proprietors and LLCs. Product has used Chime public pages only for broad business-model context; Chime is not affiliated with Harborline, and no Chime process is being copied as a legal or operational fact. **Missing facts:** We do not yet know the partner’s provisional-access rules, vendor match thresholds, review service levels, permitted account restrictions, treatment of foreign owners, or which entity records must be refreshed after onboarding. **Legal/product advice requested:** Please advise on the permissible provisional-access design, KYB and beneficial-owner collection requirements, disclosures, escalation rules, record retention, and the smallest launch scope that fits BSA/AML and banking-partner requirements.

## 02 — Using Background Checks for Account and Payment Risk

**Business goal:** Harborline Financial wants to use a third-party background-check product to reduce losses from synthetic identities, account takeovers, and payment fraud without sending every new customer to manual review. **Proposed flow:** At signup, Harborline would request consent, send identity and device data to the vendor, receive risk signals and possible criminal-record or sanctions-related matches, and route high-risk applicants to a review queue. The product would show a plain-language adverse-action message when access is denied or limited. **Actors:** the senior product manager, consumer onboarding, fraud operations, privacy, the banking partner, the background-check vendor, applicants, and reviewers. **Timing:** The team wants a vendor decision this month and an A/B test before the next quarterly fraud review.

**Known facts:** The intended users include consumers and business representatives, and the same platform supports payment activity after account opening. The vendor markets separate identity, fraud, and background-screening modules. Harborline has not decided whether any criminal-history data will be used for a decision; the initial proposal combines several modules under one API. **Missing facts:** We need the exact data fields, source jurisdictions, permissible-purpose analysis, consent language, dispute process, retention period, vendor subprocessors, model explainability, and whether the banking partner will make or require the final decision. **Legal/product advice requested:** Please advise which checks may be used, whether consumer-reporting rules apply, how to separate fraud screening from employment-style background checks, what notices and dispute rights are needed, and how to design an experiment without creating inconsistent or unfair outcomes.

## 03 — Delayed Settlement for Higher-Risk Payment Recipients

**Business goal:** Harborline Financial wants to reduce chargeback and money-laundering exposure by delaying settlement for selected business recipients while allowing ordinary recipients to receive funds on the normal schedule. **Proposed flow:** A payment-risk service would score each recipient and transaction. Low-risk payments would settle in one business day; selected higher-risk payments would be held for up to seven calendar days, with a message in the dashboard and an appeal path. The hold could apply to card-funded and bank-funded payments, but not to payroll or other categories product has not yet defined. **Actors:** the senior product manager, payments engineering, the banking partner, card networks and processors, business recipients, senders, risk operations, and customer support. **Timing:** Finance wants the first rule live before the next high-volume sales period, in approximately eight weeks.

**Known facts:** Harborline performs substantial payments work and uses a banking partner for account products. The proposal is based on internal loss data and broad business-model context from Chime public pages only; Chime is fictional-context reference material, not a Harborline affiliate or source of payment rules. The team expects some holds to be released automatically. **Missing facts:** We lack the contractual settlement rights, network constraints, state money-transmission analysis, maximum hold periods, interest or fee treatment, notice content, appeals service level, and criteria for release or escalation. **Legal/product advice requested:** Please advise whether the proposed risk-based holds are allowed for each payment rail, what terms and notices are required, how to avoid unfair or unexplained discrimination, and what operational controls should be in place before launch.

## 04 — Deactivating Dormant Accounts With Open Payment Activity

**Business goal:** Harborline Financial wants to lower servicing and fraud costs by deactivating accounts that have been inactive for twelve months while preserving a reliable path for customers to recover funds and resolve pending payments. **Proposed flow:** The system would send notices at 60, 30, and 7 days, block new activity at deactivation, keep a limited servicing record, and send remaining funds to a verified external account or issue a check when required. An account with an unresolved chargeback, dispute, investigation, or scheduled payment would enter an exception queue. **Actors:** the senior product manager, account-platform engineering, servicing operations, the banking partner, payments operations, consumers, business customers, and state escheatment teams. **Timing:** Operations wants a design recommendation within three weeks and implementation before year-end.

**Known facts:** Harborline supports both consumer and business bank accounts and payment transactions. Existing terms mention closure rights but do not describe this exact inactivity process. Product believes the banking partner may apply different dormancy standards than Harborline’s proposed twelve-month rule. **Missing facts:** We need the partner’s closure and escheatment procedures, state-by-state notice rules, treatment of ACH returns and card disputes, business-record retention requirements, abandoned-property deadlines, accessible notice formats, and the account states that cannot be closed automatically. **Legal/product advice requested:** Please advise on a compliant deactivation policy, notice sequence, funds-return process, exception logic, customer support script, and whether consumer and business accounts need separate product flows.

## 05 — Privacy Controls for Payment Recipient Data

**Business goal:** Harborline Financial wants to improve payment confirmations and fraud detection by retaining recipient names, invoice descriptions, contact details, and device signals across transactions and using selected data to suggest future recipients. **Proposed flow:** After a payment, the system would store the recipient profile, display a shortened name in the sender’s history, share necessary information with the banking partner and processors, and use internal history to prefill later payments. Users could hide a recipient from the interface, but the current design does not delete the underlying record. **Actors:** the senior product manager, payments and data engineering, privacy, security, the banking partner, processors, senders, recipients, and support agents. **Timing:** The team plans a design freeze in four weeks.

**Known facts:** Payment records are generated for consumer and business accounts. Harborline expects some recipients to be sole traders or individuals, and some invoice descriptions may contain sensitive personal information. The company has a general privacy notice and retention schedule, but neither was written for recipient suggestions. **Missing facts:** We need the data inventory, purposes for each field, processor and partner roles, retention and deletion constraints, user-access workflow, cross-border processing locations, sensitive-data filtering, opt-out behavior, and whether the suggestions are used for fraud decisions. **Legal/product advice requested:** Please advise on notice and consent needs, purpose limitation, data-minimization rules, deletion versus suppression, access and correction handling, vendor contracts, and a product design that keeps useful payment history without creating avoidable privacy risk.

## 06 — Marketing a “No Monthly Fee” Business Account

**Business goal:** Harborline Financial wants to attract early-stage companies with a campaign describing its business account as having “no monthly fee,” while promoting payment tools and optional expedited transfers as paid add-ons. **Proposed flow:** A landing page would lead with the no-fee claim, show an account-opening call to action, and place a fee table below the primary button. A customer who opens an account would see onboarding messages for payment acceptance, instant payout, and a premium support package. **Actors:** the senior product manager, growth marketing, product marketing, compliance, the banking partner, payment processors, business applicants, and the creative agency. **Timing:** Marketing wants final copy approved in ten business days for a campaign starting next month.

**Known facts:** Harborline provides consumer and business bank accounts through a banking partner and has substantial payments work. The campaign team used Chime public pages only to understand broad fintech account-marketing patterns; Chime is not affiliated with Harborline and is not an authority for Harborline fees or disclosures. Some business customers may pay transaction, expedited-transfer, or partner-imposed fees even when they do not pay a monthly account fee. **Missing facts:** We need the complete fee schedule, partner approval requirements, eligibility limits, geographic scope, material conditions, tax or regulatory wording, landing-page hierarchy, and evidence for any comparative or savings claims. **Legal/product advice requested:** Please advise whether “no monthly fee” is accurate as presented, what qualifiers must be adjacent to the claim, how to disclose payment-related fees, and whether the proposed campaign needs separate consumer and business versions.

## 07 — Reinstating Customers After Automated Deactivation

**Business goal:** Harborline Financial wants to reduce support time by allowing eligible customers to restore a deactivated account through self-service instead of requiring a phone review. **Proposed flow:** A customer would authenticate with a previously trusted device, confirm identity, answer a small set of questions, and upload a document if the account has a pending risk flag. The system would reopen basic account access immediately for low-risk cases and send other cases to operations; payment functionality would remain limited until checks finish. **Actors:** the senior product manager, identity engineering, fraud and AML operations, customer support, the banking partner, document-verification vendor, consumers, and business administrators. **Timing:** Support wants a pilot in five weeks because deactivation-related contacts are rising.

**Known facts:** Automated deactivation may occur after inactivity, account takeover signals, failed verification, or a compliance review. Harborline wants a single recovery experience, but account types and deactivation reasons have different operational consequences. **Missing facts:** We need the complete reason taxonomy, partner instructions, applicable notice and appeal duties, identity-proofing standard, sanctions and AML re-screening triggers, authorized-agent rules for business accounts, accessibility needs, and audit-log requirements. **Legal/product advice requested:** Please advise which cases can use self-service, which require human review, what disclosures and appeal rights apply, how to prevent an attacker from restoring an account, and how to keep the recovery flow proportionate to the original deactivation reason.

## 08 — Sharing Transaction Data With a Merchant-Analytics Partner

**Business goal:** Harborline Financial wants to offer business customers a dashboard that compares payment conversion, refund rates, and customer segments by using an external analytics partner. **Proposed flow:** Harborline would send pseudonymized transaction records each night, including merchant category, amount band, timestamp, location region, refund status, and a stable customer token. The partner would return charts and benchmarks embedded in Harborline’s dashboard; Harborline would not sell the data, but the proposal allows the partner to use aggregated outputs to improve its service. **Actors:** the senior product manager, business payments, data engineering, privacy, security, the analytics partner, the banking partner, merchants, and consumers whose transactions appear in reports. **Timing:** Sales has promised a beta to ten merchants in seven weeks.

**Known facts:** The product will cover payment activity linked to Harborline business accounts. Harborline has not confirmed whether the banking partner permits this use or whether the partner’s aggregation is sufficient for small merchant cohorts. The proposal treats a stable token as anonymous without a completed re-identification assessment. **Missing facts:** We need the data-flow map, exact contract terms, re-identification testing, cohort minimums, retention period, deletion process, cross-border locations, customer notices, opt-out design, and whether any output could affect credit, underwriting, or account-risk decisions. **Legal/product advice requested:** Please advise on anonymization versus pseudonymization, permissible secondary use, contract and security controls, merchant and consumer disclosures, cohort suppression, and the minimum beta design that can be tested safely.

## 09 — Targeted Offers for Faster Payment Settlement

**Business goal:** Harborline Financial wants to increase adoption of instant settlement by offering selected merchants a discounted first month and showing higher-value offers to merchants with frequent payment volume. **Proposed flow:** Marketing would use account age, payment volume, refund rate, industry, and support history to select recipients. A merchant would see an in-product offer, accept terms, and receive an automatic discount for thirty days; the offer would expire if the account enters a risk hold or is deactivated. **Actors:** the senior product manager, lifecycle marketing, payments pricing, risk, privacy, the banking partner, processors, merchants, and customer support. **Timing:** The growth team wants the offer live for a limited test in six weeks.

**Known facts:** Harborline’s payment service can settle funds through more than one rail, and instant settlement may carry a fee after the promotional period. The proposed audience includes new and existing businesses, including sole proprietors. Marketing says the campaign is not credit, but the selection model uses behavior and financial activity. **Missing facts:** We need the fee and discount terms, selection variables and exclusions, partner and network rules, state marketing restrictions, adverse-action implications, accessibility and language coverage, data-use permissions, and the process for correcting an inaccurate offer decision. **Legal/product advice requested:** Please advise whether the selection and messaging create fair-lending, payments, unfair-practices, or privacy concerns, what terms must be shown before acceptance, and how to structure a measurable test with clear eligibility and appeal handling.

## 10 — Collecting Additional Information After a Sanctions Alert

**Business goal:** Harborline Financial wants to reduce unnecessary account closures by giving a customer more time to resolve a possible sanctions or watchlist match before restricting account and payment access. **Proposed flow:** When a screening provider returns a possible match, Harborline would place a temporary restriction on specified transactions, send a secure in-app request for date of birth, nationality, address history, or business-ownership documents, and route the response to trained operations staff. If the alert clears, access would be restored; if it does not, the account would remain restricted or be closed under a documented process. **Actors:** the senior product manager, AML compliance, sanctions operations, the banking partner, screening vendor, security, consumers, business owners, beneficial owners, and support agents. **Timing:** Compliance wants a product decision in two weeks because the existing process creates long holds and repeat contacts.

**Known facts:** Harborline serves consumers and businesses and processes payments that may involve domestic and cross-border counterparties. The screening vendor supplies match scores and source identifiers, but Harborline has not approved the fields that product wants to collect. Product wants customer-facing status updates without revealing sensitive screening logic. **Missing facts:** We need the partner’s required controls, alert-handling deadlines, permissible customer disclosures, data-retention and access rules, treatment of false positives, business-authority verification, language support, escalation thresholds, and payment types that must remain blocked. **Legal/product advice requested:** Please advise on the lawful and useful information request, temporary restriction design, customer communications, recordkeeping, false-positive review, and the boundary between helpful status information and prohibited disclosure of investigative or screening details.
