# Company profile

## Company Name

Northstar Pay

## Website Url

Not provided.

## Summary

Fictional U.S. fintech (not affiliated with Affirm) providing merchant checkout financing, an app-based pay-in-4 product, and longer-term installment products for eligible purchases, working with bank and lending partners, payment processors, and a broad merchant network. Legal owner is Alex Morgan.

## Business Model

Consumer installment financing (pay-in-4 and longer-term installments) offered at merchant checkout and via the Northstar Pay app, in partnership with FDIC-insured bank or lending partners, payment processors, and a merchant network; serves consumers seeking flexible payment timing and merchants wanting a checkout financing option, with consumer and merchant support, servicing, and account-management teams.

## Products Services

Merchant checkout financing; app-based pay-in-4; longer-term installment products for eligible purchases; consumer and merchant support, servicing, and account management.

## Jurisdictions

Phased national rollout; no state is automatically available. Currently live in California, Colorado, Georgia, Illinois, New York, Texas, and Washington for pay-in-4; longer-term installment products limited to a smaller approved state list after partner review. States are excluded until legal, operations, and the relevant lending partner confirm availability, licensing, rate and fee limits, disclosures, servicing, collections, and reporting requirements; the same customer-facing experience may not be used in every state. Different FDIC-insured bank or lending partners are used by product and state, and the responsible entity, servicing vendor, payment processor, and contract terms may differ. The exact partner map is still being finalized and is an open implementation fact legal must confirm before launch; Alex Morgan should see a state-by-state product and partner matrix, including excluded states, before approving expansion.

## Regulatory Context

Consumer-finance regulation across the full lifecycle: state-by-state product limits, licensing, rate and fee limits, lending-partner responsibilities, servicing controls, collections conduct, credit-reporting accuracy, dispute handling, marketing claims, electronic consent, privacy, and vendor oversight.

## Data Practices

For this fictional implementation, Northstar Pay may share account and transaction information with the applicable bank or lending partner, payment processor, servicing provider, fraud-prevention vendor, credit-reporting vendor when reporting is enabled, and other service providers that act under written instructions. Merchants should receive aggregated or de-identified performance reporting by default. A strategic merchant may receive more detailed reporting only after a separate legal review confirms the purpose, data fields, contract, customer notice, access controls, and state limits. Northstar Pay should not permit a merchant or advertising partner to use identifiable repayment history or sensitive customer data for unrelated marketing without a documented legal basis and the required customer choice. Assume current customer controls include access to privacy information, an ability to manage certain marketing preferences, and account support for questions or requests. Do not assume a universal opt-out for every use, and do not assume consent covers a new purpose. New personalized offers, merchant promotions, analytics, fraud uses, and partner sharing must be mapped to the applicable notice, consent, opt-out, and contract requirements. Sensitive data and precise location should be minimized and used only when justified. Northstar Pay should use role-based access, vendor restrictions, audit records, and deletion or suppression workflows. For planning, retain records for the period required by applicable law, contract, dispute, credit-reporting, tax, and litigation needs, with a documented retention schedule rather than an indefinite default. Account closure should trigger a review for deletion, suppression, or restricted retention. These are operating assumptions for the fictional company and remain subject to state, product, partner, and data-map confirmation. Alex Morgan should approve any material change to customer-facing terms, sharing, retention, or merchant data access.

## Risk Posture

Moderate. Moves quickly on useful products but will not trade customer clarity or sustainable repayment principles for growth; customer-facing experiences must clearly disclose amounts due, payment dates, fees, material conditions, and choices before acceptance. Product teams state assumptions, preserve records, and route material judgment calls to legal owners (Alex Morgan).
