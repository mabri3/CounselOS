    process I''d apply before publication.


    ## Claim-by-claim assessment


    | Claim | Assessment | Key issue |

    |---|---|---|

    | "Pay in four" | **Permitted with qualification** | Accurate for pay-in-4 only;
    must not appear adjacent to longer-term installment products without distinguishing
    them. State availability varies — claims should not imply universal availability.
    |

    | "No interest" | **Modify** | True for pay-in-4, but longer-term products may
    carry interest. Must be qualified by product ("no interest on pay-in-4 purchases")
    and paired with late-fee disclosure, since "no interest" standing alone can be
    a deceptive omission of material conditions. |

    | "Easy approval" | **Modify** | Not every customer qualifies. "Easy" implies
    guaranteed or near-guaranteed approval — a classic deceptive-claim risk. Safer:
    "quick to apply," "see if you qualify," with clear disclosure that approval is
    not guaranteed and eligibility criteria apply. |

    | "Build your credit" | **Modify — highest risk** | Credit reporting may not be
    available for every product, state, or partner. Claim requires (a) limiting to
    products/states where reporting is actually live, (b) disclosure that on-time
    payments don''t guarantee credit improvement and late payments may hurt credit,
    and (c) confirmation of the partner/state reporting map before use. |

    | "More control over your budget" | **Permitted with care** | Puffery-adjacent,
    but avoid pairing with affordability implications. Do not suggest the product
    makes purchases affordable or is suitable for customers with financial difficulty.
    |


    **Representative payment examples and credit-card comparisons:** both are permitted
    but regulated. Payment examples must be accurate, representative, and clearly
    state amounts, dates, fees, and any interest. Credit-card comparisons must be
    substantiated (e.g., typical card APR vs. actual product terms), use current/typical
    figures, and avoid cherry-picked comparisons.


    ## Compliance process before publication


    1. **Substantiation file** — for each claim, document the basis (product terms,
    partner data, reporting availability) before launch. This is the FTC standard
    and your defense if challenged.

    2. **Clear-and-conspicuous qualifiers** — disclosures must be proximate to the
    claim, unavoidable, and not buried in fine print or a separate screen. Same standard
    across paid search (character limits are not an excuse), social, banners, email,
    app screens, and influencer content.

    3. **Influencer controls** — written agreements with required claim language,
    prohibited claims list, pre-approval of scripts/posts, clear "material connection"
    disclosure (#ad), and monitoring/takedown rights. Influencers are agents for FTC
    purposes; you own the liability.

    4. **Merchant co-branding** — merchants and agencies must submit co-branded materials
    for legal approval before publication; contractually obligate merchants to use
    only approved creative and prohibit claim modification.

    5. **State/product gating** — claims must be mapped to the state-by-state product
    and partner matrix (pay-in-4 vs. longer-term, reporting on/off, fee limits). "Build
    your credit" and "no interest" in particular cannot run uniformly.

    6. **Pre-launch signoff** — a claims matrix (claim × product × state × required
    qualifier × substantiation source) approved by legal before any channel goes live.


    ## Assumptions and gaps


    - I''m assuming the campaign targets only currently live states (CA, CO, GA, IL,
    NY, TX, WA for pay-in-4) — if it will run nationally, the state-gating issue becomes
    the gating item.

    - The credit-reporting partner/state map is the open fact that determines whether
    "build your credit" can run at all.

    - One-month launch is tight; influencer agreements and merchant approvals are
    the longest-lead items.


    **Recommended next steps:** (1) confirm the state/product/reporting matrix with
    the lending partners, (2) build the claims matrix for legal signoff, (3) get influencer
    and merchant approval workflows in place now.

    research on any specific claim (e.g., FTC precedent on "build your credit" or
    "easy approval")?'

    Campaign goal (increasing checkout and app use) is materially undercut. |
