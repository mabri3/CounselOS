# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

Legal standards for fintech BNPL marketing claims: substantiation and qualification requirements for "pay in four," "no interest," "easy approval," "build your credit," and "more control over your budget"; representative payment examples; credit-card comparisons; clear-and-conspicuous disclosures across paid search, social, banners, email, app screens, and influencer content; influencer controls and material-connection disclosure; merchant co-branded material approvals; and state/product gating of claims for pay-in-4 vs longer-term installment products.

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay's marketing team wants a multi-channel campaign (paid search, social, merchant banners, email, app screens, influencers) launching in one month, promoting "pay in four," "no interest," "easy approval," "build your credit," and "more control over your budget." Known facts contradict parts of these claims: not all customers qualify, late fees may apply, credit reporting is not universal, and longer-term products carry interest. Legal (Brian Harris) must determine which claims are permitted, which need qualification, and what pre-publication compliance process applies.

## Decision question

Which of the five proposed claims can Northstar Pay run as-is, which require qualifiers or rewording, and what substantiation, disclosure, influencer, and merchant-approval controls must be in place before the campaign launches in one month — given that misstatements about cost, approval odds, or credit benefits would create FTC Act, Reg Z/Reg B, UDAAP, and state deceptive-practices exposure across the pay-in-4 and longer-term products?

## Open questions

- Which products and states will the campaign cover, and is credit reporting actually enabled for pay-in-4 in each live state (CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA)?
- What are the actual late-fee terms and approval decline rates needed to substantiate "easy approval" and "no interest" qualifiers?
- Will influencers be paid or receive free product, and what contractual controls and FTC material-connection disclosures are planned?
- What is the merchant co-branding approval workflow for banners on merchant sites?
- What substantiation files exist today for each claim (fee schedules, approval data, credit-reporting partner confirmations)?

## Likely rules and issues

**Claim-by-claim analysis (generated analysis; verify citations before publication):**

1. **"Pay in four"** — Permitted if accurate for the pay-in-4 product, but must not appear adjacent to or be implied for longer-term installment products. Requires product gating: the claim is product-specific, not brand-wide. Qualify if payment timing or amounts vary by state or partner.

2. **"No interest"** — High risk. True only for pay-in-4; longer-term loans carry interest. Under FTC Act §5 and UDAAP standards, an unqualified "no interest" claim that reaches consumers who end up in interest-bearing products is deceptive. Requires either (a) product-gated placement, or (b) a clear and conspicuous qualifier ("no interest on pay-in-4; longer-term plans may carry interest"). Late fees also undercut a general "no cost" impression if the creative implies free.

3. **"Easy approval"** — High risk. Not every customer qualifies. FTC precedent treats unqualified approval-ease claims as deceptive when a material number of applicants are declined. Requires qualification ("easy to apply," "approval decisions in seconds," "subject to approval") and substantiation of actual approval rates if any specific claim is made.

4. **"Build your credit"** — Highest risk. Credit reporting is not available for every product. A credit-benefit claim without the reporting actually occurring is a deceptive performance claim (FTC Act; also potentially FCRA-adjacent concerns if reporting is inconsistent). Permitted only where and for products where reporting is confirmed enabled, with a qualifier otherwise ("may be reported," "credit reporting not available for all products"). State-by-state confirmation needed.

5. **"More control over your budget"** — Puffery-adjacent but risky if paired with late fees and non-universal approval. Likely acceptable as aspirational if not tied to specific outcomes, but should not appear with claims implying no consequences for missed payments.

**Representative payment examples** — Permitted if representative of typical transactions (not best-case cherry-picking), with material terms (amounts, dates, fees) disclosed clearly and conspicuously. Reg Z closed-end credit advertising rules (§1026.24) apply to longer-term installment products if they are credit; triggering-term analysis needed.

**Credit-card comparisons** — Permissible comparative advertising under FTC standards only if substantiated, apples-to-apples (comparable amounts, terms, fees), and not misleading by omission (e.g., ignoring late fees or Northstar's own costs). Avoid implying credit cards are uniformly worse.

**Channel-specific disclosure issues:**
- Paid search/social: character limits do not excuse missing material qualifiers; the claim itself must be qualified or the ad restructured.
- Influencer content: FTC Endorsement Guides require clear and conspicuous disclosure of material connections (#ad, paid partnership) — placement in the endorsement itself, not buried.
- Merchant banners: co-branded materials need merchant approval and consistency with Northstar's approved claim library.
- Email/app screens: more room for full disclosures; proximity to the claim still required.

## Viable paths

**Path A (recommended): Gated claim library + channel matrix.** Build an approved-claims matrix keyed to product (pay-in-4 vs. longer-term) and state (live-state list), with mandatory qualifiers per claim. All creative routed through a pre-publication legal review against the matrix. Influencers and merchants receive the claim library and prohibited-claims list contractually. This fits the one-month timeline if the matrix is built in week 1–2.

**Path B: Simplified claims only.** Strip to low-risk claims ("split purchases into four payments, subject to approval; late fees may apply") uniformly across channels. Faster, lower risk, less marketing punch.

**Path C: Full substantiation-first review.** Require documented substantiation (approval-rate data, fee schedules, credit-reporting confirmations) before any claim runs. Most defensible but may not fit one month unless data already exists.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Confirmation that credit reporting is enabled for pay-in-4 in all live states (would allow "build your credit" with lighter qualification).
- Actual late-fee structure (zero late fees would materially strengthen "no interest" positioning).
- Whether longer-term products will be marketed in the same campaign (if fully separated, "no interest" gating is cleaner).
- Approval-rate data showing high approval (would support "easy approval" with lighter qualification).
- Whether influencers are unpaid (reduces material-connection disclosure burden but doesn't eliminate accuracy requirements).

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm current FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) disclosure placement standards for social/influencer formats.
- Verify Reg Z advertising trigger-term rules (§1026.24) apply to the longer-term installment products given the bank-partner structure.
- Check state-specific advertising/deceptive-practices statutes for the seven live states, particularly CA (Rosenthal/UDAP) and NY, for BNPL-specific marketing guidance.
- Confirm CFPB BNPL interpretive rule/circulars on credit-reporting and marketing claims for pay-in-4 products.
- Verify actual approval-rate and fee data with product/ops before signing off on any qualified claims.

**Recommendation:** Path A — build the product/state-gated claim matrix with mandatory qualifiers now, route all creative (including influencer scripts and merchant banners) through legal review against it, and hold "build your credit" and "easy approval" until substantiation is confirmed. The decision on final claim language and launch approval remains with Brian Harris.
