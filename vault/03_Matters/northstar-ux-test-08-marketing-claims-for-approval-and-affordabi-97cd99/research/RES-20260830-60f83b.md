# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Northstar UX Test — 08 — Marketing Claims for Approval and Affordability?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay's marketing team wants to launch a multi-channel campaign (paid search, social, merchant banners, email, app screens, influencers) promoting pay-in-4 and installment products as a "simple and flexible way to pay over time." Proposed claims include "pay in four," "no interest," "easy approval," "build your credit," and "more control over your budget." Known facts cut against some claims: not all customers qualify, late fees may apply, credit reporting is not universal, and longer-term loans may carry interest. Launch is scheduled in one month; legal owner is Brian Harris.

## Decision question

Which of the five proposed claims can run as written, which need qualifiers or rewrites, and what substantiation, disclosure, influencer, and merchant-approval controls must be in place before the campaign publishes in one month — given that an unqualified "no interest" or "build your credit" claim could trigger FTC Act, UDAAP, and state advertising exposure across the seven live pay-in-4 states and the smaller installment-state list?

## Open questions

- Which products and states will the campaign actually run in, and is credit reporting enabled for each product/state (controls "build your credit")?
- What is the actual approval/decline rate and qualification standard (controls "easy approval")?
- What substantiation exists for "more control over your budget" and the representative payment examples?
- What influencer and agency contracts, disclosure requirements (#ad, material connection), and pre-approval workflows exist?
- Do merchants have contractual rights to approve or reject co-branded materials, and are lending-partner marketing rules triggered?

## Likely rules and issues

**Claim-by-claim analysis (generated from matter facts and general consumer-finance advertising law; not yet source-verified):**

1. **"Pay in four"** — Likely permissible for pay-in-4, but only where that product is actually offered. Misleading if shown to customers in states/products where only longer-term installments are available. Needs state/product gating.
2. **"No interest"** — True only for pay-in-4 and only if no late fees or other charges are implied away. FTC/UDAAP clear-and-conspicuous standard requires proximate disclosure of late fees ("no interest, but late fees may apply"). Cannot be used for longer-term installment products. High-risk claim if run unqualified across channels.
3. **"Easy approval"** — "Easy" is a subjective qualification claim; if approval rates are low or underwriting is credit-based, this is a deceptive "ease of approval" claim (an FTC/CFPB enforcement focus in BNPL). Needs substantiation of actual approval rates or softening ("quick application," "see if you qualify with no impact to your credit score" — the latter itself needs verification).
4. **"Build your credit"** — Only defensible where credit reporting is actually enabled for that product and state, and only with disclosure that on-time payment behavior is reported and reporting may vary. Materially misleading for pay-in-4 in states where reporting is off. Highest-risk claim.
5. **"More control over your budget"** — Puffery-adjacent but risky in a credit product context; CFPB has treated "budget control" framing in BNPL marketing skeptically. Safer as "a way to spread out payments" with clear fee/date disclosure.

**Cross-cutting issues:**
- **UDAAP (CFPB) and FTC Act §5** substantiation and net-impression standard; the overall "simple and flexible" framing plus fine-print fees can create a deceptive net impression.
- **State advertising/licensing rules** — campaign must be gated to the seven live pay-in-4 states and the approved installment-state list; the same creative cannot run everywhere.
- **Representative payment examples** — must reflect typical terms, not best-case; comparison to credit cards must be substantiated and fair (comparing pay-in-4 to a high-APR card while omitting late fees is a classic deceptive comparison).
- **Influencer content** — FTC Endorsement Guides: clear and conspicuous disclosure of material connection; Northstar Pay is responsible for influencer claims and needs contractual claim restrictions and pre-approval.
- **Merchant co-branding** — merchant agreements likely require approval workflows; lending partners may impose their own marketing/brand rules and true-lender sensitivity.
- **Channel-specific disclosure limits** — paid search and social character limits make proximate qualifiers hard; may need claim-specific channel rules (e.g., "no interest" only where a qualifier fits).

## Viable paths

1. **Claim matrix + gating (recommended):** Build a claim-by-channel-by-product/state matrix. Approve "pay in four" and modified "no interest (late fees may apply)" for pay-in-4 states only; condition "build your credit" on confirmed reporting coverage; reject or rewrite "easy approval" and "more control over your budget." Gate ad serving by state and product.
2. **Conservative rewrite:** Drop the two highest-risk claims entirely, run only product-descriptive claims with proximate qualifiers. Faster to clear in one month, lower conversion lift.
3. **Phased launch:** Launch merchant banners and app screens (full disclosure control) first; hold paid search/social/influencer until influencer contracts and substantiation files are complete.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Credit reporting enabled/disabled per product and state (decisive for "build your credit").
- Actual approval rates and whether a soft credit pull is used (decisive for "easy approval").
- Late fee amounts and frequency (decisive for "no interest" qualifier language).
- Whether the campaign can be technically gated by state/product at the ad-serving level.
- Existence of influencer contracts with claim restrictions and pre-approval rights.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the state-by-state product/partner matrix (flagged in the company profile as still being finalized) before approving any state-wide claim.
- Pull actual approval-rate and late-fee data from product/ops as the substantiation file for "easy approval" and "no interest."
- Verify current FTC Endorsement Guides disclosure expectations for influencer posts and confirm agency contracts include claim pre-approval.
- Check lending-partner marketing agreements for required disclaimers or prohibited claims.
- Confirm merchant agreement terms on co-branded material approval.
