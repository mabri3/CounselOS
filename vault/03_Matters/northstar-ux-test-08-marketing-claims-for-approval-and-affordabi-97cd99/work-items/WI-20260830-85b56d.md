# # Orient to the request

## Business objective and timing

Northstar Pay marketing wants to increase use of Northstar Pay at merchant checkout and in the standalone app. The proposed campaign presents Northstar Pay as a simple and flexible way to pay over time. Launch is planned in one month, so claim substantiation, disclosure design, partner input, merchant approvals, and influencer controls must run in parallel.

## Scope and actors

This review covers paid search, social media, merchant-site banners, email, app screens, and influencer content. The working actors are Northstar Pay marketing, merchants, agencies, influencers, customers, and lending partners. The review must distinguish pay-in-four products from longer-term installment loans, and must distinguish products and states where credit reporting or fees differ.

## Known facts

- Not every customer qualifies.

- Late fees may apply in some situations.

- Credit reporting may not be available for every product.

- Longer-term loans may carry interest.

- The team wants representative payment examples.

- The team wants comparisons with credit cards.

- The five proposed headline claims are “pay in four,” “no interest,” “easy approval,” “build your credit,” and “more control over your budget.”

## Initial issue map

1. Whether each claim is accurate for the specific product, state, partner, and channel.

2. Whether “no interest” omits late-fee or longer-term-interest conditions.

3. Whether “easy approval” suggests that approval is guaranteed or broadly available.

4. Whether “build your credit” is supportable when reporting is not universal and when payment history can have mixed effects.

5. Whether “more control over your budget” could become an affordability or suitability claim when paired with purchase or repayment messaging.

6. Whether payment examples are representative and complete about amount, timing, fees, and interest.

7. Whether credit-card comparisons use current, typical, and comparable terms without cherry-picking.

8. Whether disclosures are clear, conspicuous, and close to the claim in every channel.

9. Whether influencers, merchants, and agencies use only approved language.

10. Whether the campaign is gated by state, product, partner, and reporting availability.

## Working claim direction

- “Pay in four”: permitted only when the creative is limited to the pay-in-four product and does not imply universal availability.

- “No interest”: modify to a product-specific statement such as “no interest on eligible pay-in-four purchases,” with any required late-fee and eligibility qualifier.

- “Easy approval”: modify. Safer direction is “quick to apply” or “see if you qualify,” with an approval-not-guaranteed qualifier.

- “Build your credit”: highest-risk claim. Hold for a confirmed reporting map and use only where reporting is live and the required credit-outcome qualifier is approved.

- “More control over your budget”: permitted with care. Do not state or imply that Northstar Pay makes a purchase affordable or is suitable for financial difficulty.

- Payment examples and credit-card comparisons: allowed only with accurate, representative, current support and complete terms.

## Assumptions

- The campaign may use a limited set of currently live states, but national use is not confirmed.

- “Pay in four” and longer-term installment loans have different terms and may need separate creative.

- The one-month date is a target launch date, not a commitment to publish before legal signoff.

- The generated claim assessment is a working analysis, not a verified legal conclusion.

## Missing facts and unverified leads

- Missing: state-by-state product and partner availability.

- Missing: which products and states report payment activity, to which agencies, and when reporting began.

- Missing: fee schedules, late-fee triggers, waiver rules, and product-specific interest terms.

- Missing: approval criteria and the basis for any speed or approval-time statement.

- Missing: data supporting representative payment examples.

- Missing: source, date, population, and methodology for any credit-card comparison.

- Missing: owner and turnaround time for merchant and influencer approvals.

- Missing: final channel layouts and disclosure character limits.

- Unverified lead: the initial review refers to FTC substantiation, clear-and-conspicuous disclosure, and influencer material-connection principles. Treat these as leads for legal verification before publication. No external source was checked in this browser-only run.

## Work items

1. Build a claim matrix by claim, product, state, partner, channel, qualifier, and substantiation source.

2. Confirm the state/product/reporting matrix with lending partners.

3. Confirm fee, interest, and eligibility facts with product and compliance owners.

4. Draft channel-specific copy and disclosure pairings.

5. Review payment examples for representativeness and full repayment terms.

6. Review the credit-card comparison for current and comparable data.

7. Put influencer agreements, script/post pre-approval, material-connection disclosure, monitoring, and takedown controls in place.

8. Put merchant and agency co-brand approval and version-control steps in place.

9. Record legal approval before any channel goes live.

## Channel examples to carry into the memo

- Paid search: pair the short claim with a short qualifier; do not use “easy approval” as a headline.

- Social: keep the product qualifier in the same frame or caption as “no interest”; do not rely on a later comment.

- Merchant banner: name the applicable product and link to terms without separating the qualifying language from the headline.

- Email: place the qualifier next to the claim and include the payment example assumptions.

- App screen: show product selection, approval limits, fees, and payment timing in the same user flow.

- Influencer: require approved script language and a clear material-connection disclosure in the post itself.

## Orientation outcome

The request is understood as a time-sensitive marketing-claims review. The next step is focused research and evidence gathering. The recommendation should separate permitted, modified, and held claims, and the final decision should approve only a gated campaign process with the missing facts resolved.
