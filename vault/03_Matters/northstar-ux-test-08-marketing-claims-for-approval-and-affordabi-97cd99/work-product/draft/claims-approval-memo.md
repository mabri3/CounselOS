## 1. Purpose and bottom line

Northstar Pay proposes a campaign to increase use at merchant checkout and in the standalone app. The campaign will appear in paid search, social media, merchant-site banners, email, app screens, and influencer content. The proposed messages describe Northstar Pay as a simple and flexible way to pay over time and include “pay in four,” “no interest,” “easy approval,” “build your credit,” and “more control over your budget.”

An app screen may explain reporting only after the user selects an eligible product and after the reporting terms are available in that flow. An influencer may not make a personal score-improvement statement or imply that reporting is universal.

Evidence needed: M1, partner agreements, reporting agency and product scope, state availability, reporting start date, timing, customer disclosures, and operational controls for inaccurate data and disputes. If M1 is not confirmed by the end of week 1, remove this claim from launch creative and consider it only in a later gated release.

### 3.5 “More control over your budget”

Status: Permitted with care.

This is a broad benefit statement. It may be used as descriptive marketing if it does not become a promise that financing makes a purchase affordable, is suitable for financial difficulty, or removes repayment risk.

Permitted direction: “Plan your scheduled payments in one place.” A phrase such as “More control over your budget” should be paired with a neutral explanation of payment timing and should not be tied to high-pressure purchase language.

Do not use: “Buy what you need even when you cannot afford it,” “the affordable way to shop,” or “never worry about your budget again.”

Channel example: An app screen may show the payment calendar and say “See your scheduled payments.” An email may describe organization and predictability while reminding the reader to review terms. A merchant banner should not pair the phrase with “shop now, pay later when money is tight.”

Evidence needed: final product UX, payment schedule, fee disclosure, and a review for any adjacent affordability or urgency language.

## 4. Representative payment examples

Payment examples must be accurate and representative of the product and audience. Retain the purchase amount, number and dates of payments, fees, interest, assumptions, eligibility conditions, and the data set used to select the example. Do not present the lowest-fee or easiest example as typical unless the data supports that description.

Example structure for review: “For an eligible $400 purchase, 4 scheduled payments of $100 are due on [dates]. Late fees may apply in some situations. See terms.” The $400 figure and dates are illustrative only and must not be published until M3, M4, and M5 are resolved. If the example is not representative, label it clearly as an example and explain the assumptions in the same user flow.

In paid search, the copy must not show “$100 today” while hiding later payments. In a merchant banner, the amount and schedule should be close to the call to action. In email, the example should include a short assumptions paragraph next to the example, not only in a footer. In the app, the customer should see the schedule before acceptance.

## 5. Credit-card comparisons

A comparison may be used only with current, comparable, and supportable figures. The comparison must identify the card population, source, date, product terms, and whether the comparison is to an APR, fee, repayment schedule, or another defined feature. Do not compare Northstar Pay’s best-case pay-in-four example with a high-end or outdated card APR.

A compliant draft might say: “Compare the cost and timing of this eligible pay-in-four option with the card terms shown below,” followed by the actual assumptions. A headline such as “Better than credit cards” is too broad. Any “save versus a card” language needs M5 and a documented methodology.

The source and date for a typical-card APR is an unverified lead in this run. Legal must verify it before use. Marketing must retain the source file, calculation, and review approval.

## 6. Disclosure design by channel

Paid search has limited space. The limit does not remove the need for a material qualifier. Use only claims whose necessary qualifier fits in the ad, or omit the claim.

Social media must place the product and eligibility qualifier in the same frame, caption, or immediate view. Do not rely on a comment, profile page, or later swipe for a material condition.

Merchant-site banners must use approved co-branded creative. The product name, claim, and key qualifier must stay together. The merchant must not edit the qualifier or substitute a different product link.

Email should place the claim and the payment or product qualifier in the same section. The footer may carry additional terms, but it must not be the only place that explains a material limit.

App screens should show the product selected, eligibility status, payment amounts and dates, fees, and applicable interest before acceptance. Avoid a broad “no interest” label on a page where the customer can move into a longer-term product without a new disclosure.

Influencer content must use the approved script and a clear material-connection disclosure. The creator must not add approval, credit-building, affordability, or comparison claims from personal experience unless Legal approves the exact language.

## 7. Controls and pre-publication process

Northstar Pay should maintain one claim matrix with these fields: claim; product; state; lending partner; channel; permitted wording; prohibited wording; qualifier; evidence file; owner; approval date; and expiration or review date.

Marketing owns the creative brief. Product and operations own the product terms, payment schedule, fee data, approval data, and routing logic. Lending partners own confirmations about interest and reporting. Legal owns the claim matrix signoff. Merchants and agencies must submit co-branded materials for approval and use only the approved version. Influencer agreements must require approved language, a clear disclosure, pre-approval of scripts and posts, monitoring, and takedown rights.

The four-week plan is:

- Week 1: obtain M1–M5 from lending partners, operations, product, and finance; confirm influencer compensation; name merchant approval owners.

- Week 2: complete the claim matrix, resolve or hold blocked claims, and approve channel qualifier patterns.

- Week 3: route creative by state and product; complete influencer and merchant approvals; run spot checks on links, geo-routing, product selection, and disclosure proximity.

- Week 4: launch only approved variants; monitor complaints, edits, influencer posts, and mis-routed creative; preserve the final evidence and signoff packet.

If M1 or M2 is not available by the end of week 1, use the fallback set: qualified “pay in four” and carefully framed budget-organization language. Do not add “build your credit” or “easy approval” informally during the campaign.

This memo recommends a gated launch path, not a blanket approval of the five proposed claims. The claims matrix and evidence packet are the publication gate. On the current record, launch-ready directions are limited to qualified pay-in-four messaging and careful payment-planning language. Interest, approval, and credit-building claims require the missing facts and revised wording identified above.
