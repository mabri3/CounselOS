# Working Legal Analysis — Marketing Claims Research (First Pass)

**Status:** First-pass research complete. This is a working analysis, not court-ready advice. External verification was not available in this run; all legal standards below are generated analysis pending last-mile citation verification by counsel.

## Scope

The five proposed claims ("pay in four," "no interest," "easy approval," "build your credit," "more control over your budget"), representative payment examples, credit-card comparisons, channel disclosures (paid search, social, merchant banners, email, app screens, influencer content), influencer controls, merchant co-branded material approvals, and state/product gating for pay-in-4 vs. longer-term installment products.

## Evidence: Working Legal Analysis by Topic

### 1. The five proposed claims

| Claim | Working analysis | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| "Pay in four" | Accurate only for the pay-in-4 product; must be product-gated and not implied for longer-term installments. Qualify if timing/amounts vary by state or partner. | Low–moderate |
| "No interest" | True only for pay-in-4; longer-term loans carry interest. Unqualified use reaching consumers in interest-bearing products is deceptive under FTC Act §5 / UDAAP standards. Requires product-gated placement or a clear and conspicuous qualifier; late fees undercut any "free" impression. | High |
| "Easy approval" | Not every customer qualifies. Unqualified approval-ease claims are deceptive when a material number of applicants are declined. Requires qualification ("easy to apply," "subject to approval") and substantiation of approval rates for any specific claim. | High |
| "Build your credit" | Credit reporting is not available for every product. A credit-benefit claim without actual reporting is a deceptive performance claim; possible FCRA-adjacent concerns if reporting is inconsistent. Permitted only where reporting is confirmed enabled, with qualifiers otherwise. | Highest |
| "More control over your budget" | Puffery-adjacent; acceptable as aspirational if not tied to specific outcomes and not paired with claims implying no consequences for missed payments. | Low–moderate |

### 2. Representative payment examples
Permitted if representative of typical transactions (not best-case cherry-picking), with material terms — amounts, dates, fees — disclosed clearly and conspicuously. Reg Z closed-end credit advertising rules (§1026.24) likely apply to longer-term installment products if they are credit; triggering-term analysis needed. *(Unverified — see Leads.)*

### 3. Credit-card comparisons
Permissible comparative advertising only if substantiated, apples-to-apples (comparable amounts, terms, fees), and not misleading by omission (e.g., ignoring Northstar's own late fees). Avoid implying credit cards are uniformly worse.

### 4. Channel-specific disclosures
- **Paid search / social:** character limits do not excuse missing material qualifiers; the claim itself must be qualified or the ad restructured.
- **Influencer content:** FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) require clear and conspicuous disclosure of material connections (#ad, paid partnership), placed in the endorsement itself, not buried.
- **Merchant banners:** co-branded materials need merchant approval and consistency with the approved claim library.
- **Email / app screens:** more room for full disclosures; proximity to the claim still required.

### 5. Influencer controls
Written agreements with required claim language, prohibited-claims list, pre-approval of scripts/posts, material-connection disclosure, and monitoring/takedown rights. Influencers are agents for FTC purposes; Northstar owns the liability.

### 6. Merchant approvals
Merchants and agencies must submit co-branded materials for legal approval before publication; contractually obligate merchants to use only approved creative and prohibit claim modification.

### 7. State/product gating
Claims must be mapped to the state-by-state product and partner matrix (pay-in-4 vs. longer-term, credit reporting on/off, fee limits). "Build your credit" and "no interest" cannot run uniformly. Live pay-in-4 states: CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA; longer-term products limited to a smaller approved list.

## Assumptions

1. Campaign targets only currently live pay-in-4 states (CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA); if national, state gating becomes the gating item.
2. Longer-term installment products may be marketed in the same campaign (drives the "no interest" qualification requirement).
3. Influencers will be compensated or receive free product (material-connection disclosure required).
4. Late fees apply in some situations and approval is not universal (per the request's known facts).

## Missing Facts

- Which products and states the campaign will cover; whether credit reporting is enabled for pay-in-4 in each live state.
- Actual late-fee terms and approval/decline rates (needed to substantiate "easy approval" and "no interest" qualifiers).
- Whether influencers are paid or receive free product; planned contractual controls.
- Merchant co-branding approval workflow for banners on merchant sites.
- Existing substantiation files (fee schedules, approval data, credit-reporting partner confirmations).

## Unverified Leads (Last-Mile Verification)

- Confirm current FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) disclosure placement standards for social/influencer formats.
- Verify Reg Z advertising trigger-term rules (§1026.24) applicability to the longer-term installment products given the bank-partner structure.
- Check state-specific advertising/deceptive-practices statutes for the seven live states, particularly CA and NY, for BNPL-specific marketing guidance.
- Confirm CFPB BNPL interpretive rule/circulars on credit-reporting and marketing claims for pay-in-4 products.

## Facts That Could Change the Analysis

- Credit reporting confirmed enabled for pay-in-4 in all live states (lighter qualification for "build your credit").
- Zero late fees (materially strengthens "no interest" positioning).
- Longer-term products fully separated from this campaign (cleaner "no interest" gating).
- High approval-rate data (supports "easy approval" with lighter qualification).
- Unpaid influencers (reduces material-connection burden, does not eliminate accuracy requirements).
