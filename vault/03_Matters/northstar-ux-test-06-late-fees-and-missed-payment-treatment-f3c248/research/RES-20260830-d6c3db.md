# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

Late fees and missed-payment treatment for pay-in-4 (BNPL) and longer-term installment loans in CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA: permissible fee amounts and caps, grace periods, pre-fee notices and reminders, required disclosures, first-fee waiver rules, failed/returned payment fees, credit reporting and collections balances, state variation, and bank-partner true-lender/preemption issues.

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay (Product requester; legal owner Brian Harris) is redesigning its late-fee policy for pay-in-4 and longer-term installment products ahead of the next major product release. The proposed policy includes a grace period, pre-fee reminders, in-app/account-history fee display, and a possible first-late-fee waiver. Pay-in-4 is live in CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, and WA; longer-term installments run on a smaller, still-unfinalized state/partner list. The goal is to recover servicing costs while reducing complaints and keeping the product affordable.

## Decision question

What late-fee structure (fee amount, grace period, reminder timing, first-fee waiver criteria, and failed-payment treatment) can Northstar Pay lawfully implement for pay-in-4 and longer-term installments in each live state, and what disclosures and controls must be in place before the next major product release so fees are not charged incorrectly?

- Which payment methods are in scope (ACH, debit, card), and is a separate returned-payment fee proposed?

## Likely rules and issues

**1. Permissible late fees by product and state (generated analysis; verify before reliance).**

- **Pay-in-4 (BNPL).** The CFPB's 2024 interpretive rule treated BNPL providers like credit card issuers for Regulation Z purposes (TILA/Reg Z § 1026.58, CARD Act late-fee provisions). Under that framework, late fees on pay-in-4 are constrained by credit-card late-fee rules: a fee is generally permitted only after a 21-day grace period, must be disclosed, and cannot exceed card-issuer late-fee limits (historically $29/$40; the $8 cap rule is in litigation — status uncertain). The CFPB under new leadership has moved to rescind the BNPL interpretive rule; confirm current status before finalizing. If the bank-partner structure makes the product bank-originated credit, federal preemption may apply to the originating bank, but state UDAP/UDAAP and licensing theories (CA DFPI's BNPL treatment under the California Financing Law; NY's BNPL licensing law) still apply.
- **Longer-term installments.** Closed-end installment loans governed by state law and the applicable licensing regime: CA CFL fee caps; TX OCCC fee schedules; IL Predatory Loan Prevention Act's 36% all-in cap (whether late fees count toward it matters); NY licensed-lender rules; GA Installment Loan Act; CO and WA consumer-loan statutes. Whether the bank-partner/true-lender structure imports the bank's home-state rates (valid-when-made/Madden issues in NY) is a threshold question per state.

**2. Grace period and notice.** Reg Z (if the BNPL interpretive rule applies) requires a 21-day grace period before a late fee. State installment-loan laws vary; some require written notice of default before a fee. The proposed pre-fee reminders are consistent with — and may exceed — minimums, but timing must be mapped per state.

**3. Disclosures.** Late-fee amount, trigger, and timing must appear in the loan agreement, at checkout (pre-acceptance, per company risk posture), in the app, and in account history. Reg Z periodic-statement analogs may apply to pay-in-4 if the interpretive rule stands. Disclosed fee must match configured fee logic exactly.

**4. Fee-waiver / fair lending.** A first-late-fee waiver with objective, documented criteria (e.g., autopay enrollment, first occurrence, good standing) is generally fine; discretionary or inconsistently applied waivers create disparate-treatment and UDAAP consistency risk. Criteria should be automated, uniformly applied, and auditable.

**5. Failed payments.** Distinguish late fees from returned-payment/NSF fees. State law and Reg Z treat them differently; some states cap NSF fees separately, and charging both for the same event is a common complaint driver. Fee differences by payment method must be disclosed and justified; card-network rules may constrain card-recovery fees.

**6. Reporting and collections.** Late fees generally may be included in the balance due and, if credit reporting is enabled, must be accurately reported (FCRA accuracy and dispute duties; Reg V furnisher rules). Collection referrals of fee-inclusive balances must comply with FDCPA (third-party collectors) and state collections statutes. Confirm partner contracts allocate fee ownership and reporting responsibility.

**7. Controls.** State/product/partner fee-configuration matrix; fee-calculation and waiver logic tested against the matrix; refund/reversal workflow; complaint monitoring tagged to late fees; audit logs; and a pre-launch reconciliation proving disclosed fee equals charged fee in every state/product/partner combination.

## Viable paths

1. **Uniform conservative policy (recommended for launch timing):** single late-fee amount at or below the lowest state cap, 21-day grace period, pre-fee reminder, clear disclosures, automated first-fee waiver with objective criteria. Fastest to implement; lowest incorrect-fee risk.
2. **State-tiered fee schedule:** higher fees where permitted, lower/capped where not. Better economics but requires the finalized state/partner matrix, per-state legal sign-off, and more configuration controls — slower and higher incorrect-fee risk.
3. **No late fees on pay-in-4 only:** rely on reminders and recover servicing costs elsewhere; avoids the uncertain Reg Z/BNPL late-fee landscape for that product while keeping fees on longer-term installments under state schedules.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Rescission or litigation outcome on the CFPB BNPL interpretive rule and the credit-card late-fee cap rule.
- The finalized longer-term installment state/partner list (and whether the true-lender structure supports preemption in each state).
- Whether late fees count toward rate caps (especially IL's 36% PLPA cap).
- Whether fees will be credit-reported or referred to collections with balances.
- Proposed fee amounts and grace-period length versus state minimums.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm current status of the CFPB BNPL interpretive rule and late-fee cap litigation before finalizing pay-in-4 fees.
- Pull the specific late-fee/returned-payment provisions of each live state's applicable statute/regulation for the actual lending partner and license type (CA CFL/DFPI, NY licensed lender + BNPL law, IL PLPA, TX OCCC, CO, GA, WA).
- Confirm the state/partner matrix with operations and each lending partner before any fee schedule is locked.
- Have compliance test the fee-configuration and waiver logic against the final matrix, and document the waiver criteria for fair-lending consistency.

**Recommendation (separate from your decision):** Path 1 — launch with a uniform, conservatively capped late fee, 21-day grace period, pre-fee reminders, full disclosures, and an automated first-fee waiver — then revisit state-tiered pricing once the partner matrix and the CFPB rule landscape settle.
