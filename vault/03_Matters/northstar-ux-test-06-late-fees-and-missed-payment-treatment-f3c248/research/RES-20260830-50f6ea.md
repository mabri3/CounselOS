# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Northstar UX Test — 06 — Late Fees and Missed-Payment Treatment?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay (Product requester; legal owner Brian Harris) is redesigning its late-fee policy for pay-in-4 and longer-term installment products ahead of the next major product release. The proposed policy includes a grace period, pre-fee reminders, in-app/account-history fee display, and a possible first-late-fee waiver. Pay-in-4 is live in CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, and WA; longer-term installments run on a smaller, still-unfinalized partner/state list. The goal is to recover servicing costs while reducing complaints and keeping the product affordable.

## Decision question

What late-fee structure (fee amount, grace period, notice timing, waiver criteria, and failed-payment treatment) can Northstar Pay lawfully implement for pay-in-4 and longer-term installments in each live state, and what disclosures and controls must be in place before the next major product release so fees are not charged incorrectly?

## Open questions

- What are the proposed fee amounts, grace-period length, and waiver criteria? (Not supplied; drives state-cap analysis.)
- Which states and lending partners are approved for longer-term installments? (Partner map still being finalized.)
- Are late fees intended to be credit-reported or included in collection balances?
- Which payment methods are in scope (ACH, debit, card), and is a separate returned-payment fee proposed?
- What is the launch date for the next major release?

## Likely rules and issues

*(Generated analysis from the first-pass research packet; verify before reliance.)*

**1. Permissible late fees by product and state.**
- **Pay-in-4 (BNPL).** The CFPB's 2024 interpretive rule treated BNPL providers like credit card issuers for Regulation Z purposes, importing credit-card late-fee rules: a fee generally permitted only after a 21-day grace period, must be disclosed, and capped at card-issuer late-fee limits (historically $29/$40; the $8 cap rule is in litigation). The CFPB has since moved to rescind the BNPL interpretive rule — confirm current status before finalizing. Bank-partner origination may support federal preemption, but state UDAP/UDAAP and licensing theories (CA DFPI's BNPL treatment under the California Financing Law; NY's BNPL licensing law) still apply.
- **Longer-term installments.** Closed-end installment loans governed by state law and licensing regime: CA CFL fee caps, TX OCCC regulated-loan fee schedules, IL Predatory Loan Prevention Act 36% all-in cap (whether late fees count toward it matters), NY licensed-lender rules, GA Installment Loan Act, CO and WA consumer-loan statutes. True-lender/valid-when-made (Madden in NY) is a threshold question per state.

**2. Grace period and notice.** Reg Z (if the BNPL rule applies) requires a 21-day grace period before a late fee. State installment-loan laws vary; some require written default notice before a fee. Pre-fee reminders are consistent with minimums but timing must be mapped per state.

**3. Disclosures.** Late-fee amount, trigger, and timing must appear in the loan agreement, at checkout (pre-acceptance), in the app, and in account history — and must match configured fee logic exactly. Reg Z periodic-statement analogs may apply to pay-in-4 if the interpretive rule stands.

**4. Fee-waiver / fair lending.** A first-late-fee waiver with objective, documented, automated criteria is generally fine; discretionary or inconsistently applied waivers create disparate-treatment and UDAAP consistency risk.

**5. Failed payments.** Distinguish late fees from returned-payment/NSF fees; states cap these separately, and charging both for one event is a common complaint driver. Payment-method fee differences must be disclosed; card-network rules may constrain card-recovery fees.

**6. Reporting and collections.** Late fees generally may be included in the balance due; if credit reporting is enabled, FCRA/Reg V accuracy and dispute duties apply. Collection referrals must comply with FDCPA and state collections statutes. Partner contracts must allocate fee ownership and reporting responsibility.

**7. Controls.** State/product/partner fee-configuration matrix; fee-calculation and waiver logic tested against it; refund/reversal workflow; complaint monitoring tagged to late fees; audit logs; pre-launch reconciliation that disclosed fee equals charged fee in every combination.

## Viable paths

1. **Uniform conservative policy (recommended for launch timing):** single late-fee amount at or below the lowest state cap, 21-day grace period, pre-fee reminder, clear disclosures, automated first-fee waiver with objective criteria. Fastest, lowest error risk.
2. **State-tiered fee schedule:** higher fees where permitted, capped where not. Better economics but requires the finalized state/partner matrix, per-state sign-off, and heavier configuration controls — slower, higher incorrect-fee risk.
3. **No late fees on pay-in-4 only:** rely on reminders and recover servicing costs elsewhere; avoids the uncertain Reg Z/BNPL late-fee landscape while keeping fees on longer-term installments under state schedules.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Rescission or litigation outcome on the CFPB BNPL interpretive rule and the credit-card late-fee cap rule.
- The finalized longer-term installment state/partner list (and whether the true-lender structure supports preemption in each state).
- Whether late fees count toward rate caps (especially IL's 36% PLPA cap).
- Whether fees will be credit-reported or referred to collections with balances.
- Proposed fee amounts and grace-period length versus state minimums.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the current status of the CFPB BNPL interpretive rule and late-fee cap litigation before finalizing pay-in-4 fees.
- Pull the specific late-fee/returned-payment provisions of each live state's applicable statute/regulation for the actual lending partner and license type (CA CFL/DFPI, NY licensed lender + BNPL law, IL PLPA, TX OCCC, CO, GA, WA).
- Confirm the state/partner matrix with operations and each lending partner before any fee schedule is locked.
- Have compliance test the fee-configuration and waiver logic against the final matrix, and document the waiver criteria for fair-lending consistency.

**Recommendation (separate from your decision):** Path 1 — launch with a uniform, conservatively capped late fee, 21-day grace period, pre-fee reminders, full disclosures, and an automated first-fee waiver — then revisit state-tiered pricing once the partner matrix and the CFPB rule landscape settle.
