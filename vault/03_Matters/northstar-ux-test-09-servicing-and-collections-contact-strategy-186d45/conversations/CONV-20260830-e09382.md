    contact strategy test.


    ## The decision

    Product wants to run an 8-week test of a redesigned delinquency contact flow (app,
    email, SMS, phone) with payment links, hardship forms, date-change options, and
    possible third-party placement. The legal question is what guardrails make the
    test safe to run without creating FDCPA/TCPA/state-collection exposure.


    ## Key issue map


    **1. Consent and channel rules (TCPA)**

    - SMS and prerecorded/artificial-voice calls to a cell phone generally need prior
    express consent; the scope of consent obtained at origination matters (marketing
    vs. servicing contacts).

    - Revocation: customers must be able to opt out of texts/calls; honor disputed
    contact info.

    - Quiet hours: 8 a.m.–9 p.m. local time for calls/texts is the floor; several
    states are stricter.


    **2. FDCPA-style conduct — even for first-party/creditor communications**

    - Northstar as creditor isn''t an FDCPA "debt collector," but the collections
    vendor is — and vendor conduct is attributed to you via contract and CFPB scrutiny.
    Also, several states (e.g., California''s Rosenthal Act) extend FDCPA duties to
    first-party collectors.

    - Content limits: no false/misleading statements, no unfair practices, no third-party
    disclosure of the debt (shared phone numbers and household members are a real
    risk — verify you''re talking to the customer before revealing account details).

    - Employer contact: generally prohibited for consumer debts; restrict in the contact
    engine.

    - Validation notice: required within 5 days of initial communication if a "debt
    collector" is involved; dispute handling must pause escalation pending verification.


    **3. Contact frequency and harassment**

    - No bright-line federal cap, but repeated/multi-channel daily contact invites
    harassment claims. Set per-channel and aggregate caps (e.g., ~7 attempts/week,
    1 conversation per day), with early- vs. late-delinquency tiers as Product proposes.


    **4. Permissible content in messages**

    - FDCPA §1692f(6)-style limits and state analogs: messages should identify sender,
    avoid disclosing debt status to third parties who may see the device, and avoid
    "overshadowing" (statements that rush the validation/dispute window).

    - Payment links: ensure the link destination is secure, branded, and doesn''t
    leak account data.


    **5. Call recording**

    - Two-party-consent states (CA, WA, and others) require all-party consent before
    recording; get consent prompt or disable recording by state.


    **6. Placement with third-party vendor**

    - Placement trigger should be documented (e.g., 60–90 days delinquent, post-validation,
    post-hardship review).

    - Vendor oversight: written agreement with FDCPA/FCRA/GLBA compliance reps, audit
    rights, complaint monitoring, chain-of-attorney rules, and state licensing/collection-agency
    registration checks.


    **7. Multi-loan and language-preference handling**

    - Contact engine must dedupe across loans (no stacking of messages per loan),
    respect language preference, and suppress accounts with active disputes or hardship
    requests.


    ## Recommended test guardrails

    1. Channel caps + quiet hours + employer-contact block built into the contact
    engine before launch.

    2. Consent/revocation check at each send; suppress disputed numbers.

    3. Validation-notice and dispute workflow confirmed before any third-party placement
    (probably exclude placement from the 8-week test, or run it only on a small, reviewed
    cohort).

    4. Vendor due diligence and contract terms in place before any placement.

    5. Test monitoring: complaint log, escalation log, weekly legal review of message
    templates and contact-frequency data.


    ## Assumptions and gaps

    - I''m assuming the bank/lending partner is the creditor of record in some states
    — the responsible entity for communications may differ by partner, which affects
    who must send notices and how vendor oversight flows. The partner map is still
    open per the company profile.

    - Whether call recording is even proposed is unclear; if not, that issue drops
    out.

    - Hardship form responses likely trigger servicing obligations (loss-mit style
    handling) — worth confirming the intake and decision workflow with the partner.


    Want me to run first-pass research on the state-specific contact limits and validation-notice
    requirements, or draft the issue map and recommendations into the matter files
    first?'

    ## Path 1: Conservative (recommended) — first-party only

    App, email, consent-verified SMS; manual calls only, recording disabled/consent-gated
    in CA/WA; no third-party placement.


    **Pros**

    - **Removes the highest-risk variables:** no vendor FDCPA attribution, no validation-notice
    obligation at placement, no vendor licensing exposure, no automated-dialing TCPA
    risk.

    - **Faster to launch:** no vendor diligence dependency; the only pre-launch gates
    are consent-language review and contact-engine controls.

    - **Still tests the core hypothesis:** the redesigned messaging, payment link,
    hardship form, and date-change option are the experiment — placement isn''t needed
    to measure repayment lift.

    - **Cleaner data:** fewer confounding variables means the 8-week results are easier
    to attribute to the messaging changes.


    **Cons**

    - **Doesn''t test the escalation endpoint:** Product''s goal includes "avoid unnecessary
    escalation," but the test never reaches the placement decision, so you learn nothing
    about how the new early-stage messaging affects downstream placement rates.

    - **Deferred work doesn''t disappear:** vendor diligence, licensing checks, and
    the placement-trigger design still have to happen — just after the test.

    - **Late-delinquency cohort is underserved:** accounts at 45+ days get "final
    first-party notices" only, which may under-test the late-stage messaging tiers
    Product proposed.


    ## Path 2: Full-scope — include placement and recording

    Everything in Path 1 plus third-party placement and call recording.


    **Pros**

    - **Tests the full lifecycle,** including the placement handoff and vendor conduct
    — the riskiest real-world moment.

    - **Placement and recording diligence gets done now,** on a forced deadline, rather
    than being deferred indefinitely.


    **Cons**

    - **Vendor dependency blocks the timeline:** licensing confirmation in all seven
    states, written FDCPA/FCRA/GLBA agreement, audit rights, and complaint monitoring
    must all be in place before launch — unlikely inside an 8-week window that also
    has to run the test.

    - **Partner map is still open:** you can''t confirm who sends validation notices
    at placement until the partner map is finalized. Launching placement without that
    is a compliance gap, not just a risk.

    - **Recording adds state-by-state consent complexity** (CA/WA all-party) for marginal
    test value.

    - **Confounded results:** if repayment improves, you can''t tell whether the messaging
    or the placement threat drove it.


    ## Path 3: Phased (middle option)

    Start with Path 1; add placement mid-test for a small cohort once diligence completes.


    **Pros:** captures some placement data if diligence finishes in time; preserves
    the fast launch.

    **Cons:** mid-test design change confounds the results; creates a two-regime test
    that''s harder to analyze in 8 weeks; diligence timing is uncertain, so the placement
    phase may never materialize.


    ## Bottom line


    | | Launch speed | Risk | Tests full lifecycle | Result clarity |

    |---|---|---|---|---|

    | **Conservative** | Fast | Low | No | High |

    | **Full-scope** | Slow (diligence-gated) | High | Yes | Low |

    | **Phased** | Fast start | Medium | Partially | Muddled |


    The conservative path wins on speed, risk, and result clarity — and its main cost
    (not testing placement) can be recovered in a phase 2 once the partner map and
    vendor diligence close. The full-scope path''s only real advantage is testing
    placement now, but it''s gated on facts you don''t yet have (partner map, vendor
    identity/licensing), which is exactly why the research flagged it as premature.


    The recommendation stands as drafted: **Path 1 for the 8-week test, with placement
    diligence running in parallel** so phase 2 isn''t delayed. Should this become
    a durable decision?'

    ## 3. Channel-specific controls


    ### App notifications

    In-app messaging reaches only the authenticated customer, so third-party disclosure
    risk is minimal. Every notification must identify Northstar Pay as sender, state
    the amount due and due date plainly, and present the payment link, hardship form,
    and date-change option with equal prominence. Messaging must not use urgency language
    that overshadows dispute rights. Language preference must be honored, and all
    messaging must be deduplicated across multiple loans so a customer with several
    delinquent loans sees one consolidated view rather than parallel threads.


    ### Email

    Email controls center on sender identification and non-disclosure to household
    members or others with device access. The from-line and body must identify Northstar
    Pay; subject lines and preview text must not disclose debt status (e.g., "Update
    on your Northstar Pay account," not "Your overdue payment"). Payment links must
    be secure, branded, and must not embed account numbers or passcodes in the URL.
    Disputed email addresses must be suppressed with one-click action, and unsubscribe/revocation
    must be honored promptly. Quiet-hours rules do not apply to email, but batching
    sends into business hours reduces perceived harassment.


    ### SMS (consent-gated)

    SMS may be sent only where origination consent verifiably covers servicing and
    collections messaging — this must be confirmed against current consent language
    before launch (open fact). Revocation must be honored through any reasonable channel,

    federal floor, with stricter state rules verified before launch. Per-channel caps

    and three to four per week per customer. Message content must identify the sender,
    state the amount and due date, avoid account-detail disclosure on a shared device,
    and link to the secure payment page rather than embedding data.

    Calls must be manually dialed — no autodialed, prerecorded, or artificial-voice

    are enforced. **Recording is disabled by default in California and Washington**

    verification. Employer contact is prohibited: the contact engine must block numbers
    identified as workplace or employer lines. Agent scripts must avoid false or misleading
    statements, threats of action not intended, and any statement that overshadows
    dispute or validation rights. Agents must verify identity before discussing the
    account and must not disclose debt status to third parties who answer.
