## Summary

Northstar Pay (Product team) wants to redesign servicing and collections communications for customers with missed payments, testing over the next eight weeks. The flow uses app notifications, email, SMS, and possibly phone calls, offering payment links, hardship forms, and payment-date changes; persistently delinquent accounts may go to a third-party collections vendor. Lending partners, servicing agents, and collections vendors participate. Legal (Brian Harris) must advise on communication rules, vendor oversight, escalation, and product controls before the test launches.

## Decision question

Which communication rules, contact-frequency limits, required notices, consent and call-recording requirements, and vendor-oversight controls must Northstar Pay build into the eight-week delinquency-contact test so it improves repayment without violating federal and state collections, TCPA, FDCPA, and lending-partner requirements — and at what delinquency point may an account be placed with a third-party collector?

## Open questions

- Which entity (Northstar Pay vs. the bank/lending partner) is the creditor/owner of the delinquent account in each state, and does that change who is a "debt collector" under the FDCPA?
- What consent language and records exist for each channel (app push, email, SMS, calls), and does TCPA prior express consent cover collections messaging?
- Which states are in scope for the test, since quiet hours, contact-frequency limits, recording consent (one-party vs. all-party), and required notices vary by state?
- What do the lending-partner agreements say about servicing standards, escalation timing, and third-party placement authority?
- What hardship and payment-date-change policies apply, and do they trigger loss-mitigation or Regulation B/FCU notice obligations?
