# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

For CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA: state rules on collections contact — quiet hours, contact-frequency limits, first-party (creditor) and third-party collection statutes (e.g., Rosenthal), validation notices and dispute handling, debt collector licensing/registration, call-recording consent, employer-contact restrictions, and digital message (SMS/email) content limits.

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay's Product team wants to redesign servicing and collections communications for customers with missed payments, testing the new flow over eight weeks. Channels include app notifications, email, SMS, and possibly phone calls, offering payment links, hardship requests, and payment-date changes. Accounts that remain delinquent may be placed with a third-party collections vendor. Legal (Brian Harris) is involved because the flow touches TCPA consent, FDCPA-style conduct rules, state collection statutes, call-recording consent, and vendor oversight across the seven live pay-in-4 states (CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA).

## Decision question

What contact-frequency caps, quiet hours, message-content limits, validation/dispute handling, call-recording consent mechanics, and third-party placement triggers must the eight-week test include so that Northstar Pay improves repayment rates without exposing itself, its lending partners, or its collections vendor to TCPA, FDCPA, Rosenthal, and state licensing/recording liability in CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, and WA?

## Open questions

- Which entity (Northstar or the bank/lending partner) is the creditor of record for each product/state, and who must send required notices? The partner map is still being finalized.
- Are third-party placement and call recording actually in scope for the eight-week test, or should they be deferred to a later phase?
- What is the current consent capture for SMS/calls at origination, and does it cover servicing/collections use and revocation handling?
- Which collections vendor is contemplated, and is it licensed/registered in all seven states?
- Do any state-specific quiet hours or frequency limits (stricter than the federal 8 a.m.–9 p.m. floor) apply in the live states?

---

## Likely rules and issues

**Federal floor (applies everywhere):**
- **TCPA:** Prior express consent required for autodialed/prerecorded/artificial-voice calls and texts to wireless numbers; consent scope from origination may not clearly cover collections messaging; revocation must be honored through any reasonable channel. Quiet hours 8 a.m.–9 p.m. *local* time for artificial/prerecorded calls.
- **FDCPA:** Northstar as first-party creditor is likely not a "debt collector," but the third-party vendor is — full FDCPA conduct rules apply at placement (validation notice within 5 days of initial communication, dispute/verification pause, no third-party disclosure, no employer contact, no harassment, no false/misleading statements). Northstar's own conduct should mirror FDCPA standards as a compliance-design choice.

**State-by-state highlights (generated analysis; verify before launch):**

| State | Key points |
|---|---|
| **CA** | Rosenthal Act extends FDCPA duties to first-party creditors — Northstar itself must follow FDCPA-style conduct. All-party consent for call recording. |
| **CO** | Collection agency licensing required for third-party collectors; state conduct rules largely mirror FDCPA. |
| **GA** | Debt collector registration; conduct rules generally FDCPA-aligned. |
| **IL** | Collection Agency Act licensing; conduct rules generally FDCPA-aligned. |
| **NY** | NYC adds local debt-collection rules (contact frequency, disclosure requirements) if NYC customers are in the test; state conduct rules generally FDCPA-aligned. |
| **TX** | Third-party debt collector licensing/registration; conduct rules generally FDCPA-aligned. |
| **WA** | Collection Agency Act licensing; all-party consent for call recording. |

**Cross-cutting design issues:**
- **Frequency:** No bright-line federal cap, but multi-channel daily contact invites harassment claims. Set per-channel and aggregate caps with early/late delinquency tiers; dedupe across multiple loans so a customer with three loans doesn't get triple contact.
- **Message content:** Identify sender, avoid debt-status disclosure visible to third parties on shared devices, don't overshadow validation/dispute rights, use secure branded payment links.
- **Disputed contact info:** Suppress disputed numbers/emails pending verification.
- **Hardship/payment-date changes:** Hardship requests should pause escalation pending review; date changes may implicate partner modification/re-aging rules.

## Viable paths

1. **Recommended — conservative test scope:** Run the eight-week test with first-party communications only (app, email, SMS with verified consent, manual calls with recording disabled or consent-prompted in CA/WA). Defer third-party placement and call recording to a later phase after vendor diligence. This keeps the test inside well-understood rules.
2. **Full-scope test:** Include third-party placement and recording, but only after (a) vendor licensing confirmed in all seven states, (b) written vendor agreement with FDCPA/FCRA/GLBA reps and audit rights, (c) documented placement trigger (e.g., 60+ days delinquent, post-validation, post-hardship-review), and (d) state-specific recording consent flows. Higher lift, longer timeline.
3. **Phased:** Start with path 1, add vendor placement mid-test for a small cohort once diligence completes.

## Facts that could change the answer

- The partner map: if the bank partner is creditor of record, notice obligations and vendor oversight flow differently than if Northstar is.
- Whether consent language at origination covers servicing/collections messaging — if not, SMS/call volume drops sharply or re-consent is needed.
- Whether NYC customers are in the test (NYC local rules add frequency and disclosure requirements).
- Whether the vendor is already licensed in all seven states.
- Whether Product's "possibly phone calls" includes automated dialing (TCPA exposure) vs. manual calls only.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the Rosenthal Act's current application to first-party creditors and any recent CA amendments (e.g., SB 903 debt-collection licensing).
- Verify current licensing/registration status of the contemplated collections vendor in each of the seven states.
- Confirm all-party recording consent requirements in CA and WA and any state-specific quiet-hour rules stricter than the federal floor.
- Confirm the origination consent language's scope for collections messaging and the revocation workflow.
- Confirm the partner map and which entity must send validation notices at placement.

**Recommendation (separate from your decision):** Proceed with path 1 — first-party-only test scope with mirrored FDCPA conduct standards, per-channel and aggregate contact caps, deduplication across loans, disputed-contact suppression, and recording disabled or consent-gated in CA/WA. Defer third-party placement until vendor diligence and the partner map are confirmed. This lets Product test the messaging strategy on schedule while keeping the highest-risk elements out of the eight-week window.
