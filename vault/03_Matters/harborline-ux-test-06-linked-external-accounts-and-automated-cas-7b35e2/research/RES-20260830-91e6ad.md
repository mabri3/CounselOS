# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Harborline UX Test — 06 — Linked External Accounts and Automated Cash Sweeps?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Harborline (U.S. fintech operating with a sponsor bank partner) wants to let business customers link external bank accounts and set automated sweep rules that pull funds via ACH debit when the Harborline balance falls below a set minimum. The flow uses a data-aggregation provider for ownership/balance data, with Harborline or its bank partner originating ACH entries, and role-based permissions for finance teams. Product targets release in twelve weeks. Counsel must advise on authorization, account validation, Nacha, EFT, privacy, and security requirements before build decisions are locked.

## Decision question

What consent, account-validation, and notice design should Harborline adopt for automated external-account sweeps — including the exact authorization and renewal process, whether each debit requires advance notice, how revoked authority and returned entries are handled, and which roles may create or edit sweep rules — so the feature launches in twelve weeks without violating Nacha rules, EFTA/Regulation E, or partner-bank requirements?

## Likely rules and issues

**Authorization and consent (Nacha Rules)**
- Nacha requires the Originator to obtain a written or similarly authenticated authorization for each debit before the first entry; the authorization must be retained and provided to the ODFI/RDFI on request. An online click-through with MFA is likely sufficient if it clearly identifies the external account, amount or amount-determination method (sweep rule), timing/frequency, and revocation procedure.
- Sweeps triggered by a balance condition are "variable amount" debits — the authorization must describe how the amount is determined (the minimum-balance rule) and the daily check cadence.
- Renewal: Nacha does not require periodic reauthorization, but the authorization must remain accurate; a change in the linked account, rule, or amount-determination method requires new consent. Decide whether Harborline will impose a periodic reconfirmation (e.g., annual) as a risk control.

**Account validation (Nacha account-validation rule)**
- For WEB debits, Nacha mandates account validation on first use and after account changes. This is a TEL/CCD/PPD context (business accounts), where validation is not universally mandated but is strongly expected by ODFI agreements and fraud controls. Options: micro-deposits, instant account verification through the aggregator, or Nacha-recognized validation methods. The missing fact of validation method is a build decision that must be made early.
- Ownership validation is the harder issue: the aggregator confirms the account belongs to the *user who linked it*, not that the user is authorized to act for the *business*. Controls needed: link only accounts held in the business's name (or matching TIN), require admin-level approval for new linked accounts, and block or manually review personal-name accounts and affiliate-owned accounts.

**Advance notice per debit**
- Nacha does not require advance notice of each entry under a standing authorization, but the authorization must state the timing/frequency. For a balance-triggered sweep, the authorization should disclose that debits occur on any day the balance falls below the threshold, up to a cap. Consider a notification (push/email) after each sweep as a fraud and dispute-reduction control, not a legal prerequisite.
- EFTA/Regulation E likely does not apply (business accounts, ACH not covered by Reg E's consumer protections), but check whether any consumer sole proprietors are treated as consumer accounts — that changes the analysis materially.

**Revoked authority and returned entries**
- The authorization must state how the customer revokes (in-app, written) and that Harborline will stop debits within a defined window (Nacha requires stopping after receipt of revocation notice to the Originator/ODFI). Build an immediate rule-disable on revocation.
- Returned entries: handle R01 (insufficient funds), R02, R05 (unauthorized), R07, R08, R10 (unauthorized per customer statement), R29. R05/R10/R29 patterns can trigger Nacha return-rate thresholds (unauthorized return rate 0.5%, overall 15%) — monitor and have a suspension protocol. Multiple R10s can lead to ODFI termination of origination privileges.

**Roles and permissions**
- Recommend: only administrators can create/edit sweep rules and link external accounts; employees can view. MFA for rule changes is already planned — extend MFA to linking new external accounts. Log all changes with actor, time, and rule version.

**Privacy and data use**
- Showing external balances pulled via the aggregator: the aggregator's own terms and the customer's aggregator login constitute consent for retrieval, but Harborline's privacy notice should disclose that external-account data is collected, used for sweep decisions, and retained. Using balances in automated decisions is not FCRA "consumer report" use for business accounts, but if any sole-proprietor consumer accounts are in scope, revisit. GLBA safeguards apply to the financial data; ensure the aggregator contract covers data minimization, retention limits, and breach notice.

**Security**
- MFA for rule changes (planned), plus: step-up authentication for new linked accounts, velocity/amount limits on sweeps, monitoring for anomalous rule changes, and audit logs. Vendor oversight of the aggregator under partner-bank third-party risk expectations.

## Viable paths

1. **Conservative launch (recommended):** Admin-only linking and rule creation; aggregator-based instant account verification plus name/TIN match to the business; authorization flow with explicit disclosure of amount-determination, frequency, revocation, and error resolution; post-sweep notifications; return-rate monitoring with auto-suspension; annual reconfirmation of standing rules. Launch in twelve weeks is feasible.
2. **Faster launch:** Micro-deposit validation only, employee-initiated linking with admin approval, no periodic reconfirmation. Faster but higher unauthorized-return and dispute risk; ODFI may push back.
3. **Deferred:** Hold feature pending partner-bank sign-off on origination responsibility split (who is the Originator — Harborline or the bank). This is a threshold question that should be resolved now, not deferred.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Whether any sole proprietors or consumer accounts are in scope (Reg E / EFTA analysis flips).
- Whether Harborline or the bank partner is the ODFI-side Originator (allocates Nacha obligations, indemnities, and return-rate exposure).
- The account-validation method chosen (aggregator IAV vs. micro-deposits) and whether the aggregator can confirm business ownership vs. individual login ownership.
- Whether affiliate-owned or joint accounts will be permitted (changes authorization and ownership-control design).
- Debit limits and whether sweeps can overdraw the external account (affects return rates and RDFI relations).

## Open questions

- Who is the Originator of record for the ACH entries — Harborline or the sponsor bank — and what does the ODFI agreement require for account validation and return-rate monitoring?
- Will the first release include sole proprietors or any consumer-purpose accounts, or strictly business entities?
- What account-validation method will the build use, and can the aggregator verify that the linked account is owned by the business rather than the individual user?
- Will affiliate-owned or joint external accounts be permitted, and if so under what controls?
- What per-debit and aggregate sweep limits will apply, and what is the protocol for R05/R10/R29 returns and Nacha return-rate thresholds?

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm current Nacha Rules text on authorization content, retention, revocation, and return-rate thresholds (Rules are amended annually — verify the operative version at launch).
- Review the ODFI/bank-partner origination agreement for validation, indemnity, and return-rate obligations.
- Review the aggregator contract and Harborline's privacy notice for external-balance data use, retention, and sharing disclosures.
- Confirm with Product whether any consumer-purpose accounts are in scope before finalizing the Reg E conclusion.
