## Business ask
Ship a limited beta in four months of a Business Payments API where software platforms onboard their small-business customers, accept card/ACH payments, and direct proceeds among business, platform, and Harborline fees — with funds held at the partner bank.

## The decision that drives everything
**Funds flow and account titling.** The three undecided items (pooled vs. individual accounts, platform refund control, reserve duration) determine whether Harborline or the platforms trip money-transmission licensing, stored-value, or payment-facilitator obligations. Legal needs to pick the acceptable funds flow before engineering finalizes architecture.

## Issue map

**1. Money transmission (state licensing).** If funds are held at the partner bank at all times and neither Harborline nor the platform takes possession/control, the "sponsored payment"/agency exemption usually holds. Risk concentrates in: platform-controlled refunds, platform fee deduction before settlement, and any pooled balance held for the platform's benefit.

**2. Payment facilitator (card networks).** Splitting card proceeds among business, platform, and Harborline fees will likely make Harborline (or the bank) the payment facilitator. Requires network registration, sub-merchant onboarding standards, and reserve/chargeback rules. This is a partner-bank and network conversation, not just internal.

**3. Funds transfer / agency.** Who is the originator/financial institution under UCC 4A (ACH) and whether Reg E applies (consumer payers?). Who issues payment instructions — platform, Harborline, or bank — determines agency characterization and error-resolution duties.

**4. Stored value.** Pooled custodial accounts with delayed allocation are the main risk; prompt allocation to bank-held subaccounts mitigates.

**5. Compliance allocation.** CIP/CDD on sub-merchants (platform vs. Harborline vs. bank), sanctions screening, SAR ownership, Reg E error-resolution timelines, and return/chargeback responsibility split.

## Facts to confirm before architecture is finalized
- Partner bank's position on account titling, reserves, and platform-issued instructions
- Card network registration requirements for the split-payment model
- ACH originator/ODFI role for each transfer
- Whether payers include consumers (Reg E trigger)

## Suggested next steps
1. Run first-pass research on the money-transmission and payment-facilitator analysis for this structure
2. Draft a recommendation on the acceptable funds flow (my preliminary lean: individual subaccounts at the partner bank, Harborline-issued instructions only, platform fee deducted at settlement by Harborline — not pre-settlement by the platform)
3. Create work items for the partner-bank and network confirmations
