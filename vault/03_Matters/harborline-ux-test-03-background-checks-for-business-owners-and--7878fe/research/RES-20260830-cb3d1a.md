# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Harborline UX Test — 03 — Background Checks for Business Owners and High-Risk Merchants?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Harborline's risk team wants enhanced screening for business-account applicants in higher-risk sectors (money services, online marketplaces, digital assets, adult content, firearms, cross-border trade). The proposed process combines identity verification, sanctions/PEP screening, adverse-media searches, criminal-record checks, business-registration records, and manual review, with Harborline analysts deciding and escalated cases going to the partner bank. Product wants a six-week pilot with applicants from all 50 states, and checks may cover owners, directors, and key employees. No vendor, lookback periods, disqualifying criteria, or dispute process has been chosen yet.

## Decision question

Which background checks can Harborline lawfully run on business applicants and their owners, directors, and key employees in a six-week pilot across all U.S. states, and what notice, consent, adverse-action, and dispute process must be built in — including whether FCRA and state consumer/employment reporting laws apply to account-eligibility screening — so that vendor selection and the operating procedure can be finalized before launch?

## Open questions

- Will criminal-record checks be obtained through a consumer reporting agency (CRA) or via public records directly? This determines whether FCRA's user obligations (permissible purpose, authorization, pre-adverse-action notice, dispute rights) attach.
- Which individuals will be screened (ownership threshold, directors, key employees), and will any be treated as consumers vs. business representatives under state mini-FCRA laws?
- What disqualifying findings and lookback periods will the risk team apply, and are they documented as risk-based criteria rather than proxies for protected classes?
- What does the partner bank require contractually or under its BSA/AML program for escalated cases, and does it mandate specific screening standards or vendor certifications?
- What data retention limits and deletion rights will apply to adverse-media and criminal-record data, given state privacy laws (e.g., California CCPA/CPRA) and data-minimization principles?

## Likely rules and issues

**FCRA applicability (the threshold issue).** FCRA applies if Harborline obtains "consumer reports" from a CRA for account eligibility. FCRA's permissible purposes include legitimate business need in connection with a business transaction initiated by the consumer (15 U.S.C. § 1681b(a)(3)(F)) — so account-eligibility screening can trigger FCRA even though it is not employment or credit underwriting. If criminal-record or adverse-media data comes from a CRA, Harborline must: (1) provide a clear and conspicuous standalone disclosure and obtain written authorization before pulling the report; (2) send a pre-adverse-action notice with a copy of the report and the Summary of Rights, with a reasonable waiting period (typically at least 5 business days); and (3) send a final adverse-action notice with the CRA's contact information. Sanctions/PEP screening and identity verification generally fall outside FCRA (regulatory compliance purpose, not a consumer report), but adverse-media searches from a CRA may qualify. *Generated analysis — verify current CFPB guidance on FCRA scope for account opening.*

**State mini-FCRA and employment-reporting laws.** Several states (e.g., California ICRAA, Washington, New York) regulate background checks for "employment purposes" broadly; some may reach business-owner screening if the individual is treated as an applicant for a business relationship. California's ICRAA in particular has been applied expansively. State criminal-record inquiry restrictions (ban-the-box analogues for non-employment contexts) and restrictions on reporting older convictions (California's 7-year rule, similar limits in other states) may cap lookback periods. *Unverified — state-by-state mapping needed.*

**BSA/AML and partner-bank overlay.** CIP/CDD obligations under 31 C.F.R. § 1020.220 and beneficial-ownership rules (31 C.F.R. § 1010.230) already require identity verification and ownership screening; sanctions screening (OFAC) is mandatory and cannot be delegated away. The partner bank retains ultimate compliance responsibility, so its third-party risk management expectations (Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships) will shape vendor selection, escalation SLAs, and audit rights.

**Privacy and data minimization.** Adverse-media and criminal-record data on non-applicant individuals (directors, key employees) raises consent and notice issues; state comprehensive privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA and others) may grant access/deletion rights. Retention should be limited to documented risk-management needs, with confidential monitoring information protected.

**Discrimination risk.** Disqualifying criteria must be documented, risk-based, and consistently applied. Blanket criminal-history exclusions can create disparate-impact exposure under ECOA (if credit-adjacent) and state anti-discrimination laws; the partner bank will expect a written risk matrix.

## Viable paths

1. **FCRA-compliant track (recommended).** Treat criminal-record and adverse-media checks as consumer reports: standalone disclosure + written authorization at onboarding, pre-adverse-action notice with report copy and Summary of Rights, 5-business-day window for the applicant to respond, then final adverse-action notice with dispute rights routed to the CRA. Automate identity, sanctions/PEP, and business-registration checks; require human review for all criminal-record, adverse-media, and PEP hits before any decline.
2. **Non-FCRA track.** If criminal-record data is pulled directly from public records (not a CRA), FCRA user obligations do not attach, but accuracy, dispute, and state-law risks increase and the partner bank may not accept it. Generally weaker for a 50-state pilot.
3. **Phased pilot.** Launch with identity, sanctions/PEP, business-registration, and adverse-media screening only; add criminal-record checks after FCRA workflow and vendor diligence are complete. Reduces six-week timeline risk.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Whether the chosen vendor is a CRA and which products are "consumer reports."
- Whether key employees (non-owners) are screened — this expands consent and state-law exposure significantly.
- The partner bank's contractual screening requirements and whether it mandates its own approved vendor list.
- Whether any pilot applicants are California residents (ICRAA and 7-year reporting limits).
- Whether disqualifying criteria include criminal history at all, or only sanctions/PEP/adverse-media.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm FCRA scope for account-eligibility screening against current CFPB guidance and the vendor's report definitions.
- Map state mini-FCRA and criminal-record reporting limits for all 50 states (or at minimum CA, WA, NY, IL, TX) before pilot launch.
- Confirm the partner bank's third-party risk requirements and escalation protocol in the program agreement.
- Have the risk team document disqualifying criteria and lookback periods as a written risk matrix reviewed for disparate-impact risk.
- Verify the vendor's dispute/reinvestigation process and data-retention capabilities against FCRA § 1681i and state privacy law obligations.
