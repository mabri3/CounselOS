# Original Request

Harborline’s risk team wants stronger screening for business-account applicants that operate in higher-risk sectors, including money services, online marketplaces, digital assets, adult content, firearms, and cross-border trade. The proposed process would combine identity verification, sanctions and politically exposed person screening, adverse-media searches, criminal-record checks where available, business-registration records, and manual review. The business applicant would authorize the review during onboarding. Harborline analysts would review results and could request documents or decline the application. Our partner bank would receive escalated cases. Product wants to pilot this process in six weeks. We know that the initial applicants may come from all U.S. states and that some checks could include owners, directors, and key employees. We have not chosen the screening provider, established lookback periods, defined disqualifying results, or decided whether applicants can challenge inaccurate records.

Please advise which background checks Harborline may lawfully perform, which notices and consents are required, and whether the Fair Credit Reporting Act or state employment and consumer-reporting laws could apply even though the checks concern account eligibility rather than employment or credit. We need a clear decision on which checks can be automated, which findings require human review, how adverse action must be communicated, and what correction or appeal process is necessary. Please also identify privacy, retention, discrimination, and partner-bank oversight requirements that should shape vendor selection and the operating procedure.
