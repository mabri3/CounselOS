Confirmed. Here's the orientation and the path forward.

## What you're asking for

1. **Lawfulness of each check** — identity verification, sanctions/PEP, adverse media, criminal records, business registration, manual review — for business-account applicants in higher-risk sectors.
2. **Notices and consents** required at onboarding.
3. **Whether FCRA or state consumer-reporting/employment laws apply** even though this is account eligibility, not employment or credit.
4. **Operational decisions**: automation vs. human review, adverse-action communication, correction/appeal process.
5. **Cross-cutting requirements**: privacy, retention, discrimination, partner-bank oversight — to shape vendor selection and the operating procedure.

## Key legal frame (first pass)

- **FCRA is the threshold question.** If Harborline uses a consumer reporting agency to obtain criminal-record or adverse-media data on individuals (owners, directors, key employees), those individuals are "consumers" under FCRA regardless of the business purpose. The likely trigger is the "legitimate business need" permissible purpose (§1681b(a)(3)(F)) for account transactions — but if the check is used to *decline*, FCRA adverse-action notice (§615) likely applies. This is the single most consequential issue for the pilot.
- **Sanctions/PEP screening** is generally required by BSA/AML obligations and is not FCRA-regulated in the same way.
- **Criminal-record checks** are the highest-risk component: state laws (e.g., ban-the-box, state FCRA analogs like California ICRAA) and accuracy/dispute obligations attach.
- **Partner-bank oversight**: the sponsor bank retains BSA/AML responsibility, so the escalation protocol and vendor due diligence need to be documented in the partner agreement and procedures.

## Material gaps to resolve

- Screening provider selection (drives FCRA analysis — is it a CRA?)
- Lookback periods and disqualifying criteria (drives discrimination/fairness analysis)
- Whether applicants can challenge inaccurate records (drives dispute process design)

## Proposed next step

I'd run first-pass research on the FCRA applicability question and state-law overlay, then draft an issue map and recommendation covering the automation/human-review split, adverse-action flow, and vendor-selection criteria. Want me to start that research now?
