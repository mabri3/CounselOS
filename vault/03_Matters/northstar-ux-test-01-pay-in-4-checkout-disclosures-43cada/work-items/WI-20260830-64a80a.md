# Orient to the request

Business objective and scope

Northstar Pay wants to redesign the merchant checkout for pay-in-4 purchases to improve completion while showing customers a clear payment schedule before acceptance. The proposed flow will show the purchase amount, four scheduled payments, payment dates, applicable fees, the amount due today, and a link to the loan agreement. Identity and eligibility checks will occur in checkout. The requested legal work covers federal and state disclosure requirements, agreement delivery, electronic-consent language, and UX changes needed before a next-quarter launch.

Working assumptions

This first pass assumes the transaction is a consumer-purpose deferred-payment product and that Northstar Pay or a bank partner may be the creditor or lender depending on the live configuration. It also assumes that checkout may run on a merchant website, mobile web, or in the Northstar Pay app, and that the same core experience may need channel-specific implementation. Pay-in-4 may be interest-free, but late fees or other charges may apply. The analysis must therefore separate no-fee and fee-bearing configurations and must not treat one state or one channel as representative of all launches.

Known participants and facts

The merchant, Northstar Pay, the customer, and a payment processor participate in the transaction. The launch timing is next quarter, but no exact date, live-state list, creditor structure, agreement owner, or fee schedule is confirmed. The core open issues are whether Regulation Z applies to each configuration, what must be disclosed before approval versus before acceptance, how payment timing and fees must be presented, how the agreement must be delivered, and what electronic-consent flow is required across the three channels.

Facts that could change the advice

The highest-impact missing facts are the legal creditor in each state and product configuration; the states and channels in the initial launch; the applicable fees and fee caps; whether the product is structured as credit in every configuration; and the current E-SIGN consent and record-delivery flow. Until these facts are confirmed, the safe product direction is to design a schedule-first experience that can show state-specific terms, deliver the agreement before acceptance where required, and block launch configurations that lack the required disclosures or consent record.
