# Northstar UX Test — 01 — Pay-in-4 Checkout Disclosures advice

Working answer

Recommendation

Product should receive a conditional go for a next-quarter launch only after the checkout is changed to present the complete payment obligation before acceptance and Northstar Pay confirms the creditor, fee, state, and consent facts listed below. The proposed schedule-first approach is sound for completion and clarity, but the current request does not include total of payments, creditor identity, or fee trigger conditions. Those items must be visible in the acceptance screen and must be consistent across merchant web, mobile web, and the Northstar Pay app.

Federal disclosure timing and content

On the stated assumption that this is consumer-purpose credit, treat each pay-in-4 configuration as potentially subject to TILA and Regulation Z until the product and creditor analysis is confirmed. The fact that the plan may carry no interest does not by itself resolve the analysis if late fees or other charges apply. Before the customer becomes obligated, the experience should show the four payment amounts and specific dates, the amount due today, the total of payments or total cost, every applicable fee and the event that triggers it, and the identity of the creditor. The identity and eligibility checks should have a defined adverse-action process for declined applicants. The CFPB BNPL interpretive-rule status is an unverified lead in this run and is the largest fact that could expand the disclosure and servicing requirements.

Agreement delivery and electronic consent

A link to the loan agreement is useful but is not, by itself, electronic consent. Before relying on electronic delivery, the flow should obtain affirmative E-SIGN consent that explains what records will be delivered electronically, how consent may be withdrawn, the right to receive a paper copy, the hardware and software needed to access the record, and how the customer may request a paper copy. The flow should confirm that the customer can access the record and should retain the consent event, the delivered agreement, and the version of the disclosures shown at acceptance. The app may already have a consent record from account opening; merchant web and mobile web may not. Product must prove that any existing consent covers this agreement and this delivery method.

State overlays and launch scope

A single national presentation should not be assumed to satisfy every state. For the initial seven-state working scope (California, Colorado, Georgia, Illinois, New York, Texas, and Washington), create a state matrix for creditor, partner, licensing, fees, timing, and required text. California SB 975 and any other BNPL-specific rule are unverified leads and must be checked against the actual live configuration. Confirm each late fee and other charge against the applicable state cap and contract terms. A practical implementation recommendation is one core disclosure set built to the strictest confirmed requirement, with state-specific overlays for fee, partner, creditor, and agreement differences. Do not label the California approach as legally sufficient until the rule and effective requirements are verified.

Required UX changes

On merchant web, place the complete schedule, amount due today, total of payments, creditor identity, and fee triggers before the acceptance control. Add the explicit electronic-consent step before electronic agreement delivery. On mobile web, keep the same substantive content and do not hide fees or dates in a tooltip, collapsed section, or content that appears only after the acceptance action; test that the schedule and consent state are visible on a small screen. In the Northstar Pay app, confirm whether account-level consent covers this agreement, preserve the same substantive disclosures, and provide a durable agreement record. All channels should use the same terms service or data source so that the schedule and fee values cannot drift.

Gating facts and work plan

Before finalizing, obtain the creditor and lending-partner matrix for every launch state, the fee schedule and fee-cap analysis, the product classification and any bank-partner allocation, the current E-SIGN language and record-retention behavior, the exact launch states and channels, and verified federal and state rule status. Product should then map each required disclosure to a visible UI location and acceptance timestamp, and Legal should review a test matrix for no-fee and fee-bearing transactions, approval decline paths, and agreement delivery failures.

Recorded decision status

This document is a recommendation, not a durable decision. My proposed path is conditional go after the six gating items above and after Product adds the missing pre-acceptance terms. No final approval is recorded in this memo. A separate decision record should capture whether Northstar Pay adopts the CA-baseline disclosure set with state overlays and the conditions for launch.
