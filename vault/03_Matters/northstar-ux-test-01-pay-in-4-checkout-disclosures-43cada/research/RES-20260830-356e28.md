# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

Regulation Z applicability to pay-in-4 BNPL products, required federal disclosure timing and content, E-SIGN consent for electronic loan agreement delivery, payment schedule and fee presentation requirements, and state-law BNPL disclosure variation (CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA)

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay is redesigning its merchant checkout experience for pay-in-4 purchases, aiming to increase completion rates while showing customers a clear payment schedule before acceptance. The proposed flow shows purchase amount, four scheduled payments, dates, fees, amount due today, and a loan-agreement link, with identity and eligibility checks in-flow. Product wants to launch next quarter. Legal (Brian Harris) must confirm required federal and state disclosures, agreement delivery, e-consent, and UX changes before launch.

## Decision question

Which disclosures must appear before approval versus before acceptance in the pay-in-4 checkout flow, and can a single presentation be used across all live states (CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA) and channels (merchant web, mobile web, app), given Reg Z, E-SIGN, and state BNPL rules — so Product can launch next quarter without a disclosure or consent defect requiring redesign?

## Open questions

- Is the pay-in-4 product structured as closed-end credit subject to Reg Z (bank-partner model may or may not change the analysis), and which entity is the creditor for disclosure purposes?
- Which lending partner is used in each live state, and does the responsible entity differ by state (affecting disclosure owner and agreement terms)?
- What late fees or other charges apply by product and state, and do any state fee caps or format rules differ from the proposed presentation?
- Has the customer already provided E-SIGN consent to electronic delivery of the loan agreement, or must consent be obtained in this flow?
- Do any live states (e.g., CA, NY) impose BNPL-specific disclosure timing or format requirements beyond Reg Z?

---

## Likely rules and issues

**1. Regulation Z applicability (federal).**
- Pay-in-4 is typically closed-end credit under Reg Z if a finance charge is or may be imposed (late fees generally count as finance charges unless properly structured). Even "no interest" pay-in-4 with late fees is likely "credit" under TILA/Reg Z.
- **CFPB interpretive rule (2024, effective July 30, 2025)** treats BNPL providers as credit card providers under Reg Z: pay-in-4 is generally treated like a credit card for disclosure purposes, requiring periodic statements, dispute rights, and the ability-to-repay considerations. This is a material change from the older "closed-end loan" framing and should be verified against the current rule status and any litigation.
- If Reg Z applies: disclosures must be provided **before the consumer becomes obligated** (i.e., before acceptance), clearly and conspicuously, including amount financed, payment schedule, and fees.

**2. Disclosure timing and content.**
- **Before approval:** ECOA/Reg B notice (if adverse action occurs, a notice within 30 days); no affirmative pre-approval disclosure is federally required, but state rules may differ.
- **Before acceptance/obligation:** the full payment schedule (four payment amounts, dates), amount due today, all fees (late fees, rescheduling fees), total of payments, and identity of the creditor. The proposed UX covers most of this; the gap is likely total-of-payments and fee-trigger conditions.
- **Adverse action:** if eligibility checks decline the customer, Reg B adverse action notice obligations apply.

**3. E-SIGN / electronic consent.**
- Electronic delivery of the loan agreement requires E-SIGN consent (or UETA where applicable): reasonable notice of rights, confirmation of ability to access, and affirmative consent. A bare link to the agreement is not consent; the flow needs an explicit consent step with record retention.
- Consent language should identify what is being delivered electronically, how to withdraw, and hardware/software requirements.

**4. State variation (CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA).**
- **California:** BNPL-specific law (SB 975, effective 2024) requires specific disclosures including late fee terms, reinstatement rights, and prohibits certain practices; format requirements may exceed Reg Z.
- **New York:** general consumer protection and licensing-driven requirements; no BNPL-specific statute confirmed in this pass.
- **IL, CO, GA, TX, WA:** primarily general consumer credit/UDAP rules; fee caps and licensing vary. Illinois has notable BNPL scrutiny.
- Partner/responsible-entity differences by state may change which entity is the "creditor" for disclosure purposes.

**5. UX changes likely needed.**
- Fee disclosure prominence (late fee amount and trigger conditions) — currently only "any applicable fees" is shown.
- Total of payments / total cost display.
- Explicit E-SIGN consent step before agreement delivery.
- Cross-channel consistency: same substantive disclosures required regardless of channel; presentation may adapt but content must be equivalent.

## Viable paths

1. **Single national presentation with state-specific overlays** — build one core disclosure set meeting the strictest common denominator (likely CA), with state-specific fee and partner variations injected. Fastest to launch, most maintainable.
2. **State-by-state presentation variants** — highest compliance confidence but slower and harder to maintain across three channels.
3. **Defer launch pending CFPB BNPL rule confirmation** — most conservative; likely unnecessary if the interpretive rule status is confirmed stable.

**Recommendation:** Path 1. Build the core flow to Reg Z + CA standards, confirm the CFPB interpretive rule's current status, add an explicit E-SIGN consent step, and use state overlays for fee and partner differences. This fits the next-quarter timeline.

## Facts that could change the answer

- The CFPB interpretive rule's current status (litigation, amendment, or rescission) — this materially changes the federal disclosure set.
- Whether any live state's lending partner structure makes Northstar Pay (rather than the bank) the creditor for TILA purposes.
- Actual fee schedule by state — if any state caps late fees below the product default, presentation must vary.
- Whether the app channel already has E-SIGN consent on file from account creation, versus merchant web where the customer may be new.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the current status and requirements of the CFPB's BNPL interpretive rule (effective July 2025) and any pending litigation or amendments.
- Verify California SB 975's specific disclosure text and timing requirements against the proposed UX.
- Confirm the state-by-state partner matrix (which entity is creditor in each live state) before finalizing disclosure ownership.
- Have E-SIGN consent language reviewed against 15 U.S.C. § 7001(c) requirements and UETA state variations.
- Confirm late fee amounts and triggers by state against state fee caps.
