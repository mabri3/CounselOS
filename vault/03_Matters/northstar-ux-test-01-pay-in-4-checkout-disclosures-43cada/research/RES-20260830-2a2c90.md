# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Northstar UX Test — 01 — Pay-in-4 Checkout Disclosures?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay (fintech offering pay-in-4 and installment products with bank/lending partners) is redesigning its merchant checkout experience for pay-in-4 purchases. Product wants to launch next quarter, aiming to raise completion rates while showing the payment schedule before acceptance. Legal (Brian Harris) must confirm required federal and state disclosures, agreement delivery, e-consent language, and UX changes before launch. Pay-in-4 is live in seven states; the experience may vary across merchant web, mobile web, and the app.

## Decision question

Which disclosures must appear before approval versus before acceptance in the redesigned pay-in-4 checkout, and can one presentation be used across all live states and channels (merchant web, mobile web, app) for a next-quarter launch, or must the experience be state- and channel-specific to satisfy Reg Z, state BNPL rules, and E-SIGN consent requirements?

## Open questions

- Which entity is the creditor/lender in each live state (Northstar Pay vs. bank partner), since this determines who owes Reg Z disclosures and whose agreement is delivered?
- Do any live-state BNPL statutes or regulations impose timing, format, or fee-cap rules beyond Reg Z (e.g., California DFPI BNPL rules)?
- Is the pay-in-4 product a "credit" product subject to Reg Z in each configuration (late fees, no interest), or does any state treat it differently?
- What e-consent flow currently exists (E-SIGN consent before delivering the loan agreement electronically), and is it consistent across all three channels?
- What late fees or other charges apply by state and product, since fee disclosure prominence and state fee limits drive the presentation?

## Likely rules and issues

**Federal**
- **Truth in Lending Act / Reg Z (closed-end credit):** If pay-in-4 is credit, §1026.17 requires clear and conspicuous disclosures before consummation, including amount financed, payment schedule (amounts and dates), and fees. The CFPB's 2024 BNPL interpretive rule treats pay-in-4 as a credit card under Reg Z, which would bring billing-statement, periodic-statement, and dispute rights into scope — this materially changes the checkout disclosure set and post-transaction servicing. *(Unverified lead — confirm current status of the interpretive rule and any litigation/stay.)*
- **ECOA / Reg B:** Adverse action notice when identity/eligibility checks in the checkout flow result in denial (30-day requirement, specific reasons).
- **E-SIGN:** Reasonable demonstrable consent before electronic delivery of the loan agreement; consent language, hardware/software requirements, and right to paper copy. UETA may govern in some states for non-federal-required notices.
- **Privacy:** GLBA privacy notice delivery timing if not already satisfied; alignment of payment-processor data flows with the privacy notice.

**State**
- Live states (CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA) may impose BNPL-specific disclosure formats, fee caps, licensing-driven presentation differences, or different responsible-entity requirements. California's DFPI BNPL rules are the most developed example. *(Unverified lead — confirm each state's current requirements.)*
- Different lending partners by state may change the named creditor in disclosures and the agreement content.

**UX issues**
- Disclosure prominence and proximity to the accept action; payment schedule (amount due today, four amounts, dates) must be clear and conspicuous.
- Fee (late fee) disclosure where applicable.
- Cross-channel consistency: the same presentation may not be usable in every state or channel.

## Viable paths

1. **Single national presentation with state overlays:** Build one compliant baseline UX (Reg Z + E-SIGN + ECOA) and layer state-specific fee/disclosure variations via configuration. Fastest to launch; requires a state-by-state matrix confirming where overlays are needed.
2. **Channel-specific designs with a shared disclosure core:** Same disclosure content, adapted presentation for merchant web, mobile web, and app. More design work but better conversion per channel.
3. **Delay launch until the state/partner matrix is confirmed:** Safest but misses the next-quarter target; the company profile already flags the partner map as an open implementation fact legal must confirm.

Recommended: Path 1, with the state-by-state product/partner matrix as the gating work item.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Whether the CFPB BNPL interpretive rule (pay-in-4 as credit card) is in effect, stayed, or rescinded at launch — this changes the entire disclosure architecture.
- Which entity is creditor in each state (bank partner vs. Northstar Pay) — changes who owes disclosures and the agreement content.
- Actual fee structure by state (late fees present or not) — changes whether fee disclosures and state fee-cap analysis are needed.
- Whether the app flow differs materially from web flows in e-consent mechanics.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm current status of the CFPB BNPL interpretive rule and any applicable state BNPL regimes in the seven live states (primary sources: CFPB, DFPI, state regulators).
- Obtain the state-by-state product/partner matrix from operations and lending partners before approving any presentation.
- Verify the existing E-SIGN consent flow and language against the three channels with the product team.
- Confirm Reg Z closed-end disclosure content requirements (§1026.17, §1026.18) against the proposed screen content, and Reg B adverse action notice process for in-flow denials.

*Note: This is a first-pass issue map based on matter facts and general regulatory knowledge; the specific regulatory citations above (CFPB BNPL rule status, state BNPL rules) are unverified leads requiring primary-source confirmation before launch decisions.*
