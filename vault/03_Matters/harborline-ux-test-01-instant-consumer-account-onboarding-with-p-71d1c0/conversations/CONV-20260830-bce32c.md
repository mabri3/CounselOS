Confirmed. I've oriented to the request and set up the matter workspace. Here's the first pass.

**Bottom line:** The provisional-access model is workable in principle — fintech programs commonly offer limited pre-verification access — but permissibility turns almost entirely on (1) whether the partner bank's CIP is satisfied before the account is "opened" for BSA purposes, (2) whether the provisional period is treated as a pending application rather than an open account, and (3) whether the restricted functions (view-only + inbound ACH credit only) hold until final approval. The inbound ACH credit is the riskiest feature and the one to pressure-test with the partner bank.

**Key issues I see:**

1. **CIP timing / "account opening."** Under 31 C.F.R. § 1020.220, the bank must form a reasonable belief it knows the customer's identity before account opening, though verification can occur within a reasonable time after opening in limited circumstances. The core question for the partner bank: does the provisional period count as account opening (triggering CIP completion, CDD, and sanctions screening before any access), or is it a pre-opening application status? This drives everything else.
2. **Sanctions.** OFAC screening must effectively precede any customer interaction with funds. Receiving an inbound ACH credit into a provisional account before sanctions clearance is the biggest exposure — the funds would be held for a customer whose screening hasn't cleared. Failed checks prevent opening (per your known facts), so the model needs a clear answer for what happens to a credit received before a failure.
3. **Funds availability / Reg CC.** If the account is deemed open when the credit arrives, the availability clock may start running before verification completes. The provisional period length and deposit cap must be set so the bank can complete verification well inside any availability window.
4. **Consumer disclosures / status language.** Applicants must not be led to believe they have a fully active account. Interface language, deposit insurance statements (FDIC insurance attaches only when the account is actually opened at the partner bank), and Reg E error-resolution obligations during the provisional period all need review.
5. **Partner-bank and vendor dependencies.** The bank must approve the CIP procedures (known fact), and the identity vendor's role must fit within the bank's program — contract terms on data use, screening scope, escalation of inconclusive results, and audit rights should be mandatory.

**Three facts that would most change the analysis:**

1. What maximum provisional period is Product proposing (hours vs. days)? This determines whether "reasonable time" verification and Reg CC timing work.
2. What deposit cap, if any, is contemplated for the provisional period?
3. Has the partner bank seen this model yet, and does it have a view on whether the account legally exists pre-approval?

I've logged the open items in the matter. Next useful step is a first-pass research memo on the CIP-timing and provisional-access question — want me to run that, or would you rather resolve the three facts above first?
