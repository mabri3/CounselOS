# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Harborline UX Test — 01 — Instant Consumer Account Onboarding with Provisional Access?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Harborline Financial, a U.S. fintech, plans a mobile consumer onboarding flow giving applicants provisional app access while its partner bank completes CIP and sanctions screening. During the provisional period, applicants could view account details and receive an incoming ACH credit, but not send money, use a virtual card, or withdraw. Harborline runs the interface and support; the partner bank holds the account; a third-party vendor performs identity verification. The launch is tied to an October growth campaign, and the provisional period length, deposit cap, inconclusive-result handling, and account legal status are unsettled.

## Decision question

Which functions may Harborline make available during the provisional pre-verification period — specifically, whether receiving an incoming ACH credit is permissible before CIP and sanctions screening complete — and what maximum provisional period, deposit cap, inconclusive-identity handling, and interface status language must be settled with the partner bank and verification vendor before the October launch?

## Likely rules and issues

**1. Customer Identification Program (CIP) — 31 U.S.C. § 5318(h); 31 C.F.R. § 1020.220**
- The partner bank's CIP must be completed before an account is "opened" for a customer. The key regulatory question is whether granting provisional access with limited functionality constitutes "opening an account" under the CIP rule before verification is complete. Banks generally may not establish account relationships until CIP is satisfied, though the rule allows collection of information before account opening.
- The partner bank must approve Harborline's CIP procedures as its service provider; the bank retains responsibility for CIP compliance even when delegated (see FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual on third-party fintech relationships).

**2. BSA/AML — account existence and risk**
- Whether the account "legally exists" before final approval matters for SAR obligations, recordkeeping, and customer due diligence. If the partner bank opens a restricted/pending account, it is a "customer" relationship for BSA purposes from opening.
- Receiving an incoming ACH credit before verification creates funds-flow risk: if verification fails, the credit must be returned (ACH return rules, NACHA) and the customer relationship may trigger CIP-related obligations.

**3. Sanctions — OFAC**
- Failed sanctions checks prevent opening (known fact). The material issue is the gap between provisional access and screening completion: if an incoming ACH credit is received from or to a sanctioned party before screening completes, OFAC exposure arises. Screening should complete before any funds are accepted, or credits should be held unposted pending clearance.

**4. Funds availability — Regulation CC / Reg E**
- If the account is technically open, Regulation CC availability rules and Reg E error-resolution obligations may attach to the incoming ACH credit. A provisional period that delays availability or restricts access to received funds needs careful structuring to avoid triggering Reg E/Reg CC timelines for a "consumer account."

**5. Consumer disclosure — UDAP/UDAAP and deposit insurance**
- Status language must not imply a fully active account (company operating principle; also FTC Act § 5 / Dodd-Frank UDAAP risk). Deposit insurance disclosures must be accurate — if the account is not yet open, FDIC insurance statements must reflect that.

## Viable paths

- **Path A (most conservative):** No funds accepted until CIP and sanctions screening complete. Provisional access limited to viewing app features and account details only. Cleanest compliance position; some abandonment-reduction benefit retained.
- **Path B (middle):** Incoming ACH credits accepted but held in a pending/unposted state at the partner bank until verification completes; no customer access to funds. Requires partner bank operational capability and clear NACHA return handling if verification fails.
- **Path C (as proposed):** Credits posted to a restricted account visible to the customer. Highest risk — likely triggers CIP "account opening" analysis, Reg E/Reg CC questions, and OFAC gap risk. Only viable with explicit partner bank sign-off and tight provisional-period limits.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Whether the partner bank treats the provisional period as pre-account-opening or a restricted open account (changes CIP, Reg E/Reg CC, and BSA analysis).
- Maximum provisional period and whether it can be capped contractually and operationally.
- Whether the verification vendor can complete screening in real time or near-real time (shortens the gap and reduces Path B/C risk).
- Deposit cap amount and whether incoming credits are held unposted or posted.
- Whether the partner bank's CIP approval covers the delegated model and the vendor's verification methods.

## Open questions

- Does the partner bank consider the account legally open during the provisional period, and will it accept credits during that window?
- What is the maximum provisional period and deposit cap Product is proposing?
- How will inconclusive identity results be handled — manual review queue, denial, or extended provisional status?
- What real-time screening SLA can the verification vendor commit to, and does its methodology satisfy the partner bank's CIP standard?
- What NACHA return path applies if an ACH credit is received and verification subsequently fails?

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the partner bank's position on account status during the provisional period in writing before any UX decisions are finalized.
- Verify current 31 C.F.R. § 1020.220 text and FFIEC guidance on fintech partnerships and CIP timing.
- Check NACHA Operating Rules on returns for credits received into a pending/unposted account.
- Review Reg E (12 C.F.R. Part 1005) and Reg CC applicability to restricted or pending accounts with the partner bank's compliance team.
- Confirm OFAC screening timing relative to funds acceptance with the partner bank's sanctions team.
