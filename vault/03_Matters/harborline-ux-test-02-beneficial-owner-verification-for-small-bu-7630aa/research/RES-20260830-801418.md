# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

Under FinCEN's CDD rule (31 CFR 101.0.230) and related beneficial-ownership requirements, can a partner bank permit a small-business deposit account to receive funds and issue payment credentials after the control person passes identity verification while one or more beneficial owners' (≥25% equity) identity checks remain pending? Cover: who must certify ownership information, whether the control-person and beneficial-owner prongs can be completed on different timelines, what evidence must be retained (5-year recordkeeping), how the Corporate Transparency Act / BOI regime and any 2024–2026 FinCEN changes affect bank collection or verification, how ownership details must be refreshed (risk-based triggers), and what a fintech/BaaS program should do when an owner ignores an invitation, disputes submitted information, or fails screening after the account has received funds. Also flag entity types (nonprofits, trusts, foreign owners, layered ownership) that require a different flow or exclusion.

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Harborline plans a Q1 launch of business checking accounts for single-member LLCs and small corporations (U.S.-formed, ≤5 owners) through its partner bank. Product proposes staged activation: the applicant funds the account and receives payment credentials once the control person passes identity verification, even if one beneficial owner's (≥25%) check is still pending. The partner bank has not confirmed whether Harborline may apply exception handling without manual bank approval. Legal must decide whether staged activation satisfies CDD/beneficial-ownership requirements and define certification, evidence, refresh, and remediation rules.

## Decision question

Can Harborline's partner bank lawfully allow a small-business deposit account to receive funds and issue payment credentials after the control person passes verification while one or more ≥25% beneficial owners' identity checks remain pending — and if so, under what conditions (certification, evidence, timelines, exception approval, entity-type exclusions) — or must the account stay restricted until all beneficial-owner checks complete?

## Open questions

- Has the partner bank confirmed in writing that Harborline may apply exception handling without manual bank approval, and what is the bank's CDD program standard for pending-owner accounts?
- Will nonprofits, trusts, foreign owners, or layered ownership structures be eligible in the first release, or excluded?
- What is the actual funding/credential timing product needs (days of delay being avoided), and can funds be held in a restricted/incoming-only state pending completion?
- Which identity vendor is used, and what is its verification method and evidence output for the 5-year recordkeeping file?
- What is the partner bank's risk-based refresh cycle and trigger list for ownership changes?

## Likely rules and issues

**Core framework — FinCEN CDD Rule, 31 CFR 101.230** (note: the correct cite is 31 CFR 101.230; "101.0.230" in the research question appears to be a typo). The rule has four pillars; the relevant one is the beneficial-ownership requirement for "legal entity customers" at account opening:

- **Two prongs, one certification.** The bank must identify and verify (a) each **beneficial owner** (each individual owning ≥25% of equity, subject to the bank's risk-based discretion to set a lower threshold) and (b) one **control person** (individual with significant responsibility). The standard certification form (Appendix A) is completed by "the individual opening the account on behalf of the legal entity customer" — i.e., the applicant/authorized representative, **not** each owner. So the person starting the application certifies ownership information; individual owners do not certify, they are the subjects of verification.
- **Verification standard.** The bank must verify the identity of each beneficial owner and the control person using documentary or non-documentary methods (same standard as CIP for individuals). The rule does not require the bank to verify the *accuracy* of the ownership percentages themselves — that is the certifying individual's responsibility — but it must verify each named individual's identity.
- **Timing.** The CDD rule requires beneficial-ownership identification and verification "in connection with the opening of a new account." FinCEN's FAQs treat this as part of account opening, but the rule does not contain an express provision allowing an account to open with a pending beneficial-owner verification. In practice, banks handle this through their risk-based CDD program: some permit account opening with restricted functionality pending completion, others require completion first. **This is a partner-bank program decision, not something Harborline can unilaterally set.**

**Staged activation analysis.** The proposed flow is defensible only if:
1. The **control person** (who is also an authorized signer/applicant) has been fully identified and verified — this satisfies the control-person prong.
2. The **certification** of ownership information is completed and signed by the applicant before funding.
3. The account is treated as **conditionally open with restrictions** — e.g., incoming deposits only, no outbound payments, no credential issuance — until all beneficial-owner verifications complete, OR the bank's written CDD program expressly permits full activation with a pending owner under defined risk limits and timeframes.
4. A **hard deadline and escalation path** exists (e.g., 30 days) with automatic restriction/closure if not resolved.

The risk of issuing payment credentials (outbound capability) while a ≥25% owner is unverified is that the bank cannot complete its CDD file for a customer already transacting — an examination finding and a potential gap if the pending owner turns out to be a sanctions hit or otherwise prohibited. The safer staged design: **fund/incoming-only permitted after control-person verification; outbound payments and credentials gated on full beneficial-owner completion or documented bank-approved exception.**

**CTA / BOI regime (2024–2026 changes).** Important: the Corporate Transparency Act's BOI reporting regime does **not** replace or relax the CDD rule's beneficial-ownership collection at account opening. Banks may not rely on the CTA database (FinCEN's BOI registry was never made accessible to banks for CDD purposes, and following *National Small Business United v. Yellen* and subsequent FinCEN actions in 2024–2025, domestic-entity BOI reporting requirements were suspended/limited; FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule removed BOI requirements for U.S. domestic companies and U.S. persons). The practical effect: **the bank must still collect and verify beneficial ownership directly from the customer under the CDD rule** — the CTA does not provide a shortcut. Separately, FinCEN's 2024 proposed AML/CFT program rule and ongoing CDD-rule revision work signal possible future changes (e.g., a risk-based approach to the 25% threshold), but nothing final as of the research date changes current obligations. This should be flagged for monitoring but does not alter the Q1 launch analysis.

**Recordkeeping — 31 CFR 101.230(b)(4) / 101.0.230 recordkeeping generally.** The bank must maintain records for **five years** after the account is closed: the identifying information obtained for each beneficial owner and the control person, and a description of the documentary/non-documentary methods and results used to verify each. Harborline, as the fintech operating the flow, should retain: the signed certification, identity-verification results from the vendor (with method and outcome), timestamps, invitation/reminder logs for each owner, and any exception approvals.

**Risk-based refresh.** The CDD rule requires ongoing monitoring and risk-based updating of customer information, including beneficial ownership, as part of ongoing CDD — triggered by events (ownership change reported, suspicious activity, transaction anomalies) and periodic review cycles set by the bank's program (commonly 1–3 years by risk tier). There is no fixed statutory refresh interval; the partner bank's program governs.

**Entity types requiring different flow or exclusion:**
- **Nonprofits** — excluded from the CDD beneficial-ownership requirement (not "legal entity customers" for this purpose), but still require a control-person identification and CIP; a different, simpler flow is appropriate.
- **Trusts** — not legal entity customers under the CDD rule; beneficial ownership is assessed under the bank's trust CDD procedures. Different flow; consider excluding from release 1.
- **Foreign owners / foreign-formed entities** — the stated scope is U.S.-formed entities, but foreign *persons* as owners raise sanctions, tax-form (W-8/W-9), and enhanced due diligence issues. Recommend excluding non-U.S.-resident owners from release 1 or routing to manual review.
- **Layered ownership** — the 25% ownership test requires looking through intermediary entities; the proposed flow (direct owner entry) cannot capture this reliably. Exclude or route to manual review.
- **Sole proprietors** — not legal entity customers; no beneficial-ownership certification needed, only individual CIP.

## Viable paths

**Path A — Restricted-activation staging (recommended).** Control person verified + certification signed → account may receive incoming funds (deposits held, no outbound). Full activation (payment credentials, outbound ACH/cards) only when all beneficial-owner verifications complete. Hard 30-day clock with automatic escalation to restriction/closure. Lowest regulatory risk; preserves most of the speed benefit since deposits can land immediately.

**Path B — Full staged activation with bank-approved exception.** Same as A but outbound/credentials permitted with a pending owner, only under the partner bank's written exception procedure with defined risk caps (e.g., transaction limits, single pending owner, entity otherwise low-risk) and manual or documented automated bank approval. Requires the bank's explicit written confirmation — currently missing. Moderate risk; examinable but defensible if documented.

**Path C — No staging.** Full verification of all owners before any account functionality. Safest, slowest; loses the product benefit entirely.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Partner bank's written position on exception handling without manual approval (currently unconfirmed — this is the gating fact for Path B).
- Whether product can accept an incoming-only restricted state (Path A) as sufficient speed.
- Whether any owner is a foreign person, trust beneficiary, or layered structure — flips to exclusion/manual review.
- Any final FinCEN CDD-rule revision before launch (monitor; nothing final currently changes the analysis).
- Whether the identity vendor's output satisfies the bank's documentary/non-documentary verification record requirements.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the correct citation is **31 CFR 101.230** (not 101.0.230) and pull the current rule text and FinCEN CDD FAQs (especially FAQ re: account opening timing and the certification form, Appendix A).
- Verify the current status of the CTA/BOI regime and FinCEN's 2025 interim final rule on domestic entities, plus any pending CDD-rule revisions, as of the advice date — this area moved repeatedly in 2024–2025.
- Obtain the partner bank's written CDD program excerpt on pending-verification accounts and exception approval authority.
- Confirm the 5-year recordkeeping retention location and format for certification and verification evidence (bank vs. Harborline obligations under the BaaS/service arrangement).

**Recommendation (separate from decision):** Proceed with Path A (restricted activation: incoming funds permitted after control-person verification and signed certification; outbound payments and credentials gated on full beneficial-owner completion, with a 30-day hard clock). Exclude trusts, layered ownership, and non-U.S.-resident owners from release 1; route nonprofits and sole proprietors to a simplified flow. Escalate the exception-handling question to the partner bank in writing now, since it gates any move to Path B.
