# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Harborline UX Test — 02 — Beneficial-Owner Verification for Small-Business Accounts?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary
Harborline plans a Q1 launch of business checking accounts for single-member LLCs and small corporations (U.S.-formed, ≤5 owners) through its partner bank. Product proposes staged activation: the applicant funds the account and receives payment credentials once the control person passes identity verification, even if one ≥25% beneficial owner's check is still pending. The partner bank has not confirmed whether Harborline may apply exception handling without manual bank approval. Legal must decide whether staged activation satisfies CDD/beneficial-ownership requirements and define certification, evidence, refresh, and remediation rules.

## Decision question
Can Harborline's partner bank lawfully allow a small-business deposit account to receive funds and issue payment credentials after the control person passes verification while one or more ≥25% beneficial owners' identity checks remain pending — and if so, under what conditions (certification, evidence, timelines, exception approval, entity-type exclusions) — or must the account stay restricted until all beneficial-owner checks complete?

## Open questions
- Has the partner bank confirmed in writing that Harborline may apply exception handling without manual bank approval, and what is the bank's CDD program standard for pending-owner accounts?
- Will nonprofits, trusts, foreign owners, or layered ownership structures be eligible in the first release, or excluded?
- What is the actual funding/credential timing product needs (days of delay being avoided), and can funds be held in a restricted/incoming-only state pending completion?
- Which identity vendor is used, and does its verification output satisfy the bank's documentary/non-documentary record requirements for the 5-year retention file?
- What is the partner bank's risk-based refresh cycle and trigger list for ownership changes?

## Likely rules and issues
- **FinCEN CDD Rule, 31 CFR 101.230** (the "101.0.230" in the research question is a typo). Two prongs — each ≥25% beneficial owner plus one control person — identified and verified "in connection with the opening of a new account." The rule has no express provision allowing an account to transact with a pending owner verification; whether to permit it is a **partner-bank CDD program decision**, not something Harborline can set unilaterally.
- **Certification.** The Appendix A-style certification is completed by the individual opening the account on behalf of the entity (the applicant/authorized representative). Owners do not certify; they are the subjects of verification. The bank verifies each named individual's identity but not the ownership percentages themselves.
- **CTA/BOI does not help.** FinCEN's 2025 interim final rule removed BOI reporting for U.S. domestic entities, and banks never received registry access for CDD. The bank must still collect and verify ownership directly. Monitor FinCEN's pending CDD-rule revisions, but nothing final changes the Q1 analysis.
- **Recordkeeping.** Five years post-closure: identifying information per owner/control person plus verification method and results. Harborline should retain the signed certification, vendor verification results, timestamps, invitation/reminder logs, and exception approvals.
- **Refresh.** No fixed statutory interval — risk-based per the bank's program (commonly 1–3 years by risk tier) plus trigger events (ownership change, suspicious activity, transaction anomalies).
- **Entity types.** Nonprofits and sole proprietors are not "legal entity customers" (simplified flow, control-person/CIP only). Trusts, layered ownership, and non-U.S.-resident owners need different flows or exclusion from release 1.
- **Remediation.** Ignored invitations → reminders then a hard clock → restrict outbound → close/return funds per bank procedures. Disputes → freeze changes, re-certification, manual review. Post-funding screening failure → restrict outbound immediately, escalate to the bank, consider SAR implications, keep screening reasons confidential.

## Viable paths
- **Path A — Restricted activation (recommended).** Control person verified + signed certification → account opens incoming-only (deposits land immediately). Outbound payments and credentials gated on completion of all beneficial-owner verifications. Hard 30-day clock with automatic escalation to restriction/closure.
- **Path B — Full staged activation under bank-approved exception.** Outbound/credentials permitted with a pending owner only under the partner bank's written exception procedure with risk caps (single pending owner, transaction limits, low-risk entity). Currently unconfirmed — this is the gating fact.
- **Path C — No staging.** Full verification before any functionality. Safest, slowest; loses the product benefit.

## Facts that could change the answer
- Partner bank's written position on exception handling without manual approval (gates Path B).
- Whether product accepts incoming-only staging as sufficient speed.
- Presence of foreign persons, trust beneficiaries, or layered structures — flips to exclusion/manual review.
- Any final FinCEN CDD-rule revision before launch.
- Whether the identity vendor's output satisfies the bank's verification record requirements.

## Recommended last-mile verification
- Confirm the citation is **31 CFR 101.230** and pull current rule text plus FinCEN CDD FAQs (account-opening timing, Appendix A certification form).
- Verify current CTA/BOI status and FinCEN's 2025 interim final rule, plus any pending CDD-rule revisions, as of the advice date — this area moved repeatedly in 2024–2025.
- Obtain the partner bank's written CDD program excerpt on pending-verification accounts and exception approval authority.
- Confirm retention location and format for certification and verification evidence under the BaaS arrangement (bank vs. Harborline obligations).

**Recommendation (separate from the lawyer's decision):** Proceed with Path A — restricted activation with a 30-day hard clock; exclude trusts, layered ownership, and non-U.S.-resident owners from release 1; route nonprofits and sole proprietors to a simplified flow; and escalate the exception-handling question to the partner bank in writing now, since it gates any move to Path B.
