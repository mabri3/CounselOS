# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Northstar UX Test — 07 — Refunds, Returns, and Transaction Disputes?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay (Product requester; legal owner Brian Harris) wants to standardize its flow for merchant refunds, customer returns, and payment disputes before expanding to additional retail categories. The flow will apply merchant refunds to outstanding balances, recalculate future payments, and notify customers in-app; customers may also report unauthorized transactions, defective goods, or non-delivery through support. Merchants issue full or partial refunds at varying times, sometimes without item-level detail, and customers may still owe payments while disputes are pending. Counsel is involved to define customer rights, merchant obligations, notices, and operational timelines.

## Decision question

What refund-allocation, dispute-investigation, notice, and timeline rules should govern Northstar Pay's standardized refund/dispute flow — including whether customers must keep paying during a pending dispute and how quickly repayment schedules must be adjusted — before Product launches the flow ahead of retail-category expansion?

## Open questions

- Which entity (Northstar Pay vs. bank/lending partner) is the creditor/servicer of record for error-resolution and dispute duties, and does that vary by product and state?
- Are the installment loans credit-card-like products subject to Regulation Z error-resolution (§1026.13) or only state-law dispute/servicing rules, and which states' rules apply?
- What do the card-network and bank-partner agreements require for chargeback/dispute handling and refund pass-through?
- How should refunds be allocated (future installments first vs. pro rata) when merchants send partial or non-itemized refunds?
- What merchant contract terms exist today on refund notification timing and data format?

## Likely rules and issues

**Generated analysis** (no external sources verified; based on the request and company profile):

1. **Regulation Z error resolution (15 U.S.C. §1666; 12 C.F.R. §1026.13)** — If any product is treated as open-end or credit-card-like, customers get billing-error rights: acknowledgment within 5 days, investigation within 2 cycles/90 days, and no obligation to pay the disputed amount (or related finance charges) during investigation. Most BNPL installment loans are closed-end and fall outside §1026.13, but the CFPB has signaled BNPL dispute rights should mirror card protections — a design choice, not just a compliance floor.
2. **CFPB BNPL interpretive rule (2024)** — Treats BNPL providers as card issuers for purposes of Regulation Z dispute rights and refund credits, requiring provisional credit/refund of disputed amounts and chargeback-style processes. Confirm current status and scope.
3. **State servicing and dispute rules** — State-by-state installment-loan servicing requirements (live states: CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA) on refund application, schedule adjustment timing, and customer notices.
4. **Merchant contract obligations** — Refund notification SLAs, data format (item-level vs. order-level), liability for duplicate refunds, and allocation instructions.
5. **Card network rules** — If the underlying payment is a card, network chargeback timelines and refund processing rules constrain the flow.
6. **Merchant-conduct disputes (defective goods, non-delivery)** — Not payment-authorization errors; these implicate network dispute/chargeback rights and possibly state UDAP claims about how Northstar Pay describes its dispute process.
7. **Notices and disclosures** — In-app refund confirmation, adjusted-schedule notice, dispute acknowledgment and resolution notices, and E-SIGN/electronic consent for those notices.

## Viable paths

- **Path A (recommended):** Design to the CFPB's card-equivalent dispute standard across all products — provisional credit for unauthorized-transaction disputes, 5-day acknowledgment, defined investigation window, clear "you don't owe the disputed amount during review" rule — plus state-law overlay for the live states. Simplest single customer experience; highest compliance margin.
- **Path B:** Tiered treatment — full Reg Z-style rights for unauthorized transactions; merchant-conduct disputes (defect/non-delivery) handled as network chargebacks with a defined pass-through process and continued payment obligations disclosed up front. Lower operational cost; requires careful disclosure to avoid UDAP exposure.
- **Refund allocation:** Default rule of applying refunds to remaining installments in reverse order (last payments first), with customer notice of the recalculated schedule; document the rule in the customer agreement rather than case-by-case.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Whether any product is open-end/revolving (triggering §1026.13 directly).
- The bank/lending partner map by product and state — who holds the error-resolution duty.
- Whether the CFPB interpretive rule's status has changed (litigation/revision since 2024).
- Whether merchants can be contractually required to send item-level refund data.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm current status and scope of the CFPB BNPL interpretive rule and any litigation.
- Check Reg Z §1026.13 applicability to each product structure with the lending partners.
- Verify state servicing/dispute requirements for CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA.
- Review card-network and bank-partner agreement dispute/refund provisions.
- Confirm merchant contract refund-data terms before finalizing the allocation design.
