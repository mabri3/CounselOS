# Orient to the request

Northstar Pay wants a standard operating flow for merchant refunds, customer returns, and payment disputes before it expands into additional retail categories. The business goal is to reduce duplicate refunds, apply merchant credits once to the customer’s outstanding balance, recalculate future installment payments when a purchase is canceled or reduced, and notify the customer clearly in the app.

The proposed process has six main actors: the customer, the merchant, Northstar Pay, card networks, banks or lending partners, and dispute-resolution vendors. Merchants may issue full or partial refunds at different times. Some merchants may not reliably send item-level information. Customers may report unauthorized transactions, defective goods, or merchant non-delivery through Northstar Pay support. A customer may continue to owe scheduled payments while a dispute is under review.

The initial issue map covers refund intake and duplicate suppression; allocation of full and partial refunds to the outstanding balance; recalculation, pause, or continuation of future payments; customer and merchant notices; unauthorized-transaction error resolution; merchant-conduct disputes involving defective goods or non-delivery; card-network and bank-partner dispute handling; and merchant data and contract obligations.

Assumptions are marked as assumptions, not confirmed facts. Assumption: the first release may cover pay-in-4 and other installment products, but the legal treatment may differ by product and state. Assumption: Northstar Pay may use a bank or lending partner as creditor or servicer of record. Assumption: the flow will need an idempotent refund identifier or equivalent control to prevent double application. Assumption: customer-facing notices will be delivered in-app, with another channel if required by the governing product terms or law.

Missing facts are material. Northstar Pay must confirm which entity is creditor and servicer of record for each product, which products and states are in scope, whether any funding instrument is subject to Regulation E or Regulation Z error-resolution rules, what the card-network and bank-partner agreements require, what merchant contracts say about refund timing and data fields, how non-itemized partial refunds are allocated, whether payments are paused or still due during each dispute path, and what notice channels and retention rules apply.

The work items are to confirm the responsible legal entity and product scope; map applicable federal and state dispute rules; capture card-network and bank-partner requirements; define a refund allocation and duplicate-suppression rule; define merchant data, timing, and escalation duties; design customer acknowledgment, interim, error-resolution, and final notices; and document an operational timeline with owners and fallback handling for missing item-level data.

The expected legal work product is a first-pass research record, an issues-and-recommendations memo in full paragraphs, a separate working recommendation for Product, and a reviewed fictional decision record. No source has been supplied by Product in the request. Any research lead without direct verification must be labeled an unverified lead.
