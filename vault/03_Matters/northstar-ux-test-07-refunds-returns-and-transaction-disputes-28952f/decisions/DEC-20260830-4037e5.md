# Refund and Dispute Flow Standard — Card-Equivalent Dispute Rights and Last-Payment-First Refund Allocation

## Chosen path

Adopt a uniform card-equivalent dispute standard for the MVP: for unauthorized-transaction disputes, the disputed amount is not owed during investigation, with acknowledgment within five business days and resolution within two complete billing cycles or ninety days; merchant-conduct disputes route through card-network chargebacks with continued payment obligations only where clearly disclosed up front and a documented case-specific forbearance path. Adopt last-payment-first refund allocation as a customer-agreement term, with an order-level fallback when item-level refund data is missing. Amend merchant contracts to require refund SLAs, item-level data where available, unique refund identifiers, liability for retransmitted refunds, and investigation cooperation.

## Rationale

Reviewed and edited by Alex Morgan. A uniform card-equivalent standard gives the simplest single customer experience and the highest compliance margin for the MVP, subject to product- and state-specific changes if verified law or partner requirements require them. Last-payment-first allocation with an order-level fallback handles unreliable merchant item-level data. Merchant contract amendments are needed to support refund SLAs, data quality, duplicate suppression, and investigation cooperation. Continued payments for merchant-conduct disputes are acceptable only with clear up-front disclosure and a documented support path for case-specific forbearance.

## Conditions

- Verify current status and scope of the CFPB BNPL interpretive rule before launch
- Complete the state-law overlay for all live states (CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA)
- Confirm card-network and bank-partner agreement dispute and refund requirements
- Confirm the creditor/servicer-of-record map with lending partners
- Implement the idempotent refund identifier and duplicate-suppression control
