      Northstar Pay offers merchant checkout financing, an app-based pay-in-4 product,
      and longer-term installment products, in partnership with FDIC-insured bank
      or lending partners, payment processors, and a merchant network. Pay-in-4 is
      currently live in California, Colorado, Georgia, Illinois, New York, Texas,

      through Northstar Pay support. The relevant actors are customers, merchants,

      vendors.

      margin, and the strongest defense against state UDAP claims about how the dispute
      process is described.

# Issues and Recommendations Memo — Refunds, Returns, and Transaction Disputes

Matter: Northstar UX Test — 07 — Refunds, Returns, and Transaction Disputes

Prepared for: Alex Morgan, Luna High lawyer, Northstar Pay

Status: Draft — privileged and confidential attorney work product

Provenance note: This memo is generated analysis built on the visible matter record and first-pass research. No external source was verified in this run. Unverified leads, assumptions, missing facts, and generated analysis are labeled throughout.

Question Presented

What refund-allocation, dispute-investigation, notice, and timeline rules should govern Northstar Pay's standardized flow for merchant refunds, customer returns, and payment disputes — including whether customers must keep paying while a dispute is pending and how quickly repayment schedules must adjust after a refund — before Product launches the flow ahead of expansion into additional retail categories?

Facts

Northstar Pay offers merchant checkout financing, an app-based pay-in-4 product, and longer-term installment products, in partnership with FDIC-insured bank or lending partners, payment processors, and a merchant network. Pay-in-4 is currently live in California, Colorado, Georgia, Illinois, New York, Texas, and Washington; longer-term installment products are limited to a smaller approved state list. The responsible entity, servicing vendor, payment processor, and contract terms may differ by product and state, and the partner map is still being finalized.

Product proposes a flow that receives refund information from the merchant, applies the amount to the customer's outstanding balance, recalculates future payments when needed, and notifies the customer in the app. Customers may also report unauthorized transactions, defective goods, or merchant non-delivery through Northstar Pay support. The relevant actors are customers, merchants, Northstar Pay, card networks, banks or lending partners, and dispute-resolution vendors.

Supplied facts from the request: merchants may issue full or partial refunds at different times; some merchants do not reliably send item-level information; and a customer may continue to owe payments while a dispute is under review. No source documents were supplied by Product.

Issues

Refund intake and duplicate suppression — preventing a merchant refund from being applied twice.

Refund allocation — how full and partial refunds apply to the outstanding balance and remaining installments.

Schedule adjustment — whether and when future payments recalculate, pause, or continue.

Customer notices — refund confirmation, schedule-change notice, dispute acknowledgment, and resolution notices.

Unauthorized-transaction disputes — the investigation process, timelines, and payment obligations during review.

Merchant-conduct disputes — defective goods and non-delivery, which are not authorization errors.

Card-network and bank-partner requirements — chargeback handling and refund pass-through obligations.

Merchant contract obligations — refund notification timing, data format, and liability allocation.

State-law overlay — servicing, dispute, and notice requirements in the live states.

Analysis

Dispute standard. If any Northstar Pay product were open-end or credit-card-like, Regulation Z's billing-error provisions (15 U.S.C. §1666; 12 C.F.R. §1026.13) would directly apply: acknowledgment within five days, investigation within two complete cycles or 90 days, and no obligation to pay the disputed amount during investigation. Most BNPL installment loans are closed-end and fall outside §1026.13, but the CFPB has signaled that BNPL dispute rights should mirror card protections. The CFPB's 2024 interpretive rule treating BNPL providers as card issuers for dispute and refund purposes is an unverified lead — its current status and scope must be confirmed before launch. The generated recommendation is to design to the card-equivalent standard across all products regardless of whether it is strictly required, because it produces a single customer experience, the highest compliance margin, and the strongest defense against state UDAP claims about how the dispute process is described.

Payment during dispute. For unauthorized-transaction disputes, the card-equivalent standard means the customer does not owe the disputed amount while the investigation is pending. For merchant-conduct disputes (defective goods, non-delivery), the analysis differs: these are not authorization errors, and the natural path is card-network chargeback routing where the underlying payment was a card, with continued payment obligations disclosed clearly up front. The risk of a tiered approach is customer confusion and UDAP exposure if the distinction is not disclosed prominently at the point of dispute and in the customer agreement.

Refund allocation. The generated recommendation is a default rule applying refunds to remaining installments in reverse order — the last scheduled payments are reduced or eliminated first — with prompt in-app notice of the recalculated schedule. Applying refunds last-payment-first reduces the customer's total remaining obligation most visibly and is simple to explain. The rule should be documented in the customer agreement rather than applied case by case, so that allocation is a contractual term rather than an operational improvisation. Where merchants send partial refunds without item-level detail, the default rule should apply to the order as a whole, and merchant contracts should be amended to require item-level data where available.

Duplicate suppression. The flow needs an idempotent refund identifier or equivalent control so that a merchant refund transmitted more than once, or through more than one channel, is applied only once. This is an assumption about the technical design, not a confirmed fact, and it should be confirmed with engineering before launch.

Merchant obligations. Merchant contracts should be amended to specify refund notification SLAs, the data format for refund messages (including item-level detail where available), liability for duplicate refunds caused by merchant retransmission, and allocation instructions for partial refunds. Current merchant contract terms on refund timing and data format are a missing fact.

Card networks and bank partners. Where the underlying payment is a card, network chargeback timelines and refund processing rules constrain the flow. Bank or lending partner agreements may allocate the error-resolution duty to the partner or to Northstar Pay as servicer. Both are unverified leads requiring review of the actual agreements.

State law. Each live state may impose servicing requirements on refund application, schedule-adjustment timing, and notices. These are unverified leads requiring state-by-state verification for California, Colorado, Georgia, Illinois, New York, Texas, and Washington, plus the longer-term installment state list.

Customer Rights

Customers should receive, at minimum: (1) prompt in-app confirmation when a merchant refund is applied, showing the amount, the adjusted balance, and the recalculated schedule; (2) acknowledgment of any dispute within five business days; (3) for unauthorized-transaction disputes, no obligation to pay the disputed amount during investigation and resolution within two complete cycles or 90 days; (4) for merchant-conduct disputes, clear disclosure at the time of filing that payments may continue while the dispute is reviewed, and routing through the applicable card-network chargeback process; and (5) written or in-app notice of the investigation outcome and any resulting schedule change. These rights are generated analysis reflecting the recommended card-equivalent standard; the precise legal floor depends on the unverified leads above.

Merchant Obligations

Merchants should be contractually required to: (1) transmit refund notifications within a defined SLA of issuing the refund; (2) include item-level detail where available; (3) use a unique refund identifier to enable duplicate suppression; (4) accept liability for refunds retransmitted in error; and (5) cooperate with dispute investigations within network timelines. Current contract terms are a missing fact; the recommendation assumes amendments are feasible before launch.

Notices

The notice set should include: refund-applied confirmation (amount, new balance, new schedule); schedule-change notice when a refund alters future payments; dispute acknowledgment within five business days; interim notice if an investigation extends beyond the initial window; and resolution notice stating the outcome, any credit or resumed obligation, and the recalculated schedule. In-app delivery is the recommended primary channel, with an alternative channel where the governing product terms or applicable state law requires one. E-SIGN consent for electronic delivery should be confirmed for the customer base. These are generated recommendations; required notice content and timing under state law are unverified leads.

Operational Timelines

Refund received from merchant — intake and duplicate check in real time using the idempotent refund identifier.

Refund applied to outstanding balance and schedule recalculated — same business day.

In-app refund confirmation and adjusted-schedule notice — within one business day of application.

Dispute filed by customer — acknowledgment within five business days.

Unauthorized-transaction investigation — completed within two complete billing cycles or 90 days, whichever is shorter; disputed amount not owed during review.

Merchant-conduct dispute — routed to the applicable card-network chargeback process within network timelines; payments continue unless Northstar Pay elects forbearance.

Resolution notice — within five business days of the investigation outcome.

Schedule adjustment following resolution — applied within one business day of the outcome.

Fallback for missing item-level data — default allocation rule applied at the order level, with the gap logged and escalated to the merchant relationship team.

These timelines are generated analysis calibrated to the card-equivalent standard; they are not yet confirmed against state law, network rules, or partner agreements.

Risks

The principal risks are: (1) designing to a standard that is later found insufficient if the CFPB interpretive rule or state law imposes stricter requirements; (2) UDAP exposure if payment obligations during merchant-conduct disputes are not disclosed clearly; (3) duplicate refunds if the idempotency control is not implemented as assumed; (4) misallocation of non-itemized partial refunds, causing customer confusion and complaints; (5) conflict with card-network or bank-partner agreement requirements that have not been reviewed; and (6) state-by-state servicing violations if the live states impose different refund-application or notice rules than the uniform design assumes.

Assumptions

The first release may cover pay-in-4 and other installment products, but legal treatment may differ by product and state.

Northstar Pay may use a bank or lending partner as creditor or servicer of record; the partner map is not finalized.

The flow will use an idempotent refund identifier or equivalent control to prevent double application.

Customer-facing notices will be delivered in-app, with another channel where required by the governing product terms or law.

Recommendation

Adopt the card-equivalent dispute standard across all Northstar Pay products: provisional suspension of the disputed amount for unauthorized-transaction disputes, five-business-day acknowledgment, investigation within two complete cycles or 90 days, and clear up-front disclosure that payments may continue during merchant-conduct disputes, which route through card-network chargebacks. Adopt a last-payment-first refund allocation rule documented in the customer agreement, with same-day application, next-business-day in-app notice of the recalculated schedule, and an idempotent refund identifier to prevent duplicates. Amend merchant contracts to require refund SLAs, item-level data where available, unique refund identifiers, and merchant liability for retransmitted refunds. Before launch, verify the CFPB interpretive rule status, the state-law overlay for all live states, and the card-network and bank-partner agreement requirements, and confirm the creditor/servicer-of-record map with the lending partners.
