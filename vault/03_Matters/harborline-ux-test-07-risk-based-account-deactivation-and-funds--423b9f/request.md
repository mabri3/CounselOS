# Original Request

Harborline needs a standard process for deactivating consumer and business accounts after fraud, AML, sanctions, partner-bank, or prohibited-use concerns arise. Operations proposes three states: restricted, pending closure, and closed. A restricted customer could view statements but could not send payments. During pending closure, incoming deposits would normally be rejected, pending card and ACH items would be resolved, and remaining funds would be returned by check or transfer after review. The partner bank could direct immediate closure in some cases. Harborline support and compliance teams would use internal reason codes and customer-facing templates. We want the process ready within eight weeks. We know that some investigations must remain confidential. We have not decided maximum hold periods, how to handle negative balances or disputed funds, whether customers may choose the return method, or when business administrators should lose access.

Legal must approve the state model, notice timing, customer-facing reasons, and process for returning funds. Please advise how the rules differ when deactivation is based on ordinary risk policy, suspected fraud, a sanctions concern, a law-enforcement request, or an unresolved customer identification problem. We need a decision on when Harborline may block access without advance notice, which records and statements must remain available, what appeal or escalation path should exist, and how to avoid disclosures that could amount to prohibited tipping off. Please also identify any state unclaimed-property, consumer-protection, funds-availability, or contract requirements that affect the closure timeline.
