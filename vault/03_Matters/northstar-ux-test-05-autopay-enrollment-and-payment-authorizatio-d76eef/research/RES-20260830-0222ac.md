# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What are the legal requirements for consumer autopay enrollment and recurring payment authorization for installment loans in the U.S., including Regulation E and NACHA rules for preauthorized ACH transfers, recurring debit-card authorization and card-network rules, required authorization language and consent standards, advance notice when payment amounts change (refunds, disputes, adjustments), permissible retry and backup-payment-method practices after failed payments, cancellation rights and procedures, pre-withdrawal reminders and notice timing, and any limits on offering a discount or incentive for autopay enrollment?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay (fintech installment lender, working with bank/lending partners, payment processors, and servicing vendors) wants to make autopay enrollment easier for installment-loan customers at checkout or in-app, with pre-withdrawal reminders, payment-method changes, and possibly an enrollment incentive. Payment amounts can change after refunds, disputes, or adjustments, and Product is considering retries and backup payment methods after failed payments. Launch is targeted in six weeks. Legal (Brian Harris) must confirm the authorization, notice, retry, cancellation, and incentive rules before launch.

## Decision question

Can Northstar Pay launch the simplified autopay enrollment flow in six weeks using ACH and/or debit-card recurring authorization, and if so, what authorization language, advance-notice timing for amount changes, retry/backup-method limits, cancellation procedure, and enrollment-incentive terms must be built into the UX to stay within Regulation E, NACHA, card-network, and state-law limits?

## Open questions

- Which states/products are in scope (pay-in-4 vs. longer-term installments), and which lending partners and payment processors apply per state — the partner map is still unconfirmed.
- Is autopay ACH, recurring debit, or both? The rules differ materially.
- Retry policy specifics: number, timing, fees, and whether the backup method is separately authorized at enrollment.
- Nature of the enrollment incentive (fee discount vs. promotional credit) and its terms.
- How payment-amount changes are communicated before withdrawal, and whether cancellation is fully self-service.

## Likely rules and issues

**1. Preauthorized ACH transfers (Regulation E + NACHA).** Regulation E (12 C.F.R. § 1005.10) requires a written authorization for preauthorized transfers from a consumer account, which must be "readily identifiable" as an authorization and provide a copy to the consumer; the consumer must be able to stop payment (§ 1005.10(c)) and get error-resolution protections (§ 1005.11). NACHA rules require the authorization to state the amount or the method of computing it, the timing/schedule, the right to revoke, and the originator's identity; it must be retained and available for the RDFI for two years. For varying amounts, NACHA and Reg E practice require advance notice of the changed amount — commonly at least 10 days before the transfer unless the consumer agrees to shorter notice (e.g., 2 business days) in the authorization. This directly hits the refund/dispute/adjustment scenario: the flow must detect amount changes and trigger notice before withdrawal.

**2. Recurring debit-card payments.** Card-network rules (Visa/Mastercard stored-credential and merchant-initiated transaction frameworks) require the initial authorization to state the recurring nature, the amount or method of computing it, and the cancellation right; subsequent charges must be flagged as recurring/merchant-initiated with proper network transaction indicators. Cancellation must be effective on request (network rules generally require honoring stop requests). Reg E's debit-card recurring protections are thinner than ACH, but the CFPB and UDAP/UDAAP standards still require clear disclosure of the recurring nature and easy cancellation.

**3. Consent and enrollment UX.** Consent must be clear, affirmative, and unbundled — no pre-checked boxes, no dark patterns (CFPB circulars on dark patterns and "negative option" practices; FTC Negative Option Rule / ROSCA principles for recurring charges). Authorization language should state: who is debiting, the account, the amount or computation method, the schedule, the right to cancel and how, and the notice the customer will get for amount changes. E-SIGN/UETA governs whether electronic consent and disclosures are valid — need demonstrable consent to electronic delivery.

**4. Failed payments, retries, and backup methods.** NACHA permits re-initiation of returned entries only under limited conditions (e.g., the entry was returned for insufficient or uncollected funds), and the re-initiated entry must be identified as a retry; repeated retries beyond the amount owed are improper. Reg E and state law limit returned-payment/late fees — several states cap or prohibit them for consumer installment loans, and fee amounts must be disclosed. A backup payment method requires its own specific authorization — you cannot sweep a second card or account based on the primary enrollment consent. Retry counts, timing, and any fees should be capped and disclosed at enrollment.

**5. Enrollment incentive.** A modest autopay discount is common and generally permissible, but: (a) if the installment product is credit, the discount interacts with disclosed APR/finance-charge calculations and state rate/fee caps — the discounted price must still be within limits and Truth-in-Lending disclosures must reflect actual charges; (b) UDAAP risk if the incentive nudges enrollment without clear disclosure of the recurring-debit commitment and cancellation rights; (c) some state installment-loan laws restrict rebates/incentives — check per-state.

**6. Reminders and notices.** Pre-withdrawal reminders are good practice and, for varying amounts, legally required advance notice. Messaging channel matters: SMS reminders implicate TCPA (prior express consent for automated texts) and the customer's communication preferences; email/push are safer defaults.

**7. Cancellation.** Reg E gives a stop-payment right for preauthorized ACH; network rules require honoring card cancellation. Best practice (and CFPB expectation): in-app self-service cancellation effective before the next scheduled withdrawal, with confirmation. A support-call-only flow is a UDAAP risk.

**8. Servicing/vendor oversight and state availability.** Per company policy, no state is automatically available — the autopay experience must be validated per state/product/partner, and servicing-vendor and processor responsibilities confirmed before launch.

## Viable paths

- **Path A (recommended): Launch in six weeks with a compliant-by-design flow.** Build: (1) affirmative, unbundled autopay consent with authorization language covering amount/computation, schedule, cancellation, and amount-change notice; (2) automatic advance notice (default 10 days, or shorter with express consent) whenever the payment amount changes; (3) capped, disclosed retry policy (e.g., limited retries only for NSF-type returns, no fees where state-capped); (4) separate opt-in for any backup payment method; (5) self-service cancellation effective before the next withdrawal; (6) a simple, clearly disclosed enrollment incentive validated against state rate/fee limits. Requires confirming the state/partner matrix and ACH-vs-debit mix within ~2 weeks.
- **Path B: Phased launch.** Ship the enrollment flow, reminders, and cancellation in six weeks; defer retries/backup-method logic and the incentive to a fast-follow after state-by-state validation. Lower risk if the partner map isn't confirmed in time.
- **Path C: Delay.** Only if the state/partner matrix or retry design can't be validated — six weeks is tight but workable for Path A if facts arrive quickly.

## Facts that could change the answer

- ACH vs. debit-card mix (different rule sets for authorization, retries, and cancellation).
- Whether the "installment loans" are longer-term credit (TILA/state rate caps apply to the incentive) or pay-in-4 (lighter regime, but UDAAP still applies).
- State list — some states restrict returned-payment fees, retry practices, or incentives.
- Whether the backup method is pre-authorized at enrollment (needs separate consent) or requested per-incident.
- Whether reminders include SMS (TCPA consent needed).

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the state-by-state product/partner matrix and the ACH/debit mix with Product and the lending partners (open implementation fact per company policy).
- Verify current NACHA Operating Rules provisions on re-initiation of returned entries and advance-notice timing for varying amounts, and the applicable card-network stored-credential/MIT rules for the specific processors used.
- Check state-by-state limits on returned-payment/late fees, retry practices, and any incentive restrictions for the in-scope states.
- Have the final authorization language, amount-change notice, retry disclosure, and incentive terms reviewed by Brian Harris before launch — this first pass is not court-ready and the specific rule citations should be confirmed against current versions of Reg E, NACHA rules, and network rules.

**Recommendation (separate from the decision):** Proceed on Path A — build the compliant-by-design flow now, with the state/partner matrix and ACH-vs-debit confirmation as gating work items in the next two weeks; fall back to Path B if those facts slip. The decision to launch remains with Brian Harris.
