# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Northstar UX Test — 05 — Autopay Enrollment and Payment Authorization?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary
Northstar Pay (fintech installment lender working with bank/lending partners, payment processors, and servicing vendors) wants to make autopay enrollment easier for installment-loan customers at checkout or in-app, with pre-withdrawal reminders, payment-method changes, and possibly an enrollment incentive. Payment amounts can change after refunds, disputes, or adjustments, and Product is considering retries and backup payment methods after failed payments. Launch is targeted in six weeks. Legal (Brian Harris) must confirm the authorization, notice, retry, cancellation, and incentive rules before launch.

## Decision question
Can Northstar Pay launch the simplified autopay enrollment flow in six weeks using ACH and/or debit-card recurring authorization, and what authorization language, advance-notice timing for amount changes, retry/backup-method limits, cancellation procedure, and enrollment-incentive terms must be built into the UX to stay within Regulation E, NACHA, card-network, and state-law limits?

## Open questions
- Which states/products are in scope (pay-in-4 vs. longer-term installments), and which lending partners and payment processors apply per state — the partner map is still unconfirmed.
- Is autopay ACH, recurring debit, or both? The rules differ materially.
- Retry policy specifics: number, timing, fees, and whether the backup method is separately authorized at enrollment.
- Nature of the enrollment incentive (fee discount vs. promotional credit) and its terms.
- How payment-amount changes are communicated before withdrawal, and whether cancellation is fully self-service.

## Likely rules and issues
1. **Preauthorized ACH (Reg E § 1005.10 + NACHA).** Written, readily identifiable authorization with a copy to the consumer; stop-payment and error-resolution rights; NACHA content requirements (amount or computation method, schedule, revocation, originator identity, two-year retention). Varying amounts require advance notice — commonly 10 days unless the consumer agrees to shorter notice in the authorization. This directly hits the refund/dispute/adjustment scenario.
2. **Recurring debit-card payments.** Card-network stored-credential/merchant-initiated-transaction rules require the initial authorization to state the recurring nature, amount or method, and cancellation right; subsequent charges must carry proper network indicators. Cancellation must be honored on request.
3. **Consent and enrollment UX.** Clear, affirmative, unbundled consent — no pre-checked boxes or dark patterns (CFPB circulars; FTC negative-option/ROSCA principles). Authorization language should state who debits, the account, amount or computation method, schedule, cancellation mechanics, and amount-change notice. E-SIGN/UETA for electronic consent and disclosures.
4. **Failed payments, retries, backup methods.** NACHA permits re-initiation only under limited conditions (e.g., NSF returns) and retries must be identified as such; state law may cap or prohibit returned-payment/late fees. A backup payment method requires its own specific authorization — the primary enrollment consent does not cover sweeping a second account or card.
5. **Enrollment incentive.** If the product is credit, the discount interacts with APR/finance-charge calculations, TILA disclosures, and state rate/fee caps; UDAAP risk if the incentive nudges enrollment without clear disclosure of the recurring commitment and cancellation rights; some state installment-loan laws restrict incentives.
6. **Reminders and notices.** Pre-withdrawal reminders are good practice and, for varying amounts, legally required advance notice. SMS reminders implicate TCPA (prior express consent for automated texts); email/push are safer defaults.
7. **Cancellation.** Self-service, in-app cancellation effective before the next scheduled withdrawal with confirmation; a support-call-only flow is a UDAAP risk.
8. **State availability and vendor oversight.** Per company policy, no state is automatically available — the autopay experience must be validated per state/product/partner before launch.

## Viable paths
- **Path A (recommended): Launch in six weeks with a compliant-by-design flow.** Build: (1) affirmative, unbundled consent with full authorization language; (2) automatic advance notice (default 10 days, or shorter with express consent) whenever the amount changes; (3) capped, disclosed retry policy (NSF-type returns only, no fees where state-capped); (4) separate opt-in for any backup method; (5) self-service cancellation effective before the next withdrawal; (6) a clearly disclosed incentive validated against state rate/fee limits. Requires confirming the state/partner matrix and ACH-vs-debit mix within ~2 weeks.
- **Path B: Phased launch.** Ship enrollment, reminders, and cancellation in six weeks; defer retries/backup-method logic and the incentive to a fast-follow after state-by-state validation. Lower risk if the partner map isn't confirmed in time.
- **Path C: Delay.** Only if the state/partner matrix or retry design can't be validated.

## Facts that could change the answer
- ACH vs. debit-card mix (different rule sets for authorization, retries, and cancellation).
- Whether the installment loans are longer-term credit (TILA/state rate caps apply to the incentive) or pay-in-4 (lighter regime, but UDAAP still applies).
- State list — some states restrict returned-payment fees, retry practices, or incentives.
- Whether the backup method is pre-authorized at enrollment (needs separate consent) or requested per-incident.
- Whether reminders include SMS (TCPA consent needed).

## Recommended last-mile verification
- Confirm the state-by-state product/partner matrix and the ACH/debit mix with Product and the lending partners (open implementation fact per company policy).
- Verify current NACHA Operating Rules on re-initiation of returned entries and advance-notice timing for varying amounts, and the applicable card-network stored-credential/MIT rules for the specific processors used.
- Check state-by-state limits on returned-payment/late fees, retry practices, and incentive restrictions for the in-scope states.
- Have the final authorization language, amount-change notice, retry disclosure, and incentive terms reviewed by Brian Harris before launch — this first pass is not court-ready and rule citations should be confirmed against current versions of Reg E, NACHA rules, and network rules.

**Recommendation (separate from the decision):** Proceed on Path A — build the compliant-by-design flow now, with the state/partner matrix and ACH-vs-debit confirmation as gating work items in the next two weeks; fall back to Path B if those facts slip. The launch decision remains with Brian Harris.
