# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Harborline UX Test — 05 — Real-Time Payments with Risk-Based Holds?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Harborline (fintech operating through a partner bank) plans to launch instant outbound and inbound payments for eligible consumer and business customers before the holiday payment peak. Product proposes a risk engine that releases low-risk payments immediately, delays medium-risk transfers up to 24 hours for review, rejects high-risk transfers, and makes suspicious inbound payments visible but unavailable. Legal (Brian Harris) must determine whether the holds, rejects, and balance displays are consistent with network rules, account agreements, funds-transfer law, funds-availability duties, and unfair-practices risk, and which operating decisions need partner-bank approval.

## Decision question

May Harborline delay, reject, or restrict availability of instant payments based on its risk engine — including showing credited-but-unavailable inbound funds — without violating network rules, the account agreement, funds-transfer law, funds-availability duties, or unfair-practices standards, and what notice, cancellation rights, and partner-bank approvals must be in place before the holiday-peak launch?

## Open questions

- What are the transfer limits, review service levels, and proposed notice language (none fixed yet)?
- Where is the line between a fraud hold and an AML investigation, and who owns each decision?
- Which instant payment rail/network is being used (RTP vs. FedNow vs. both), since network rules differ?
- What does the current account agreement and partner-bank oversight framework already say about holds, delays, and availability?
- How will the "visible but unavailable" balance display work in practice for consumer vs. business accounts?

## Likely rules and issues

**Outbound holds and rejections**
- **Network rules (RTP/FedNow, card networks if applicable):** Instant payment rails generally treat payments as final and irrevocable once accepted. Network rules may impose obligations on the sending institution to process or reject within defined windows; a 24-hour delay may conflict with network expectations for instant payments. Need to confirm the specific network's rules on delayed release and rejection.
- **UCC Article 4A (business/wire-type transfers):** For covered funds transfers, the bank's right to reject or delay depends on the funds-transfer agreement; a receiving bank may reject a payment order before acceptance, but once accepted, obligations attach. The account agreement must clearly authorize holds and delays.
- **Regulation E (consumer EFTs):** If the instant payments are EFTs for consumers, Reg E governs error resolution, disclosure of holds, and consumer protections. Reg E does not generally prohibit holds, but disclosures and the consumer's right to stop payment/cancel must be handled correctly. Note: Reg E's coverage of account-to-account instant payments depends on the transfer characterization.
- **Funds availability:** For consumer deposit accounts, Regulation CC generally does not cover wire/instant transfers, but the account agreement and state law (and unfairness analysis) still govern when funds may be held. The "credited but unavailable" inbound display raises Reg E error-resolution and disclosure questions for consumers.

**Balance display — "visible but unavailable"**
- Showing funds in the visible balance while restricting availability is a significant unfair-practices (FTC Act §5 / CFPA §1036 / UDAP-UDAAP) risk if it misleads consumers about spendable funds. The display should clearly segregate held funds (e.g., "pending review") rather than showing them as available.
- For AML-related holds, notice must not tip off the customer about a suspicious activity investigation (31 U.S.C. §5318(g) confidentiality).

**Fraud hold vs. AML investigation**
- Fraud holds (customer-protective) can generally be disclosed more openly ("we're reviewing this payment for your protection").
- AML/suspicious-activity holds require careful, non-tipping-off language and coordination with the partner bank's BSA/AML program, since the bank retains compliance oversight.

**Mistaken and unauthorized payments**
- Instant payments are generally final; recovery of mistaken payments depends on network rules (e.g., RTP's request-for-return process) and recipient cooperation. Unauthorized-payment claims for consumers run through Reg E error resolution (liability limits, investigation timelines) if the transfer is an EFT.
- Business customers are generally outside Reg E; UCC Article 4A and contract terms control.

**Sanctions alerts**
- Blocking/rejecting for sanctions (OFAC) is mandatory and non-negotiable; notice must not disclose the sanctions basis in a way that tips off or facilitates evasion. Partner bank coordination is essential since the bank is the regulated institution.

**Recipients who are not Harborline customers**
- Harborline has limited ability to compel return from non-customer recipients; recovery depends on the receiving institution's cooperation and network rules. No privity; no direct contractual claim.

**Consumer vs. business differences**
- Consumers: Reg E error resolution, disclosure duties, UDAP exposure, potentially different funds-availability expectations.
- Businesses: UCC 4A, contract freedom, but still unfair-practices and network-rule exposure.

**Partner-bank approval**
- Because the partner bank holds the accounts and retains compliance oversight, operating decisions that touch account-level actions — holds, rejections, availability restrictions, sanctions blocking, AML investigation triggers, and the risk-engine criteria themselves — likely require partner-bank sign-off under the oversight agreement. This is a governance issue, not just a legal one.

## Viable paths

1. **Contract-first path (recommended):** Amend/confirm the account agreement and partner-bank oversight framework to expressly authorize risk-based delays, rejections, and availability restrictions, with clear consumer-facing disclosure language, before launch. This is the cleanest path to defensibility.
2. **Narrow-launch path:** Launch with conservative limits (low dollar thresholds for immediate release, shorter review windows) and a clear "pending review" balance display, expanding after partner-bank and network sign-off.
3. **Fraud-hold-first path:** Structure all delays as customer-protective fraud reviews (not AML holds) with transparent notice, routing AML-flagged items to a separate, non-disclosed process. This reduces UDAP exposure but requires a clean operational separation.

## Facts that could change the answer

- The specific payment network(s) and their rules on delayed release and rejection.
- Whether the account agreement already authorizes risk-based holds and delays.
- Whether the transfers are Reg E-covered EFTs for consumers (account-to-account characterization).
- The actual notice language and balance-display design.
- The partner-bank oversight agreement's allocation of authority over account restrictions.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the specific network's rules (RTP and/or FedNow) on delayed release, rejection windows, and return processes — verify against the current network rulebook.
- Confirm Reg E applicability to these specific transfer types with the CFPB's current positions on account-to-account instant payments.
- Review the actual account agreement text and partner-bank oversight agreement for existing hold/availability authority.
- Have the partner bank's compliance team confirm which risk-engine decisions require their approval before launch.

*This is a first-pass research orientation based on the matter record and general legal frameworks; the specific network rules, agreement text, and partner-bank requirements should be verified before finalizing advice.*
