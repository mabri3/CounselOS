Confirmed. Here's my orientation and first-pass issue map for the real-time payments with risk-based holds proposal.

## Bottom line

The proposal is workable, but the three highest-risk design points are (1) **crediting inbound payments to the visible balance while making funds unavailable** (unfairness/deception risk under UDAAP and funds-availability duties), (2) **delaying outbound instructions up to 24 hours** (network rules, Reg E / UCC 4A timing, and account-agreement consistency), and (3) **notice language that doesn't tip off customers under AML monitoring** while still satisfying consumer-protection disclosure duties. The fraud-hold vs. AML-investigation line is the structural decision that drives all three.

## Issue map

**1. Outbound holds and rejects**
- Network rules (RTP/FedNow, card networks): finality of settlement, whether the network permits delayed execution, and any service-level commitments Harborline has made.
- Consumer: Reg E error-resolution and timing duties if an instruction is delayed or rejected; whether a "hold" is a revocation of an EFT authorization or a new disclosure trigger.
- Business: UCC 4A — whether Harborline is the bank, whether a funds-transfer system rule on execution timing applies, and the "reasonable time" execution standard.
- Account agreement: does it currently authorize delayed execution and rejection? If not, amendment may be needed before launch.

**2. Inbound suspicious payments — credited but unavailable**
- Visible balance vs. available balance display is the single biggest UDAAP exposure. A balance shown but not spendable, without clear disclosure, is a classic deception/unfairness theory.
- Funds-availability duties (Reg CC does not apply to instant rails, but partner-bank policy and network rules may set expectations).
- If the payment is later returned or frozen, who bears the loss and what notice goes to the recipient.

**3. Cancellation during review**
- Whether customers can cancel during a 24-hour hold depends on whether the instruction has been executed. Pre-execution, cancellation should generally be permitted (and is a strong unfairness mitigant); post-execution, finality controls.

**4. Notice without tipping off**
- Fraud holds: customers can generally be told a hold is pending review.
- AML/sanctions: notice must not reveal the existence of a suspicious activity report or investigation. Standard practice: generic "security review" language, no detail, no right to appeal the underlying reason.
- This is where the fraud-hold/AML-investigation line must be drawn operationally, not just legally.

**5. Third parties and edge cases**
- Mistaken payments (misdirected funds): recovery depends on rail and whether the recipient is a Harborline customer.
- Unauthorized payments: Reg E consumer liability framework; business customers fall outside Reg E.
- Recipients who are not Harborline customers: limited ability to claw back; network rules govern.
- Sanctions alerts: blocking vs. rejecting, reporting duties, and coordination with the partner bank.

**6. Consumer vs. business differences**
- Reg E (consumers only) vs. UCC 4A (business/commercial); different notice and liability frameworks; different unfairness exposure.

**7. Partner-bank approval items**
- Likely requiring bank sign-off: hold/reject matrix, balance-display design, notice templates, sanctions/AML escalation procedures, and any change to the account agreement or funds-availability disclosures.

## Material questions before drafting the recommendation

1. **Which rails?** RTP, FedNow, or both — network rules differ materially on execution timing and finality.
2. **Is the account agreement being amended for launch?** This determines whether delayed execution is contractually authorized.
3. **Who is the bank of record for the transfer?** Whether the partner bank or Harborline (as BaaS operator) is the "bank" under UCC 4A changes the analysis for business customers.
