# # Orient to the request

## Business objective and timing

Northstar Pay wants to improve the customer experience for financing applications before the holiday shopping season. The intended flow will show an approval, a conditional approval, or a decline in real time. For a declined application, Product wants to give a useful general reason without disclosing fraud controls or creating confusion. A written notice may follow by email or postal mail.

As Alex Morgan, I frame the legal task as launch readiness for a consumer-credit decision flow. The work must connect the decision shown in the product to the legally responsible creditor, the correct adverse-action or counteroffer notice, the reason content, the delivery channel, and the operating owner. The answer must work for decisions made by Northstar Pay and for decisions made by a lending partner.

## Known facts

Decisions may use credit-report information, application data, bank-account information, fraud signals, and automated models. Some declines are made by Northstar Pay. Other declines are made by a lending partner. The actors are Northstar Pay, lending partners, customers, credit-reporting vendors, and customer support teams.

## Initial issue map

1. Identify the creditor, applicant-facing decision-maker, and notice issuer for each product and decision path.

2. Classify approval, conditional approval, counteroffer, decline, incomplete application, and abandonment after a decision.

3. Determine when the Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B adverse-action rules apply, including the content and timing of specific reasons.

4. Determine when Fair Credit Reporting Act obligations apply and how consumer-report users and vendors support the notice.

5. Define an explainable reason-code process that produces truthful principal reasons while keeping fraud controls confidential.

6. Define delivery, consent, record retention, escalation, and state-law overlays for the launch states.

## Facts needed before final launch language

Missing facts include product structure, creditor identity per flow, who pulls a consumer report, who makes each credit, fraud, and identity decision, the role of each lending partner, the launch-state list, notice channels and consent status, real-time message timing, written-notice timing, model inputs, reason codes, and whether conditional approval is a counteroffer or a final approval on modified terms.

## Assumptions and limits

Assumptions: the product is consumer credit covered by ECOA and Regulation B; FCRA duties may apply when a consumer report is used; the creditor may differ by flow; and electronic notices must satisfy applicable ESIGN requirements. These are not verified against contract, product, or state files. Any state overlay and any model-specific conclusion is an unverified lead until the launch list, product structure, and authoritative source materials are confirmed.

## First-pass work plan

Next, complete the fact record and issue map, review first-pass federal and state research, test a decision-maker and reason-code matrix, and then draft the notice and customer-communication package. Keep the recommendation separate from a later recorded decision. Do not treat a missing fact or unverified lead as a reason to stop useful work; mark it and design the launch controls around it.
