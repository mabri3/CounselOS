# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Northstar UX Test — 04 — Adverse Action Notices for Declined Applications?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay is redesigning its real-time application decision flow to show approvals, conditional approvals, and declines, with general decline reasons in-app and a written notice by email or mail. Decisions may be made by Northstar Pay or by lending partners, using credit data, application data, bank-account information, fraud signals, and automated models. Product wants to launch before the holiday shopping season. Counsel is involved to determine who must issue adverse action notices, when, with what reasons, and how to handle conditional approvals and abandoned applications.

## Decision question

Who must issue the adverse action notice for each decline path (Northstar Pay vs. lending partner), what reasons and timing are required under ECOA/Reg B and FCRA, and what exact customer communications must ship with the new flow before the holiday launch?

## Likely rules and issues

- **ECOA / Regulation B (15 U.S.C. § 1691; 12 C.F.R. Part 1002)** — Adverse action notice required within 30 days of a completed application; must state the specific principal reasons for the action or the right to obtain them. Key issue: whether Northstar Pay or the lending partner is the creditor for each decline path, and whether the in-app message plus written notice together satisfy the timing and content rules.
- **FCRA § 615(a) (15 U.S.C. § 1681m)** — If the decline is based in whole or in part on consumer-report information, the notice must include the CRA's name and contact information, the right to a free copy, and the right to dispute. If a credit score was used, the risk-based pricing / credit-score disclosure rules (§ 615(h)) may apply.
- **Specific vs. general reasons** — Reg B requires accurate, specific principal reasons. "Inability to verify identity" and fraud-based declines raise the tension between specificity and not exposing fraud controls; Reg B permits withholding reasons that would reveal fraud-prevention methods, but the notice must still be accurate.
- **Automated models / AI decisioning** — CFPB scrutiny of "black box" reasons; model-reason documentation must map the actual decision drivers to the stated reasons (CFPB Circular 2022-03 and adverse-action guidance on algorithmic credit decisions).
- **Conditional approvals** — Counteroffers and incomplete applications have distinct Reg B notice paths (§ 1002.9(a)(2), (b)); "conditional approval" UX language must be mapped to the correct regulatory category.
- **Abandoned applications** — Whether an abandoned application counts as "incomplete" (notice of incompleteness under § 1002.9(c)) or as a completed application triggering adverse action duties.
- **State law** — State credit statutes, retail installment sales acts, and state UDAP/notice requirements in live states (CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA) may impose additional or faster notice requirements.
- **Vendor responsibilities** — Credit-reporting vendor and servicing contracts must allocate notice issuance, data accuracy, and recordkeeping; who "furnishes" the decision determines who notices.

## Viable paths

1. **Dual-path notice matrix (recommended)** — Build a state-by-state, partner-by-partner matrix identifying the responsible entity for each decline type (Northstar Pay decline vs. partner decline), the applicable notice regime, timing, and required content; then design the in-app message as a supplement to, not substitute for, the compliant written notice.
2. **Partner-contract allocation** — Negotiate with lending partners so the party making the decision issues the notice, with Northstar Pay handling delivery as agent under documented vendor controls.
3. **Reason-code mapping project** — Map each automated-model decline reason to approved, accurate customer-facing reason language, with documentation supporting that stated reasons reflect actual decision drivers.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Whether Northstar Pay or the lending partner is the creditor/decision-maker for each product and state (partner map still being finalized per company profile).
- Whether declines are based on consumer-report information, credit scores, or solely on internal/fraud data.
- Whether "conditional approval" is legally a counteroffer, an incomplete application, or a completed application with conditions.
- Whether abandoned applications occur before or after a decision is rendered.
- Which states are in scope for the holiday launch.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the partner-by-product-by-state matrix before finalizing who issues notices.
- Verify current Reg B model notice forms (C-1 through C-5) and FCRA § 615 disclosures against the final UX copy.
- Confirm state-specific notice timing/content requirements for each launch state.
- Review the automated model's reason-code documentation with the data science team to confirm stated reasons match actual decision drivers.

## Open questions

- Which entity (Northstar Pay or which lending partner) is the decision-maker for each product/state combination in the holiday launch?
- Are declines based on credit scores (triggering § 615(h) score disclosures) or other report data?
- Does "conditional approval" in the UX correspond to a counteroffer, an incomplete application, or a conditioned approval?
- What happens operationally when a customer abandons the application after a decision is rendered but before viewing it?
- Which states are included in the holiday launch scope?
