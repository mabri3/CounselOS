# # Research Memo — Adverse Action Notices for Declined Applications

## Scope and evidence status

This is an edited first-pass memo for Alex Morgan, Northstar Pay. It addresses the real-time approval, conditional approval, and decline flow, including decisions made by Northstar Pay and by lending partners. The source record available in the matter is the product request, the issue map, and the first-pass internal research packet. No lender agreement, product terms, model card, reason-code dictionary, state matrix, or delivery-consent evidence was provided.

The legal points below are launch-planning guidance, not a court-ready opinion. Statutory and regulatory references are unverified leads until counsel checks the current authoritative text and the actual product structure. The central conclusion is nevertheless operational: build the experience so the legally responsible creditor and any separate consumer-report user can each be identified, and do not let a missing fact produce a missing notice.

## Regulation B / ECOA

For a completed application, the creditor generally must provide notice of the action taken within the applicable Regulation B timing window. An adverse-action notice must state the specific principal reasons for the action, or provide the permitted statement-of-reasons process. A generic statement such as “does not meet our requirements” is not a safe substitute for accurate principal reasons.

A decline is adverse action. An application that is incomplete requires its own classification and notice handling. A conditional approval must be classified before launch. If the changed terms are a counteroffer, non-acceptance or silence can trigger counteroffer notice duties, including notice of the right to receive the original terms where applicable. If the product is instead designed as a final approval on modified terms, the notice analysis changes. This is a product-and-legal decision, not a copywriting detail.

The issuer is tied to the creditor and the action taken. In a bank-partner model, the partner may owe the Regulation B notice for a partner-made credit decision. Northstar Pay may still have a separate duty if it independently takes adverse action based on a consumer report or its own decision process. Treat the creditor, decision-maker, report user, and notice issuer as separate fields until facts prove they are the same.

## FCRA overlay

When a person takes adverse action based in whole or in part on a consumer report, the FCRA adverse-action notice generally requires the report's principal reasons, the reporting agency's contact information, and the customer's rights to obtain a free report within the permitted period and to dispute inaccurate information. The notice must be sent within a reasonable time after the action. The exact content and timing must be checked against the current authoritative text and the actual role of each party.

The main operational risk is a dual-notice gap. A lending partner may issue the creditor's Regulation B notice, while Northstar Pay may be the entity that obtained and relied on a report for a fraud, identity, or other decision. Contracts can allocate operations, but they do not by themselves erase a statutory duty. The launch matrix must state who issues each notice, who supplies reason and bureau fields, who retains evidence, and who handles disputes.

## Reasons and model documentation

The internal decision engine must preserve the principal reasons that actually drove the outcome. Legal should approve a versioned mapping from model or rules output to customer-facing reasons. The mapping should record the input or output that controlled the decision, the reason-code priority when multiple factors apply, the effective date, the approving owner, and the customer wording.

Candidate customer reasons such as “insufficient credit history,” “high existing obligations,” and “unable to verify information in your application” may be usable only when accurate for the particular decision. Fraud-control protection does not justify inventing a harmless reason or using a vague catch-all. The product can avoid disclosing detection thresholds, vendor scores, investigative rules, or other sensitive controls while still giving the principal reason at a useful level. Support scripts must use the same approved reason family and must not speculate beyond the notice.

## Channel, timing, and abandonment

Use the real-time screen as a clear interim or courtesy message unless counsel confirms that it satisfies all written-notice requirements for the applicable flow. The written notice should follow promptly and within the applicable federal and state deadlines. Electronic delivery requires a valid ESIGN consent and access/retention process; otherwise use postal mail. Preserve proof of generation, delivery or mailing, and the exact version of the notice.

Do not treat abandonment after a decision as a way to avoid a notice duty. First classify whether the application was complete, incomplete, abandoned before a decision, or abandoned after a decision. Silence after a counteroffer must be handled under the counteroffer rules. The product should maintain a status event that records the decision, the reason codes, the notice deadline, the channel, and the issuer even if the customer leaves the flow.

## State and vendor issues

The state list is not confirmed. The current work item identifies CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, and WA for review, but the long-term installment list may differ. State credit, lending, and credit-reporting laws may add notice content, timing, language, licensing, or recordkeeping requirements. Verify by product, creditor, state, and notice type before enabling the launch flag.

Credit-reporting vendors can supply report data, agency contact fields, and codes, but Northstar Pay and its lending partners remain responsible for using accurate reasons and operating their required notices. Vendor contracts should state data fields, correction and dispute support, service levels, audit rights, retention, and the division of notice generation. Customer support must have one escalation path for notice questions and report disputes.

## Launch recommendation from the research

Adopt a dual-track design: show the decision and accurate principal reasons in-app, then send a full written notice by email only where ESIGN conditions are met, otherwise by postal mail. Use a per-flow matrix that names the creditor, decision-maker, report user, notice issuer, reason set, deadline, channel, and support owner. Do not launch the affected decline paths until Product and each lending partner confirm the matrix, Legal approves the reason mapping, and Operations proves the notice and delivery evidence.
