Launch-Readiness Memo — Longer-Term Installment Loan at Checkout (Purchases Above $500)

Matter: Northstar UX Test — 02 — Longer-Term Installment Loan Compliance

Status: Lawyer-editable draft; reviewed by Alex Morgan

Prepared for: Legal, Product, Compliance, Engineering, Operations, and the lending partner

1. Executive recommendation

Northstar Pay can pursue an eight-week launch only with a narrowed phase-one scope. The recommended v1 is a neutral, standardized closed-end installment product for purchases above $500. The checkout should show generic, non-personalized term examples, use one approved underwriting and pricing model for similarly situated customers, and exclude merchant-funded subsidies and selected-customer promotional rates. This scope implements the separate recorded decision to defer merchant subsidies and promotions to phase 2.

This memo is a recommendation and work plan. It is not launch approval. The critical path is the state-by-state partner matrix, not only the federal disclosure template. The launch state list must be limited to states where the creditor, Northstar Pay's role, required licenses, servicing arrangements, and rates and fees are confirmed.

2. Legal approvals

Before launch, legal must approve four packages. First, Alex Morgan must review the state-by-state matrix, including the excluded-state list and the reason for every exclusion. Second, legal must approve the actual Regulation Z disclosure set and customer experience: the multi-term display, pre-acceptance disclosure, final offer, loan agreement, and ESIGN delivery flow. Approval must use real term lengths, APR ranges, fees, and down-payment values.

Third, legal and the lending partner must allocate underwriting, adverse-action, and credit-reporting duties. The record must state who pulls a consumer report, who makes the decision, who sends the notice, and who handles disputes. Fourth, compliance must approve the servicing and vendor-control package, including contract allocation, audit rights, collections conduct, complaints, retention, account closure, and regulator-response duties.

3. State-by-state lender, broker, servicer, and licensing matrix

For each candidate state, the matrix must identify the creditor, lending entity, true-lender analysis, Northstar Pay's role, lender or broker status, servicer, payment processor, licenses held and needed, rate and fee limits, state disclosure rules, and contract owner for each control. It must also identify the customer-facing differences required by state and partner. Engineering must not lock state routing until the matrix is approved.

States with missing licenses, unconfirmed creditor allocation, or pricing outside the applicable limits must remain excluded. The partner map is still being finalized. This is a material missing fact. The practical plan is to draft the matrix in weeks one and two, obtain legal review in week three, and defer any state that needs a new license or unresolved partner work beyond the eight-week window.

4. Regulation Z and checkout display

The safest first-pass treatment is to treat the multi-term checkout display as advertising under Regulation Z section 1026.24. Showing a payment amount, number of payments, repayment period, or finance charge may be a triggering term. If the display shows several terms, the corresponding material information must be available for each term shown, including the amount financed or cash price, down payment, payment schedule, APR, and total of payments. Estimates must be clearly labeled, and the experience should state that final terms depend on underwriting, state, and creditworthiness.

The recommended UX uses generic examples before underwriting. Personalized terms based on a credit pull before term selection would create a different prequalification or offer analysis and could add permissible-purpose, pricing-notice, and adverse-action issues earlier in the flow. After underwriting, the final offer must provide the applicable closed-end disclosures before acceptance. Electronic delivery requires a compliant ESIGN consent and access flow.

5. Underwriting, adverse action, and credit reporting

The partner matrix must assign the creditor and decision-maker for each configuration. If consumer reports are used, each pull needs a permissible purpose. Denials and less-favorable counteroffers require accurate principal reasons and other applicable Reg B and FCRA notice content. The system must preserve the decision record, reason codes, score information when required, and the entity responsible for sending each notice.

Credit reporting for repayment history is still an open fact. If enabled, the furnishing entity needs accuracy, dispute, and direct-dispute controls. If not enabled, the v1 scope should state that fact so product and marketing do not imply credit-building benefits. Either choice needs a documented owner and approved customer language.

6. Rate and fee controls

Product must maintain a versioned pricing and fee matrix keyed by state, creditor, term, APR, fees, down payment, and promotional status. Engineering must consume the approved version as the single source of truth for the checkout display and final offer. The displayed estimate and final offer should use the same Appendix J-compliant calculation engine.

Legal must validate each state entry against rate caps, fee limits, term restrictions, licensing rules, and any bank-partner or rate-export analysis. A state that does not fit must be excluded. Any pricing change requires legal review because it can change the disclosures, advertising copy, state availability, and fair-lending analysis. The displayed matrix version must be logged with the offer and acceptance.

7. Recorded merchant-rate decision

The recorded decision is to defer merchant-subsidized interest and promotional rates to phase 2. Phase one assumes no merchant-funded subsidy, no selected-customer promotional rate, neutral pricing, and neutral term presentation. This decision is separate from the recommendation in this memo, and neither is launch approval.

If phase 2 is pursued, the prerequisites are objective eligibility criteria, a documented business purpose, disclosure review, fair-lending analysis of selection criteria and outcomes, anti-steering controls, and contract language assigning disclosure, pricing, underwriting, complaint, and servicing responsibilities. Merchant materials must not suggest that protected characteristics affect pricing or eligibility.

8. Servicing and vendor controls

The servicing package must cover payments, collections, complaints, hardship requests, disputes between displayed and final terms, credit-reporting accuracy if applicable, account closure, and regulator inquiries. Contracts must allocate compliance duties and include audit and remediation rights. Operations needs approved support scripts and escalation routes.

Retention must use a documented schedule based on legal, contract, dispute, reporting, tax, and litigation needs. Closure must trigger the approved deletion, suppression, or restricted-retention workflow. These controls must be confirmed for every partner and servicing vendor before launch.

9. Conditional launch gates

The gates are: approved state and partner matrix; approved Reg Z display and disclosure set; assigned underwriting and adverse-action duties; a versioned and validated pricing matrix; approved servicing and vendor controls; implementation of the merchant-rate deferral; and final legal sign-off. None of these gates is closed by this draft. The recommended mitigation for the eight-week target is to launch only in the smallest set of already-licensed and operational states, with other states as fast-follow.

10. Facts, assumptions, missing facts, and unverified leads

Supplied facts are the purchase threshold above $500, multiple repayment terms, estimated monthly payment, APR, total amount paid, down payment, underwriting after term selection, final offer and acceptance, different lending entities by state, and an eight-week target after legal approval.

Assumptions are generic rather than personalized pre-underwriting terms, no v1 merchant subsidy or selected-customer promotion, and a partner that is already licensed in each candidate state. Missing facts are the creditor and partner by state, Northstar Pay's role, actual APRs and fees, term lengths, credit-reporting choice, servicing vendor, processor, and ESIGN flow.

Unverified leads require last-mile confirmation. These include the specific state statutes, current partner licenses, rate and fee limits, the precise classification of the final UX, and any partner-specific disclosure or servicing rules.
