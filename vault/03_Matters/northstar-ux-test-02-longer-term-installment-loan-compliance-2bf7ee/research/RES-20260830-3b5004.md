# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

Regulation Z/TILA requirements for a multi-term longer-term installment loan offered at merchant checkout (advertising, APR and payment representations, disclosures before acceptance and at consummation, underwriting and adverse action), state lender/broker/servicer licensing and rate/fee limits, merchant-subsidized interest and promotional rates, fair-lending and steering risks, lending-partner allocation, and servicing/vendor controls.

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay (Product requester; legal owner Brian Harris) wants to add a longer-term installment option at merchant checkout for purchases above $500, showing multiple repayment terms, estimated monthly payment, APR, total paid, and down payment, followed by underwriting, a final offer, and loan acceptance. The product may run through different lending entities by state, with rates, fees, terms, and licensing varying by state. The product team has not decided whether merchants may subsidize interest or offer promotional rates. Target launch is eight weeks after legal approval.

## Decision question

What legal approvals, product constraints, and operating controls must Northstar Pay satisfy before launching a multi-term longer-term installment loan at checkout — specifically, which Regulation Z disclosures and advertising rules apply to the pre-selection term display, which state licensing/rate/fee regimes govern each lending partner's footprint, and what limits apply to merchant-subsidized interest and promotional rates — so the product can launch on the eight-week timeline without rework?

## Open questions

- Which lending partner(s) will be the creditor in each launch state, and is Northstar Pay acting as broker/servicer or lender anywhere (this drives licensing and Reg Z creditor status)?
- Will merchants subsidize interest or offer promotional rates to selected customer groups, and if so, on what targeting basis (fair-lending/steering exposure)?
- What are the actual term lengths, APR ranges, and fee schedule by state, and do any exceed state rate caps or trigger additional licensing?
- Will credit reporting be enabled for this product (adverse action and FCRA obligations)?
- Is the pre-selection term display "advertising" or a prequalification/offer requiring Reg Z §1026.24 treatment before underwriting?

## Likely rules and issues

**Regulation Z / TILA (federal).** Longer-term installment loans above $500 are closed-end credit subject to full Reg Z. Key issues:

1. **Advertising (§1026.24).** The checkout display showing multiple terms, estimated payment, APR, and total paid is likely an advertisement. If it states a rate of finance charge or payment amount, it is a "triggering term" requiring additional disclosures (APR, terms, repayment schedule, down payment). If rates vary by creditworthiness, the ad must state that ("subject to credit approval; rates vary"). Estimated payments must be labeled as estimates.
2. **Pre-selection vs. prequalification.** If the customer sees personalized terms before underwriting, that may be a prequalification offer (§1026.24(e) — "screen" disclosures, "prequalification" label, no misleading impressions of firm terms). If terms are generic until underwriting, standard advertising rules apply. This distinction shapes the UX.
3. **Disclosures before consummation (§1026.17, .18).** Final offer must present TILA disclosures (APR, finance charge, amount financed, total of payments, payment schedule, late fees, prepayment provisions) in the required form and timing, before the customer accepts the loan agreement. E-sign / ESIGN consent flow needed for electronic delivery.
4. **APR accuracy (§1026.22).** APR shown in the term display and final offer must be computed per Appendix J; tolerance rules apply. Merchant-subsidized interest affects finance charge and APR computation.
5. **Underwriting and adverse action.** If credit reporting is used, FCRA/Reg B adverse action notices are required for denials or counteroffers. Reg B (ECOA) timing: 30 days. Fair lending: targeting promotional rates to selected customer groups must have a documented, non-discriminatory basis.

**State law.**

- **Licensing:** Whether Northstar Pay or its lending partner needs a lender, broker, or CFL license varies by state; the entity holding the credit risk determines obligations. Bank-partner structures may preempt state rate caps (valid-when-made / Section 27 national bank preemption), but non-bank lending partners face state rate caps and licensing.
- **Rate/fee limits:** State usury and fee caps (late fees, origination fees) vary; longer-term products may fall under installment loan statutes rather than small-dollar rules.
- **Servicing:** State debt collection and servicing conduct rules apply to servicing vendors.

**Merchant-subsidized interest / promotional rates.** Generally permissible, but: (a) the subsidy must be reflected in the disclosed APR and finance charge; (b) promotional-rate advertising has specific Reg Z rules (§1026.24); (c) targeting selected customer groups raises fair-lending/steering risk — merchants steering customers to specific credit terms can create liability; (d) state laws may restrict merchant participation in credit terms.

**Lending-partner allocation.** Different entities by state means different contracts, disclosures, and responsibility allocation; the partner map must be finalized before launch (per company profile, this is an open implementation fact legal must confirm).

**Servicing/vendor controls.** Vendor oversight, servicing conduct, collections compliance, credit-reporting accuracy, dispute handling, and record retention per the company's documented retention schedule.

## Viable paths

1. **Bank-partner model (preferred for speed).** Use an FDIC-insured bank partner as creditor in launch states, leveraging preemption of state rate caps where available. Northstar Pay acts as broker/servicer under program agreement. Fastest to the 8-week timeline; requires solid vendor oversight and true-lender risk mitigation (substance over form — the bank must retain predominant economic interest).
2. **Non-bank licensed lender model.** Northstar Pay or a non-bank lending partner obtains state licenses. Slower (licensing timelines often exceed 8 weeks) but avoids true-lender risk. Only viable in states where licensing is already in place.
3. **Hybrid (matches current structure).** Bank partner in some states, licensed lender in others, per the existing partner map. Most flexible but requires state-by-state disclosure and fee variations — the "same customer-facing experience may not be used in every state" constraint applies.

**Recommended sequencing:** finalize the partner map first, then build the disclosure set per state/partner, then design the checkout UX around the prequalification vs. advertising distinction.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Whether the term display shows personalized (pre-underwritten) terms or generic ranges — changes the Reg Z advertising vs. prequalification analysis.
- Whether merchants subsidize interest or target promotional rates — changes disclosure treatment and adds fair-lending controls.
- Whether credit reporting is enabled — adds FCRA adverse action obligations.
- Whether any launch state's rate caps or fee limits conflict with the proposed pricing.
- Whether Northstar Pay is the creditor in any state (vs. always the partner) — changes licensing and Reg Z creditor status.
- Term lengths and whether any loan exceeds state thresholds triggering different statutes (e.g., certain states treat loans above/below dollar or term thresholds differently).

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the state-by-state partner matrix (entity, product, rate/fee limits, disclosures) with the lending partners before approving launch — this is flagged as an open implementation fact in the company profile.
- Verify current state licensing status for each entity in each launch state.
- Have the final disclosure templates reviewed against Reg Z §§1026.17, .18, .22, .24 and Appendix J APR calculations with actual product parameters.
- Confirm the E-sign flow for electronic disclosure delivery and loan acceptance.
- If promotional rates proceed, document the targeting basis and run a fair-lending review of the selection criteria.
- Verify servicing vendor contracts allocate compliance responsibility and include audit rights.
