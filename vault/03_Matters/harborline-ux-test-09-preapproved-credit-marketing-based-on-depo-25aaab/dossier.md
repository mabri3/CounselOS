## Summary

Harborline's growth team wants to market a partner-issued credit product to consumer account holders selected by analyzing deposit history (payroll deposits, average balances, overdraft history, account tenure). Selected users would see an in-app "preapproved" banner with a proposed limit range and could apply with the issuing partner; Harborline earns a marketing fee per successful origination. The partner bank makes the final credit decision. The team wants to launch the test next month, and key design facts (credit report use, firm-offer status, decline handling, pre-click data sharing) are undetermined.

## Decision question

May Harborline use consumer deposit-account data to select and target customers for a partner-issued credit offer, and may the banner say "preapproved," under FCRA firm-offer rules, ECOA/fair-lending requirements, financial-privacy and state-privacy law, and unfair-practices standards — and if so, with what disclosures, opt-outs, audience exclusions, partner data-exchange boundaries, and record retention before the campaign launches next month?

## Open questions

- Will a consumer report be used in selection, or only Harborline's own account data? (Determines FCRA firm-offer/§604(d) prescreening analysis.)
- Will deposit/transaction data be shared with the partner before the customer clicks, and under what customer consent or exception?
- How will declined applicants be handled (adverse action notice responsibility — partner vs. Harborline)?
- What does the selection model actually use (exact variables, proxies for protected characteristics, overdraft history as a potential proxy)?
- What do Harborline's privacy notice and partner-bank agreement currently permit regarding use of account data for marketing and third-party sharing?
