# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Harborline UX Test — 09 — Preapproved Credit Marketing Based on Deposit Activity?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Harborline's growth team wants to market a partner-issued credit product to consumer account holders selected by analyzing deposit history (payroll deposits, average balances, overdraft history, account tenure). Selected users would see an in-app "preapproved" banner with a proposed limit range and could apply with the issuing partner; Harborline earns a marketing fee per successful origination. The partner bank makes the final credit decision. The team wants to launch the test next month, and key design facts (credit report use, firm-offer status, decline handling, pre-click data sharing) are undetermined.

## Decision question

May Harborline use consumer deposit-account data to select and target customers for a partner-issued credit offer, and may the banner say "preapproved," under FCRA firm-offer rules, ECOA/fair-lending requirements, financial-privacy and state-privacy law, and unfair-practices standards — and if so, with what disclosures, opt-outs, audience exclusions, partner data-exchange boundaries, and record retention before the campaign launches next month?

## Open questions

- Will a consumer report be used in selection, or only Harborline's own account data? (Determines FCRA firm-offer/§604(d) prescreening analysis.)
- Will deposit/transaction data be shared with the partner before the customer clicks, and under what customer consent or exception?
- How will declined applicants be handled (adverse action notice responsibility — partner vs. Harborline)?
- What does the selection model actually use (exact variables, proxies for protected characteristics, overdraft history as a potential proxy)?
- What do Harborline's privacy notice and partner-bank agreement currently permit regarding use of account data for marketing and third-party sharing?

## Likely rules and issues

**FCRA (15 U.S.C. §1681 et seq.)**
- If selection uses consumer reports, the banner is a "firm offer of credit" under §603(l) and must comply with §615(d) prescreening: firm-offer disclosures, the credit information used, and the right to prohibit use (opt-out notice, including the FCRA-prescribed opt-out language and toll-free/online mechanism).
- If selection uses only Harborline's own account data, FCRA prescreening rules don't directly apply — but the "preapproved" claim must still be truthful (FTC Act §5 / CFPA UDAAP).
- Harborline may be a "consumer reporting agency" risk if it assembles account data to furnish to the partner for credit decisions — structure the data exchange to avoid that.

**ECOA / Reg B**
- Fair-lending testing of the selection model: overdraft history and deposit patterns can proxy for race, age, or other protected bases. Disparate-impact testing of the audience list before launch is the expected control.
- Adverse action: if the partner denies, Reg B notice obligations sit with the creditor (partner), but Harborline's UX should not obscure who decided and why.

**Financial privacy**
- GLBA does not itself bar sharing with the partner for marketing, but the partner-bank agreement and Harborline's privacy notice govern. Sharing deposit data pre-click with a non-affiliated third party for marketing may require notice/opt-out under state law (e.g., California, Vermont) and the partner's own obligations.
- Sponsor-bank oversight: the partner bank will likely require review of the campaign as part of third-party risk management.

**State privacy**
- CCPA/CPRA (if applicable): use of transaction data for marketing may implicate "sharing"/cross-context behavioral advertising rules and opt-out rights; check whether Harborline is covered and whether the partner relationship triggers "sale/share" analysis. Other state privacy laws (VA, CO, etc.) similarly require opt-outs for targeted advertising/profiling uses.

**UDAAP / truthfulness of "preapproved"**
- "Preapproved" implies a commitment. If the partner can still decline or set a lower limit, the word is likely deceptive unless qualified (e.g., "pre-selected" or conditional language) or the selection genuinely meets firm-offer standards. The proposed "limit range" compounds the risk if final limits fall below the range shown.

**Marketing consent / referral fees**
- Marketing fee for originations: check whether any state credit-marketing or referral-fee rules apply, and TCPA/email (CAN-SPAM) rules for the campaign channel — in-app banner reduces but doesn't eliminate these.

## Viable paths

1. **Own-data selection, qualified language (fastest, lowest risk):** Use only Harborline's account data (no consumer reports), change "preapproved" to "pre-selected" or "you may qualify," add clear "final credit decision by [Partner]" language, no data shared pre-click, decline path routes to partner's adverse-action process. Requires privacy-notice check and fair-lending test of the audience model.
2. **True FCRA prescreen (slower):** Partner prescreens using consumer reports; banner is a compliant firm offer with §615(d) disclosures and opt-out. Requires partner operational readiness — likely not achievable for next month.
3. **Hybrid:** Harborline selects on own data; partner runs a soft pre-qualification before the banner shows. Reduces "preapproved" accuracy risk but adds data-sharing questions pre-click.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Whether a consumer report is used in selection (switches the FCRA analysis entirely).
- Whether deposit data goes to the partner before customer action (switches the privacy/data-exchange analysis).
- Whether Harborline's privacy notice already discloses marketing use/sharing of account data.
- Whether overdraft history or other variables in the model show disparate impact.
- Whether the partner will commit to honoring the advertised limit range.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the exact model variables and data sources with the growth team (no consumer report? no third-party data?).
- Review Harborline's current privacy notice and the partner-bank agreement's data-use and marketing provisions.
- Run a disparate-impact test on the proposed audience list before launch.
- Confirm the partner's adverse-action notice process and who bears it.
- Verify current FCRA §615(d) disclosure/opt-out language and CFPB guidance on "preapproved/pre-selected" claims if Path 2 is chosen.
- Check state privacy-law coverage (CCPA thresholds, other state laws) for transaction-data marketing use.

**Recommendation (separate from your decision):** Path 1 — own-data selection with "pre-selected" wording, no pre-click data sharing, partner-decision disclosure, and a pre-launch fair-lending test — is the only path realistically compatible with a next-month launch. Record a durable decision only if you instruct it.
