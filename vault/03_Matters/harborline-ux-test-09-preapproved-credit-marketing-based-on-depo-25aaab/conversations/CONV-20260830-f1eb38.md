Here's my first-pass orientation on the preapproved credit campaign, with the key calls and open facts flagged.

## Bottom line

Harborline can likely run this campaign, but **not as currently described**. The two gating issues are (1) whether the deposit-data selection model makes this a "firm offer of credit" under FCRA, and (2) whether "preapproved" is accurate given that Harborline — not the partner bank — is doing the pre-screening on data the partner doesn't hold. Both are fixable with structure and wording, but they drive everything else.

## Key issues, in order of consequence

**1. FCRA firm offer / prescreening (highest risk).**
If Harborline selects recipients using its own account data (payroll deposits, balances, overdraft history) and the partner issues credit based on that selection, this looks like a prescreened solicitation under FCRA §615(d) — but with a twist: the "consumer report" here is arguably Harborline's own transaction data, not a traditional credit bureau file. Two paths:
- **Path A (cleaner):** Harborline transmits selection criteria or a candidate list to the partner; the partner pulls prescreened credit reports and makes the firm offer. Then FCRA firm-offer disclosures are required, and the offer must be actually available to everyone selected (with limited exceptions).
- **Path B (messier):** Harborline screens on internal data alone and the partner does a full underwrite at application. Then "preapproved" is likely **inaccurate** — the customer hasn't been approved by the entity extending credit, and the banner risks being a deceptive practice (UDAAP) and possibly an ECOA/Reg B adverse-action problem for declined applicants.

**2. "Preapproved" wording.** Unless the partner has actually committed to extend credit to the selected population, the banner should say something like "You may qualify" or "Based on your account history, you're invited to apply." Reserve "preapproved" for a true firm offer.

**3. Declined applicants / adverse action.** If a credit report is used at application, Reg B adverse-action notices and FCRA risk-based-pricing duties sit with the partner — but the partner agreement should allocate that clearly, and Harborline's UX shouldn't promise approval.

**4. Data sharing before click.** Sharing deposit-level transaction data with the partner pre-consent raises GLBA privacy (likely permissible as a joint-marketing/service-provider arrangement with notice/opt-out under the privacy rules, but the notice must actually cover this) and state privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA "sharing for cross-context behavioral advertising" and similar state provisions — a financial-incentive/notice analysis is needed). Practical fix: share only a **coarse eligibility flag or hashed list**, not raw transaction data, and only after the customer clicks.

**5. Marketing consent / TCPA-adjacent.** In-app banner to logged-in users is low-risk, but if email/SMS follow-up is planned, check the E-SIGN/TCPA and marketing-consent posture separately.

**6. Fair lending.** Deposit-history-based targeting can proxy for protected classes (overdraft history and income patterns correlate with race/age). Recommend disparate-impact testing of the selection model before launch and documentation of the business justification.

**7. Marketing fee.** Harborline receiving a per-origination fee is a referral-fee/compensation issue — fine for a non-bank fintech in a partner-bank model, but the partner's regulator will care, and the arrangement should be papered in the partner agreement.

## What I need from you to sharpen this

1. **Who pulls what data when** — does the partner receive the candidate list before or after the customer clicks, and does the partner use a credit report in the prescreen?
2. **What does "preapproved" mean operationally** — is the partner committing to extend credit to everyone selected, or is it a soft invitation?
3. **Which states** are in scope for the initial test (drives the state privacy analysis)?
