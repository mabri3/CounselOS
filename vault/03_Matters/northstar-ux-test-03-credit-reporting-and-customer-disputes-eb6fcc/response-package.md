# Response Package — Limited Credit-Reporting Pilot Launch-Readiness Review

Privileged & confidential attorney work product. Prepared for the Product team. Not yet sent. This package is separate from the recommendation, proposed decision, and recorded decision documents.

Business ask

Product asked for a regulatory and launch-readiness review of a limited pilot to report eligible installment-loan payment activity to one or more consumer reporting agencies, with a hoped-for start next quarter. The stated business objective is to help customers build credit and reduce losses by encouraging on-time payments. The proposed flow discloses reporting at onboarding, sends account-opening, balance, payment, delinquency, and closure data through a reporting vendor, and lets customers view reporting information in-app and submit disputes through customer support. Known complications include incomplete historical data for some accounts and payment dates affected by bank holidays, failed transfers, refunds, and payment-plan changes. This review covers product and customer eligibility, reportable fields and timing, notices, accuracy and investigation controls, dispute intake and escalation, corrections and suppression, retention, vendor and servicing-agent responsibilities, and the minimum pilot process.

Legal recommendation

Legal recommends a narrow, verified-data pilot rather than full-population reporting with post-hoc correction. The pilot should cover only eligible longer-term installment loans in approved states, using only accounts with complete and independently verified history. Pay-in-4 should be excluded pending a separate classification analysis of whether it is reportable "credit." Accounts with known data gaps or unresolved disputes should be excluded from the initial population. Before any negative information is furnished, the dispute workflow, tradeline-level suppression and release controls, payment-date normalization, required notice flow, vendor controls, and evidence retention must be live, tested, and owned. The reasoning is straightforward: Northstar's known data gaps create material accuracy risk under FCRA furnisher duties if reporting starts broad and relies on corrections after the fact. A narrow pilot demonstrates accuracy before furnishing, tests the control path for the known payment-date complications, and produces measurable evidence on dispute volumes, correction rates, suppression events, and vendor performance before any expansion decision. All citations in the underlying analysis (Reg V sections, CFPB guidance, state notice rules) are first-pass research leads that must be verified against current CFR, CFPB, CRA, and state sources before launch or external use.

Recorded decision and its limits

A durable decision has been recorded by Alex Morgan: Northstar Pay may proceed with controlled preparation for a limited credit-reporting pilot next quarter, but live furnishing is not authorized until every launch gate is passed and Alex Morgan approves the final launch. The approved population is eligible longer-term installment loans in approved states with complete and independently verified history. Pay-in-4, accounts with known data gaps, accounts with unresolved disputes, and any expansion beyond the approved population are excluded. The decision's key limit: preparation is authorized; furnishing is not. No live consumer-reporting data may be sent until all ten launch gates below are passed and final launch approval is given by Alex Morgan.

Pilot scope

In scope: eligible longer-term installment loans in approved pilot states; complete and reconciled payment history; account-opening, balance, payment, delinquency, and closure fields; the vendor transmission cycle; direct disputes; and any CRA channel Northstar accepts. Out of scope: pay-in-4, incomplete or unverifiable accounts, unresolved disputes, and any expansion without a separate decision.

Required owners

Brian Harris is the matter owner; Alex Morgan is the reviewing product lawyer and final decision-maker. Product or Eligibility owns the approved population; Operations owns dispute investigation and aging; Engineering owns suppression, release, and audit logging; Customer Support owns intake and consumer communications; Vendor Management owns vendor performance and contract controls; Legal and Compliance own current-source verification, escalations, and launch review. Named individuals must be confirmed before launch — owner assignment is itself a launch gate.

Launch gates

Furnisher of record confirmed for every product and state.

Pilot products, states, and complete-data exclusion list approved and tested.

Payment-date rules tested for holidays, failed transfers, refunds, and plan changes.

Required notices live, logged, and verified for timing.

Direct-dispute and CRA/e-OSCAR/ACDV workflows pass end-to-end dry runs.

Tradeline-level suppression and release controls pass negative-data tests.

Vendor contract includes accuracy, transmission, dispute, audit, incident, retention, and correction terms.

Retention schedule, evidence model, and access owners confirmed.

Owners assigned and trained.

Legal review of current citations and state rules complete.

Unresolved facts

The partner-by-state furnishing map; the extent and location of incomplete historical data; the pilot state list; the CRAs to be used and whether e-OSCAR/ACDV intake applies; the vendor contract status; retention tooling; and available Customer Support and Operations capacity. Each has an open work item in the matter workspace.

Unverified leads

Current Reg V sections 1022.41–1022.43 and 1022.42; FCRA §1681s-2 and negative-information notice timing; CFPB Circular 2022-07 and Bulletin 2013-07; state notice rules including Cal. Civ. Code §1785.26; CRA onboarding, Metro 2, and e-OSCAR requirements; and whether pay-in-4 is reportable credit. These must be verified against current authoritative sources before launch or external use.

Next actions

Confirm furnisher of record per product/state (highest priority — determines whether Northstar builds the full control set or shifts to partner contract oversight).

Verify all citations against current CFR, CFPB, CRA, and state sources.

Build the pilot exclusion list and eligibility gate.

Implement payment-date normalization rules and test each scenario.

Execute the vendor contract with required terms.

Implement the negative-information notice flow with per-account logging.

Build and test the dispute workflow with suppression logic.

Document the retention schedule and audit trails.

Run the 90-day go/no-go review against the ten launch gates.
