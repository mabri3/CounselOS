# Build dispute workflow with suppression logic

Purpose and scope. This work item defines the minimum control process for a limited Northstar Pay credit-reporting pilot. It covers customer-support direct disputes and any CRA/e-OSCAR or ACDV channel that the company accepts. The pilot should include only longer-term installment accounts with complete, verified payment history. Pay-in-4 remains outside scope until Northstar confirms that product's legal classification and reporting approach. This is a first-pass legal and operational draft, not a final legal opinion.

Working legal position. The current working assumption is that Northstar Pay is the furnisher of record for the pilot, while the reporting vendor is a service provider or intermediary. That assumption must be confirmed for each product, state, and lending-partner arrangement. If a bank or lending partner is the furnisher, Northstar still needs contract oversight and evidence controls, but the partner may own the statutory furnishing and dispute duties. The vendor contract should assign the operational steps without treating delegation as a transfer of Northstar's risk.

Intake and triage. Every dispute must receive a unique case ID, received timestamp, source channel, account and tradeline identifiers, consumer assertions, supporting evidence, CRA or ACDV reference when present, and the employee or system owner. Operations should route direct disputes to a trained dispute owner on the day received. The owner should identify whether the dispute is complete, whether the account is in the pilot population, whether a systemic issue may affect other consumers, and whether negative furnishing must be suppressed while the facts are unresolved. A complaint that alleges identity theft, repeat error, regulatory contact, or a pattern across accounts must escalate to Legal and Compliance.

Investigation and timing. The dispute owner should compare the source ledger, contractual due dates, actual receipt dates, bank-return and holiday records, refunds, plan changes, servicing notes, prior corrections, and the exact fields sent to each CRA. The investigation should use reasonable, documented steps and preserve the evidence reviewed. The current draft assumes a 30-day response target for a direct dispute, a written result to the consumer, correction notices to each affected CRA, and a five-business-day notice process for any frivolous or irrelevant dispute determination. These timing and notice statements are verification flags. Confirm the current Reg V text, the applicable FCRA provisions, any CRA operating rules, and state requirements before launch.

Suppression and correction. When a dispute concerns a negative field and Northstar cannot verify the field promptly, the system should place a furnishing hold or suppression flag on the affected tradeline and prevent the next negative update from passing the pre-furnishing gate. The case owner should record the start and end of the hold, the fields affected, the reason, and the approving role. If the investigation finds an error, Northstar should correct its source ledger, send the required correction to every affected CRA, notify the consumer of the result, and test that the next furnishing file carries the corrected status. If the investigation confirms the data, the owner should document the evidence and the basis for resuming furnishing. No post-hoc correction should substitute for a pre-furnishing accuracy control.

Channel ownership and vendor controls. Customer Support owns intake and consumer communications; Operations owns investigation and case aging; Engineering owns suppression, audit logging, and release controls; Vendor Management owns vendor performance; Legal and Compliance own escalations, policy interpretation, and launch approval. The vendor must provide documented intake and transmission SLAs, field-level validation, file rejection reporting, correction support, audit access, incident notice, retention support, and a named escalation contact. Northstar should test both the direct-dispute path and the e-OSCAR/ACDV path, if applicable, with synthetic cases before any live furnishing. CRA-specific onboarding rules, Metro 2 requirements, and e-OSCAR timelines remain unverified leads until confirmed from current CRA and CFPB materials.

Evidence and launch gates. Retain the original dispute, all consumer and CRA communications, investigation steps, source records, payment-date calculations, vendor submissions and acknowledgments, suppression events, corrections, notice-delivery evidence, approvals, and system audit logs under a documented schedule. Before launch, require: confirmed furnisher of record; approved pilot states and products; complete-data exclusion list; tested negative-information notice flow; tested payment-date normalization; end-to-end direct-dispute and CRA-channel dry runs; working suppression and release controls; vendor contract terms; assigned owners; and a legal review of current citations and state rules. The pilot should pause if any gate fails or if a systemic error cannot be bounded.

Assumptions, missing facts, and unverified leads. Assumptions are that Northstar is the furnisher, the vendor is an intermediary, the pilot excludes pay-in-4, and the company can log and suppress each affected tradeline. Missing facts include the partner-by-state map, pilot state list, CRA selection, vendor contract status, current retention tooling, exact historical-data gaps, dispute channel coverage, and service-level capacity. Unverified leads include the current text and application of Reg V provisions, FCRA notice requirements, CFPB Circular 2022-07 and Bulletin 2013-07, Cal. Civ. Code section 1785.26, CRA onboarding and Metro 2 rules, e-OSCAR timelines, and whether pay-in-4 is reportable credit. These items must be checked against current authoritative sources before external use.

Recommendation. Recommend a narrow pilot only after the launch gates pass. Start with longer-term installment accounts whose history is complete and independently reconciled. Suppress negative reporting for an open dispute when the field is not yet verified, and require documented investigation and correction before release. Keep this recommendation separate from the later business decision. The proposed decision should be recorded only after Alex Morgan reviews the pilot scope, assigns accountable owners, confirms the unresolved facts, and edits the rationale.
