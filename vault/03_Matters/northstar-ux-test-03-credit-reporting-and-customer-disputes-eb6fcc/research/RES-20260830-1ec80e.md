# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Northstar UX Test — 03 — Credit Reporting and Customer Disputes?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay (a U.S. fintech offering pay-in-4 and installment loans with bank/lending partners) wants to begin reporting eligible installment-loan payment activity — account opening, balance, payment, delinquency, and closure data — to consumer reporting agencies through a reporting vendor. Customers would be told at onboarding, could view reporting info in the app, and would submit disputes through customer support. Product hopes to pilot next quarter. Known data-quality gaps (incomplete historical data; payment dates affected by holidays, failed transfers, refunds, plan changes) make FCRA accuracy and dispute-handling obligations the central legal risk.

## Decision question

What legal and operational minimum must Northstar Pay satisfy before the pilot — which products/customers may be reported, what data fields, what accuracy validation, and what dispute-investigation process — and can the pilot launch next quarter given the known incomplete historical data, or must reporting be limited to accounts with verified data?

## Open questions

- Which entity (Northstar Pay or the bank/lending partner) is the furnisher of record for each product/state, and does the partner's contract control or permit reporting?
- Which products (pay-in-4 vs. longer-term installments) and which state populations are in scope for the pilot, given the phased state rollout?
- How complete is the historical data, and can affected accounts be excluded from the pilot population?
- Will disputes arrive only via customer support (direct disputes under FCRA §623(b)/Reg V) or also via CRAs (e-furnisher/E-OSCAR), and is the vendor equipped for both?
- Is credit reporting already covered in current customer notices/consents, or does onboarding language need updating before the pilot?

## Likely rules and issues

**Furnisher accuracy (FCRA §1681s-2(a); Reg V 12 C.F.R. Part 1022, Subpart B)**
- Duty to furnish only complete, accurate information; prohibition on reporting information the furnisher knows or has reasonable cause to believe is inaccurate.
- Reasonable cause standard: the known data-quality issues (incomplete history, payment-date distortions from holidays, failed transfers, refunds, plan changes) directly implicate this. Reporting accounts with known gaps is the highest-risk item.
- Duty to correct and update previously furnished information (§1681s-2(a)(2)) — closure, paid-in-full, and status updates.
- Established policies/procedures requirement (Reg V §1022.42): written policies covering accuracy, integrity, and updating, plus identity-theft and dispute blocks.

**Dispute handling (FCRA §1681s-2(b); Reg V §1022.43)**
- Direct disputes from customers must be investigated within 30 days (Reg V allows up to 45 in limited circumstances); disputes received via CRAs must be investigated and results reported back within the FCRA dispute cycle (typically 30 days).
- Duty to review all relevant information provided by the customer/CRA, report results, and correct/delete inaccurate or unverifiable information with all CRAs furnished.
- Reg V §1022.43(e) lets you reject facially frivolous/irrelevant direct disputes, but only with a notice explaining why.

**Negative information and unresolved billing disputes**
- FCRA §1681s-2(a)(3): notice to the customer before or within 30 days after furnishing negative information (delinquency). Onboarding notice may satisfy this if properly scoped.
- No FCRA rule categorically bars reporting negative information during an unresolved dispute, but furnishing information you have reasonable cause to believe is inaccurate is prohibited — so an open billing dispute about the amount or payment status should trigger a hold or flag on that tradeline pending investigation. This is a policy decision to make explicit.

**Notices and consumer-facing flow**
- Onboarding disclosure that data may be reported; app display of reporting status; Reg V direct-dispute address/flow requirements (customers must have a way to dispute directly — customer support must be a documented, compliant channel).
- State overlay: some states impose additional credit-reporting or dispute-handling requirements; the phased state rollout means the pilot state list must be checked.

**Vendor oversight**
- The reporting vendor is a service provider under vendor-overship obligations; contract should address accuracy controls, dispute routing, data security, audit rights, and responsibility allocation. FCRA liability for furnishing stays with the furnisher regardless of vendor error.

**Retention**
- Reg V and FCRA don't set a single retention period, but dispute-investigation records, furnishing records, and corrections must be retained long enough to demonstrate compliance (commonly aligned with a documented retention schedule; state law and partner contracts may set minimums).

## Viable paths

1. **Limited pilot (recommended):** Report only accounts with complete, verified data; exclude accounts with known historical gaps; implement written furnisher policies (Reg V §1022.42), a 30-day direct-dispute process through customer support, negative-information notice at onboarding, and a hold/flag rule for accounts with open billing disputes. Launch next quarter is feasible if the vendor contract and policies are ready.
2. **Full-population launch:** Higher FCRA exposure given known data gaps; not recommended without a documented accuracy validation showing the gaps don't create reasonable cause to believe inaccuracy.
3. **Delay:** Only if the partner-entity question (who is furnisher of record) or vendor contract can't be resolved in time.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Whether the bank/lending partner, not Northstar Pay, is the furnisher of record (changes who owes the FCRA duties and who needs the policies).
- The actual scope and severity of the incomplete historical data.
- Whether the pilot includes pay-in-4 (short-term, often not reported) or only longer-term installment loans.
- Whether the reporting vendor already provides Reg V-compliant dispute workflows.
- Whether current customer notices already disclose credit reporting.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the furnisher-of-record entity per product/state with the lending partners and check partner contract terms on reporting.
- Verify the pilot state list against state-specific credit-reporting/dispute requirements.
- Confirm the reporting vendor's contract, metro-2 format compliance, and dispute-routing capabilities.
- Have the data team quantify the incomplete-history population and confirm it can be excluded at the file level.
- Legal owner (Brian Harris) should sign off on the written furnisher policies and the negative-information/dispute-hold policy before pilot launch.
