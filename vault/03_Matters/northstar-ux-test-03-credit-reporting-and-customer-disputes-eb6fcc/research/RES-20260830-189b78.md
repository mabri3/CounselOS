# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What are the FCRA and related regulatory requirements for a fintech installment-loan lender acting as a furnisher of consumer-reporting information in a limited pilot: furnisher duties (accuracy, reasonable investigation of disputes under FCRA §623 / 15 U.S.C. §1681s-2, Reg V 12 C.F.R. Part 1022 Subpart E), required notices and disclosures, dispute handling timelines, Metro 2 reporting standards and timing, suppression of negative reporting during unresolved billing disputes, vendor/service-provider contract controls, record retention, and CFPB guidance on furnishers?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay, a fintech offering pay-in-4 and longer-term installment products with bank/lending partners, wants to report eligible installment-loan payment activity to consumer reporting agencies to help customers build credit and reduce losses. The flow would disclose reporting at onboarding, send account data through a reporting vendor, and let customers view and dispute reporting in-app. Product wants a pilot next quarter. Known complications include incomplete historical data for some accounts and payment dates affected by bank holidays, failed transfers, refunds, and plan changes.

## Decision question

What FCRA furnisher duties, notices, dispute-handling timelines, Metro 2 reporting standards, vendor controls, and retention rules must Northstar Pay implement before launching a limited credit-reporting pilot next quarter, and what is the minimum operational process (validation, dispute investigation, suppression of negative reporting during unresolved billing disputes) that satisfies those requirements without delaying the pilot?

## Open questions

- Which entity (Northstar Pay or the bank/lending partner) is the legal furnisher of record for each product and state, and does the partner's contract control or permit Northstar to furnish?
- Which products (pay-in-4 vs. longer-term installments) and which accounts (given incomplete historical data) will be in the pilot scope?
- Which CRAs will be used, and what are their specific furnisher onboarding, Metro 2, and dispute-transmission (e-OSCAR) requirements?
- Will disputes arrive only through customer support, or also via e-OSCAR/ACDVs from CRAs, and who owns each channel?
- What retention schedule and audit-trail capabilities exist today for dispute records and furnishing logs?

## Likely rules and issues

**Furnisher accuracy duties — FCRA §623 / 15 U.S.C. §1681s-2; Reg V, 12 C.F.R. Part 1022 Subpart E**
- §1681s-2(a)(1): duty to furnish accurate information; §1681s-2(a)(2): duty to correct and update previously furnished information.
- Reg V §1022.42: reasonable procedures to ensure maximum possible accuracy, including reviewing account records before furnishing negative information (e.g., delinquency) and not furnishing information the furnisher knows or has reasonable cause to believe is inaccurate.
- **Direct application to the known facts:** incomplete historical data and payment dates distorted by holidays, failed transfers, refunds, and plan changes are exactly the accuracy risks Reg V §1022.42(b) targets. Accounts with unreliable historical data should be excluded from the pilot or furnished only with verified data.
- §1681s-2(a)(3): duty to notify CRAs of disputes previously furnished (voluntary direct-dispute channel), and §1681s-2(b): reasonable investigation of disputes received from CRAs.

**Dispute handling — Reg V §1022.43 (direct disputes) and §1022.41 (e-OSCAR/ACDV disputes)**
- Direct disputes: reasonable investigation within 30 days of receipt; report results to the consumer within 30 days; if furnished information is inaccurate or incomplete, notify each CRA to which it was furnished and correct.
- ACDV disputes (via CRA): investigate and respond within the CRA's cycle (typically 30 days), including the results and any corrections.
- The proposed in-app/customer-support dispute channel is a direct dispute under Reg V §1022.43 — the 30-day clock and result-reporting duties apply.
- Reg V §1022.43(e): a furnisher may reject a direct dispute as frivolous/irrelevant only with reasonable basis, and must notify the consumer within 5 business days of that determination.

**Notices and disclosures**
- §1681s-2(a)(7): negative-information notice (30-day advance notice before furnishing negative information) — required for first-time negative reporting on an account.
- Onboarding disclosure that payment data may be reported: supports accuracy and consumer-awareness goals but is not itself an FCRA substitute for the §1681s-2(a)(7) negative-information notice.
- No general FCRA requirement to disclose furnishing at onboarding, but state law (e.g., California Civil Code §1785.26) and the company's own risk posture favor clear disclosure.

**Metro 2 standards and timing**
- Metro 2 is the industry-standard format (CDIA) for furnishing account data. CRAs generally require monthly furnishing on a fixed cycle; data must be internally consistent (dates, balances, status codes).
- Payment dates affected by holidays, failed transfers, refunds, and plan changes must be normalized to the actual contractual due date and actual payment receipt date, not the processing date, to avoid furnishing inaccurate delinquency data.

**Suppression of negative reporting during unresolved billing disputes**
- No FCRA provision expressly bars furnishing negative information during a billing dispute, but §1681s-2(a)(1) accuracy duties and Reg V §1022.42(b) require the furnisher not to report delinquency it has reasonable cause to believe is disputed/inaccurate. CFPB furnisher guidance (e.g., CFPB Circular 2022-07 on furnishing inaccurate information after a dispute; CFPB Bulletin 2013-07 on furnisher responsibilities) treats furnishing disputed-but-unverified negative data as a §1681s-2(a) violation risk.
- Practical rule: suppress or flag disputed tradelines pending investigation outcome; do not furnish new negative data on an account with an open dispute until the dispute is resolved.

**Vendor/service-provider controls**
- The reporting vendor acts as a service provider. FCRA does not impose a formal vendor-management rule on furnishers, but the furnisher remains liable for accuracy and dispute handling regardless of vendor error. Contract must include: accuracy obligations, Metro 2 compliance, dispute-transmission SLAs, audit rights, breach/incident notification, data-security and retention terms, and indemnification.
- CFPB expectations (Bulletin 2013-07; Circular 2022-07) treat furnishers as responsible for vendor-caused errors.

**Record retention**
- FCRA has no express retention period for furnishers, but Reg V §1022.43(c) requires the furnisher to conduct a reasonable investigation, which presupposes retaining the records relied on. CFPB supervisory expectations and litigation-hold practice favor retaining dispute files, investigation records, and furnishing logs for at least the applicable limitations period (typically 2–5 years depending on state and claim). A documented retention schedule (per company profile) is the operating standard.

**CFPB guidance**
- CFPB Bulletin 2013-07 (furnisher responsibilities for accuracy and dispute handling) and Circular 2022-07 (furnishing inaccurate information after a dispute is raised) are the key supervisory positions. Both emphasize that furnishers cannot rely on vendor or partner processes to discharge §1681s-2 duties.

## Viable paths

**Path A — Narrow pilot with verified data only (recommended)**
- Limit the pilot to accounts with complete, verified historical data and stable payment records; exclude accounts with known data gaps or active disputes.
- Implement the full dispute workflow (30-day investigation, 5-day frivolous-rejection notice, correction notification to CRAs) before launch.
- Furnish via the vendor under a contract with the controls above; suppress negative reporting on disputed accounts pending resolution.
- Pros: lowest FCRA risk, achievable next quarter. Cons: smaller pilot population; requires data-quality gating before launch.

**Path B — Full-population launch with post-hoc corrections**
- Report all eligible accounts immediately, relying on the dispute process to catch errors.
- Pros: fastest to full scale. Cons: high §1681s-2(a) accuracy risk given known data-quality issues; CFPB Circular 2022-07 exposure if disputed data is furnished; not recommended.

**Path C — Delay launch until data quality and partner roles are confirmed**
- Hold the pilot until the partner map (who is the furnisher of record per product/state) and historical-data remediation are complete.
- Pros: cleanest legal position. Cons: misses the next-quarter pilot window; may be unnecessary if Path A's gating is workable.

## Facts that could change the answer

- If the bank/lending partner, not Northstar Pay, is the furnisher of record for some products, Northstar's role shifts to service-provider/vendor oversight and the contract controls become the primary legal workstream.
- If the incomplete historical data affects only a small, identifiable account subset, Path A's exclusion list is straightforward; if it is pervasive, Path C becomes more likely.
- If the pilot includes pay-in-4 accounts, additional state-law and product-specific questions (e.g., whether pay-in-4 is a "credit" transaction reportable to CRAs) arise and may narrow scope.
- If disputes will arrive via e-OSCAR/ACDV as well as in-app, the operational process must cover both channels and the CRA cycle timing.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Confirm the furnisher-of-record entity for each product and state with the lending partners and the reporting vendor contract.
- Read Reg V §§1022.41–.43 and CFPB Bulletin 2013-07 and Circular 2022-07 directly to confirm the dispute timelines and suppression expectations stated above before finalizing the operational process.
- Confirm the specific CRAs' Metro 2 onboarding requirements and dispute-transmission mechanics (e-OSCAR vs. direct).
- Verify state-specific negative-information notice requirements (e.g., California Civil Code §1785.26) for the pilot states.
- Confirm the retention schedule with litigation-hold and credit-reporting-vendor contract requirements.
