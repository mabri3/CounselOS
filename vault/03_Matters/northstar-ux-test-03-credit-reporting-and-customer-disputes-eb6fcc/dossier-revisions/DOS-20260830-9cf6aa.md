## Summary

Northstar Pay (a U.S. fintech offering pay-in-4 and installment loans with bank/lending partners) wants to begin reporting eligible installment-loan payment activity — account opening, balance, payment, delinquency, and closure data — to consumer reporting agencies through a reporting vendor. Customers would be told at onboarding, could view reporting info in the app, and would submit disputes through customer support. Product hopes to pilot next quarter. Known data-quality gaps (incomplete historical data; payment dates affected by holidays, failed transfers, refunds, plan changes) make FCRA accuracy and dispute-handling obligations the central legal risk.

## Decision question

What legal and operational minimum must Northstar Pay satisfy before the pilot — which products/customers may be reported, what data fields, what accuracy validation, and what dispute-investigation process — and can the pilot launch next quarter given the known incomplete historical data, or must reporting be limited to accounts with verified data?

## Open questions

- Which entity (Northstar Pay or the bank/lending partner) is the furnisher of record for each product/state, and does the partner's contract control or permit reporting?
- Which products (pay-in-4 vs. longer-term installments) and which state populations are in scope for the pilot, given the phased state rollout?
- How complete is the historical data, and can affected accounts be excluded from the pilot population?
- Will disputes arrive only via customer support (direct disputes under FCRA §623(b)/Reg V) or also via CRAs (e-furnisher/E-OSCAR), and is the vendor equipped for both?
- Is credit reporting already covered in current customer notices/consents, or does onboarding language need updating before the pilot?
