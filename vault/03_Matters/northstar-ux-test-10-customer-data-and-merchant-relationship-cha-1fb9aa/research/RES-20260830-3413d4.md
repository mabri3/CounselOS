# First-Pass Research Packet

## Question

What material source-based issues should counsel research for Northstar UX Test — 10 — Customer Data and Merchant Relationship Changes?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary

Northstar Pay (Product requester; legal owner Brian Harris) wants to expand use of customer and transaction data this quarter for product analytics, fraud prevention, personalized offers, and merchant performance reporting, using purchase categories, repayment history, device info, approximate location, and app activity. Existing privacy notices and contracts were written for a narrower product. Some merchants want Northstar Pay transaction data for their own marketing, and a new commercial program may change merchant underwriting rules, pricing, settlement timing, and data access. Counsel must define the permitted data-use model, required notices and agreements, and legal limits on changing merchant and customer-facing terms before launch.

## Decision question

Which data uses in the proposed program can Northstar Pay launch this quarter under its existing notices and contracts, and which require new customer notice/consent, merchant agreement amendments, or partner/vendor agreements first — given that merchants want transaction data for their own marketing and the commercial program would change merchant underwriting, pricing, settlement, and data-access terms?

## Open questions

- What do the current privacy notice and customer terms actually permit for analytics, personalized offers, location/device data, and merchant promotions — and where are the gaps?
- Which strategic merchants would receive detailed (non-aggregated) reports, and what data fields, purpose, and access controls would that involve?
- What do current merchant and lending-partner contracts say about data ownership, sharing, and unilateral changes to underwriting, pricing, settlement timing, or data access?
- What retention periods apply to the new analytics and reporting datasets, and do any uses involve sensitive data or precise location?
- Which states in the live footprint (CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, WA) impose consent, opt-out, or sensitive-data limits that constrain the program?

## Likely rules and issues

**Generated analysis** (no external sources supplied; based on matter facts and company profile):

1. **Permitted data uses / purpose limitation.** Existing notices were written for a narrower product; new uses (personalized offers, merchant promotions, detailed merchant reporting) are new purposes. Company policy: do not assume consent covers a new purpose; map each use to notice, consent, opt-out, and contract requirements.
2. **Sensitive data and location.** Approximate location, device information, and app activity may be sensitive or regulated under state privacy laws (e.g., CA/CO opt-in for sensitive data, cross-context behavioral advertising limits). Minimize; justify each use.
3. **Merchant use of transaction data for own marketing.** Company policy prohibits merchants or advertising partners using identifiable repayment history or sensitive customer data for unrelated marketing without documented legal basis and customer choice. This is likely a hard "no" absent a separate legal basis and opt-in.
4. **Strategic merchant detailed reporting.** Default is aggregated/de-identified. Detailed reporting requires separate legal review of purpose, data fields, contract, customer notice, access controls, and state limits.
5. **Contract change limits.** Changing merchant underwriting rules, pricing, settlement timing, or data access likely requires amendment rights under existing merchant agreements; unilateral changes risk breach or state unfair-practices exposure. Customer-facing term changes need Alex Morgan approval per company policy.
6. **Vendor and partner sharing.** Analytics vendors, advertising partners, and service providers need written-instruction agreements; lending partners may have their own data-sharing and reporting obligations.
7. **Retention.** New datasets need a documented retention schedule, not indefinite retention; account closure should trigger deletion/suppression review.

## Viable paths

- **Path A (recommended): Tiered launch.** Launch this quarter only the uses that fit existing notices and contracts (internal analytics, fraud prevention, aggregated merchant dashboards). Gate personalized offers, merchant promotions, and detailed strategic-merchant reporting on a notice update, consent/opt-out flow, and amended agreements.
- **Path B: Full program with updated paperwork first.** Update privacy notice, add consent/opt-out flows, amend merchant and vendor agreements, then launch everything — slower, likely misses the quarter.
- **Path C: Reject merchant-owned marketing use of transaction data** while permitting aggregated dashboards; require separate documented legal basis and customer choice for anything more.

## Facts that could change the answer

- Actual text of the current privacy notice and customer terms (whether new uses are already covered).
- Whether "approximate location" is precise enough to be sensitive under applicable state law.
- Whether strategic-merchant reports would include identifiable or merely aggregated data.
- Amendment/change-of-terms provisions in merchant and lending-partner contracts.
- Which states' privacy laws apply to the live footprint and whether Northstar Pay meets exemption thresholds.

## Recommended last-mile verification

- Pull and read the current privacy notice, customer terms, merchant agreement template, and lending-partner data provisions against the proposed use list.
- Confirm the state-by-state product/partner matrix (still being finalized per company profile) before approving any data-sharing expansion.
- Verify state privacy-law applicability and sensitive-data treatment for CA, CO, and other live states.
- Confirm with Product which uses are actually needed this quarter to scope the tiered launch.

*Note: no external legal sources were supplied in this run; the analysis above is generated from matter facts and company policy assumptions and should be verified against current law before delivery.*
