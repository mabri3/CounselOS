# # Privacy and Data Memo — Expanded Data Program

## Status and scope

Edited first-pass draft for Alex Morgan, product lawyer for fictional Northstar Pay. This memo analyzes the proposed customer and transaction data program and related merchant relationship changes. It is a draft work product. It is not a recorded decision. The matter request is the supplied source. No external legal source was verified in this run.

## Executive view

Northstar Pay should use a tiered launch. Tier 1 may include internal product analytics, tightly controlled fraud prevention, underwriting changes only after model-input and adverse-action review, and aggregated merchant performance dashboards. Tier 2 should hold personalized offers, merchant promotions, and detailed strategic-merchant reports until notice, customer-choice, state, contract, and control work is complete. Tier 3 should reject merchant-owned marketing using identifiable Northstar Pay transaction or repayment data and advertising-partner sharing for now. Merchant commercial terms and customer-facing terms require separate contract and approval tracks.

## Purpose-by-data analysis

Product analytics may use purchase categories, repayment history, device information, approximate location, and app activity to measure checkout completion and improve the product. This is an internal-use purpose. The current notice and data inventory must be checked before launch. Use the minimum fields, remove direct identifiers from analysis where possible, restrict access, and set a retention period.

Fraud prevention may use transaction, device, approximate-location, and app-activity signals to detect suspicious behavior. Fraud use is separate from advertising and from marketing. The fraud vendor, if any, must act only on written instructions and must not reuse the data. Document false-positive handling, human escalation, retention tied to fraud and dispute needs, access logs, and customer support paths.

Underwriting may use purchase categories, repayment history, device information, approximate location, and app activity only if each input is documented and approved for credit use. Determine whether an input changes eligibility, amount, pricing, repayment terms, or another credit term. If it does, test accuracy and fairness, document reasons, align customer notices and adverse-action explanations, and obtain lending-partner and compliance review. Do not treat an underwriting input as ordinary product analytics or marketing data.

Personalized offers may use purchase categories, repayment history, and app activity to offer new Northstar Pay financing products or merchant promotions. This is a new-purpose question under the narrower existing notice. Separate service messages from marketing. Build a clear customer choice, suppression, and preference process. Do not use sensitive data or a proxy for sensitive data until the applicable state analysis and customer-choice design are complete.

Merchant performance reporting should default to aggregate or de-identified dashboards. Use minimum group sizes, suppression for small cohorts, field-level review, and controls that reduce re-identification. Detailed reports for selected strategic merchants need a separate review of fields, purpose, access, contract, customer notice, state limits, and onward use. Repayment history should not be included in detailed merchant reports unless a documented legal basis and customer choice support it.

Merchant-owned marketing is different from merchant performance reporting. A merchant request to use identifiable transaction or repayment data for its own marketing is outside the safe default. Do not provide customer-level data or an audience derived from repayment history without a separate legal basis, explicit customer choice where required, strict contract limits, and a new legal review.

Advertising-partner sharing is also separate. Do not send raw events, identifiers, device data, location data, repayment history, or matched audiences to advertising partners until Northstar Pay maps whether the activity is cross-context behavioral advertising, confirms state requirements, updates notice, implements opt-out or opt-in controls, and signs a use-restricted agreement. Data collected for fraud or underwriting must not be repurposed for advertising by assumption.

## Actor and data-flow analysis

Customers provide or generate purchase, repayment, device, location, and app-activity data. Northstar Pay should collect and use only the fields needed for the stated purpose. Northstar Pay may share limited outputs with merchants, lending partners, analytics vendors, advertising partners, and service providers.

Merchants should receive aggregate performance measures by default. Merchant agreements must define permitted purposes, fields, aggregation, access, retention, no re-identification, no onward sharing, audit rights, deletion, and incident duties. Lending partners require a review of data ownership, credit use, reporting duties, model governance, and partner-specific restrictions. Analytics and fraud vendors need written instructions, confidentiality, security, deletion, and no secondary use. Advertising partners need a separate agreement and cannot be treated as ordinary service providers without confirming the actual data flow. Service providers must be classified by function and contract.

## Notices, consent, and opt-out

Compare the proposed field and purpose matrix against the existing privacy notice, customer terms, cookie or tracking disclosures, marketing preferences, and prior consent records. Update the notice if it does not clearly describe analytics, fraud prevention, underwriting inputs, personalized offers, merchant reporting, or sharing with each actor class.

Provide a customer-facing choice for personalized offers and merchant promotions. Honor the choice across app, email, push, and merchant channels. Provide a simple opt-out and a suppression process. Determine whether sensitive-data or advertising rules require opt-in. Do not use a bundled choice that hides underwriting, fraud, or advertising inside a marketing choice.

## Sensitive data, location, retention, and security

Confirm whether approximate location is precise location, whether device or app signals reveal sensitive traits, and whether purchase categories can reveal health, religion, political views, or other sensitive information. Minimize or exclude such fields. Confirm the live states and applicability thresholds before relying on a state-law conclusion. State-specific conclusions below are unverified leads, not verified law.

Set purpose-specific retention. Do not keep analytics, reporting, fraud, or marketing datasets indefinitely. Define deletion, suppression, account-closure, legal-hold, and vendor-deletion workflows. Use role-based access, audit records, encryption, field-level restrictions, and re-identification testing for merchant reporting.

## Merchant and customer term changes

Review each merchant agreement before changing underwriting rules, pricing, settlement timing, or access to customer data. Confirm amendment rights, notice periods, approval steps, termination rights, grandfathering, and older templates. Do not announce the commercial program before this review.

Treat customer-facing pricing, underwriting, sharing, retention, or other material term changes as a separate approval track. Alex Morgan must review the final customer notice and terms. Determine whether advance notice, re-consent, migration, or customer support changes are needed.

## Launch gates

Tier 1 gate: field-level data map; notice review; vendor and lending-partner review; underwriting model-input and adverse-action sign-off; aggregation standards; retention and access controls.

Tier 2 gate: updated notice; customer consent or opt-out flow; preference suppression; state-law review; merchant and vendor amendments; detailed-report field approval; customer-support readiness.

Tier 3: merchant-owned marketing with identifiable transaction or repayment data and advertising-partner sharing are rejected for this launch. If Product wants either use, open a new scoped review with a documented legal basis, customer-choice design, data-flow map, and contracts.

Separate gate: merchant contract amendment-rights review and Alex Morgan approval for customer-facing term changes.

## Assumptions, missing facts, and unverified leads

Assumptions: the program is still in design; no new sharing has started; the current notice and contracts cover a narrower product; sensitive categories and precise location are not approved; and Northstar Pay can stage the launch.

Missing facts: governing jurisdictions and customer states; customer age groups; exact fields and derived scores; whether any field affects credit; current notice and customer terms; merchant and lending-partner contracts; vendor roles and locations; retention schedule; aggregation thresholds; opt-out mechanics; strategic merchants and report fields; and the business uses needed this quarter.

Unverified leads: whether CA, CO, GA, IL, NY, TX, or WA impose a particular consent, opt-out, or sensitive-data limit on the proposed flow; whether existing agreements permit each use; whether aggregate reports are sufficiently de-identified; and whether the existing notice covers Tier 1. These points require last-mile verification.
