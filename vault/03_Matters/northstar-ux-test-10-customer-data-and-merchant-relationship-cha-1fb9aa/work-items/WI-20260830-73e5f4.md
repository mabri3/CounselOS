# # Orient to the request

Northstar Pay wants to expand customer and transaction data use this quarter. The business goals are to improve product analytics and underwriting, prevent fraud, personalize customer offers, and show merchants which checkout experiences lead to completed purchases. The request also includes a possible change to merchant commercial terms and to customer-facing product terms.

## Lawyer and decision frame

I am Alex Morgan, the product lawyer for fictional Northstar Pay. The immediate legal decision is whether the proposed data program and merchant relationship changes can proceed this quarter, and under what controls. The work must produce an organized first pass for product, privacy, commercial, and compliance owners. A legal-perfect answer is not required at this stage. A usable foothold is required.

The work will run in order: Intake, Research, Explore, Generate, Respond, then Closed. This record is the Intake orientation. I will keep recommendations separate from any final recorded decision.

## Known facts

Northstar Pay proposes to use purchase categories, repayment history, device information, approximate location, and app activity. The actors are customers, Northstar Pay, merchants, lending partners, analytics vendors, advertising partners, and other service providers.

The proposed purposes are not one single use. They are: product analytics; fraud prevention; underwriting; personalized offers for new financing products or merchant promotions; merchant performance reporting; and possible merchant or advertising-partner marketing. Merchants may receive aggregated dashboards. Selected strategic merchants may receive more detailed reports.

Northstar Pay already has privacy notices and contracts written for a narrower product. Some merchants want to use Northstar Pay transaction data for their own marketing. Northstar Pay may change a merchant's underwriting rules, pricing, settlement timing, or access to customer data as part of a new commercial program.

## Initial issue map

Fraud prevention: determine which device, location, app, and transaction signals are needed; who may access them; how false positives are handled; and the retention, sharing, and customer-notice basis for the use.

Underwriting: determine whether purchase categories, repayment history, device information, approximate location, or app activity affect eligibility, credit amount, pricing, or other credit terms. If they do, identify testing, notice, explanation, adverse-action, fair-lending, and lending-partner review needs. Underwriting is distinct from product analytics and marketing.

Personalization: determine whether offers are for Northstar Pay financing, merchant promotions, or both. Separate service communications from marketing. Define opt-in or opt-out paths, audience rules, sensitive-data limits, suppression, and how a customer can stop personalized offers.

Merchant reporting: default to aggregate and de-identified reporting where possible. Define minimum group sizes, data fields, purpose limits, access controls, retention, and whether detailed strategic-merchant reporting can identify or be used to re-identify customers. Treat merchant dashboards as a separate disclosure and contract question.

Advertising: treat advertising partners as a separate, higher-risk sharing path. Do not assume that data used for fraud or underwriting can be reused for advertising. Determine whether any identifiers, event data, or audiences leave Northstar Pay, whether cross-context behavioral advertising is involved, and what notice, consent, opt-out, contract, and deletion controls apply.

Merchant and product changes: identify which proposed changes affect existing merchant contracts, customer agreements, pricing, underwriting, settlement, access, or disclosures. Confirm notice, amendment, termination, grandfathering, and implementation timing requirements before launch.

## Assumptions

The company has not supplied governing jurisdictions, customer segments, merchant agreements, lending-partner agreements, vendor contracts, data inventory, data lineage, retention schedule, or technical design. I assume the program is still at design stage and no new sharing has started. I assume the request does not authorize use of sensitive categories or precise location. These are working assumptions, not confirmed facts.

## Missing facts to obtain

Confirm jurisdictions and customer states; whether customers are adults or include minors; the exact data fields and derived scores; whether any field is sensitive or a proxy for a sensitive trait; whether data affects a credit decision or only internal reporting; data-controller/processor/service-provider roles; vendor locations and onward sharing; retention and deletion timing; customer notice and consent history; current opt-out mechanics; merchant reporting granularity; minimum aggregation thresholds; contract amendment rights; and required review owners.

Also confirm whether merchants may market their own products using transaction data, whether Northstar Pay would send customer-level data or only audiences, whether advertising partners receive raw events or matched segments, and whether the program changes customer pricing, underwriting rules, settlement timing, or data access.

## Unverified leads

The request itself is the supplied source. No external source has been verified in this Intake step. Any legal rule, deadline, or agency requirement identified later must be labeled verified source, supplied source, or unverified lead. Do not imply that a citation was checked when it was not.

## Proposed work items

1. Build a purpose-by-data matrix that separates fraud, underwriting, personalization, merchant reporting, analytics, and advertising.

2. Identify notice, consent, opt-out, contract, and data-minimization changes for each purpose.

3. Map actors and data flows, including lending partners, analytics vendors, advertising partners, merchants, and service providers.

4. Review merchant and customer term-change mechanics, including pricing, settlement, underwriting rules, and access to customer data.

5. Produce a long-form privacy/data memo with assumptions, missing facts, unverified leads, controls, and launch gates.

6. Produce a separate recommendation for the business decision.

7. Prepare a fictional decision for lawyer review and edit it before recording.

## Initial recommendation direction

Proceed only with a staged design review. Keep fraud prevention, underwriting, personalization, merchant reporting, and advertising in separate workstreams. Start with low-risk internal analytics and tightly controlled fraud analysis while facts and agreements are confirmed. Do not launch customer-level merchant marketing, advertising-partner sharing, or detailed merchant reporting until the data map, notices, opt-out/consent design, aggregation safeguards, contracts, and credit-impact review are complete. This is a working recommendation and is not the final decision.
