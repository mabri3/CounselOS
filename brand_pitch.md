## Brand core

### Brand Essence

> "Themis helps lawyers feel clear, capable, and supported when their judgment matters most."

### What the essence means

**Clear**  
The lawyer can see the request, business objective, material facts, assumptions, open questions, sources, and next action without rebuilding the matter from scattered files and chat history.

**Capable**  
The lawyer has accurate, well-supported analysis and useful drafts to work with. Themis is designed to reduce the time spent gathering, prompting, checking, and assembling work so the lawyer has more capacity to examine the issues and exercise judgment. Accuracy and time savings must be measured in actual use.

**Supported**  
Themis stays with the matter. It supplies a thoughtful second perspective, tests assumptions, and carries relevant company and matter context forward. Support includes a well-reasoned challenge when the lawyer may have missed something. The lawyer remains in control.

### Brand promise

More capacity for the work only the lawyer can do.

**Relationship promise:** The lawyer makes the call. Themis makes sure they do not face it alone.

### Product role

Themis is a capable colleague for legal judgment. It turns an ambiguous request into an oriented, researched, editable starting point. It helps the lawyer understand the terrain, test a path, create useful work, and preserve the reasoning behind the final call.

The first experience should feel like a capable junior associate working alongside a senior lawyer: quick, succinct, substantive, and aware of the business context. Themis helps explain an unfamiliar issue and asks useful questions without making the lawyer feel examined or sending them through a compulsory lesson.

### Positioning statement

For in-house product lawyers and small legal teams who must make consequential decisions under pressure and with incomplete facts, Themis is a matter-centered legal workspace designed to help them deliver accurate, well-supported answers and useful work faster. It brings relevant company context, facts, questions, research, recommendations, work product, and recorded decisions together over time. By reducing setup work and making assumptions, alternatives, and missing facts visible, Themis helps the lawyer feel clear, capable, and supported when judgment matters most. The lawyer chooses the path and owns the decision.

### The practical promise behind the feeling

More capacity for the work only the lawyer can do.

Themis earns that promise by doing useful work, showing its basis, and remembering what matters. Efficiency means less total lawyer effort to reach a usable result, including prompting, verification, correction, formatting, and finding prior context. A fast draft that takes longer to repair does not fulfill the promise.

Accuracy is a core requirement. Themis should give its strongest useful answer, show the available support, and explain the facts or interpretations that would change it. The lawyer's responsibility for the final decision does not excuse careless model work. Incomplete evidence must be visible while useful analysis remains available.

### The human truth

The hard part of product-legal work is not only finding information or writing a draft. It is making a decision while knowing that an unseen fact, issue, or consequence could change the answer.

### The product truth

Themis reduces setup work and context reconstruction. It gives the lawyer the forest before the trees, shows what needs attention, produces a useful first pass, and keeps recommendations separate from decisions the lawyer explicitly records.

### The strategic thesis

Model intelligence will spread. Workflow features can be copied. A product that repeatedly helps a lawyer feel clearer, more capable, and properly supported at the moment of judgment can earn a durable place in the lawyer's work.

This is a thesis, not yet a proven moat. It depends on lawyers returning because the work is useful and the context remains dependable. Stored files alone do not establish that advantage.

### Initial customer and adoption test

Start with solo general counsel and in-house legal teams of fewer than ten people, initially through product counsel questions. Their work combines business context, research, communication, and judgment under time pressure. Prove value for an individual lawyer first. The current product is a local, single-user MVP; shared matters are a proposed expansion.

The practical alternative is the lawyer's current combination of general-purpose AI, sources, documents, colleagues, and manual tracking. Themis must remove enough work and improve enough context to justify becoming another tool. Model choice is an implementation capability; the benefit to lead with is dependable help in the lawyer's actual work.

### Founder insight: understand what the answer depends on

A lawyer may ask for a yes or no while needing a broader understanding: the relevant rules, alternative interpretations, adjacent issues, and facts that would change the conclusion. Themis should answer the question and make that surrounding structure quickly accessible, with reliable citations and room to explore further.

The supplied facts are a starting point. A requester may omit a detail, misunderstand a relationship, or misstate when something happens. Themis should signal material assumptions and overlooked possibilities in the manner of a helpful colleague: “I wonder whether this is happening. It could change the analysis.” The lawyer chooses whether to explore that branch.

Brian's leaf-and-branch analogy explains the depth: a single answer is a leaf; the lawyer may need the branch to understand where changing facts could lead. Use this as an internal explanation of the product, while keeping the flashlight as the deck's primary visual metaphor.

### Two sources of capacity

**Individual capacity:** Less effort supplying repeated context, steering research, challenging overconfident output, and assembling the legal picture. More attention for evaluating alternatives, learning an unfamiliar area, and responding to the business.

**Team capacity — proposed expansion:** Shared matters could let another lawyer quickly understand the question, research, open issues, and current state. That could make it easier to hand off a whole matter or divide the work when demand is uneven. Brian experienced the cost of transferring context while balancing workloads at fast-moving startups. This is a founder-observed need, not a shipped collaboration feature or a measured customer outcome.

## Narrative system

### Primary metaphor: the flashlight

The flashlight explains how Themis supports judgment. It makes the legal terrain, assumptions, hazards, and edge of current knowledge easier to see. The lawyer directs the light, chooses the path, and owns the decision.

The metaphor must not imply that Themis knows the one correct path, removes all uncertainty, or guarantees a safe result.

### Supporting metaphor: the subway

The subway explains the product boundary. Themis carries the lawyer from raw intake to an organized, researched, editable foothold near the decision. The lawyer completes the last mile with judgment, company context, and authority.

Use the flashlight sparingly in the external story. Keep the subway as an internal explanation of scope. Once the promise is clear, use concrete lawyer work to carry the argument.

### Core message hierarchy

1. **Emotional promise:** You do not have to face the judgment alone.
2. **Human problem:** Legal decisions must often be made before the full terrain is visible.
3. **Product thesis:** Themis makes uncertainty visible enough for the lawyer to exercise judgment.
4. **Functional proof:** The matter carries the request, facts, research, recommendations, work product, decisions, and next action.
5. **Trust proof:** The product behaves with humility, curiosity, presence, persistence, support, and fidelity.
6. **Strategic claim:** Repeated support at the point of judgment can become more durable than a feature advantage.

## Brand character

Themis should be:

- calm, not theatrical;
- precise, not cold;
- candid, not defensive;
- useful, not ceremonial;
- quietly confident, not absolute;
- human in conduct, not human by pretense; and
- respectful of the lawyer's authority, not passive about helping.

### Voice

- Lead with the answer, implication, or next action.
- Use plain language and strong hierarchy.
- State material uncertainty without turning it into a refusal.
- Give the useful first pass before exhaustive detail.
- Ask the question that can change the analysis.
- Describe what the product did. Do not overclaim what it knows.

### Message guardrails

Say:

- Themis strengthens legal judgment.
- Themis helps the lawyer see what could change the answer.
- Themis turns scattered inputs into an organized matter.
- Themis preserves context and the reasoning behind a decision.
- The lawyer remains the author and owner of the decision.

Do not say:

- Themis replaces the lawyer.
- Themis guarantees the correct legal answer.
- Themis removes all uncertainty or prevents every missed issue.
- Themis records an agent recommendation as the lawyer's decision.
- Themis has proven customer trust, retention, or defensibility before evidence exists.

### Editorial assessment

The original pitch had a strong emotional center but repeated the same claim across several slides. It also understated practical capacity and accuracy, treated broad research as one user signal, and led product proof with an outdated test count.

After the founder interview, the investment sequence assigns each slide a separate job: capacity promise, pressure, discovery, why now, first experience, partnership, example, customer and team expansion, behavior, repeat use, founder, next milestone, and investment invitation. The Brand Essence, exact source-slide transcript, accepted Slide 6 title and comparison, and four title alternatives remain intact.

The central test is whether Themis earns the feeling of support through accurate, useful work and dependable memory. That connects the emotional promise to business value without assigning the lawyer's choice of path to the product.
