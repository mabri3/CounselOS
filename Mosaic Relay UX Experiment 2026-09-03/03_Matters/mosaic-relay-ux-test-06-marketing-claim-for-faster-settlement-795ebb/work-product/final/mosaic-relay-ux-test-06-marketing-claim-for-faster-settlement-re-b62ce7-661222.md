# First-pass legal response — marketing claim for faster settlement

## Executive summary
Do not launch the statement “instant settlement for every merchant” in its current form. It is an express, measurable claim. The known facts do not support a universal service level. Timing varies with payment method, risk review, bank processing, reserves, disputes, and merchant configuration. A reasonable merchant may also read “settlement” to mean receipt in its external bank account, which Mosaic Relay does not control.

Marketing can use qualified language only after Mosaic Relay defines the claimed event, eligibility, exclusions, geography, and measurement method, and has competent and reliable evidence before launch. The safest near-term path is to use a capability description, not a speed promise, and to remove paid-search speed claims until the required qualification can fit clearly.

## Facts and assumptions
Known facts supplied by the requester:
- Mosaic Relay is payment infrastructure. It does not offer bank accounts or hold deposits.
- Selected transactions have shown same-day or near-real-time availability.
- There is no universal service level.
- Timing depends on payment method, risk review, bank processing, reserves, disputes, and merchant configuration.
- The campaign would run on the website, in sales presentations, and in paid search in four weeks.

Material assumptions:
- U.S.-primary review and FTC Act Section 5 are the initial legal framework.
- “Instant settlement for every merchant” is an express factual claim, not puffery.
- The audience includes growing marketplaces and their decision makers.

Missing facts:
- Whether “settlement” means processor settlement, Mosaic Relay-controlled availability, or external-bank receipt.
- Aggregate timing data, measurement window, denominator, and percentage meeting the proposed speed.
- Qualifying merchant and transaction segments; exclusions; geographic scope; and the role of bank or processor dependencies.
- The paid-search networks and their character limits.

## Issues
1. Is the absolute claim substantiated before dissemination?
2. Could “settlement” create a misleading net impression about money reaching a bank account?
3. Are qualifications clear, conspicuous, close to the claim, and understandable in each channel?
4. Can the business support an “up to” or “as fast as” claim with reliable segmented evidence?
5. Do sales materials and scripts preserve the same qualification as the website and ads?

## Research notes
VERIFIED in the saved research packet: the working FTC Section 5 framework treats a material representation likely to mislead a reasonable audience as deceptive, and express factual claims must have competent and reliable evidence before they are made. The more absolute and specific the claim, the stronger the evidence required. The packet also records the channel distinction: a website has room for full qualification; written sales materials need the qualification even if a salesperson can add context; paid search has limited space and is the highest-risk channel for a bare speed headline.

UNVERIFIED LEAD: the saved packet identifies FTC “Dot Com Disclosures” guidance as relevant to clear and conspicuous online disclosures. Confirm the current primary source and apply the final placement test before launch. No source should be treated as verified unless counsel confirms it.

## Analysis
The proposed wording combines two absolutes: “instant” and “every merchant.” The current examples are anecdotes for selected transactions, not evidence of a universal result. A disclaimer cannot repair a headline that creates a contrary net impression. “Up to” language is not self-proving: the claimed maximum must be real, representative of eligible transactions, and supported by dated records. If the claim concerns dashboard availability, say so. If it concerns external-bank receipt, measure that end point and account for bank timing.

Mosaic Relay should not imply that it holds deposits or operates a bank account. State that funds may be made available through processor and payout controls, subject to conditions, and that availability is not the same as processor settlement or receipt in a merchant bank account.

## Required qualifications and disclosures
Before any speed claim:
- Define the event: processor settlement, Mosaic Relay-controlled availability, or receipt in an external bank account.
- State the exact claimed time, measurement window, and the eligible population.
- Put material conditions next to the claim. Include payment-method, risk-review, reserve, dispute, bank-processing, merchant-configuration, and geographic limits when applicable.
- Explain that timing is not guaranteed and may vary outside Mosaic Relay’s control.
- Keep the qualification in the same medium and near the claim. Do not rely only on a sales script or a buried website footer.
- Maintain a substantiation file with source data, methodology, exclusions, and approval date.

## Channel guidance and replacement language
Website (only after evidence): “Eligible transactions may become available in Mosaic Relay’s payout workflow as fast as [measured time], subject to payment method, risk review, reserves, disputes, merchant configuration, and bank processing. Availability in Mosaic Relay is not a promise of processor settlement or receipt in your external bank account. See full eligibility and timing details.”
Until evidence exists: “Mosaic Relay helps marketplaces manage payment and payout workflows. Timing varies by transaction and configuration.”

Sales presentations: “For eligible transactions, Mosaic Relay can support faster availability in the payout workflow. Timing is not universal or guaranteed and depends on payment method, risk review, reserves, disputes, configuration, processors, and banks. Do not describe this as instant settlement or as receipt in a merchant bank account.” Add the same text to the slide, not only the speaker notes.

Paid search: Do not run “instant settlement” or any numeric speed claim until a short, adjacent qualification is possible and the network format has been checked. Safer interim copy: “Faster payout workflows for eligible marketplaces.” Landing-page copy must repeat the qualification and define availability. Do not assume a landing page alone cures an unqualified headline.

## Evidence plan
Product and Operations must produce dated, segmented records by payment method, merchant segment, risk outcome, reserves, disputes, bank path, configuration, and geography. Record total transactions, eligible transactions, the measurement start and end points, denominator, distribution or percentage meeting the claim, and outliers. Processor and payout partners must confirm their role. Compliance should confirm the final claim, qualification, and channel execution before launch. Re-test the evidence when product or processor controls change.

## Recommendation (not a decision)
1. Reject the absolute wording for all channels.
2. Pause paid-search speed claims.
3. Use the interim capability wording above while the evidence plan runs.
4. Require a defined settlement event, measured timing data, eligibility/exclusion matrix, geographic scope, and channel review before approving any “up to” claim.
5. Re-review any claim that says or implies external-bank receipt.

## Decision status
No durable decision has been recorded. Recommendation is separate from decision. The current matter is ready for lawyer approval of the response, but the underlying marketing launch remains blocked by missing substantiation and undefined conditions.

## Compliance and blockers
This is a fictional local matter. No external message or campaign was sent. Blockers are: no aggregate timing data; no defined eligibility conditions or exclusions; settlement meaning and geographic scope undecided; and paid-search disclosure space untested. Sources are labeled VERIFIED or UNVERIFIED LEAD as stated above.
