      ## Preliminary framing (before research results return)


      These are working notes, not conclusions. They will be updated as research runs

      ### FTC Section 5 deception framework (working)

      - Section 5 of the FTC Act prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices.
      A representation, omission, or practice is deceptive if it is likely to mislead
      a reasonable consumer acting reasonably under the circumstances and is material.

      - Express factual claims (like a speed-of-funds claim) must be substantiated
      with competent and reliable evidence **before** the claim is made.

      - The substantiation standard is tied to the specificity of the claim: the more
      specific and absolute the claim, the higher the evidentiary bar.

      - **UNVERIFIED LEAD:** FTC''s "Dot Com Disclosures" guidance addresses how disclosures
      must be clear and conspicuous and placed so consumers notice them. Requires
      confirmation.


      ### The settlement-meaning distinction (working)

      The claim''s meaning determines what must be substantiated and disclosed:

      - **Processor settlement** — funds move through the card network/processor settlement
      cycle. Timing governed by network rules and processor controls, not Mosaic Relay.

      - **Platform-controlled availability** — funds become available in a Mosaic
      Relay-controlled workflow (e.g., a balance or payout queue). This is within
      Mosaic Relay''s control but is NOT receipt in the merchant''s bank.

      - **External-bank receipt** — funds land in the merchant''s external bank account.
      This is the meaning a merchant most likely cares about and the hardest to substantiate
      because it depends on bank processing outside Mosaic Relay''s control.


      **Working implication:** If the claim says "settlement" but the merchant reasonably
      understands it as "money in my bank account," the claim must be substantiated
      at the external-bank-receipt level, or clearly disclosed that it means something
      narrower. This is the core deception risk.


      ### Channel differences (working)

      - **Website:** room for full qualification and disclosure; can use footnotes,
      tooltips, and full eligibility language.

      - **Sales presentations:** live context allows verbal qualification, but written
      materials must still carry the same disclosures.

      - **Paid search:** strict character limits and limited disclosure space; a qualified
      claim may not fit, and a bare "instant settlement" headline without qualification
      is the highest-risk form.

      - Eligibility conditions/exclusions not defined.

      - Settlement meaning not decided.

      - Geographic scope not decided.

      ## Part 2 — Issue Map


      ### Core issue

      The proposed claim "instant settlement for every merchant" is not substantiable
      as written. No universal service level exists, and timing depends on payment
      method, risk review, bank processing, reserves, disputes, and merchant configuration.
      This holds under every possible meaning of "settlement."


      ### Issue branches


      **1. Substantiation (FTC Section 5)**

      - 1a. Absolute claim fails. "Instant" + "every merchant" is an express factual
      claim requiring competent and reliable evidence. No such evidence exists.

      - 1b. Even qualified claims need data. "Up to X" / "as fast as X" / "for eligible
      merchants" still require measured data: a defined measurement window, a denominator,
      and the percentage of transactions meeting the stated speed. None exists yet.

      - 1c. Substantiation must precede the claim. Evidence must exist before the
      ad runs, not after.


      **2. The settlement-meaning fork (most decision-changing)**

      - 2a. Processor settlement — governed by network/processor rules outside Mosaic
      Relay''s control.

      - 2b. Platform-controlled availability — within Mosaic Relay''s control but
      not bank receipt.

      - 2c. External-bank receipt — what merchants most likely understand; hardest
      to substantiate (depends on bank processing).

      - Fork: If the claim uses "settlement" but merchants reasonably read it as "money
      in my bank," the claim must be substantiated at the external-bank level or clearly
      narrowed. This is the core deception risk.


      **3. Qualification and disclosure requirements**

      - 3a. Website: full qualification possible (footnotes, tooltips, eligibility
      language).

      - 3b. Sales presentations: verbal context helps, but written materials must
      carry the same disclosures.

      - 3c. Paid search: character limits and limited disclosure space make a bare
      speed headline the highest-risk form.


      **4. Missing facts that gate any claim**

      - 4a. No aggregate timing data (work item open).

      - 4b. Eligibility conditions/exclusions not defined.

      - 4c. Geographic scope not decided.

      - 4d. Settlement meaning not decided.


      ### Priority ranking (by how much the answer moves)

      1. **Settlement meaning** (2) — determines what must be substantiated and disclosed.

      2. **Timing data** (4a) — no claim of any kind can be substantiated without
      it.

      3. **Eligibility conditions** (4b) — required for any qualified claim.

      4. **Channel-specific disclosure** (3) — shapes replacement language.

      5. **Geographic scope** (4c) — determines applicable regime.


      ### Decision points for counsel

      - Which meaning of "settlement" will the claim use?

      - What speed can be truthfully claimed once data exists?

      - Which eligibility conditions and exclusions apply?

      - What geographic scope?

      - Which replacement language per channel?

# First-Pass Research Memo — Marketing Claim for Faster Settlement

Date: 2026-09-03
Scope: FTC Section 5 substantiation/deception; material qualification and disclosure per channel; the settlement-meaning distinction; substantiation standard for "up to"/"as fast as" claims.

### Source labeling convention
Per counsel instruction, all sources are labeled as either:
- **VERIFIED** — authoritative primary source (statute, regulation, FTC guidance, agency enforcement action) confirmed against the source.
- **UNVERIFIED LEAD** — secondary commentary, blog, or unconfirmed reference that requires confirmation before reliance.

### Preliminary framing (working notes, not conclusions; update as runs complete)

**FTC Section 5 deception framework (working):**
- Section 5 of the FTC Act prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices. A representation, omission, or practice is deceptive if it is likely to mislead a reasonable consumer acting reasonably under the circumstances and is material.
- Express factual claims (like a speed-of-funds claim) must be substantiated with competent and reliable evidence **before** the claim is made.
- The substantiation standard is tied to the specificity of the claim: the more specific and absolute, the higher the evidentiary bar.
- **UNVERIFIED LEAD:** FTC's "Dot Com Disclosures" guidance addresses how disclosures must be clear and conspicuous and placed so consumers notice them. Requires confirmation.

**The settlement-meaning distinction (working):**
- **Processor settlement** — funds move through the card network/processor settlement cycle. Timing governed by network rules and processor controls, not Mosaic Relay.
- **Platform-controlled availability** — funds become available in a Mosaic Relay-controlled workflow (e.g., a balance or payout queue). Within Mosaic Relay's control but NOT receipt in the merchant's bank.
- **External-bank receipt** — funds land in the merchant's external bank account. The meaning a merchant most likely cares about; hardest to substantiate because it depends on bank processing outside Mosaic Relay's control.
- **Working implication:** If the claim says "settlement" but the merchant reasonably understands it as "money in my bank account," the claim must be substantiated at the external-bank-receipt level, or clearly disclosed that it means something narrower. This is the core deception risk.

**Channel differences (working):**
- **Website:** room for full qualification and disclosure; footnotes, tooltips, full eligibility language.
- **Sales presentations:** live context allows verbal qualification, but written materials must still carry the same disclosures.
- **Paid search:** strict character limits and limited disclosure space; a qualified claim may not fit, and a bare "instant settlement" headline without qualification is the highest-risk form.

## Part 2 — Issue Map

### Core issue
The proposed claim "instant settlement for every merchant" is not substantiable as written. No universal service level exists, and timing depends on payment method, risk review, bank processing, reserves, disputes, and merchant configuration. This holds under every possible meaning of "settlement."

### Issue branches

**1. Substantiation (FTC Section 5)**
- 1a. Absolute claim fails. "Instant" + "every merchant" is an express factual claim requiring competent and reliable evidence. No such evidence exists.
- 1b. Even qualified claims need data. "Up to X" / "as fast as X" / "for eligible merchants" still require measured data: a defined measurement window, a denominator, and the percentage of transactions meeting the stated speed. None exists yet.
- 1c. Substantiation must precede the claim. Evidence must exist before the ad runs, not after.

**2. The settlement-meaning fork (most decision-changing)**
- 2a. Processor settlement — governed by network/processor rules outside Mosaic Relay's control.
- 2b. Platform-controlled availability — within Mosaic Relay's control but not bank receipt.
- 2c. External-bank receipt — what merchants most likely understand; hardest to substantiate (depends on bank processing).
- Fork: If the claim uses "settlement" but merchants reasonably read it as "money in my bank," the claim must be substantiated at the external-bank level or clearly narrowed. This is the core deception risk.

**3. Qualification and disclosure requirements**
- 3a. Website: full qualification possible (footnotes, tooltips, eligibility language).
- 3b. Sales presentations: verbal context helps, but written materials must carry the same disclosures.
- 3c. Paid search: character limits and limited disclosure space make a bare speed headline the highest-risk form.

**4. Missing facts that gate any claim**
- 4a. No aggregate timing data (work item open).
- 4b. Eligibility conditions/exclusions not defined.
- 4c. Geographic scope not decided.
- 4d. Settlement meaning not decided.

### Priority ranking (by how much the answer moves)
1. **Settlement meaning** (2) — determines what must be substantiated and disclosed.
2. **Timing data** (4a) — no claim of any kind can be substantiated without it.
3. **Eligibility conditions** (4b) — required for any qualified claim.
4. **Channel-specific disclosure** (3) — shapes replacement language.
5. **Geographic scope** (4c) — determines applicable regime.

### Decision points for counsel
- Which meaning of "settlement" will the claim use?
- What speed can be truthfully claimed once data exists?
- Which eligibility conditions and exclusions apply?
- What geographic scope?
- Which replacement language per channel?

## Part 3 — Evidence Plan

### Purpose
Define the evidence required to substantiate any speed-of-funds-availability claim before it runs, and the process to produce and validate that evidence.

### Evidence required (before any claim runs)

**1. Aggregate timing data (blocking — no claim can run without it)**
- What: Dated, segmented records of actual settlement/availability timing.
- Segmentation by: payment method, risk review outcome, bank processing, reserves, disputes, and merchant configuration.
- Metrics: a defined measurement window, a denominator (total transactions), and the percentage of transactions meeting any stated speed.
- Owner: Product and Operations (work item open).
- Status: Not yet produced. Only unverified examples of same-day/near-real-time availability exist.

**2. Eligibility conditions and exclusions (blocking for any qualified claim)**
- What: Defined qualifying merchant segments, excluded transaction types, and any geographic differences in processor/payout controls.
- Owner: Product, Operations, Processor, and Compliance confirmation.
- Status: Not yet defined.

**3. Settlement-meaning definition (blocking — determines what to measure)**
- What: A decision on whether "settlement" means processor settlement, platform-controlled availability, or external-bank receipt.
- Why: The chosen meaning determines which timing data is relevant and what must be disclosed.
- Status: Not yet decided.

**4. Geographic scope (blocking for substantiation scope)**
- What: Decision on U.S.-only vs. broader scope.
- Status: Not yet decided.

### Validation process
- Timing data must be **dated** and **segmented** — not anecdotal examples.
- Data must be produced **before** the claim runs (substantiation precedes the claim).
- All sources relied on must be labeled **VERIFIED** (primary/authoritative) or **UNVERIFIED LEAD** (secondary, requires confirmation).

### Deliverables once evidence is in hand
- Replacement language per channel (website, sales, paid search) tied to the substantiated speed.
- Required qualifications and disclosures per channel.
- A substantiation file documenting the data, measurement window, denominator, and percentage.

- Settlement meaning undecided.
- Geographic scope undecided.
