# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Review the proposed marketing claim "instant settlement for every merchant" for website, sales presentations, and paid search ads launching in four weeks. Determine required qualifications/disclosures, the substantiation standard, and acceptable replacement language. Marketing has confirmed a qualified framing ('up to', 'as fast as', 'for eligible merchants') is acceptable.

## Decision question

Review the proposed marketing claim "instant settlement for every merchant" for website, sales presentations, and paid search ads launching in four weeks. Determine required qualifications/disclosures, the substantiation standard, and acceptable replacement language. Marketing has confirmed a qualified framing ('up to', 'as fast as', 'for eligible merchants') is acceptable.

## Material facts

- Marketing proposes advertising Mosaic Relay as providing 'instant settlement for every merchant' across website, sales presentations, and paid search ads.
- Campaign is planned to begin in four weeks.
- The platform can make some funds available quickly through processor and payout controls, but actual timing depends on payment method, risk review, bank processing, reserves, disputes, and merchant configuration.
- Mosaic Relay does not offer bank accounts or hold deposits.
- There are examples of same-day or near-real-time availability for selected transactions, but no universal service level.
- Qualifying conditions, exclusions, geographic scope, and supporting evidence for the claim have not been defined.
- It has not been decided whether 'settlement' means processor settlement, availability in a Mosaic Relay-controlled workflow, or receipt in the merchant's external bank account.
- Business goal is to increase qualified leads among growing marketplaces.
- What does 'settlement' mean for this claim? — Not yet decided — this is part of what we need to determine
- Is the claim intended as a universal promise ('every merchant, always'), or is a qualified framing acceptable to marketing (e.g. 'up to', 'as fast as', 'for eligible merchants')? — Qualified framing acceptable — 'up to', 'as fast as', 'for eligible merchants'
- What actual timing data exists to substantiate any speed claim — what share of transactions meet the claimed speed, and over what measurement window? — We only have examples of same-day or near-real-time availability for selected transactions. We do not have aggregate timing data, a defined measurement window, a denominator, or a percentage of transactions meeting a stated speed. Treat the examples as supplied but unverified leads until Product and Operations produce dated, segmented records.

## Assumptions

- U.S.-primary advertising scope (consistent with company profile); non-U.S. expansion subject to separate review.
- FTC substantiation standard (Section 5) applies to the advertising claims.
- The claim is treated as an express factual claim requiring substantiation rather than puffery.
- The 'instant settlement for every merchant' claim is not substantiable as written under any of the three possible meanings of 'settlement', because there is no universal service level and timing depends on many factors.
- With a qualified framing accepted, the remaining work is to pin down the specific speed claim, eligibility conditions, and supporting data.

## Issues and workstreams

- Absolute/universal claim ('every merchant', 'instant') cannot be substantiated given no universal service level and timing dependent on many factors — FTC Section 5 deception risk.
- Ambiguity of 'settlement' (processor settlement vs. Mosaic Relay-controlled availability vs. receipt in merchant's external bank account) materially changes what must be substantiated and disclosed.
- No defined qualifying conditions, exclusions, geographic scope, or supporting evidence for the claim.
- Channel differences: paid search ads have strict character limits and disclosure constraints vs. website and sales presentations.
- Even a qualified claim ('up to X') requires real measured data and clearly disclosed eligibility conditions to be substantiated.

## Open questions

- Actual measured settlement/availability timing data and the percentage of transactions meeting any claimed speed.
- Defined qualifying conditions and exclusions for the fast-availability claim.
- Geographic scope of the claim.
- Decision on which meaning of 'settlement' the claim will use.
- What eligibility conditions and exclusions apply (payment methods, reserves, disputes, risk review, bank processing)?
- What geographic scope does the claim cover?
