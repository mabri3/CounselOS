# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay’s marketing team proposes advertising “instant settlement for every merchant” across website, sales presentations, and paid search ads beginning in four weeks. Product confirms the platform can make some funds available quickly through processor and payout controls, but actual timing depends on payment method, risk review, bank processing, reserves, disputes, and merchant configuration. Counsel is involved because the claim is an express, universal speed claim that currently lacks substantiation, defined eligibility conditions, and a settled meaning of “settlement.” The matter is closed with a recorded decision to use only interim capability wording until substantiation and channel review are complete.

## Decision question

Can Mosaic Relay substantiate and disclose a speed-of-funds claim using “settlement” to mean processor settlement, platform-controlled availability, or receipt in the merchant’s external bank account, and which meaning must the claim use to avoid FTC Section 5 deception risk given the four-week launch timeline and the absence of aggregate timing data?

## Material facts

- Marketing proposes advertising Mosaic Relay as providing 'instant settlement for every merchant' across website, sales presentations, and paid search ads.
- Campaign is planned to begin in four weeks.
- The platform can make some funds available quickly through processor and payout controls, but actual timing depends on payment method, risk review, bank processing, reserves, disputes, and merchant configuration.
- Mosaic Relay does not offer bank accounts or hold deposits.
- There are examples of same-day or near-real-time availability for selected transactions, but no universal service level.
- Qualifying conditions, exclusions, geographic scope, and supporting evidence for the claim have not been defined.
- It has not been decided whether 'settlement' means processor settlement, availability in a Mosaic Relay-controlled workflow, or receipt in the merchant's external bank account.
- Business goal is to increase qualified leads among growing marketplaces.
- What does 'settlement' mean for this claim? — Not yet decided — this is part of what we need to determine
- Is the claim intended as a universal promise ('every merchant, always'), or is a qualified framing acceptable to marketing (e.g. 'up to', 'as fast as', 'for eligible merchants')? — Qualified framing acceptable — 'up to', 'as fast as', 'for eligible merchants'
- What actual timing data exists to substantiate any speed claim — what share of transactions meet the claimed speed, and over what measurement window? — We only have examples of same-day or near-real-time availability for selected transactions. We do not have aggregate timing data, a defined measurement window, a denominator, or a percentage of transactions meeting a stated speed. Treat the examples as supplied but unverified leads until Product and Operations produce dated, segmented records.
- What eligibility conditions and exclusions apply to any speed claim? Which merchants/payment methods qualify, and what are the exclusions (reserves, disputes, risk review, bank processing, merchant configuration)? — Eligibility conditions and exclusions are not yet defined. Timing depends on payment method, risk review, bank processing, reserves, disputes, and merchant configuration. We have not mapped qualifying merchant segments, excluded transaction types, or whether processor/payout controls differ by geography. Treat all conditions as open facts requiring Product, Operations, Processor, and Compliance confirmation.
- What geographic scope does the claim cover? — Not yet decided

## Assumptions

- U.S.-primary advertising scope (consistent with company profile); non-U.S. expansion subject to separate review.
- FTC substantiation standard (Section 5) applies to the advertising claims.
- The claim is treated as an express factual claim requiring substantiation rather than puffery.
- The 'instant settlement for every merchant' claim is not substantiable as written under any of the three possible meanings of 'settlement', because there is no universal service level and timing depends on many factors.
- With a qualified framing accepted, the remaining work is to pin down the specific speed claim, eligibility conditions, and supporting data.
- No speed claim can be substantiated until Product and Operations produce dated, segmented timing records.

## Issues and workstreams

- Absolute/universal claim ('every merchant', 'instant') cannot be substantiated given no universal service level and timing dependent on many factors — FTC Section 5 deception risk.
- Ambiguity of 'settlement' (processor settlement vs. Mosaic Relay-controlled availability vs. receipt in merchant's external bank account) materially changes what must be substantiated and disclosed.
- No defined qualifying conditions, exclusions, geographic scope, or supporting evidence for the claim.
- Channel differences: paid search ads have strict character limits and disclosure constraints vs. website and sales presentations.
- Even a qualified claim ('up to X') requires real measured data and clearly disclosed eligibility conditions to be substantiated.
- No aggregate timing data exists — no speed claim (instant, same-day, or 'up to X') can currently be substantiated.

## Open questions

- What does “settlement” mean for this claim — processor settlement, availability in a Mosaic Relay-controlled workflow, or receipt in the merchant’s external bank account?
- What aggregate timing data exists to substantiate any speed claim — what share of transactions meet the claimed speed, and over what measurement window?
- What eligibility conditions and exclusions apply to any speed claim — which merchants/payment methods qualify, and what are the exclusions (reserves, disputes, risk review, bank processing, merchant configuration)?
- What geographic scope does the claim cover?
- Can paid-search speed claims be qualified within platform character limits and disclosure constraints?

## Options or working recommendation

# Working recommendation — not a decision

Do not approve or publish “instant settlement for every merchant.” Use interim capability wording only. Hold paid-search speed claims. Require a defined settlement event, dated segmented timing data, eligibility/exclusion matrix, geographic scope, and channel-specific qualification before any numeric or “up to” statement. Any wording that implies money in an external bank account needs separate evidence at that end point. Re-review after Product, Operations, Processor, and Compliance provide the evidence.
