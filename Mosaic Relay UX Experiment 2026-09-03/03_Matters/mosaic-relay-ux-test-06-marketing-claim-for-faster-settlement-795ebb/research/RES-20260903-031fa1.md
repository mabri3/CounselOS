# What is the substantiation standard for 'up to' or 'as

## Question

What is the substantiation standard for 'up to' or 'as fast as' claims in financial/payment advertising?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay’s marketing team wants to run a campaign claiming “instant settlement for every merchant” across the website, sales decks, and paid search ads starting in four weeks. Product confirms some funds can be made available quickly, but timing depends on payment method, risk review, bank processing, reserves, disputes, and merchant configuration; there is no universal service level and no aggregate timing data. Counsel is involved because the claim is an unsubstantiated absolute performance claim in financial-services advertising, and the company must decide what qualified language, if any, can run on the four-week deadline.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay run any speed-of-settlement advertising claim in four weeks — and if so, what specific qualified wording (“up to,” “as fast as,” “for eligible merchants”), with what disclosures and channel treatment, is defensible given that no aggregate timing data, defined settlement event, eligibility matrix, or geographic scope currently exists?
## Open questions
- What does “settlement” mean for this claim — processor settlement, availability in a Mosaic Relay-controlled workflow, or receipt in the merchant’s external bank account? Each meaning requires different evidence and implies different disclosures.
- What dated, segmented timing data can Product and Operations produce before launch — what share of transactions reach what speed, over what measurement window, for which merchant segments and payment methods?
- Which eligibility conditions and exclusions (reserves, disputes, risk review, bank processing, merchant configuration, geography) will apply, and can they be expressed in plain language suitable for each channel?
- What geographic scope will the campaign cover, and does the claim hold in every jurisdiction where the ads will appear?
- Can paid search ads carry adequate qualification within their format constraints, or must speed claims be limited to website and sales materials where full disclosures can appear?

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.** The supplied public source material did not contain a substantiation standard for “up to” or “as fast as” claims; it contained only disclosure-format standards (clear-and-conspicuous, readily noticeable) from Regulation Z commentary, which govern how disclosures appear, not whether the underlying claim is substantiated. The analysis below is model-generated analysis, not retrieved authority.
**FTC Section 5 deception framework (assumed applicable).** An objective performance claim like “instant settlement for every merchant” is an express factual claim, not puffery. Under the FTC’s deception and substantiation doctrine, an advertiser must possess a reasonable basis — typically competent and reliable evidence — before disseminating the claim. For speed or performance claims, that generally means data showing the claimed result is achievable under normal circumstances by the population the claim addresses.
**“Up to” and “as fast as” claims.** FTC enforcement and guidance (e.g., the “Up to” claims enforcement policy and related cases) treat maximum-performance claims as deceptive unless the maximum is achievable by an appreciable number of consumers under normal circumstances. The claim must not imply that the maximum is typical. Disclosures must be clear and conspicuous and in close proximity to the claim. In paid search, character limits make adequate qualification difficult; the FTC has held that if a disclosure cannot be made effectively in a given medium, the claim should not run in that medium.
**Payment-industry overlay.** Because Mosaic Relay does not hold deposits and funds flow through processors and acquiring banks, any claim implying money in a merchant’s external bank account must be substantiated at that endpoint, not just at the point Mosaic Relay releases funds. Card network rules and processor agreements may also impose advertising or claim-approval requirements that have not been checked.
**State UDAP and NAD risk.** State unfair-and-deceptive-practices statutes and the National Advertising Division provide additional enforcement and challenge exposure for unsubstantiated performance claims.
## Viable paths
**Path A — Hold all speed claims (lowest risk).** Launch the campaign without any speed claim. Use capability language (“flexible payout controls,” “faster access to funds for eligible merchants”) that does not assert a specific timing result. This is achievable within four weeks with no additional data.
**Path B — Qualified claim with substantiation sprint (moderate risk, deadline-tight).** Product and Operations produce dated, segmented timing data within two weeks; Legal defines the settlement event, eligibility matrix, and geographic scope; Marketing drafts channel-specific qualified language with disclosures. Paid search likely cannot carry adequate qualification and would use non-speed language. This path is feasible only if data production is fast and clean.
**Path C — Run the claim as drafted (not viable).** “Instant settlement for every merchant” is an absolute, universal claim with no substantiation. It should not run in any channel.
## Facts that could change the answer
- If Product can produce data showing a high percentage of transactions achieve near-real-time availability under defined conditions, a qualified “up to” claim becomes substantiable for those conditions.
- If the settlement event is defined as availability within a Mosaic Relay-controlled workflow (not external bank receipt), the substantiation burden is narrower and more achievable.
- If the campaign is limited to the U.S. and to website/sales channels (dropping paid search), disclosure adequacy is easier to achieve.
- If processor or network agreements contain advertising pre-approval or claim-substantiation requirements, additional lead time is needed.

## What would change this
- **Working assumption — FTC Section 5 and the “up to” claims framework apply.** The supplied public source contained no substantiation standard; the analysis assumes the FTC deception/substantiation framework governs. If a different or additional regime applies (e.g., a specific state financial-advertising statute or a network rule with its own substantiation requirement), the analysis and required evidence could shift materially.
- **Open fork — Definition of “settlement.”** If “settlement” means availability in a Mosaic Relay-controlled workflow, substantiation is narrower and a qualified claim is more achievable. If it means receipt in the merchant’s external bank account, substantiation must cover the full funds flow including bank processing, which is harder and may not be achievable in four weeks. The answer changes substantially depending on which definition is chosen.
- **Open fork — Data availability before launch.** If Product and Operations can produce dated, segmented timing data within two weeks, Path B (qualified claim) is viable. If they cannot, Path A (no speed claim) is the only defensible option for the four-week launch. The answer flips entirely on this fact.
