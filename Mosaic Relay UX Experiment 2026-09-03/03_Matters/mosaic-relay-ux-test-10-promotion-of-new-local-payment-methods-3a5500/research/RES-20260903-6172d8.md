# Advertising and comparative-claim substantiation for a payments platform promoti

## Question

Advertising and comparative-claim substantiation for a payments platform promoting local payment methods in LATAM and Europe: FTC Section 5 substantiation principles, EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive and national advertising law, and LATAM consumer-protection/advertising rules; country-specific disclosure and pricing-transparency requirements (fees, FX, refunds, settlement); consumer-protection issues; and required approval/partner review with local processors, acquiring banks, and networks.

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay (Product, Marketing, Sales) plans a seven-week campaign promoting local payment methods in Latin America and Europe using three claims: “accept local payments everywhere,” “lower-cost payments,” and “fewer failed transactions.” The campaign targets merchants via country-specific landing pages and sales emails, with transactions routed through local processors and acquiring banks. Counsel is involved because the factual record shows no validated comparative evidence for the cost and performance claims, unknown FX/fee inclusions, and unconfirmed country-specific disclosure requirements across multiple jurisdictions. Launch is targeted for the start of the next regional sales cycle in seven weeks.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay launch the LATAM/EU campaign in seven weeks using qualified versions of the three proposed claims, or must it limit the campaign to factual availability statements for named countries and payment methods until Product and Finance produce a validated cost study, defined benchmark, and confirmed country-specific disclosure requirements?
## Open questions
- Which specific countries in LATAM and Europe are in scope, and what are the local advertising disclosure requirements for each (e.g., Brazil CDC, Mexico PROFECO, EU member-state implementations of the UCPD)?
- Will Finance confirm whether the fee figures include processor markups and foreign-exchange charges, and can they produce a like-for-like cost comparison against a defined baseline (e.g., card processing) within the seven-week window?
- Can Product validate the “fewer failed transactions” claim with data beyond the small internal pilot, or will they accept a qualified claim limited to the pilot’s scope and duration?
- Which local processors and acquiring banks require pre-launch review or approval of marketing materials under their network rules or contractual terms?
- Does “everywhere” in the current claim refer to geographic coverage, merchant eligibility, or consumer accessibility, and can Product live with a claim limited to specific named countries?

**Generated analysis (model-only):**
*Advertising substantiation and comparative claims*
- **FTC Section 5 principles** (as a benchmark for truth-in-advertising): Claims must be truthful, non-misleading, and substantiated before dissemination. “Lower-cost payments” is a comparative claim requiring a defined baseline (e.g., vs. card networks, vs. other local methods), a specified time period, and like-for-like fee treatment (including processor and FX costs). “Fewer failed transactions” is an objective performance claim requiring reliable comparative data; a small internal pilot is likely insufficient without qualification regarding sample size, duration, and conditions.
- **EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (2005/29/EC)**: Prohibits misleading actions and omissions. Comparative advertising is permitted only if it compares like-for-like goods/services, is objective, and does not create confusion. “Accept local payments everywhere” risks being misleading if “everywhere” implies universal availability within a country when eligibility is restricted by merchant type, transaction size, or consumer bank participation.
- **LATAM consumer protection regimes**: Brazil (CDC – Consumer Defense Code) and Mexico (Federal Consumer Protection Law) impose strict liability for misleading advertising and require clear, accurate information about fees, terms, and geographic limitations. Several LATAM jurisdictions require specific disclosures for financial services advertising, including total cost of credit or transaction fees in local currency.
*Pricing transparency*
- Whether fees include processor markups and FX charges is currently unknown (the internal record). Omitting material fees from advertised “lower-cost” claims likely constitutes a misleading omission under both EU and LATAM regimes, and violates FTC principles regarding clear and conspicuous disclosure of material terms.
*Consumer protection and funds flow*
- Local payment methods often have different settlement timing, refund rights, and chargeback procedures than card payments. Marketing that does not disclose material differences in consumer protections (e.g., no chargeback rights for certain bank transfer methods) may violate consumer-protection laws in both regions.
*Partner and network review*
- Local processors, acquiring banks, and payment networks typically impose contractual pre-approval requirements for marketing materials that mention their brands or services. Launching without required partner review risks breach of contract and network fines.
## Viable paths
**Path A: Qualified launch with narrowed claims (fastest, lower risk)**
Limit the campaign to factual statements about specific countries and methods confirmed live by Operations. Replace “everywhere” with “in [Country X, Y, Z].” Replace “lower-cost” with “competitive pricing” or “transparent fees” (if accurate) or omit cost claims pending the study. Replace “fewer failed transactions” with “designed to reduce failed transactions” or “high approval rates in pilot testing” (with clear pilot parameters disclosed). This path allows the seven-week launch but requires immediate confirmation of the exclusion list and country-specific limitations.
**Path B: Delayed launch with full substantiation (slower, higher confidence)**
Delay the campaign until Finance completes a validated cost study with defined benchmarks and Product obtains statistically significant approval-rate data across the target markets. This supports the original comparative claims but likely misses the seven-week window.
**Path C: Hybrid rollout (phased)**
Launch immediately in jurisdictions where Product can confirm permitted wording and disclosure requirements (likely starting with larger markets like Brazil, Mexico, Germany, France), using qualified claims. Expand to other markets as local counsel confirms wording and as substantiation data improves.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Confirmation of specific countries**: If the campaign targets only 2–3 countries with well-understood regimes (e.g., UK, Germany, Brazil), local review is manageable in seven weeks; if it targets 15+ jurisdictions, Path A or C is necessary.
- **Fee composition**: If Finance confirms that advertised fees exclude processor and FX charges, the “lower-cost” claim is likely misleading without prominent qualification, requiring a shift to Path A or B.
- **Pilot data robustness**: If the internal pilot covered thousands of transactions across multiple merchants and months, it may support a qualified performance claim; if it was a limited technical test, the claim must be dropped or heavily qualified.
- **Partner requirements**: If local processors require 4–6 weeks for marketing review, the seven-week timeline is at risk regardless of claim wording.
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Substantiation audit**: Product and Finance must produce the cost study methodology and pilot data parameters before any comparative claim is approved.
2. **Local counsel review**: Obtain confirmation of permitted wording and required disclosures for each target country (prioritize Brazil, Mexico, and the largest EU markets first).
3. **Partner clearance**: Submit final landing page and email copy to local processors and acquiring banks for contractual review before launch.
4. **Disclosure checklist**: Ensure each landing page identifies supported countries, eligibility restrictions, total fees (including FX), settlement timing, refund limitations, and any consumer-protection differences compared to card payments.
## What would change this
- **Working assumption**: That the campaign targets multiple jurisdictions with divergent advertising rules. If the campaign is limited to one country (e.g., Brazil only), the analysis simplifies significantly and local counsel review becomes the primary gate.
- **Open fork**: Whether Product can accept qualified claims (e.g., “available in select markets”) or insists on absolute claims (“everywhere”). If absolute claims are required, the launch must be delayed until substantiation is complete; if qualified claims are acceptable, the seven-week timeline is feasible.
- **Not examined**: The specific contractual terms with local processors and acquiring banks regarding marketing pre-approval. The vault does not contain these agreements, and they may impose mandatory review periods that affect the timeline.
- **Not examined**: Country-specific advertising standards for each target market in LATAM and Europe. The analysis assumes standard consumer-protection principles apply, but specific wording requirements (e.g., mandatory Spanish/Portuguese disclosures, specific font sizes for fees) were not verified.
- **Working assumption**: That the “small internal pilot” is insufficient for statistical validity. If the pilot actually covered a representative sample of transactions across the target markets, the “fewer failed transactions” claim may be supportable with qualification.
