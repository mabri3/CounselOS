# LATAM consumer-protection and advertising rules for payment-method marketing cla

## Question

LATAM consumer-protection and advertising rules for payment-method marketing claims (e.g., Mexico, Brazil, Colombia, Argentina)

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay’s Product team plans a seven-week marketing campaign promoting support for local payment methods in Latin America and Europe, using claims like “accept local payments everywhere,” “lower-cost payments,” and “fewer failed transactions.” The campaign targets merchants via country-specific landing pages and sales emails, with transactions routed through local processors. Counsel is involved because the claims lack substantiation, country-specific rules are unconfirmed, and fee/FX transparency is unresolved. The launch deadline is tight, and unsupported claims could trigger regulatory or partner pushback.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay proceed with the LATAM/EU campaign in seven weeks using the proposed claims, or must it narrow the scope, delay, or revise language to avoid regulatory or partner risk?
## Open questions
- Which specific countries and payment methods are confirmed as live and eligible for promotion?
- What evidence exists to substantiate “lower-cost” and “fewer failed transactions” claims, and can it be validated before launch?
- Do fee figures include processor and FX charges, and how will exclusions be described?
- What local disclosures or restrictions apply in each target country (e.g., Mexico, Brazil, Colombia, Argentina)?
- Are partner reviews (processors, acquirers, networks) required before launch?

### Likely rules/issues
- **Advertising and comparative-claim substantiation**: Claims like “lower-cost” and “fewer failed transactions” require robust, validated evidence. The small internal pilot is insufficient for broad claims.
- **Country-specific disclosures**: LATAM countries (e.g., Mexico, Brazil, Colombia, Argentina) have strict consumer-protection and advertising rules. Some payment methods may require local disclosures or face restrictions.
- **Pricing transparency**: Fee comparisons must clarify whether processor and FX charges are included. Omissions could mislead merchants.
- **Consumer protection**: Claims must not mislead consumers or merchants about costs, performance, or availability.
- **Partner review**: Local processors, acquirers, and networks may require pre-launch review of marketing materials.
### Viable paths
1. **Narrow the claims**: Limit “lower-cost” and “fewer failed transactions” to specific, substantiated countries/methods. Use qualifiers like “in select markets” or “based on internal pilot data.”
2. **Delay launch**: Postpone until comparative evidence is validated and country-specific rules are confirmed.
3. **Revise language**: Replace absolute claims with factual statements (e.g., “supports local payment methods in X countries”).
4. **Partner pre-clearance**: Submit campaign materials to local processors and acquirers for review before launch.
### Facts that could change the answer
- Confirmation of live countries/methods and their eligibility.
- Validation of cost and performance claims with a defined benchmark.
- Clarification on whether fees include processor and FX charges.
- Confirmation of local disclosure requirements or restrictions.
- Partner feedback on proposed claims.
### Recommended last-mile verification
- **Substantiation split**: Confirm with Product and Finance which claims can be substantiated today and which require further study.
- **Country-specific review**: Engage local counsel or compliance experts in Mexico, Brazil, Colombia, and Argentina to confirm permitted wording and disclosures.
- **Partner outreach**: Share draft campaign materials with local processors and acquirers for feedback.
- **Fee transparency**: Ensure fee comparisons clearly state whether processor and FX charges are included.
### What would change this
- **Working assumption**: The small internal pilot is the only evidence for approval-rate claims. If additional data exists, the “fewer failed transactions” claim may be supportable.
- **Open fork**: If local counsel confirms strict advertising rules in key markets (e.g., Brazil), the campaign may need to be delayed or significantly revised.
- **Not examined**: The specific terms of partner agreements (processors, acquirers) were not reviewed. These may impose additional restrictions on marketing claims.
- **Unwelcome item**: The campaign’s seven-week timeline may be unrealistic given the need for local counsel review and partner pre-clearance.
