# Card network and local processor rules on marketing payment methods

## Question

Card network and local processor rules on marketing payment methods and fee/approval-rate claims

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay (a fictional B2B payments platform) plans to launch a marketing campaign in seven weeks promoting support for local payment methods in Latin America and Europe. The campaign would use the claims “accept local payments everywhere,” “lower-cost payments,” and “fewer failed transactions” on country-specific landing pages and sales emails. Counsel is involved because the comparative cost and performance claims currently lack validated substantiation, the fee figures may not include processor and FX charges, and the specific countries, methods, and required local disclosures are not yet confirmed.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay launch the regional sales campaign in seven weeks using the three proposed claims, or must the campaign be limited to narrower, substantiated statements (and which specific claims, if any, can be approved today) given that no comparative cost study or benchmark exists and approval-rate data comes only from a small internal pilot?
## Open questions
- Which specific countries and payment methods will be live and supported at launch, and what are the exact exclusion lists for each market?
- Do the fee figures to be shown to merchants include all processor charges and foreign-exchange costs, or only Mosaic Relay’s fees?
- What validated benchmark (baseline, time period, countries, methods, like-for-like measures) will be used for the “lower-cost” and “fewer failed transactions” claims?
- Which local disclosures, wording restrictions, or consumer-protection rules apply in each target country in LATAM and Europe?
- What approval or review is required from local processors, acquiring banks, and card networks before the campaign can run?
## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.**
The analysis below is generated from the matter facts and general legal principles. It is not based on retrieved external authority.
- **Advertising and comparative-claim substantiation**: The claims “lower-cost payments” and “fewer failed transactions” are comparative performance claims. They require a reasonable basis consisting of competent and reliable evidence. The only support identified is a small internal pilot for approval rates and no validated cost study or benchmark. “Accept local payments everywhere” is an absolute claim that is factually incorrect unless Mosaic Relay supports every local payment method in every country, which the facts do not support.
- **Country-specific disclosures**: Both the EU (Unfair Commercial Practices Directive and national implementations) and LATAM countries have consumer-protection and advertising rules that prohibit misleading omissions and require material conditions to be disclosed clearly. Country-specific landing pages must accurately reflect availability, fees, settlement timing, refund behavior, and any local restrictions.
- **Pricing transparency**: If the fee comparison shown to merchants excludes processor or FX charges, the “lower-cost” claim is likely misleading. All material costs must be included or clearly disclosed.
- **Consumer-protection issues**: Merchants and consumers may rely on the claims when choosing payment methods. Inaccurate approval-rate or cost claims could constitute deceptive practices.
- **Partner and network review**: Local processors, acquiring banks, and card networks often have rules governing how their services and fees may be marketed. Approval or review may be required before launch.
## Viable paths
1. **Full launch with original claims** — Not viable on current facts. The three claims cannot be substantiated.
2. **Launch with narrowed, substantiated claims only** — Viable. Limit claims to factual statements about specific countries and methods that Operations confirms are live (e.g., “Now available: [Method] in [Country]”). Remove or heavily qualify “lower-cost” and “fewer failed transactions” until a proper study exists.
3. **Delay launch until substantiation is obtained** — Viable but likely inconsistent with the seven-week timeline unless a rapid study can be completed.
4. **Hybrid approach** — Launch with narrowed claims now and run a parallel workstream to build the required cost study and benchmark for future campaigns.
## Facts that could change the answer

- Confirmation that fee figures include all processor and FX charges.
- A confirmed list of live countries and methods with no material exclusions.
- Written confirmation from local counsel in each target country that the proposed wording complies with local advertising and consumer-protection rules.
- Written confirmation from relevant processors, banks, and networks that no additional approval is required or that approval has been obtained.
