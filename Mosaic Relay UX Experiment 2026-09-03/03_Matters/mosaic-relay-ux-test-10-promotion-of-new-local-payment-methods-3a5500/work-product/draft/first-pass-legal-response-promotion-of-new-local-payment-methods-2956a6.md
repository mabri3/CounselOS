# First-Pass Legal Response — Promotion of New Local Payment Methods (LATAM/Europe)

**Matter:** Mosaic Relay UX Test — 10 — Promotion of New Local Payment Methods
**Status:** Draft for review (first pass). Recommendations are separate from any recorded decision.
**Launch target:** Start of next regional sales campaign in seven weeks.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The campaign as proposed cannot launch as written. The three headline claims — "accept local payments everywhere," "lower-cost payments," and "fewer failed transactions" — are not substantiated on the current dossier, and the "everywhere" claim is affirmatively misleading given that support, fees, settlement, refunds, and consumer protections differ by country and method.

The viable path is a **narrower factual campaign** limited to named countries and methods that Operations confirms are live, with the three absolute claims removed or held pending a validated cost study and defined benchmark. Because the claims can be qualified with conditions/exclusions, legal can approve a qualified version of the factual claim today; the comparative cost and performance claims cannot be approved until the missing evidence exists.

This draft provides: (A) claim-by-claim substantiation analysis; (B) country-specific disclosure and pricing-transparency requirements for LATAM and Europe; (C) consumer-protection and partner/processor approval issues; (D) exact qualified campaign language legal can approve; and (E) assumptions, missing facts, and unverified leads.

---

## 2. Claim-by-claim advertising and comparative-claim substantiation

### Governing framework
- **U.S. (FTC Section 5):** Advertising claims must be truthful, non-misleading, and substantiated before dissemination. Express claims and implied claims both require competent and reliable evidence. Comparative claims require a defined, disclosed benchmark and like-for-like comparison.
- **EU (Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC, Art. 6–7):** Misleading commercial practices are prohibited; material information must be provided; comparative claims must be accurate and verifiable. National advertising law (e.g., UK CAP Code, national UCPD implementations) applies per market.
- **LATAM:** Consumer-protection and advertising regimes (e.g., Brazil CDC and CONAR, Mexico LFPC, Argentina Ley de Defensa del Consumidor, Chile Ley 19.496) prohibit misleading advertising and require substantiation of claims. Each market requires separate local review.

### The three claims

**Claim 1 — "Accept local payments everywhere"**
- **Status: NOT supportable as written.** "Everywhere" is an absolute, unqualified claim. Support, fees, settlement, refunds, and consumer protections differ by country and method; some methods require local disclosures or restrictions; and the exclusion list is not confirmed. As written, this is affirmatively misleading.
- **Approvable alternative (qualified, factual):** "Accept local payments in [named countries/methods]" — only for countries/methods Operations confirms are live. This is a factual claim that can be substantiated by confirming live support.

**Claim 2 — "Lower-cost payments"**
- **Status: NOT substantiated.** This is a comparative claim. There is no validated cost study, no defined benchmark, and it is unknown whether fee figures include processor and foreign-exchange charges. A small internal pilot does not substantiate a comparative cost claim.
- **Approvable alternative:** Only after a validated cost study with a defined benchmark (baseline, time period, countries, methods, like-for-like fee and FX measures). Until then, do not run this claim.

**Claim 3 — "Fewer failed transactions"**
- **Status: NOT substantiated.** This is a comparative performance claim. Approval-rate data is based only on a small internal pilot; no validated benchmark exists.
- **Approvable alternative:** Only after a validated approval-rate study with a defined benchmark. Until then, do not run this claim.

### Bottom line on substantiation
- **Approvable today:** Qualified factual claim limited to named live countries/methods.
- **Not approvable today:** "Everywhere," "lower-cost," "fewer failed transactions" — each requires the missing study/benchmark or removal.
- **Work item:** Product + Finance to confirm the claim-by-claim substantiation split and whether fee figures include processor/FX charges.

## 3. Country-specific disclosure and pricing-transparency requirements (LATAM and Europe)

Because support, fees, settlement timing, refund behavior, and consumer protections differ by method and country, the campaign must be country-specific. Landing pages and sales emails should identify, for each supported country and method:

- **Supported countries and methods** (only those Operations confirms are live);
- **Eligibility** (who can use each method);
- **Unavailable methods** (exclusions and country-specific limitations);
- **Fees and FX treatment** — whether published fees include processor and foreign-exchange charges; if not, disclose the additional charges clearly;
- **Settlement/refund limits** and timing;
- **Any material consumer-protection or processor conditions** (e.g., local disclosure requirements, cooling-off/refund rights, dispute handling).

### Europe
- **UCPD (2005/29/EC):** Requires that material information be provided and that claims not be misleading. Pricing must be transparent; the total price (including taxes and charges) must be disclosed. FX and processor charges must be clear.
- **Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU):** Applies to distance/off-premises contracts; requires pre-contractual information including total price and any additional charges.
- **Payment Services Directive (PSD2, 2015/2366/EU):** Governs payment services; requires transparency on charges, FX, and execution times. Relevant where Mosaic Relay or its processors provide payment services to merchants/consumers.
- **National advertising law:** Each EU market has its own UCPD implementation and advertising codes; permitted wording must be confirmed per jurisdiction.

### LATAM
- **Brazil:** CDC (Lei 8.078/90) prohibits misleading advertising; CONAR self-regulation requires substantiation. Pricing must be clear and total cost disclosed.
- **Mexico:** LFPC (Ley Federal de Protección al Consumidor) prohibits misleading advertising and requires substantiation; PROFECO enforcement.
- **Argentina:** Ley de Defensa del Consumidor (24.240) prohibits misleading advertising.
- **Chile:** Ley 19.496 (Ley del Consumidor) prohibits misleading advertising.
- Each market requires separate local review for permitted wording and disclosure specifics.

---

## 4. Consumer-protection and partner/processor approval issues

### Consumer protection
- **Misleading advertising risk** is the primary exposure across all markets. Absolute claims ("everywhere," "lower-cost," "fewer") that cannot be substantiated create the highest risk.
- **Pricing transparency:** If fees exclude processor/FX charges, the comparison is incomplete and potentially misleading. Confirm and disclose.
- **Refund and dispute handling:** Refund behavior and consumer protections differ by method/country; the campaign must not imply uniform refund rights.
- **Local disclosure requirements:** Some methods may require local disclosures or restrictions; confirm per jurisdiction.

### Partner/processor approval
- **Local processors, acquiring banks, and networks** may impose their own rules on how their brands, methods, and performance data are referenced in marketing. Confirm:
  - Whether each local processor/acquiring bank/network has approved the use of its name and any fee/approval-rate data in the campaign;
  - Any network/processor rules on comparative claims, fee disclosure, or brand usage;
  - Whether the approval-rate pilot data may be used at all (it is internal and small).
- **Approval gate:** Obtain written approval from each local processor/acquiring bank/network before the campaign references their method or data.

## 5. Exact qualified campaign language legal can approve

The following is the qualified language legal can approve **today**, subject to (i) Operations confirming the named countries/methods are live, and (ii) local review in each target market.

### Headline (qualified, factual)
> "Accept local payments in [Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Spain, Germany, France, Netherlands] — [list methods]."

### Supporting copy (factual, non-comparative)
> "Mosaic Relay supports [method] payments in [country]. Fees, settlement timing, refunds, and consumer protections vary by payment method and country. See the country-specific page for details on eligibility, fees (including any foreign-exchange charges), settlement, and refunds."

### Required disclosures (on every landing page and sales email)
> "Supported countries and methods: [list]. Not all payment methods are available in all countries. Fees shown are [inclusive/exclusive] of processor and foreign-exchange charges. Settlement and refund terms vary by method and country. [Any local consumer-protection or processor conditions.]"

### Not approvable (do not run)
- "Accept local payments everywhere"
- "Lower-cost payments"
- "Fewer failed transactions"

These may be reconsidered only after a validated cost study and defined benchmark are produced and reviewed.

## 6. Assumptions, missing facts, and unverified leads

### Working assumptions
1. **U.S. FTC Section 5 substantiation principles inform the analysis**, but the campaign markets are governed by their own regimes (EU UCPD and national advertising law; LATAM consumer-protection and advertising rules), so separate local review is required. If a market applies materially different substantiation standards, the approvable language may change.
2. **The three statements are express claims, not puffery.** If any is characterized as puffery in a given market, the substantiation burden may be lower — but this is not assumed for the headline claims.
3. **"Lower-cost" and "fewer failed transactions" are comparative claims** requiring a defined, disclosed benchmark. If they are instead treated as non-comparative, the evidence standard changes.

### Missing facts (blocking or material)
1. **Validated cost study and defined benchmark** for "lower-cost" and "fewer failed transactions" — required before those claims can run.
2. **Confirmed exclusion list and country-specific limitations** — required for the "everywhere" claim and for accurate disclosures.
3. **Permitted wording in each LATAM/EU jurisdiction** — requires separate local review per market.
4. **Which specific countries/methods Operations confirms are live** — required to scope the factual claim.
5. **Whether fee figures include processor and foreign-exchange charges** — required for pricing transparency.

### Unverified leads (to confirm)
1. Whether each local processor/acquiring bank/network has approved use of its name and data in the campaign.
2. Whether the internal approval-rate pilot may be used at all, and under what conditions.
3. Whether any specific payment method carries local disclosure or restriction requirements in a target market.
