      ## What you''re asking

      Mosaic Relay plans to replace its identity-verification flow with a vendor that

      want advice on privacy roles, notices/consent, biometric and cross-border requirements,
      data minimization, retention, vendor restrictions, and whether the pass/fail
      interface itself creates a legal problem.

      ## The central legal question

      This is a **privacy review**, not the FCRA consumer-report track from UX Test
      02. The single most decision-changing fact is **where the vendor processes the
      data**. That answer determines which biometric privacy statutes apply (Illinois
      BIPA, Texas, Washington, and others carry notice, consent, retention, and private-right-of-action
      duties) and whether cross-border transfer rules (e.g., GDPR) are triggered.
      The second pivotal fork is **who determines the purpose of processing** — that
      decides whether Mosaic Relay or the customer is the "business"/controller who
      owes the biometric notice and consent, and whether the vendor is a service provider/processor.

      I''ve saved the reported facts and flagged the key assumptions (U.S.-primary
      scope, the facial comparison produces a biometric identifier, and the vendor
      collects the biometric directly from the individual). To move this forward,
      I need answers in priority order:

      1. **Processing location** — Where will the vendor process the data? Determines
      which biometric statutes apply and whether cross-border obligations are triggered.

      2. **Purpose owner** — Who determines the purpose and means of processing, Mosaic
      Relay or the customer? Decides who is the "business"/controller and who owes
      biometric notice/consent.

      3. **Result reuse** — May customers reuse the pass/fail result for their own
      compliance programs? If so, the customer becomes a separate business/controller
      and the result may be a "sale"/"share" of biometric/sensitive information with
      opt-out implications.

      4. **Retention & model training** — Retention periods, deletion workflows, and
      whether the vendor may train models on the biometric data.

      5. **Minors & failed matches** — How these are handled in the flow.

      - **Working assumption — U.S.-primary scope.** If the vendor processes in the
      EU/U.K. or another non-U.S. location, GDPR and cross-border transfer obligations
      materially change the analysis.

      - **Open fork — who determines purpose.** If the customer determines the purpose,
      the customer is the "business" who owes biometric notice/consent and Mosaic
      Relay is a service provider; if Mosaic Relay determines it, Mosaic Relay owns
      those duties.

      - **Open fork — result reuse.** If customers reuse the result for their own
      compliance programs, the pass/fail result may be a "sale"/"share" of biometric
