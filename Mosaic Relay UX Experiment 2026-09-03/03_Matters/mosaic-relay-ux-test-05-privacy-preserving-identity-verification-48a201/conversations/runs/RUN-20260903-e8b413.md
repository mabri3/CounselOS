    ## The central legal question

    This is a **privacy review**, not the FCRA consumer-report track from UX Test
    02. The single most decision-changing fact is **where the vendor processes the
    data**. That answer determines which biometric privacy statutes apply (Illinois
    BIPA, Texas, Washington, and others carry notice, consent, retention, and private-right-of-action
    duties) and whether cross-border transfer rules (e.g., GDPR) are triggered. The
    second pivotal fork is **who determines the purpose of processing** — that decides
    whether Mosaic Relay or the customer is the "business"/controller who owes the
    biometric notice and consent, and whether the vendor is a service provider/processor.

    I''ve saved the reported facts and flagged the key assumptions (U.S.-primary scope,
    the facial comparison produces a biometric identifier, and the vendor collects
    the biometric directly from the individual). To move this forward, I need answers
    in priority order:

    1. **Processing location** — Where will the vendor process the data? Determines
    which biometric statutes apply and whether cross-border obligations are triggered.

    2. **Purpose owner** — Who determines the purpose and means of processing, Mosaic
    Relay or the customer? Decides who is the "business"/controller and who owes biometric
    notice/consent.

    3. **Result reuse** — May customers reuse the pass/fail result for their own compliance
    programs? If so, the customer becomes a separate business/controller and the result
    may be a "sale"/"share" of biometric/sensitive information with opt-out implications.

    4. **Retention & model training** — Retention periods, deletion workflows, and
    whether the vendor may train models on the biometric data.

    5. **Minors & failed matches** — How these are handled in the flow.

    **What would change this** (ranked):

    - **Working assumption — U.S.-primary scope.** If the vendor processes in the
    EU/U.K. or another non-U.S. location, GDPR and cross-border transfer obligations
    materially change the analysis.

    - **Open fork — who determines purpose.** If the customer determines the purpose,
    the customer is the "business" who owes biometric notice/consent and Mosaic Relay
    is a service provider; if Mosaic Relay determines it, Mosaic Relay owns those
    duties.

    - **Open fork — result reuse.** If customers reuse the result for their own compliance
    programs, the pass/fail result may be a "sale"/"share" of biometric or sensitive
    information, triggering CCPA/CPRA opt-out duties and making the customer a separate
    controller.

    - **Open fork — biometric statute exposure.** Whether the facial comparison produces
    a "biometric identifier" under Illinois BIPA (which has a private right of action
    and a 3-year retention limit) or only under lighter state regimes changes the
    consent and retention design.

    - **Not examined — the verification vendor''s contract and processing terms.**
    Whether the vendor is a service provider, whether it trains models on the data,
    and its sub-processor/cloud-storage arrangements are central to the vendor-restriction
    analysis, and no vendor agreement is in the vault.

    Please answer the questions above (starting with #1) and I''ll refine the analysis.'
