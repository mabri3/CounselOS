# # Orient to the request

## Business objective and timing

Mosaic Relay, a payment-infrastructure company that is not a bank and does not hold deposits, plans to replace identity verification before implementation next month. The proposed flow asks merchant applicants, beneficial owners, and payout recipients to provide an identification document and live selfie. The vendor returns only a pass/fail outcome to Mosaic Relay and the customer, while the vendor retains source images and Mosaic Relay keeps the result, audit metadata, and a limited record.

## Initial legal framing

The work must identify whether Mosaic Relay or each customer decides the purpose and essential means of processing. The vendor's role may be processor/service provider if it acts only on documented instructions, but independent use for model training or other purposes could change that analysis. Biometric information, government identifiers, and precise addresses create elevated privacy and security risk. Facial comparison may be biometric data and may trigger biometric-specific laws depending on location.

## Facts needed before launch

- Processing locations for Mosaic Relay, vendor, cloud storage, and customer access; transfer mechanism and onward transfers.

- Customer instructions and whether customers reuse results for their own compliance programs.

- Applicable user locations, including US states, Illinois, Texas, Washington, EU/EEA/UK, and other jurisdictions.

- Exact data fields, whether raw images or templates are returned, match threshold, human review, and whether any automated decision has legal or similarly significant effect.

- Notice, consent, disclosure, withdrawal, and alternative/manual-review flows.

- Retention periods, deletion triggers, deletion verification, backups, and litigation/regulatory holds.

- Vendor model training, secondary use, subcontractors, security controls, incident notice, audit rights, and data return/deletion.

- Handling of minors, failed matches, disputes, accessibility, fraud and bias testing, and customer-facing explanations.

- Implementation owner, launch gate, and whether a pilot can use synthetic/test data.

## Assumptions and missing facts

Assume Mosaic Relay wants a privacy-preserving verification service and will not sell or reuse biometric data. No jurisdiction, controller role, retention period, or training permission is confirmed. These assumptions must be labeled in the first-pass advice and validated before launch.

## Initial recommendation

Do not approve production implementation until roles, jurisdictions, notices/consent, retention/deletion, vendor restrictions, and failed-match safeguards are documented. A limited pilot may proceed only with synthetic data or a controlled jurisdictional scope and a written data map.
