# What are the Regulation E (EFTA) requirements for recurring consumer

## Question

What are the Regulation E (EFTA) requirements for recurring consumer ACH debits, including written authorization, copy of authorization, stop-payment rights, variable-amount notice, and error-resolution (Reg E §§ 1005.10 and 1005.11)?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
A Mosaic Relay merchant wants to accept recurring ACH debits from consumers for a subscription service, with checkout capturing bank details and email confirmation. Mosaic Relay would submit debits through payment partners, display status in its dashboard, and initiate refunds on cancellation. Counsel is involved because recurring consumer ACH debits trigger overlapping NACHA rules and Regulation E (EFTA) requirements for authorization, disclosure, stop-payment, and error resolution. The merchant targets launch in nine weeks (2026-11-05), but authorization language, evidence format, cancellation process, and variable-amount handling remain undecided.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay and the merchant structure the checkout, authorization flow, and back-office processes to satisfy Reg E written-authorization, copy, stop-payment, variable-amount notice, and error-resolution requirements—and NACHA standing-authorization and evidence-retention rules—before the 2026-11-05 launch, given that variable amounts, system-of-record designation, prenote practice, and unauthorized-debit claim routing are still open?
## Open questions
- **Variable vs. fixed amounts:** Will the merchant use fixed recurring amounts, variable amounts, or both? Variable amounts trigger a 10-day pre-debit notice obligation (or a consumer range election) under Reg E § 1005.10(d) and NACHA rules, which affects checkout design and notification infrastructure.
- **System of record for authorization evidence:** Will Mosaic Relay's dashboard be the system of record, and who retains and produces proof of authorization when the consumer's bank requests it? This determines architecture, immutability, exportability, and SLA commitments.
- **Prenotes vs. instant account validation:** Will the merchant use prenotification before the first debit, or rely on instant account verification? Prenotes add 3–6 days to first-debit timing and affect the instant-access UX for digital goods.
- **Unauthorized-debit claim routing:** Who responds to the consumer's financial institution when an R10 (unauthorized) return arrives, and who bears the loss? This affects contract terms, support workflows, and reserve/holdback policies.
- **Mosaic Relay's NACHA role:** Is Mosaic Relay a Third-Party Service Provider, a Third-Party Sender, or an ODFI agent? Third-Party Sender status triggers NACHA registration, risk-assessment, and warranty obligations that materially expand compliance scope.

## Likely rules and issues
### Regulation E (EFTA) — 12 C.F.R. §§ 1005.10, 1005.11
**§ 1005.10(b) — Preauthorized electronic fund transfers: written authorization and copy.**
- Recurring ACH debits from a consumer account are "preauthorized electronic fund transfers" under Reg E.
- The consumer must authorize the transfers **in writing** (electronic authorization satisfies this if it is "similarly authenticated" — clickwrap with audit trail is standard).
- The consumer must receive a **copy** of the authorization. The email confirmation can serve as the copy **only if it contains the full authorization terms** — amount or method of determination, frequency, timing, account details, revocation instructions — not merely a receipt or thank-you message.
- **Practical consequence:** the checkout must present the full recurring-debit terms before the consumer agrees, and the confirmation email must restate those terms in a retainable form.
**§ 1005.10(c) — Stop-payment rights.**
- The consumer has the right to stop payment of a preauthorized transfer by notifying their **financial institution** at least **3 business days** before the scheduled debit.
- The consumer can also **revoke authorization** by notifying the **merchant (payee)** directly; NACHA requires the authorization to specify how revocation occurs.
- **Practical consequence:** the checkout and email must disclose both paths — revocation to the merchant and stop-payment at the bank. The merchant and Mosaic Relay must honor revocations before the next debit when technically possible, and the system must not re-present debits returned R08 (payment stopped) or R07 (authorization revoked).
**§ 1005.10(d) — Variable-amount notice.**
- If the recurring debits **vary in amount**, the payee must send the consumer **written notice of the amount and date** at least **10 calendar days** before the scheduled debit, **unless** the consumer affirmatively elects to receive notice only when the amount falls outside a specified range or differs from the most recent debit by more than an agreed amount.
- **Practical consequence:** if the merchant will use variable amounts, the product must either (a) build a 10-day pre-debit notice engine, or (b) capture the consumer's range-based election in the authorization itself. Option (b) is the common UX-friendly path but requires precise drafting and a clear affirmative election at checkout.
**§ 1005.11 — Error resolution.**
- The consumer has **60 days** from the date the financial institution sends the periodic statement (or provides access to it) to report an unauthorized electronic fund transfer.
- The financial institution must investigate, provisionally credit the consumer within 10 business days if it cannot resolve within that window, and complete the investigation within 45 days (90 days for new accounts or POS/foreign-initiated transfers).
- **Practical consequence:** although the error-resolution obligation runs on the consumer's bank, the practical burden lands on the **merchant (Originator)** to produce proof of authorization to the bank (via the ODFI/RDFI) to defeat an "unauthorized" claim. Mosaic Relay's dashboard must support rapid evidence production if it is the system of record.
### NACHA Operating Rules (model-generated; not externally verified in this pass)
- **SEC code:** consumer entering bank credentials online = **WEB debit** (internet-initiated entry), even if recurring. WEB entries require (a) authorization obtained via the internet in a retainable/reproducible manner, (b) commercially reasonable fraud detection, (c) **account validation** before the first debit (prenotes, micro-deposits, or instant account verification), and (d) commercially reasonable website security.
- **Standing authorization:** NACHA permits a standing authorization for recurring debits. It must be clear, readily understandable, identify the recurring nature, frequency, and amount or method of determination, and be in writing and signed or similarly authenticated for consumer accounts.
- **Evidence retention:** Originator must retain the authorization (or an accurate record) for **two years after termination or revocation** and provide proof to the ODFI upon request (typically within 5–10 business days because RDFIs can request proof).
- **Variable-amount notice:** NACHA mirrors Reg E — 10-day advance notice of amount and date, or consumer election of a range-based notice.
- **Reinitiation limits:** NACHA caps reinitiation of returned debits (generally no more than two reinitiations, and none after R07/R08/R10 returns).
- **Return-rate monitoring:** unauthorized return rates above 0.5% trigger scrutiny; ODFIs enforce thresholds contractually.
### Consumer-protection overlay (ROSCA, FTC Negative Option Rule, state auto-renewal laws)
- **ROSCA** (Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act) applies to online negative-option/subscription plans: clear disclosure of material terms, express informed consent, and a **simple mechanism to stop recurring charges**.
- **FTC Negative Option Rule** ("click to cancel"): the 2024 rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in 2025, but ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws (e.g., California's ARL) remain operative. Monthly consumer subscriptions for digital goods sit squarely in ARL territory.
- **Practical consequence:** the merchant's cancellation path must be as easy as signup, and Mosaic Relay should require this contractually.

## Viable paths
### Path A — Fixed amounts only (simplest, fastest to launch)
- Merchant commits to fixed recurring amounts (e.g., $9.99/month).
- Checkout presents full recurring-debit terms (amount, frequency, timing, revocation instructions); consumer clicks to agree; email confirmation restates full terms.
- No 10-day variable-amount notice engine needed.
- Account validation via instant verification (fastest UX) or prenotes (adds 3–6 days to first debit).
- Dashboard is system of record; Mosaic Relay commits to 2-year retention and 5–10 business day evidence-production SLA.
- Cancellation via in-product button + support escalation; Mosaic Relay enforces stop before next debit when technically possible.
- **Timeline impact:** achievable in 9 weeks if authorization language and evidence format are finalized in weeks 1–2.
### Path B — Variable amounts with range-based election (UX-friendly, moderate complexity)
- Merchant uses variable amounts; checkout captures consumer's affirmative election to receive notice only when the amount exceeds a specified range or differs from the most recent debit by more than an agreed amount.
- Authorization language must precisely describe the range and the election.
- No per-debit 10-day notice required if the debit falls within the elected range.
- All other Path A requirements apply.
- **Timeline impact:** achievable in 9 weeks if the range-election language is finalized early and the checkout flow is tested for affirmative consent.
### Path C — Variable amounts with 10-day pre-debit notice (most complex, slowest)
- Merchant uses variable amounts without a range election; system sends written notice of amount and date ≥10 days before each debit.
- Requires a notification engine, consumer contact preferences, and fallback logic if notice bounces.
- **Timeline impact:** tight for 9 weeks unless the notification infrastructure already exists.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Variable vs. fixed amounts:** if the merchant commits to fixed amounts only, the 10-day notice obligation drops out and the launch path simplifies materially.
- **Mosaic Relay's NACHA role:** if Mosaic Relay is a Third-Party Sender (not just a TPSP/ODFI agent), it must register with NACHA, conduct risk assessments, and may warrant merchant conduct — a materially expanded compliance scope.
- **System of record:** if the merchant (not Mosaic Relay) retains authorization evidence, Mosaic Relay's dashboard obligations shrink, but the merchant must commit to retention and production SLAs.
- **Prenotes vs. instant validation:** prenotes add 3–6 days to first-debit timing, which may be unacceptable for digital-goods instant access; instant account verification is faster but requires vendor integration.
- **ODFI/processor agreement terms:** evidence-production SLAs, return-rate thresholds, and prohibited practices are often stricter than NACHA baseline; the actual contract terms control.
- **Business accounts:** if any subscribers are small businesses using business accounts, Reg E drops out for those debits and NACHA-only rules (and different return codes/timelines) apply.

## What would change this
- **Working assumption — Mosaic Relay's NACHA role:** I assumed Mosaic Relay acts as a Third-Party Service Provider or ODFI agent, not as the Originator of record or a Third-Party Sender with direct NACHA registration duties. If Mosaic Relay is the Originator or a Third-Party Sender, the warranty, registration, risk-assessment, and evidence-production sections expand materially, and the compliance timeline may extend beyond nine weeks.
- **Open fork — variable vs. fixed amounts:** if the merchant uses variable amounts, the product must either build a 10-day pre-debit notice engine or capture a precise range-based election at checkout. Fixed amounts eliminate this obligation and simplify the launch path. This decision gates the checkout design and notification infrastructure.
- **Open fork — system of record:** if Mosaic Relay's dashboard is the system of record, Mosaic Relay must guarantee immutability, exportability, 2-year retention, and a 5–10 business day evidence-production SLA — an architecture commitment, not just a policy. If the merchant retains evidence, Mosaic Relay's obligations shrink but the merchant must contractually commit to the same SLAs.
