# What are the Reg E (EFTA) requirements for consumer recurring

## Question

What are the Reg E (EFTA) requirements for consumer recurring ACH debits, including stop-payment and error-resolution rights?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payments technology provider, is assisting a merchant customer that wants to accept recurring ACH debits from consumers for a subscription service. The proposed checkout flow requires consumers to enter bank details and agree to recurring debit terms, with Mosaic Relay submitting debits through payment partners and initiating refunds upon cancellation. Counsel is involved because the feature implicates federal consumer financial protection laws (EFTA/Reg E) and NACHA operating rules, with a target launch date of November 5, 2026 (nine weeks from the request date).
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay and the merchant structure the recurring ACH debit authorization, cancellation, and error-resolution workflows to comply with Regulation E (12 C.F.R. § 1005.10) and NACHA Rules—including preauthorized transfer disclosures, stop-payment rights, and unauthorized-debit procedures—before the November 5, 2026 launch, and what specific consumer-facing language and backend controls are required to allocate liability correctly between the merchant, Mosaic Relay, and the consumer’s bank?
## Open questions
- **Variable vs. fixed amounts:** Will the merchant debit fixed monthly amounts or variable usage-based charges? This determines whether the authorization must specify a fixed amount or a range/schedule, and whether advance notice of varying amounts is required under Reg E.
- **System of record:** Will Mosaic Relay’s dashboard serve as the system of record for authorization evidence, and who retains the proof of authorization when a consumer’s bank requests it during a dispute?
- **Prenotification:** Will the merchant use prenotification (prenotes) before the first debit to verify account details, and if so, how will the three-day waiting period be built into the subscription activation flow?
- **Unauthorized-debit workflow:** Who is responsible for responding to Written Statements of Unauthorized Debit (WSUDs) and return reason codes R07/R10—the merchant, Mosaic Relay, or the payment processor?
- **Cancellation cutoffs:** What is the specific cutoff time for stopping the next debit (e.g., 3 business days before the scheduled date), and how will this be disclosed to consumers at signup?

## Likely rules and issues
**Regulation E (12 C.F.R. § 1005.10) — Preauthorized Electronic Fund Transfers**
- **Authorization requirement:** Recurring ACH debits from a consumer account must be authorized by the consumer in writing or "similarly authenticated" (electronic signature compliant with E-SIGN). The authorization must be readily identifiable as such and clearly authorize recurring transfers.
- **Copy requirement:** The consumer must receive a copy of the authorization (the proposed email confirmation satisfies this, but the authorization language itself must be presented before the consumer agrees).
- **Variable amounts:** If the amount varies, the consumer must receive notice of the amount and date at least 10 days before the debit, or the authorization must specify a range and the consumer must receive notice if the debit falls outside that range.
- **Stop-payment rights:** Consumers may stop payment of a preauthorized transfer by notifying their bank orally or in writing at least 3 business days before the scheduled date. The bank may require written confirmation within 14 days of oral notice. The merchant and Mosaic Relay must also provide a mechanism for the consumer to revoke authorization directly with the merchant (which should trigger a stop before the next debit).
- **Error resolution:** Consumers have 60 days from the bank statement date to report unauthorized debits or errors. The bank must investigate and provisionally credit within 10 business days (or 20 for new accounts). Mosaic Relay and the merchant must have procedures to handle return reason codes R07 (authorization revoked) and R10 (customer advises not authorized) and to produce evidence of authorization upon request.
**NACHA Operating Rules**
- **Authorization standards:** NACHA requires that authorizations for recurring ACH debits (SEC codes TEL, WEB, or PPD) be readily identifiable, clear, and readily understandable. For WEB debits (internet-initiated), the authorization must be obtained via a secure web session with commercially reasonable security procedures.
- **Record retention:** Originators (the merchant) must retain the original or a copy of the authorization for 2 years after termination or revocation. If Mosaic Relay acts as a Third-Party Service Provider, it must ensure the merchant retains this evidence and can produce it within 10 banking days upon request by the ODFI or RDFI.
- **Prenotification:** While optional, prenotes (zero-dollar entries) are strongly recommended to verify account numbers before the first live debit. If used, the originator must wait 3 banking days after the prenote before initiating the first debit.
- **Return rate monitoring:** NACHA monitors unauthorized return rates (R07, R10, R29). High return rates can trigger rules enforcement and fines. Mosaic Relay should monitor return rates and implement velocity checks.
**EFTA (15 U.S.C. § 1693)**
- The statute mirrors Reg E: preauthorized transfers must be authorized in writing, a copy provided to the consumer, and stop-payment rights honored with 3 business days’ notice.
## Viable paths
**Path A: Fixed-amount recurring debits (simplest)**
- **Structure:** Consumer authorizes a fixed monthly amount (e.g., $9.99/month). Authorization language specifies the exact amount, frequency, and start date.
- **Compliance:** Meets Reg E fixed-amount requirements. No 10-day advance notice needed for each debit (the initial authorization covers the schedule).
- **UX:** Checkout displays the exact amount and date; email confirmation reiterates the fixed schedule.
- **Risk:** Low. Returns likely limited to insufficient funds (R01) or closed accounts (R02), not authorization disputes.
**Path B: Variable-amount recurring debits (usage-based)**
- **Structure:** Consumer authorizes debits up to a maximum amount or within a range, with actual amounts varying based on usage.
- **Compliance:** Must either (1) obtain specific authorization for each debit (impractical for subscriptions), or (2) provide 10-day advance notice of the amount and date, or (3) authorize a range and notify only if the debit exceeds the range.
- **UX:** Checkout must disclose the range clearly; email confirmation must explain the 10-day notice process or range mechanism.
- **Risk:** Higher. Failure to provide proper notice for variable amounts violates Reg E and increases unauthorized-debit return risk.
**Path C: Hybrid with immediate cancellation**
- **Structure:** Fixed or variable amounts, but with a prominent "Cancel anytime" button that immediately stops future debits and triggers a refund for the current period if within a cooling-off period.
- **Compliance:** Must ensure the cancellation mechanism is as easy as the signup mechanism (FTC "Click to Cancel" rule, if applicable, and general EFTA revocation rights). Mosaic Relay must build a technical control to prevent submission of debits after cancellation is processed.
- **Risk:** Medium. Requires tight integration between merchant cancellation API and Mosaic Relay’s debit submission queue to avoid "zombie debits" after revocation.
## Facts that could change the answer
1. **Variable amounts confirmed:** If the merchant confirms variable amounts without a cap, the authorization language must include the 10-day advance notice mechanism or a specific range, significantly complicating the checkout flow.
2. **Mosaic Relay as system of record:** If Mosaic Relay’s dashboard is the system of record, it must build a 2-year retention and retrieval system for authorization evidence (IP logs, timestamps, signed terms) and be prepared to produce it within 10 banking days of a WSUD.
3. **Prenotification adopted:** If the merchant uses prenotes, the launch timeline must account for the 3-day waiting period between signup and first debit, affecting the "immediate access" UX for digital goods.
4. **Debit timing:** If debits are scheduled less than 3 business days after signup, the stop-payment right becomes operationally critical; Mosaic Relay must ensure its systems can intercept debits in the ACH network window.
5. **Digital goods immediate delivery:** If consumers receive digital goods immediately upon signup, the merchant faces higher risk of "friendly fraud" (consumer claims unauthorized after consuming content), requiring robust evidence of authorization and possibly immediate capture of IP/device data at signup.
## Recommended last-mile verification
Before launch, counsel should verify:
- **Authorization language:** Final checkout language explicitly states the amount (or range), frequency (monthly), duration (until cancelled), and the consumer’s right to cancel/stop payment, and is presented before the consumer enters bank details.
- **Evidence retention:** Mosaic Relay’s dashboard logs the authorization timestamp, IP address, device fingerprint, and the exact terms version agreed to, with a 2-year retention policy and API access for merchant retrieval during disputes.
- **Stop-payment workflow:** Test the cancellation flow to ensure that when a consumer cancels, the stop is transmitted to the payment processor before the next ACH file submission (typically requiring a 1–2 business day buffer before the debit date).
- **Return handling:** Confirm with the payment processor who receives and responds to R07/R10 returns and WSUDs, and establish a 10-day SLA for producing authorization evidence to the RDFI.
- **Reg E disclosures:** Ensure the email confirmation includes the mandatory Reg E disclosure: "You may stop payment of a preauthorized electronic fund transfer by notifying us orally or in writing at least three business days before the scheduled date of the transfer."

## What would change this
- **Working assumption:** The analysis assumes Mosaic Relay acts as a Third-Party Service Provider/ODFI agent and not as the Originator. If Mosaic Relay is the Originator (merchant of record), it assumes full NACHA warranty liability for authorization validity and must retain evidence directly, significantly increasing its compliance burden.
- **Open fork:** Whether the merchant uses fixed or variable amounts. If variable, the checkout must implement a 10-day advance notice system or range-based authorization, which may delay launch beyond nine weeks.
- **Not examined:** The specific NACHA Rules edition (2024 vs. 2025) and any recent amendments to Reg E or official interpretations were not retrieved from external sources. The analysis relies on the established framework of 12 C.F.R. § 1005.10 and standard NACHA Operating Rules.
- **Not examined:** State money transmitter laws or mini-EFTA statutes (e.g., New York, California) that might impose additional requirements on recurring debits or cancellation rights.
- **Not examined:** The merchant’s specific refund policy for digital goods (whether pro-rata or full-month refunds are offered upon cancellation), which affects the "refund to original payment method" requirement under Reg E § 1005.11.
