# What are the recordkeeping and customer notice obligations applicable to

## Question

What are the recordkeeping and customer notice obligations applicable to merchant onboarding and tiered verification in the U.S.?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a U.S. payments infrastructure provider, is proposing a tiered merchant onboarding flow to reduce onboarding time for small online businesses. Product wants to launch in six weeks before the holiday sales period. The proposed flow would collect business and owner information, run KYB, identity verification, sanctions screening, and risk scoring, and issue API credentials within minutes to low-risk applicants while routing higher-risk or incomplete applications to manual review. Counsel is involved because the tiered flow raises unresolved questions about KYB/KYC, sanctions, BSA/AML, recordkeeping, customer notices, transaction limits, and partner acceptance.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay launch the proposed tiered merchant onboarding flow in six weeks for U.S. small businesses, and if so, what onboarding checks, transaction limits, recordkeeping, and customer notices must be in place before launch to make the flow legally acceptable?
## Open questions
- Which payment methods and jurisdictions will the tiered onboarding flow cover, and can a single volume threshold apply uniformly across them?
- What is the proposed monthly volume threshold for the lower-verification tier, and what is the documented risk-based basis for setting it?
- Which beneficial-owner information will be collected, how often will it be refreshed, and what supporting documents will be required?
- Should a merchant be allowed to process limited transactions while their application is in manual review, and if so, under what caps, controls, and monitoring?
- Will acquiring partners and processors accept the tiered verification levels and lower threshold, and what are their contractual onboarding requirements?
## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.**
Based on the matter facts and general U.S. payments regulatory principles, the following rules and issues are likely material:
1. **BSA/AML and MSB status.** Mosaic Relay provides payment infrastructure and does not hold deposits or operate as a bank. Depending on its exact role in the funds flow, Mosaic Relay may be a money services business (MSB) under FinCEN rules, which would trigger AML program, recordkeeping, reporting, and independent testing requirements. If Mosaic Relay is acting as an agent of a bank or processor, the bank's BSA/AML obligations may flow down contractually. The MSB analysis is a threshold issue that has not been resolved.
2. **State money transmission licensing.** Mosaic Relay's activities may require state money transmitter licenses depending on the states in which it operates and the nature of its funds flow. The licensing map has not been examined.
3. **KYB/KYC and beneficial ownership.** FinCEN's Customer Due Diligence (CDD) rule requires covered financial institutions to identify and verify beneficial owners of legal entity customers. If Mosaic Relay is an MSB or acting as an agent of a covered institution, it may need to collect beneficial-owner information (ownership percentage, control person, supporting documents) and refresh it periodically. The specific data set and refresh cadence are unsettled.
4. **Sanctions screening.** OFAC sanctions screening is required before providing services. Screening must occur before API credentials or transaction access, with ongoing rescreening and escalation procedures.
5. **Card network and processor rules.** Card networks (Visa, Mastercard) and acquiring banks impose their own onboarding, underwriting, and monitoring requirements. These may include minimum KYB/KYC standards, volume thresholds, and transaction limits. Partner acceptance of the tiered flow is unconfirmed.
6. **Recordkeeping.** BSA/AML rules require retention of customer identification records, transaction records, and suspicious activity reports. The specific retention periods and record formats depend on Mosaic Relay's regulatory status and partner requirements.
7. **Customer notices.** Mosaic Relay should provide clear notices about data collection, screening, manual review, transaction limits, adverse outcomes, privacy use, and appeal/contact paths. The content and timing of these notices are not yet mapped.
8. **Tiering and transaction limits.** Lower verification for low-volume businesses must still satisfy baseline KYB/KYC, sanctions screening, and BSA/AML requirements. A single volume threshold may not be suitable across all payment methods (card vs ACH vs local) and jurisdictions. Pending-review processing is unresolved.
## Viable paths
1. **Gated pilot with full controls.** Launch the tiered flow as a gated pilot after completing the required controls: full KYB/KYC for business and owners, sanctions screening before credentials, documented risk-based threshold, partner acceptance, no pending-review processing (or strict caps and controls if approved), confirmed MSB/licensing status, and clear customer notices. This path allows the six-week launch if the controls can be evidenced in time.
2. **Delayed launch.** Delay the holiday launch until the unresolved questions are answered and the controls are fully implemented. This path reduces legal and operational risk but misses the holiday sales period.
3. **Phased launch.** Launch the tiered flow for a limited set of payment methods and jurisdictions where the threshold and controls are validated, and expand later. This path balances speed and risk but requires careful scoping.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **MSB status.** If Mosaic Relay is not an MSB and is acting purely as a technology provider to a bank or processor, the BSA/AML obligations may be contractual rather than direct. This could simplify the compliance burden but would still require partner acceptance.
- **Partner requirements.** If acquiring partners and processors impose stricter onboarding or underwriting standards than Mosaic Relay's proposed tiered flow, the flow may need to be adjusted or the launch delayed.
- **Payment methods and jurisdictions.** If the tiered flow covers only card payments in the U.S., a single volume threshold may be more defensible. If it covers ACH, local methods, or non-U.S. jurisdictions, the threshold and controls may need to be method- and jurisdiction-specific.
- **Pending-review processing.** If Product decides to allow limited transactions during manual review, the flow will need strict caps, monitoring, auto-stop triggers, and audit logging. If no transactions are allowed until review completes, the flow is simpler but may reduce conversion.
- **Beneficial-owner data set.** If the required beneficial-owner data set is minimal (e.g., only ownership percentage and control person), the onboarding flow can be faster. If supporting documents or enhanced due diligence are required, the flow will be slower.
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Confirm MSB and licensing status.** Engage counsel or a compliance consultant to confirm Mosaic Relay's MSB status and state money transmitter licensing requirements. This is a threshold issue that affects the entire compliance framework.
2. **Review partner contracts.** Review the acquiring partner and processor contracts to confirm their onboarding, underwriting, and monitoring requirements. Obtain written acceptance of the tiered flow.
3. **Document the risk-based threshold.** Work with Product and Risk to document a risk-based basis for the monthly volume threshold, including method- and jurisdiction-specific analysis.
4. **Define the beneficial-owner data set.** Work with Product and Compliance to define the required beneficial-owner data set, refresh cadence, and supporting documents.
5. **Decide pending-review processing.** Decide whether to allow limited transactions during manual review, and if so, define the caps, controls, monitoring, and audit logging.
6. **Draft customer notices.** Draft clear customer notices about data collection, screening, manual review, transaction limits, adverse outcomes, privacy use, and appeal/contact paths.
7. **Test the controls.** Test the KYB/KYC, sanctions screening, risk scoring, and manual review controls to ensure they work as intended before launch.
