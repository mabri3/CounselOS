# What are the card network and processor rules on merchant

## Question

What are the card network and processor rules on merchant onboarding, underwriting, and transaction limits that constrain tiered verification?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payments infrastructure provider, is designing a tiered merchant onboarding flow to reduce time-to-API-credentials for small online businesses. Product proposes collecting business details, running KYB/identity verification/sanctions screening, and issuing credentials within minutes for low-risk applicants while routing higher-risk or incomplete applications to manual review. Counsel is involved because the tiered structure must satisfy card network rules, processor requirements, and BSA/AML obligations before a six-week launch target ahead of the holiday sales period. The proposed monthly volume threshold for reduced verification remains unvalidated across payment methods and jurisdictions.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay launch a tiered merchant onboarding flow in six weeks that applies lower verification requirements to businesses below a proposed monthly volume threshold, and what transaction limits, beneficial-owner data collection, and customer notices must accompany that structure to satisfy card network rules, processor requirements, and BSA/AML obligations?
## Open questions
- What is the specific monthly volume threshold Product proposes for the lower-verification tier, and what risk-based justification supports it?
- Which payment methods (card, ACH, local) and jurisdictions will the tiered flow cover, and can a single threshold apply uniformly?
- What beneficial-owner information (ownership percentages, control-person details, supporting documents) will be collected, and what refresh cadence will apply?
- Will merchants be permitted to process limited transactions while manual review is pending, and if so, under what caps and controls?
- What specific underwriting and onboarding requirements do Mosaic Relay's acquiring partners and processors impose that may constrain tiering?

## Likely rules and issues
**Card network rules.** Visa and Mastercard impose merchant onboarding and underwriting obligations on acquiring banks and their sponsored payment facilitators or marketplaces. These rules typically require: (1) verification of merchant identity and business legitimacy before processing; (2) assignment of merchant category codes and risk ratings; (3) monitoring for transaction laundering, excessive chargebacks, and prohibited business types; and (4) registration of sponsored merchants with the networks. The networks do not generally prescribe a specific dollar threshold for reduced due diligence, but they require acquirers to maintain risk-based underwriting standards and to ensure that third-party agents (like Mosaic Relay) apply controls consistent with the acquirer's obligations. A tiered flow that delays full verification until after credentials are issued may conflict with network rules requiring pre-transaction underwriting.
**Processor and acquiring bank requirements.** Processors and acquiring banks typically impose contractual onboarding requirements that mirror or exceed network minimums. These often include: mandatory KYB with beneficial-owner identification for all merchants regardless of volume; sanctions screening before any transaction processing; and prohibitions on processing while underwriting is incomplete. Some processors offer "instant onboarding" products, but these typically rely on pre-approved merchant categories, strict transaction caps, and immediate suspension triggers rather than reduced verification. The proposed flow's legality depends heavily on whether Mosaic Relay's partners permit deferred verification for low-volume merchants.
**BSA/AML baseline.** Even if card network rules permit tiering, BSA/AML requirements apply independently. Mosaic Relay's status as a non-bank payment infrastructure provider does not exempt it from AML obligations if it qualifies as a money services business (MSB) or is acting as an agent of an MSB. FinCEN's customer due diligence (CDD) rule requires covered financial institutions to identify and verify beneficial owners of legal entity customers. If Mosaic Relay is an MSB or agent, it must apply CDD to all merchant customers, with no volume-based exemption. The proposed lower-verification tier may violate BSA/AML if it delays beneficial-owner identification or sanctions screening.
**Transaction limits during pending review.** Allowing merchants to process limited transactions while manual review is pending creates risk under both network rules and BSA/AML. Networks generally prohibit processing before underwriting is complete. BSA/AML requires customer identification before establishing a relationship. A "limited processing" allowance would need to be structured as a pre-relationship pilot with strict caps, no settlement of funds, and immediate termination triggers—features that may be operationally impractical within six weeks.
**Recordkeeping and notices.** Card networks require retention of merchant onboarding records, underwriting decisions, and monitoring logs. BSA/AML requires retention of CDD records, including beneficial-owner identification, for five years after account closure. Customer notices may be required under state money transmission laws, the Electronic Fund Transfer Act (if ACH is involved), and privacy laws if consumer data is collected. The tiered flow must generate auditable records showing which verification tier was applied and why.
## Viable paths
**Path A: Delay launch to validate threshold and partner requirements.** Postpone the six-week launch until Product confirms the volume threshold, obtains written confirmation from acquiring partners and processors that tiered verification is acceptable, and completes a BSA/AML risk assessment. This path is lowest risk but likely misses the holiday sales period.
**Path B: Launch with uniform baseline verification and no reduced tier.** Apply full KYB, beneficial-owner identification, and sanctions screening to all merchants regardless of volume, but automate the process to achieve near-instant credentialing for low-risk applicants. This satisfies network and BSA/AML requirements while still reducing onboarding time. Transaction limits can be imposed post-onboarding based on risk scoring rather than pre-onboarding verification tiers.
**Path C: Launch with tiered verification only for specific payment methods or jurisdictions.** If certain payment methods (e.g., card only) or jurisdictions have clearer regulatory treatment or partner acceptance, launch the tiered flow for those only, with uniform verification for others. This requires confirming which methods and jurisdictions are in scope.
**Path D: Pilot with strict transaction caps and no settlement.** Allow limited processing during manual review only if structured as a pilot with hard transaction caps, no settlement of funds to the merchant, and immediate suspension if review is not completed within a short window. This path requires explicit partner approval and robust controls.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **The specific volume threshold.** A threshold of $500/month may be defensible; $50,000/month likely is not. The risk-based justification must be documented.
- **Payment methods in scope.** ACH transactions may implicate NACHA rules and EFTA; local payment methods may have different regulatory treatment.
- **Jurisdictions in scope.** State money transmission laws vary; some states may prohibit tiered verification for licensed activities.
- **Partner requirements.** If acquiring partners explicitly prohibit deferred verification, Path A or B is required. If they offer instant onboarding products, Path C or D may be viable.
- **Mosaic Relay's MSB status.** If Mosaic Relay is not an MSB or agent, BSA/AML CDD requirements may not apply directly, but network and processor rules still constrain tiering.
- **Beneficial-owner data set.** If Product can collect required beneficial-owner information upfront (even for low-tier merchants), the tiered structure may be simplified.
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Obtain written confirmation from acquiring partners and processors** that the proposed tiered verification structure, including any deferred verification for low-volume merchants, is permissible under their underwriting standards and network rules.
2. **Validate the volume threshold** with a documented risk assessment that considers transaction laundering risk, chargeback exposure, and BSA/AML obligations. Confirm the threshold is appropriate for each payment method and jurisdiction in scope.
3. **Confirm Mosaic Relay's MSB status** and whether FinCEN's CDD rule applies directly or through partner obligations. If applicable, ensure beneficial-owner identification is collected for all merchants before any processing.
4. **Map recordkeeping requirements** under card network rules, BSA/AML, and state law, and ensure the tiered flow generates auditable records of verification tier assignment and underwriting decisions.
5. **Draft customer notices** that disclose verification tiers, transaction limits, and any restrictions on processing during pending review, consistent with state law and network requirements.
