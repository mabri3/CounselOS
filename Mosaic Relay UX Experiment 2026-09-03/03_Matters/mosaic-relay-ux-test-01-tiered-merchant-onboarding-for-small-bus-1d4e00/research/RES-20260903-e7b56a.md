# What are the baseline KYB/KYC, sanctions screening, and BSA/AML onboarding

## Question

What are the baseline KYB/KYC, sanctions screening, and BSA/AML onboarding requirements for a payments infrastructure provider in the U.S.?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payments infrastructure provider, is designing a tiered merchant onboarding flow for small online businesses to reduce onboarding time. The proposed flow would collect business details, run KYB, identity verification, sanctions screening, and risk scoring, and issue API credentials within minutes to low-risk applicants while routing higher-risk or incomplete applications to manual review. Counsel is involved because the tiered verification approach, undefined beneficial-owner requirements, and potential for processing during pending review implicate KYB/KYC, sanctions, and BSA/AML obligations. The target launch is in six weeks before the holiday sales period.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay launch a tiered merchant onboarding flow in six weeks that applies lower verification requirements to businesses below a proposed monthly volume threshold, and if so, what baseline KYB/KYC, sanctions screening, BSA/AML controls, beneficial-owner data collection, and transaction limits must be in place to make the flow legally acceptable across card, ACH, and local payment methods in the United States?
## Open questions
- Which specific payment methods (card, ACH, local) and U.S. jurisdictions will the tiered flow cover at launch, and can a single volume threshold apply uniformly across them?
- What is the proposed monthly volume threshold for the lower-verification tier, and what is the documented risk-based basis for setting it?
- Which beneficial-owner information will be collected (ownership percentages, control-person details, supporting documents), and what refresh cadence or trigger events will apply?
- Will merchants be permitted to process limited transactions while their application is in manual review, and if so, under what limits and controls?
- Will acquiring partners and payment processors accept the tiered verification levels and lower threshold, or do their underwriting requirements impose additional conditions?
## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.**
Based on the matter facts and general regulatory framework for U.S. payments infrastructure providers, the following rules and issues are likely material:
**KYB/KYC baseline requirements.** Mosaic Relay's proposal to collect legal name, registration details, owners, expected volume, countries, and payment methods aligns with standard KYB data elements. However, the proposal does not specify whether it will collect beneficial-owner information at the 25% ownership threshold and control-person details typically required under FinCEN's Customer Due Diligence (CDD) Rule (31 C.F.R. § 1010.230) for covered financial institutions. Although Mosaic Relay states it does not hold deposits or operate as a bank, its acquiring partners and payment processors are likely subject to CDD requirements and may contractually require Mosaic Relay to collect equivalent information. The lower-verification tier must still satisfy baseline identity verification and cannot skip beneficial-owner collection entirely if partners require it.
**Sanctions screening.** OFAC sanctions screening is not tiered or volume-dependent. All merchants, regardless of risk score or transaction volume, must be screened against the SDN List and other applicable sanctions lists before onboarding and on an ongoing basis. The proposal includes sanctions screening, which is correct, but the flow must ensure screening occurs before API credentials are issued, not after.
**BSA/AML program requirements.** If Mosaic Relay is classified as a money services business (MSB) under FinCEN regulations (31 C.F.R. § 1010.100(ff)), it must maintain a written AML program, designate a compliance officer, provide training, and conduct independent testing. MSBs must also file Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) and Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs) when applicable. The tiered flow does not eliminate these obligations. Lower verification for low-volume merchants is permissible only if the overall AML program remains risk-based and effective. The proposed volume threshold must be documented with a risk-based rationale, and Mosaic Relay must monitor whether low-volume merchants are being used to structure transactions or evade controls.
**Tiering and transaction limits.** Tiering based on transaction volume is a common risk-based approach, but the threshold must be calibrated to the specific risks of each payment method. Card transactions, ACH, and local payment methods have different fraud, chargeback, and money-laundering risk profiles. A single volume threshold may not be suitable across all methods. Additionally, allowing merchants to process limited transactions while manual review is pending creates risk: if the merchant is later found to be high-risk or sanctioned, Mosaic Relay may have facilitated prohibited transactions. If limited processing is allowed, it should be subject to strict transaction caps, enhanced monitoring, and immediate suspension if red flags emerge.
**Recordkeeping.** BSA recordkeeping requirements (31 U.S.C. § 5318(h), 31 C.F.R. § 1010.410) require retention of customer identification records, transaction records, and SARs for five years. The tiered flow must ensure that all collected data, screening results, risk scores, and decisions are logged and retained. If lower-tier merchants are later upgraded or if their risk profile changes, the system must capture the refresh of beneficial-owner data and any additional verification performed.
**Customer notices.** Mosaic Relay should provide clear notices to merchants about what information is collected, how it will be used, and what the tiered verification process entails. If a merchant is placed in manual review or if transaction limits are imposed, the merchant should receive timely notice. Privacy notices must comply with applicable state privacy laws (e.g., CCPA if California residents are involved).
**Partner and processor requirements.** Acquiring banks and payment processors impose their own underwriting and onboarding requirements, which may be more stringent than Mosaic Relay's proposed tiered flow. Mosaic Relay must confirm that its partners will accept the lower-verification tier and the proposed volume threshold. If partners require full KYB and beneficial-owner data for all merchants regardless of volume, the tiered flow may not be viable as proposed.
## Viable paths
**Path 1: Launch with full KYB and beneficial-owner collection for all merchants, but automate and streamline the process.** Mosaic Relay can reduce onboarding time by automating data collection, identity verification, and sanctions screening, and by using risk scoring to fast-track low-risk applicants. This path avoids the legal risk of tiering verification requirements and ensures compliance with partner and processor expectations. The trade-off is that onboarding may take longer than a few minutes for some applicants, but it is the lowest-risk path.
**Path 2: Launch a tiered flow with a documented risk-based volume threshold, but only for card payments, and only after confirming partner acceptance.** If Mosaic Relay can document a risk-based rationale for the volume threshold and confirm that acquiring partners and processors will accept lower verification for low-volume card transactions, it may proceed with a tiered flow limited to card payments. ACH and local payment methods, which have different risk profiles, would require full verification. This path requires partner buy-in and robust monitoring to detect structuring or abuse.
**Path 3: Delay launch until beneficial-owner data collection, refresh cadence, and transaction limits during pending review are settled.** If the six-week timeline does not allow sufficient time to resolve the open questions and confirm partner requirements, Mosaic Relay should delay launch. Launching with unresolved beneficial-owner requirements or unclear transaction limits creates regulatory and operational risk that outweighs the benefit of launching before the holiday sales period.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Partner and processor requirements.** If acquiring partners or payment processors require full KYB and beneficial-owner data for all merchants regardless of volume, the tiered flow is not viable as proposed.
- **Volume threshold and risk basis.** If the proposed volume threshold is set too high or lacks a documented risk-based rationale, regulators or partners may view the tiering as inadequate.
- **Payment methods in scope.** If the tiered flow is limited to card payments only, a single volume threshold may be more defensible. If it includes ACH or local payment methods, separate thresholds or full verification may be required.
- **Beneficial-owner data set.** If Mosaic Relay collects only minimal beneficial-owner data (e.g., name and ownership percentage) without control-person details or supporting documents, it may not satisfy partner or regulatory expectations.
- **Transaction processing during pending review.** If merchants are allowed to process transactions while in manual review, Mosaic Relay must implement strict caps and monitoring. If this is not feasible, the flow should not permit processing until review is complete.
## Recommended last-mile verification
Before launch, Mosaic Relay should:
1. **Confirm partner and processor requirements.** Obtain written confirmation from acquiring banks and payment processors that they will accept the tiered verification levels and the proposed volume threshold. If partners require full KYB for all merchants, adjust the flow accordingly.
2. **Document the risk-based basis for the volume threshold.** Prepare a written risk assessment that explains how the volume threshold was set, why it is appropriate for each payment method, and how Mosaic Relay will monitor for structuring or abuse.
3. **Define the beneficial-owner data set and refresh cadence.** Specify which beneficial-owner information will be collected (e.g., name, date of birth, address, ownership percentage, control-person details) and how often it will be refreshed (e.g., annually, on trigger events such as ownership changes or risk score increases).
4. **Set transaction limits for pending review.** If merchants may process limited transactions while in manual review, define the transaction caps, monitoring controls, and suspension triggers. Ensure that sanctions screening is completed before any transactions are processed.
5. **Implement recordkeeping and audit trails.** Ensure that all onboarding data, screening results, risk scores, and decisions are logged and retained for at least five years. Implement audit trails to track when beneficial-owner data is refreshed or when merchants are upgraded to higher tiers.
6. **Draft customer notices.** Prepare clear notices that explain the tiered verification process, what information is collected, how it will be used, and what merchants can expect if they are placed in manual review or subject to transaction limits.
## What would change this
- **Working assumption:** Mosaic Relay is classified as a money services business (MSB) under FinCEN regulations and is subject to BSA/AML program requirements. If Mosaic Relay is not an MSB (e.g., because it qualifies for an exemption or operates solely as an agent of another MSB), the BSA/AML analysis would change, though partner and processor requirements would still apply.
- **Open fork:** Whether acquiring partners and payment processors will accept the tiered verification levels and lower volume threshold. If partners require full KYB for all merchants, the tiered flow is not viable. If partners accept tiering, Mosaic Relay can proceed with Path 2, subject to documented risk-based controls.
- **Not examined:** The specific contractual requirements of Mosaic Relay's acquiring partners and payment processors. The analysis assumes partners impose their own onboarding and underwriting requirements, but the exact terms have not been reviewed. If partner contracts are more or less stringent than assumed, the viable paths and recommended controls would change.
- **Not examined:** State money transmission licensing requirements. The analysis focuses on federal BSA/AML and sanctions obligations. If Mosaic Relay operates in states that impose additional licensing or onboarding requirements for money transmitters, those requirements must be mapped and satisfied before launch.
- **Working assumption:** The proposed volume threshold will be set at a level that is defensible under a risk-based approach and will be monitored for structuring or abuse. If the threshold is set arbitrarily or without a documented risk basis, regulators or partners may view the tiering as inadequate, and the flow would need to be redesigned.
