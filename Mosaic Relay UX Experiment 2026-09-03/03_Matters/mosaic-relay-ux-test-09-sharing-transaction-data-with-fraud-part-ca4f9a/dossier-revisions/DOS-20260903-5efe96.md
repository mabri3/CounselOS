# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

U.S. state comprehensive privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA, VCDPA, CPA), FTC Act Section 5, controller/processor and third-party sharing standards, de-identification/pseudonymization standards, and fraud-prevention purpose/exemption analysis.

## Decision question

Advise on Mosaic Relay's proposed consortium service with fraud and identity vendors: permissible data sharing, notices and consent, de-identification standards, contractual controls, cross-border transfers, retention, consumer rights, and whether the merchant opt-in design is sufficient. Business goal is improved fraud detection before the next major product release; timing is continue-with-assumptions.

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay proposes a consortium service with fraud and identity vendors to improve fraud detection before the next major product release.
- Participating merchants would allow Mosaic Relay to share selected transaction, device, identity, and dispute signals with vendors, who would return risk indicators to improve approval and fraud detection for other participants.
- The proposed merchant dashboard would include an opt-in setting and a general explanation.
- Consumer data would be pseudonymized where possible.
- Shared data may include device identifiers, IP addresses, payment tokens, names, addresses, dispute outcomes, and identity-verification results.
- Actors include Mosaic Relay, merchants, consumers, fraud and identity vendors, processors, acquiring banks, and cloud providers.
- The lawful purpose for every data field has not been identified.
- Whether merchants will be required to participate has not been determined.
- Whether vendors may combine shared data with their own datasets has not been determined.
- How long shared signals will remain available has not been settled.
- Regional hosting, consumer access and deletion handling, vendor model training, and whether the service changes Mosaic Relay's role under privacy law have not been settled.
- Will merchant participation in the consortium be required (mandatory as a condition of using Mosaic Relay) or opt-in? — Not yet decided
- May vendors combine the shared data with their own datasets, or train models on it? — Not yet decided
- For the shared data, does Mosaic Relay act as a controller (determining purposes/means) or a processor (acting on merchants' instructions)? — Mixed / not yet determined
- How long should shared signals remain available to vendors? — Not yet decided
- Will consumers receive any notice or choice about their data being shared with the consortium? — Not yet decided
- Where will the shared data be hosted or transferred? — Not yet decided
- When does the business need the legal answer? — Continue with assumptions

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay operates primarily in the United States; cross-border analysis assumes U.S. law unless regional hosting is specified.
- The consortium is a new product change distinct from Mosaic Relay's existing fraud monitoring and identity verification services.
- Business timing: continue with assumptions (no hard deadline).

## Issues and workstreams

- Whether the merchant opt-in design is sufficient given consumers are the data subjects whose data is shared
- Whether de-identification/pseudonymization is achievable for device identifiers, IP addresses, and payment tokens
- Whether sharing with vendors changes Mosaic Relay's controller/processor role
- Whether vendors' use of data (combining, model training) is contractually constrained
- Cross-border transfer and regional hosting implications
- Retention period for shared signals
- Consumer access and deletion handling

## Open questions

- Mosaic Relay's controller vs processor role for the shared data
- Retention period for shared signals
