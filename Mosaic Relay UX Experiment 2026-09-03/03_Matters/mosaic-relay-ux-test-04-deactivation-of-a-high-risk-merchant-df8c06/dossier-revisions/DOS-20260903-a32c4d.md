# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay's risk operations team wants to deactivate a merchant following repeated sanctions-screening alerts, rapid volume growth, and transactions involving a newly identified high-risk country. The proposed action would immediately block new payments and payouts while preserving records, with a final decision on termination or reinstatement to follow within ten business days. Counsel is involved because the merchant has pending customer refunds and seller payouts, and the company must determine whether it can lawfully freeze these funds, what it must tell the merchant, and whether BSA/AML obligations (including potential SAR filing) restrict those communications. The unresolved alert is not yet confirmed as a true OFAC match, making this a discretionary risk decision rather than a mandatory blocking obligation.

## Decision question

Can Mosaic Relay immediately suspend the merchant and freeze pending customer refunds and seller payouts given one unresolved sanctions alert and high-risk country exposure, and if so, what specific communications about the suspension are permitted under BSA/AML confidentiality rules before the ten-business-day deadline for a final terminate-or-reinstate decision?

## Material facts

- Risk operations recommends deactivating a Mosaic Relay merchant after repeated sanctions-screening alerts, rapid volume growth, and transactions involving a newly identified high-risk country.
- The proposed operation immediately blocks new payments and payouts, preserves relevant records, and sends the merchant a short notice stating access is suspended under the agreement.
- A later review would decide whether to terminate the relationship or restore service.
- Two sanctions-screening alerts were false positives; one alert remains unresolved.
- The merchant has pending customer refunds and seller payouts.

- It has not been confirmed whether the contract addresses pending funds, notice timing, or post-termination access.

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay operates primarily in the U.S. and is subject to U.S. BSA/AML, OFAC sanctions, and payments regulation (per company profile).
- Mosaic Relay is classified as a money services business (MSB) subject to BSA/AML program and SAR obligations (per prior matter analysis, unverified).
- The merchant agreement is the primary contractual basis for suspension; its specific terms on pending funds, notice, and post-termination access are unverified.
- Because the unresolved alert is not yet confirmed as a true OFAC SDN match, immediate suspension is currently a discretionary risk-based action rather than a mandatory blocking obligation.

## Issues and workstreams

- Whether immediate suspension is justified given one unresolved sanctions alert and transactions involving a high-risk country
- What communications to the merchant are permitted, including whether SAR confidentiality limits disclosure
- How to handle pending customer refunds and seller payouts during suspension
- What records must be preserved
- What process should govern reinstatement or termination

## Open questions

- **SAR status**: Has a suspicious activity report been filed, or is one actively being considered, for this merchant? This controls whether Mosaic Relay may disclose the suspension rationale or must remain silent to avoid tipping off the subject.
- **Nature of the unresolved alert**: Is the remaining sanctions alert a true OFAC SDN match (which would trigger mandatory blocking and prohibit dealing with the merchant) or a false positive? The answer determines whether suspension is legally required or merely risk-based.
- **Contract terms**: What specific authority does the merchant agreement provide regarding holding pending funds, notice timing, and post-termination access? The general right to suspend is confirmed, but the mechanics of fund handling are not.
- **Pending transaction details**: What are the amounts, aging, and time-sensitivity of the pending customer refunds (potential consumer obligations) and seller payouts (third-party funds)? Are any refunds subject to statutory deadlines?
- **High-risk country status**: Is the newly identified country subject to comprehensive OFAC sanctions (e.g., Iran, North Korea, Cuba, Syria, Crimea region) or merely high-risk for AML purposes? This affects whether transactions must be blocked or can be processed with enhanced due diligence.
