# What are the SAR confidentiality and anti-tipping restrictions under 31

## Question

What are the SAR confidentiality and anti-tipping restrictions under 31 U.S.C. 5318(g)(2) on disclosing a suspension to a merchant?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay’s risk operations team wants to deactivate a merchant after repeated sanctions-screening alerts (two false positives, one unresolved), rapid volume growth, and transactions involving a newly identified high-risk country. The proposed action would immediately block new payments and payouts, preserve records, and send the merchant a short suspension notice, with a later review deciding termination or reinstatement. The merchant has pending customer refunds and seller payouts, and operations wants a decision within ten business days. Counsel is involved because the suspension implicates BSA/AML and OFAC obligations, SAR confidentiality limits on what the merchant can be told, and unresolved contract terms on pending funds and notice.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay immediately suspend this merchant now — blocking new payments and payouts while one sanctions alert remains unresolved — and what may the suspension notice say without violating SAR confidentiality under 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(2), given that a SAR has been filed or is under consideration, pending consumer refunds and seller payouts must be handled, and the decision is due within ten business days?
## Open questions
- Has a SAR actually been filed, or is one only being considered? The answer controls how strict the no-disclosure rule is and whether even an indirect reference that would reveal a SAR’s existence is prohibited.
- Is the unresolved sanctions alert a true OFAC SDN match or another false positive? A confirmed match converts this from a discretionary risk-based suspension into a mandatory blocking/rejecting obligation with a 10-business-day OFAC report.
- Which high-risk country is involved, and is it OFAC-sanctioned or merely elevated AML risk? Comprehensive-sanctions exposure changes both the suspension analysis and the funds-handling analysis.
- What do the merchant agreement and processor/acquirer rules say about pending funds, reserves, notice timing, and post-termination access? These govern whether Mosaic Relay can hold seller payouts and for how long.
- What are the amounts, aging, and deadlines on the pending customer refunds? Consumer-facing refund obligations may need to be honored even during suspension.

### SAR confidentiality and anti-tipping — 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(2)
This is the core of the research question. The framework as commonly applied:
- **The prohibition.** If a SAR has been filed, Mosaic Relay and its directors, officers, and employees are prohibited from notifying any person involved in the transaction that the transaction has been reported. The prohibition covers direct disclosure and indirect disclosure that would reveal the existence of a SAR — including statements like "we are suspending you because of suspicious activity" if that would tip the merchant that a SAR was filed.
- **What is protected vs. what is not.** The SAR itself and information that would reveal the existence of a SAR are confidential. The *underlying facts* that led to the SAR are not automatically confidential — Mosaic Relay may act on its own risk information (alerts, volume spikes, country exposure) and may describe those underlying facts, so long as the communication does not reveal that a SAR was filed or is being contemplated.
- **"Considering" a SAR.** The statutory prohibition attaches to a filed SAR. FinCEN guidance has long treated disclosure that a SAR is *being considered* as risky because it can reveal the eventual filing; the conservative operational posture is to treat pre-filing deliberations as confidential too, and to route all merchant communications through compliance/legal until the SAR decision is resolved.
- **Safe harbor.** 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(3) provides a safe harbor from liability for the SAR filer for the report itself — it does not authorize disclosure to the merchant.
- **Permitted sharing.** SAR information may be shared with FinCEN, appropriate law enforcement and supervisory agencies, and (under FinCEN guidance) within the filer's corporate structure for compliance purposes. It may not be shared with the merchant, and generally not with processors or acquiring partners in a way that reveals the SAR — partner communications should be framed around contractual risk rights, not SAR status.
- **Practical consequence for the notice.** The planned "short notice stating access is suspended under the agreement" is the right shape. It should cite the contractual suspension right and risk/compliance concerns generally, avoid the words "suspicious activity," "SAR," "report to FinCEN," or any phrasing implying a government report, and avoid confirming or denying whether any report exists if the merchant asks.
### OFAC overlay (distinct from SAR rules)
- If the unresolved alert is confirmed as a true SDN match, Mosaic Relay must block property/transactions and file a blocking report with OFAC within 10 business days. OFAC blocking obligations are strict liability and independent of the BSA SAR analysis.
- OFAC does not prohibit telling a customer their transaction was blocked under sanctions — but if the same facts also generated a SAR, the communication must still avoid revealing the SAR.
- If the country is merely high-risk for AML (e.g., FATF grey-list) but not comprehensively sanctioned, suspension remains a discretionary risk decision under the contract.
### Contract and funds handling
- The confirmed general suspension right supports immediate suspension of *new* activity. Pending funds are different: holding seller payouts and delaying consumer refunds requires contractual reserve/hold authority, and is constrained by card network rules, processor agreements, state money transmission law (safeguarding/permissible investments), and consumer refund obligations.
- Consumer refunds are the most time-sensitive: end-customer refund rights under network rules and consumer protection law do not disappear because the merchant is suspended.
### Record preservation
- BSA recordkeeping (31 C.F.R. Chapter X): SARs and supporting documentation must be retained five years; transaction and CIP/KYB records five years after account closure. Supporting documentation is deemed filed with the SAR and must be produced to FinCEN/law enforcement on request.
- Practical litigation hold: once deactivation is contemplated, preserve screening alerts and dispositions, case notes, communications, transaction logs, and the contract file.
## Viable paths
1. **Suspend now on contractual risk grounds, silent notice.** Block new payments and payouts under the general suspension right; send a short notice citing the agreement and risk/compliance concerns only; say nothing that reveals SAR status; hold pending funds pending the unresolved alert disposition and contract review. This is the lowest-risk path and fits the 10-business-day window.
2. **Resolve the alert first, then act.** Delay suspension until the third alert is dispositioned. Defensible only if interim risk is contained (e.g., payout holds, enhanced monitoring); risky given rapid volume growth and high-risk-country exposure.
3. **Partial restriction.** Allow consumer refunds to process (consumer-protection and network-rule pressure) while holding seller payouts and blocking new volume. Requires confirming refund mechanics don't create new exposure.
4. **Terminate rather than suspend.** Available later; premature now because the unresolved alert and unreviewed contract terms make the record incomplete.
## Facts that could change the answer
- SAR filed vs. merely contemplated — changes the strictness of the no-disclosure posture and internal handling.
- True SDN match — converts discretionary suspension into mandatory blocking with OFAC reporting and changes the funds analysis (blocked property cannot simply be returned).
- Country identity — comprehensive sanctions vs. elevated AML risk.
- Contract terms on reserves, notice, and post-termination access — determines how long funds can be held and what the notice must say about timing.
- Refund amounts and deadlines — may force path 3.

## What would change this
- **Working assumption — SAR status.** This analysis assumes a SAR has been filed or is actively being considered, and applies the strictest reading of § 5318(g)(2). If no SAR exists and none is contemplated, the notice can say somewhat more (e.g., reference to sanctions-screening concerns), though it still should not reveal the unresolved alert's details.
- **Working assumption — MSB status.** The SAR framework only binds Mosaic Relay if it is a BSA-covered financial institution (assumed MSB, unverified). If Mosaic Relay operates purely under partner-bank coverage, the partner's SAR program and its confidentiality rules control instead, and partner notification obligations likely expand.
- **Open fork — true match vs. false positive.** A confirmed SDN match moves this from contract-driven suspension to mandatory OFAC blocking: funds become blocked property, an OFAC report is due in 10 business days, and returning funds to the merchant is off the table without a license. A false positive keeps this a discretionary, contract-governed action.
- **Open fork — hold funds vs. pay out.** If the contract lacks reserve/hold authority, Mosaic Relay may be unable to retain seller payouts beyond a short risk-review period, forcing a faster terminate-and-settle decision; if authority exists, a longer hold with periodic review is viable.
- **Not examined — the merchant agreement itself.** The vault contains no copy of the agreement; every statement about suspension, notice, and funds-holding authority rests on the requester's summary. The actual clauses could narrow or expand each path.
- **Not examined — state money transmission and consumer refund law.** No state-by-state analysis was done of safeguarding rules or refund-timing requirements that could constrain how long payouts and consumer refunds are held; the vault could not supply this and no external authority was retrieved.
