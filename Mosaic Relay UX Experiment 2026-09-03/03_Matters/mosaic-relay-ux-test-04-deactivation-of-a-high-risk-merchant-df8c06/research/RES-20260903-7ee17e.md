# Advise on deactivating a high-risk Mosaic Relay merchant: (1) whether

## Question

Advise on deactivating a high-risk Mosaic Relay merchant: (1) whether immediate suspension is justified given one unresolved sanctions-screening alert, rapid volume growth, and transactions involving a high-risk country; (2) what communications to the merchant are permitted, including whether SAR confidentiality/anti-tipping limits disclosure; (3) how to handle pending customer refunds and seller payouts during suspension; (4) what records must be preserved; and (5) what process should govern reinstatement or termination. Consider U.S. BSA/AML, OFAC sanctions, money transmission licensing, card network/processor rules, and consumer protection.

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay's risk operations team recommends deactivating a merchant after repeated sanctions-screening alerts (two false positives, one unresolved), rapid volume growth, and transactions involving a newly identified high-risk country. The proposed operation would immediately block new payments and payouts, preserve records, and send a short suspension notice, with a later review deciding termination or reinstatement. Counsel is involved because the action implicates BSA/AML and OFAC obligations, SAR confidentiality, contractual suspension rights, pending consumer refunds and seller payouts, and record-preservation duties. Operations wants a decision within ten business days; the merchant has pending customer refunds and seller payouts that create consumer-protection and funds-handling exposure.
## Decision question
Should Mosaic Relay immediately suspend this merchant now — blocking new payments and payouts and sending a short contractual notice — while one sanctions alert remains unresolved and the high-risk country's status is unconfirmed, and if so, what may the notice say, how must pending refunds and payouts be handled, what records must be preserved, and what process governs the later reinstatement-or-termination decision?
## Open questions
- Is the unresolved sanctions-screening alert a true OFAC SDN match or a false positive? (Still under review — controls whether suspension is mandatory blocking or discretionary.)
- Which high-risk country is involved, and is it OFAC-sanctioned or merely high-risk for AML purposes? (Not confirmed — controls whether transactions must be blocked/rejected.)
- Has a SAR been filed, or is one being considered, for this merchant? (Not confirmed — controls what may be disclosed under SAR confidentiality/anti-tipping rules.)
- What do the merchant agreement's specific terms say about pending funds, notice timing, and post-termination access? (Only a general suspension right is confirmed.)
- What are the amounts, aging, and time-sensitivity of the pending customer refunds and seller payouts? (Not provided — affects consumer-protection exposure and hold decisions.)
## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.** The following is generated analysis based on general U.S. payments-regulation frameworks, not retrieved authority.
**OFAC sanctions (strict liability).** If the unresolved alert is a true SDN match, or the country is comprehensively sanctioned, Mosaic Relay must block property and reject transactions — suspension becomes mandatory, not discretionary, and funds may need to be placed in a blocked account with reporting to OFAC within 10 business days. If the alert is a false positive and the country is merely high-risk (not sanctioned), suspension is a discretionary risk-based decision under the contract.
**BSA/AML program and SAR obligations.** Assuming Mosaic Relay is an MSB (unverified), it must maintain an AML program, file SARs for suspicious transactions of $5,000 or more (within 30 days of detection), and — critically — SAR confidentiality rules (31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(2)) prohibit disclosing to the merchant that a SAR was filed or is being considered. This directly limits what the suspension notice may say.
**Money transmission licensing.** State money transmitter laws impose safeguarding and timely-transmission obligations; holding pending funds during suspension must be squared with license conditions and permissible-investment rules.
**Card network and processor rules.** Network rules and acquiring-bank agreements typically require prompt action on high-risk merchants, impose reserve/hold authority, and set chargeback and refund obligations that survive suspension.
**Consumer protection.** Pending customer refunds are potentially time-sensitive consumer obligations; indefinite holds on consumer refunds create UDAP/UDAAP exposure even where suspension of the merchant is justified.
**Contract.** The confirmed general right to suspend for risk/compliance concerns supports immediate suspension, but unconfirmed terms on pending funds, notice timing, and post-termination access create execution risk.
## Viable paths
**Path A — Immediate full suspension with calibrated notice (recommended first pass).** Suspend new payment acceptance and payouts now under the contractual risk/compliance right; send a short notice stating access is suspended under the agreement without referencing sanctions alerts, SARs, or specific compliance findings; place holds on settlement funds pending review; continue processing already-initiated consumer refunds where feasible (or route them through a controlled process); preserve all records; complete the alert resolution and country determination within the ten-business-day window; then decide reinstatement or termination. This path is defensible if the alert is unresolved-but-unconfirmed, and it preserves optionality.
**Path B — Immediate mandatory blocking (if alert confirms true match or sanctioned country).** If the unresolved alert resolves to a true OFAC match, or the country is comprehensively sanctioned, block immediately, place funds in a blocked interest-bearing account, file required OFAC reports, and do not process any transactions including refunds without an OFAC license. Notice content is further constrained.
**Path C — Enhanced monitoring without full suspension (narrower option).** If the alert resolves as a false positive and the country is merely high-risk, Mosaic Relay could continue service with enhanced due diligence, volume caps, and payout delays rather than full suspension. This preserves the commercial relationship but carries risk if facts later worsen, and may not satisfy risk operations' recommendation.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **True OFAC match** → converts discretionary suspension into mandatory blocking; changes funds handling (blocked account, OFAC reporting) and further restricts communications.
- **Country is comprehensively sanctioned** → same effect; transactions must be rejected/blocked regardless of the alert outcome.
- **SAR filed or contemplated** → prohibits telling the merchant the suspension relates to a SAR or suspicious activity; notice must be framed purely in contractual terms.
- **Contract terms on pending funds/notice** → if the agreement requires advance notice or mandates release of pending funds, immediate suspension without those steps creates breach exposure.
- **Large or time-sensitive consumer refunds** → increases UDAP/UDAAP and network-rule pressure to keep refund rails open during suspension.
- **Mosaic Relay's MSB status** → if it is not an MSB (e.g., operating under an agent-of-payee or processor exemption), SAR obligations and some BSA duties shift to the acquiring partner.
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Resolve the alert and identify the country** — have compliance complete the sanctions-alert review and confirm the country's OFAC status before execution; this determines whether Path A or Path B applies.
2. **Confirm SAR status with the BSA officer** — establish whether a SAR has been or will be filed, and have the BSA officer review the suspension notice for anti-tipping compliance before it is sent.
3. **Pull and read the merchant agreement** — verify terms on pending funds, notice timing, reserve/hold authority, and post-termination access; conform the notice and funds handling to the contract.
4. **Quantify pending refunds and payouts** — have finance/risk confirm amounts, aging, and deadlines; design a controlled process for consumer refunds during suspension.
5. **Confirm partner requirements** — check processor/acquirer agreements and network rules for mandatory action, reserve, and notification obligations triggered by high-risk merchant action.
6. **Confirm MSB classification** — verify Mosaic Relay's BSA status and which entity carries SAR obligations.
## Recommendation (separate from the lawyer's decision)
Proceed with **Path A**: immediate suspension under the contractual risk/compliance right, with a short notice that references only the agreement and does not mention sanctions alerts, SARs, or compliance findings; hold settlement funds pending review; keep consumer-refund processing alive through a controlled channel; preserve all records (screening alerts and dispositions, transaction records, communications, decision memos) for at least five years; and complete alert resolution, country confirmation, SAR-status check, and contract review within the ten-business-day window before the reinstatement/termination decision. Escalate to Path B immediately if the alert confirms a true match or the country is sanctioned. This recommendation is generated analysis for counsel's review, not a recorded decision.
