# What are the money transmitter / MSB requirements for handling

## Question

What are the money transmitter / MSB requirements for handling pending funds, refunds, and payouts upon suspension or termination?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay's risk operations team wants to deactivate a merchant following repeated sanctions-screening alerts, rapid volume growth, and transactions involving a newly identified high-risk country. The proposed action would immediately block new payments and payouts while preserving records, with a final decision on termination or reinstatement to follow within ten business days. Counsel is involved because the merchant has pending customer refunds and seller payouts, and the company must determine whether it can lawfully freeze these funds, what it must tell the merchant, and whether BSA/AML obligations (including potential SAR filing) restrict those communications. The unresolved alert is not yet confirmed as a true OFAC match, making this a discretionary risk decision rather than a mandatory blocking obligation.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay immediately suspend the merchant and freeze pending customer refunds and seller payouts given one unresolved sanctions alert and high-risk country exposure, and if so, what specific communications about the suspension are permitted under BSA/AML confidentiality rules before the ten-business-day deadline for a final terminate-or-reinstate decision?
## Open questions
- **SAR status**: Has a suspicious activity report been filed, or is one actively being considered, for this merchant? This controls whether Mosaic Relay may disclose the suspension rationale or must remain silent to avoid tipping off the subject.
- **Nature of the unresolved alert**: Is the remaining sanctions alert a true OFAC SDN match (which would trigger mandatory blocking and prohibit dealing with the merchant) or a false positive? The answer determines whether suspension is legally required or merely risk-based.
- **Contract terms**: What specific authority does the merchant agreement provide regarding holding pending funds, notice timing, and post-termination access? The general right to suspend is confirmed, but the mechanics of fund handling are not.
- **Pending transaction details**: What are the amounts, aging, and time-sensitivity of the pending customer refunds (potential consumer obligations) and seller payouts (third-party funds)? Are any refunds subject to statutory deadlines?
- **High-risk country status**: Is the newly identified country subject to comprehensive OFAC sanctions (e.g., Iran, North Korea, Cuba, Syria, Crimea region) or merely high-risk for AML purposes? This affects whether transactions must be blocked or can be processed with enhanced due diligence.

## Likely rules and issues
**Money transmission and MSB obligations.** Mosaic Relay operates as a money services business (MSB) subject to FinCEN regulations and state money transmitter laws. Upon suspension, the company must comply with:
- **BSA/AML program requirements** (31 U.S.C. § 5318(h)): Maintain records of the decision-making process and file SARs if suspicious activity is detected (31 C.F.R. § 1022.320).
- **SAR confidentiality** (31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(2)): If a SAR is filed or contemplated, Mosaic Relay and its employees are prohibited from disclosing the existence of the SAR or the underlying suspicious activity to the merchant. Violations can result in civil and criminal penalties.
- **OFAC sanctions** (50 U.S.C. § 1701 et seq.): If the unresolved alert confirms a match to the SDN List or the country is comprehensively sanctioned, Mosaic Relay must block property and reject transactions immediately. If the alert is unconfirmed, Mosaic Relay has discretion to suspend based on risk tolerance but must document the rationale.
**Pending funds and consumer protection.** State money transmitter statutes (e.g., California Money Transmission Act, New York Part 406) and the Uniform Money Services Act generally require that customer funds be held in trust or segregated accounts and not commingled with operating funds. Upon suspension:
- **Customer refunds**: These are typically obligations owed to consumers. Mosaic Relay may be required to process refunds within specific timeframes under state law or card network rules (e.g., Visa/Mastercard chargeback timeframes), regardless of the merchant's status. Freezing these funds could expose Mosaic Relay to consumer protection claims or network fines.
- **Seller payouts**: These represent funds owed to third-party sellers. Mosaic Relay must determine whether it holds these as agent of the merchant (requiring payment to the merchant's designated account) or as a stakeholder (requiring interpleader or escheatment if the merchant is terminated). State unclaimed property laws may apply if funds remain undeliverable.
**Contractual authority.** The general right to suspend for risk/compliance concerns provides a basis for immediate action, but the absence of specific terms regarding pending funds creates ambiguity. Mosaic Relay must avoid conversion claims by clearly establishing whether it acts as bailee, trustee, or debtor of the pending funds.
## Viable paths
**Path A: Immediate suspension with segregated fund handling**
- Immediately block new payment acceptance and payout initiation.
- Segregate pending customer refunds into a separate account and process them according to original payment method (crediting consumers) to avoid consumer harm and network liability.
- Hold seller payouts in a suspense account pending the ten-day review, with notice to the merchant that funds are held pending investigation.
- **Risk**: If the unresolved alert is a false positive, Mosaic Relay may face breach of contract claims for wrongful suspension or interference with business relations.
**Path B: Conditional suspension with limited processing**
- Suspend new business but allow processing of pending refunds and payouts to complete the transaction cycle, reducing consumer harm and commercial disruption.
- Require enhanced monitoring and manual review of all transactions during the wind-down period.
- **Risk**: If the merchant is actually engaged in illicit activity, Mosaic Relay may be facilitating money laundering or sanctions violations by completing pending transactions.
**Path C: Immediate termination and fund interpleader**
- Terminate the agreement immediately based on the risk/compliance clause.
- File an interpleader action or place disputed funds with a third-party escrow agent to avoid liability for deciding between the merchant and its customers/sellers.
- **Risk**: Premature termination without confirming the sanctions alert may damage the commercial relationship and expose Mosaic Relay to damages if the alert proves false.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Confirmation of OFAC match**: If the unresolved alert is confirmed as a true SDN match, Mosaic Relay must immediately block all property (including pending payouts) and cannot process refunds without an OFAC license. The analysis shifts from discretionary risk management to mandatory compliance.
- **SAR filing status**: If a SAR has been filed, Mosaic Relay cannot disclose the suspension rationale to the merchant without risking a tipping-off violation. Communications must be limited to generic contractual language.
- **State licensing requirements**: If Mosaic Relay holds money transmitter licenses in states with specific "wind-down" or "permissible investment" requirements (e.g., New York's requirement to maintain permissible investments equal to outstanding transmission obligations), the handling of pending funds must comply with those statutory trusts.
- **Card network rules**: Visa and Mastercard rules may require Mosaic Relay to process chargebacks and refunds within specific timeframes regardless of the merchant's status, creating a conflict with a blanket freeze on payouts.
## Recommended last-mile verification
Before executing the suspension, verify the following with the Compliance and Finance teams:
1. **Confirm the status of the unresolved alert** with the sanctions-screening vendor and OFAC directly if necessary. Document whether this is a true match or false positive.
2. **Review the merchant agreement** for specific clauses regarding: (a) reserve/holdback authority, (b) notice requirements for suspension, (c) treatment of pending transactions upon termination, and (d) post-termination access to funds.
3. **Determine SAR status** through the BSA Officer. If a SAR is contemplated or filed, draft merchant communications using only approved language that does not reveal the investigation.
4. **Quantify pending obligations**: Obtain a schedule of pending customer refunds (amounts, dates, consumer contact info) and seller payouts (amounts, beneficiary details, aging). Identify any refunds subject to state consumer protection deadlines or card network timeframes.
5. **Consult state money transmitter statutes** in Mosaic Relay's licensed states regarding the handling of customer funds upon suspension, specifically whether funds must be released to consumers or can be held pending investigation.

## What would change this
- **Working assumption**: The analysis assumes Mosaic Relay is a licensed money transmitter or MSB with BSA/AML obligations. If Mosaic Relay operates under an exemption (e.g., as a payment processor for a licensed entity rather than a money transmitter), the SAR confidentiality rules and fund-handling requirements may differ materially.
- **Open fork**: If the unresolved sanctions alert is confirmed as a true OFAC match, the path shifts from discretionary suspension to mandatory blocking, requiring immediate rejection of all transactions and potentially freezing of all merchant property without notice. If it is a false positive, Mosaic Relay faces potential liability for wrongful suspension and must consider immediate reinstatement.
- **Not examined**: The specific terms of the merchant agreement regarding reserve accounts, holdbacks, and post-termination fund access were not reviewed. These terms could provide contractual authority for holding funds that overrides general state law defaults.
- **Not examined**: State-specific money transmitter statutes in Mosaic Relay's licensed states were not individually reviewed. Some states (e.g., Texas, Washington) have specific requirements for the handling of customer funds upon license suspension or termination that could mandate immediate refund processing.
- **Working assumption**: It was assumed that "pending customer refunds" refers to obligations owed to end consumers, not B2B refunds. If these are B2B transactions, different commercial law rules apply regarding setoff and priority of claims.
