# What are the OFAC blocking and reporting obligations when a

## Question

What are the OFAC blocking and reporting obligations when a merchant is suspected of being a sanctioned party or transacting with a sanctioned jurisdiction?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay's risk operations team recommends deactivating a merchant after repeated sanctions-screening alerts (two confirmed false positives, one unresolved), rapid volume growth, and transactions involving a newly identified high-risk country. The proposed action would immediately block new payments and payouts, preserve records, and notify the merchant that access is suspended under the agreement. The merchant has pending customer refunds and seller payouts, and the contract's specific terms on funds handling, notice timing, and post-termination access are unverified. Operations requires a decision within ten business days (by 2026-09-17).
## Decision question
Should Mosaic Relay immediately suspend the merchant's access to payment processing and payouts based on one unresolved sanctions-screening alert and high-risk country transactions, and if so, what specific communications, funds handling, and record preservation steps must be taken to comply with OFAC and BSA obligations while preserving the ability to later terminate or reinstate the relationship?
## Open questions
- Is the unresolved sanctions alert a true OFAC SDN match or a false positive?
- Which specific high-risk country is involved, and is it comprehensively sanctioned by OFAC or merely high-risk for AML purposes?
- Has a suspicious activity report (SAR) been filed or is one being considered for this merchant?
- What do the merchant agreement's specific terms say about pending funds, notice timing, and post-termination access?
- What are the amounts, aging, and time-sensitivity of the pending customer refunds and seller payouts?

## Analysis
### Likely rules and issues
**OFAC sanctions framework.** If the unresolved alert confirms the merchant is a Specially Designated National (SDN) or is owned 50% or more by one or more SDNs, Mosaic Relay must block all property and interests in property of the merchant that come within its possession or control. This is a strict liability obligation with no de minimis exception. The blocking must be immediate, and Mosaic Relay must file a blocking report with OFAC within 10 business days. If the merchant is not an SDN but is transacting with a comprehensively sanctioned jurisdiction (e.g., Iran, North Korea, Cuba, Syria, Crimea region), Mosaic Relay must reject (not block) transactions involving that jurisdiction unless a general or specific license applies. Rejection reports must also be filed within 10 business days.
**SAR confidentiality.** If Mosaic Relay has filed or is considering filing a SAR on this merchant, federal law prohibits disclosing the existence of the SAR or any information that would reveal its existence to the merchant. This includes the fact that a SAR was filed, the contents of the SAR, or even that a SAR is being considered. The prohibition extends to any communication that would tip off the merchant. However, Mosaic Relay may still take adverse action (suspension, termination) based on its own risk assessment and may communicate the business reasons for that action without referencing the SAR.
**Contractual authority.** The merchant agreement's general right to suspend for risk or compliance concerns provides a basis for immediate suspension, but the specific terms on pending funds, notice timing, and post-termination access will control how Mosaic Relay handles the merchant's money and data during and after suspension. If the contract is silent, Mosaic Relay must look to state money transmission laws, card network rules, and processor agreements for guidance on funds handling.
**BSA/AML program obligations.** As a money services business, Mosaic Relay must maintain a risk-based AML program that includes procedures for handling high-risk merchants, escalating sanctions alerts, and making SAR filing decisions. The decision to suspend should be documented as part of this program, with clear rationale based on the unresolved alert, rapid growth, and high-risk country exposure.
### Viable paths
**Path 1: Immediate suspension with conditional funds handling.** Suspend the merchant's access immediately based on the unresolved alert and high-risk country transactions. Block new payments and payouts. For pending customer refunds, process them if they are owed to consumers and time-sensitive, as consumer protection obligations may override risk concerns. For seller payouts, hold them pending resolution of the alert, subject to contract terms and processor rules. Send a brief notice stating access is suspended under the agreement for risk and compliance reasons, without mentioning the sanctions alert or any SAR. Preserve all records related to the merchant, the alerts, and the decision-making process.
**Path 2: Delayed suspension pending alert resolution.** Delay suspension until the unresolved alert is resolved, but implement enhanced monitoring and transaction limits in the interim. This path is riskier if the alert is a true match, as it could result in processing transactions for a sanctioned party. However, it may be appropriate if the alert is likely a false positive and the merchant's business is legitimate.
**Path 3: Immediate termination.** Terminate the merchant relationship immediately based on the risk profile, without waiting for the alert to be resolved. This is the most conservative approach but may be contractually and commercially difficult if the alert turns out to be a false positive.
### Facts that could change the answer
- If the unresolved alert is confirmed as a true OFAC SDN match, immediate blocking (not just suspension) is required, and all funds must be blocked, not just held.
- If the high-risk country is comprehensively sanctioned, transactions involving that country must be rejected, and rejection reports must be filed.
- If a SAR has been filed or is being considered, communications to the merchant must be carefully crafted to avoid tipping off.
- If the merchant agreement has specific terms on funds handling, notice, or post-termination access, those terms will control.
- If the pending refunds are owed to consumers and are time-sensitive, consumer protection laws may require processing them despite the suspension.
### Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Resolve the sanctions alert immediately.** This is the single most important fact. If it is a true match, the analysis changes completely.
2. **Confirm the high-risk country's sanctions status.** Check OFAC's Sanctions Programs and Country Information page.
3. **Review the merchant agreement.** Confirm the specific terms on pending funds, notice timing, and post-termination access.
4. **Check SAR status.** Confirm with compliance whether a SAR has been filed or is being considered.
5. **Confirm funds details.** Get the amounts, aging, and time-sensitivity of pending refunds and payouts from risk, finance, and support.
