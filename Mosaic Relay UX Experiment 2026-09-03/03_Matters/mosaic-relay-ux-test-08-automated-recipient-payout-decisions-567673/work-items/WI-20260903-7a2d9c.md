# Orient to the request

Business objective and timing

Mosaic Relay plans a three-customer pilot next quarter. The system would assign payout outcomes for sellers and contractors, including individuals and small businesses, with some recipients outside the United States.

Pivotal characterization

A rejected or delayed payout must be classified before launch as (1) a sanctions or other compliance block, (2) a fraud/risk control, or (3) a marketplace/customer instruction. This classification drives the notice, review, escalation, and funds-handling rules. Mosaic Relay is fictional payment infrastructure and is not a bank or deposit holder.

Known decision paths

Release immediately; request additional information; or route to review. The inputs named today are identity-verification result, sanctions status, transaction history, country, amount, and risk score. The final model fields, thresholds, re-evaluation frequency, and override rules are not confirmed.

First missing facts

1. Who legally makes the payout decision and who controls recipient funds during review?

2. What exact event causes rejection, hold, or request for documents, and what duration applies?

3. Which countries and payout processors are in scope?

4. What notice, reason codes, correction route, appeal path, and human-review standard will recipients receive?

5. What data sources, retention period, access controls, testing, and vendor contracts apply?

6. How will small businesses and individuals be handled, and will customers be able to direct outcomes?

Initial legal framing

Treat sanctions screening and fraud screening as separate decision paths with separate reason codes and escalation. Do not describe a risk decision as a sanctions block unless compliance has confirmed that basis. Before the pilot, document accountable human ownership, evidence preservation, correction and appeal handling, model-change review, and funds treatment while a payout is under review.
