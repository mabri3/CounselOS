# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

This matter concerns automated recipient payout decisions. It builds on the baseline KYB/KYC, sanctions screening, and BSA/AML framework established in Matter 01 and the seller-screening/FCRA analysis in Matter 02, but is distinct: the decision here is about releasing or restricting payouts to recipients, not onboarding merchants or screening sellers for activation. The pivotal legal fork is the characterization of a rejected payout (compliance block vs. fraud control vs. customer-directed decision).

## Decision question

May Mosaic Relay automatically assign marketplace recipients (sellers/contractors) to payout tiers — release immediately, require additional information, or send to review — using identity-verification results, sanctions-screening status, transaction history, country, payout amount, and risk score? What legal requirements apply to automated screening and payout restrictions, notice and appeal duties, privacy and discrimination risks, human oversight, and the minimum controls needed for a three-customer pilot next quarter?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay wants to help marketplaces pay sellers and contractors by automatically assigning recipients to payout tiers.
- The proposed system uses identity-verification results, sanctions-screening status, transaction history, country, payout amount, and risk score to decide whether to release a payout immediately, require additional information, or send the payout to review.
- The recipient would see a status message and a request for documents when needed.
- Actors are sellers, contractors, marketplace customers, Mosaic Relay compliance and operations, identity and fraud vendors, and payout processors.
- Product wants to test this with three customers next quarter.
- The system may include individuals and small businesses as recipients.
- Some recipients may be outside the United States.
- The team has not determined whether a rejected payout is a compliance block, a fraud control, or a customer-directed decision.
- The explanation shown to recipients, human-review standard, correction process, retention period, and treatment of funds during review are not settled.
- The data fields used in the model and how often decisions are re-evaluated are not known.

## Assumptions

- U.S.-primary scope for the pilot; any non-U.S. recipient materially changes data-protection and sanctions obligations and requires separate review.
- Mosaic Relay's baseline KYB/KYC, sanctions screening, and BSA/AML obligations from prior matters (Matter 01) apply to recipients as they do to merchants.
- Sanctions screening is a mandatory, non-tierable gate before any payout is released, consistent with the baseline framework.
- The three-customer pilot is a controlled test with defined controls, not a full production launch.

## Issues and workstreams

- Characterization of a rejected payout: whether it is a compliance block (OFAC/BSA-AML), a fraud control, or a customer-directed decision determines which legal framework applies.
- Automated decision-making and notice duties: what explanation must be shown to recipients and whether adverse-action or FCRA notice duties attach if the decision is eligibility-based.
- Privacy and data minimization for the model inputs (identity, sanctions, transaction history, country, amount, risk score), including retention and cross-border transfer for non-U.S. recipients.
- Discrimination and fair-lending/equal-credit risk if model inputs (e.g., country, amount, risk score) produce disparate treatment of protected classes.
- Human oversight and review standard: what constitutes meaningful human review and how often decisions are re-evaluated.
- Treatment of funds during review and the correction/appeal process for recipients.

## Open questions

- Whether a rejected payout is a compliance block, a fraud control, or a customer-directed decision (the pivotal characterization).
- Which data fields the model will use and how decisions are re-evaluated over time.
- The explanation shown to recipients and the human-review standard.
- The correction/appeal process and retention period for decision records.
- How funds are treated during review (held, released, or returned).
- Which countries the non-U.S. recipients are in.
