      Can Mosaic Relay lawfully hold merchant card and ACH proceeds during fraud review,

      ## Likely rules and issues

      ### Contractual authority (merchant agreement)

      The merchant agreement is the primary source of contractual hold authority.
      Most payment-processor and platform agreements include provisions allowing the
      processor to withhold settlement, delay payout, or establish reserves when fraud,
      chargeback risk, or other risk signals are present. If Mosaic Relay''s current
      merchant agreement does not explicitly authorize temporary holds for fraud review,
      the agreement will need to be amended or the hold feature may be challenged
      as a breach of contract.

      **Key issue:** Whether the current agreement permits holds, and if not, what
      amendment process and notice are required.

      ### Card network rules (Visa/Mastercard)

      Card networks impose rules on merchants, acquirers, and payment facilitators
      regarding transaction processing, chargebacks, and fraud management. While networks
      generally do not prohibit holds on merchant proceeds, they do require:

      - Timely settlement and funding obligations between acquirers and merchants,
      subject to agreed-upon reserve or holdback terms.

      - Chargeback and dispute processes that operate independently of any internal
      fraud review hold.

      - Compliance with network fraud-monitoring and reporting requirements.

      **Key issue:** Whether the hold interferes with the merchant''s ability to respond
      to chargebacks or fund chargeback liabilities, and whether the hold must be
      disclosed to the acquiring bank or network.

      ### ACH rules (NACHA)

      NACHA rules govern ACH transactions and impose obligations on originators, third-party
      senders, and receiving depository financial institutions (RDFIs). NACHA rules
      permit holds or delays in settlement for risk management purposes, but they
      also require:

      - Timely return of unauthorized or erroneous ACH debits.

      - Compliance with warranties regarding authorization and accuracy.

      - Risk management and monitoring obligations for third-party senders.

      **Key issue:** Whether the hold on ACH proceeds affects Mosaic Relay''s ability
      to meet return deadlines or honor its warranties, and whether the hold must
      be disclosed to the originating depository financial institution (ODFI) or RDFI.

      ### Money transmission and funds-flow implications

      Holding merchant proceeds may implicate state money transmission laws if Mosaic
      Relay is deemed to be in possession or control of funds belonging to the merchant.
      Most states exempt payment processors or agents of the payee from money transmission
      licensing if certain conditions are met, but the exemption often depends on
      the contractual relationship and the flow of funds.

      **Key issue:** Whether the hold changes Mosaic Relay''s regulatory status or
      requires additional licensing, and whether the hold affects the exemption analysis.

      ### Consumer protection and disclosure

      If the merchant''s customers are consumers, consumer protection laws (such as
      the Electronic Fund Transfer Act for ACH or state UDAP laws) may require specific
      disclosures or limit the ability to reverse or delay transactions. The merchant-facing
      disclosure (the banner) must be clear, accurate, and not misleading.

      **Key issue:** What disclosure language is required, and whether the hold or
      reversal affects consumer rights or triggers additional notice obligations.

      ### Appeal and dispute resolution

      While there is generally no federal statutory requirement for a specific appeal
      process for merchant holds, network rules, state law, or the merchant agreement
      may require a dispute-resolution mechanism. Providing a clear, accessible appeal
      process is a best practice and may reduce legal risk.

      **Key issue:** Whether a specific appeal process is required, and if so, what
      it must include (e.g., timing, documentation, decision standards).
