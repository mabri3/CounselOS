# Consumer protection and merchant disclosure requirements for holds on proceeds

## Question

Consumer protection and merchant disclosure requirements for holds on proceeds

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payments technology company, wants to implement a fraud-review hold on merchant card and ACH proceeds. The proposed UX would display a banner to merchants, block payout for the affected amount, and allow merchants to submit evidence while Operations decides whether to release, extend, reverse, or deactivate processing. Counsel is involved because the company needs to confirm lawful authority, timing limits, disclosure obligations, and appeal rights before approving the design for a planned one-quarter launch.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay lawfully implement temporary fraud-review holds on merchant card and ACH proceeds, and what specific contract terms, timing limits, disclosure language, and appeal processes must be in place before the planned one-quarter launch to avoid regulatory or network violations?
## Open questions
- What does the current merchant agreement permit regarding holds, and does it require amendment to authorize the proposed fraud-review hold?
- What is the maximum permissible hold period under card network rules and state money transmission laws, and does it differ for card versus ACH transactions?
- May held funds be used to cover chargebacks, returns, or fees, or must they be segregated and returned in full if the merchant is cleared?
- What specific customer-facing disclosure language and timing are required to satisfy consumer protection and state money transmission laws?
- Is a formal appeal process required by card network rules or state law, and if so, what are the minimum procedural requirements?
---
## Likely rules and issues
**Contractual authority**
The merchant agreement is the primary source of hold authority. If the current agreement does not explicitly permit temporary fraud-review holds, Mosaic Relay must amend it before launch. The amendment should specify the trigger (transaction-risk signals), the scope (affected amount), the duration, and the merchant’s right to submit evidence.
**Timing limits**
Card network rules (Visa, Mastercard) and ACH rules (NACHA) impose limits on how long funds can be held. State money transmission laws may also cap hold periods. The maximum hold period must be defined and disclosed. Card and ACH holds may have different limits.
**Treatment of held funds**
Held funds must be segregated and not commingled with Mosaic Relay’s operating funds. Whether they can be used to cover chargebacks, returns, or fees depends on the merchant agreement and state law. If the merchant is cleared, the full amount must be released promptly.
**Disclosure language**
The banner and any customer-facing notice must clearly state that a review is in progress, the reason, the expected duration, and the merchant’s right to submit evidence. The language must be plain and not misleading.
**Appeal rights**
Card network rules and some state laws require a formal appeal process. The process must be disclosed, provide a reasonable timeframe for review, and allow the merchant to submit evidence.
**Regulatory and network requirements**
Mosaic Relay must comply with state money transmission laws, card network rules, NACHA rules, and consumer protection laws. The hold must not constitute an unfair or deceptive practice.

## Viable paths
1. **Amend the merchant agreement** to explicitly permit fraud-review holds, define the maximum hold period, and specify the treatment of held funds.
2. **Implement a formal appeal process** that meets card network and state law requirements.
3. **Draft clear disclosure language** for the banner and any customer-facing notice.
4. **Segregate held funds** and ensure they are not used for operating expenses.
5. **Test the operational controls** to ensure compliance with timing limits and appeal rights.
