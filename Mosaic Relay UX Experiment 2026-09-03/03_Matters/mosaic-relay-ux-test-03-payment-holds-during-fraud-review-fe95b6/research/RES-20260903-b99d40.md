      Can Mosaic Relay lawfully hold merchant card and ACH proceeds during fraud review,
      and if so, what maximum hold periods, fund-use restrictions, disclosure language,
      and appeal process must be in place before the one-quarter launch?

      - What does the current merchant agreement permit regarding holds, reserves,
      or setoff?

      - What maximum hold period will the business set, and will it differ for card

      money-transmission law?

      requirements?

      ---

      ## Likely rules / issues

      ### 1. Contractual hold authority (merchant agreement)

      The primary source of authority to hold proceeds is the merchant agreement.
      Most payment processors and platforms include express provisions allowing temporary
      holds, reserves, or delayed payouts for risk, fraud, or compliance review. If
      Mosaic Relay’s current agreement lacks such language, the hold could be challenged
      as breach of contract or conversion. The agreement should also address whether
      held funds may be applied to chargebacks, returns, or fees.

      ### 2. State money transmission / funds-flow rules

      Mosaic Relay does not hold deposits, but it does control the timing of payout.
      State money-transmission statutes generally regulate the receipt and transmission
      of funds, not the temporary holding of merchant proceeds under a contractual
      right. However, some states (e.g., California, New York, Texas) have specific
      rules about safeguarding funds and timely transmission. A prolonged or indefinite
      hold could be recharacterized as an unauthorized taking or could trigger state
      escheat or abandoned-property laws if funds are never released. The hold must
      be tied to a legitimate, documented fraud-review purpose and must not function
      as an indefinite freeze.

      ### 3. UCC Article 4A (funds transfers)

      UCC Article 4A governs funds transfers, including ACH credit transfers. It does
      not directly regulate a payment processor’s internal hold on merchant proceeds,
      because the hold occurs before a funds transfer is initiated to the merchant.
      However, if Mosaic Relay is the originator of an ACH credit to the merchant,
      Article 4A’s rules on payment orders, acceptance, and execution could become
      relevant if the hold delays or cancels a payment order after acceptance. The
      key issue is whether the merchant has a contractual right to demand payout at
      a specific time; if so, an unjustified hold could create liability for consequential
      damages under §4A-305.

      ### 4. NACHA rules (ACH network)

      NACHA Operating Rules govern ACH entries. Mosaic Relay’s hold applies to proceeds
      from ACH transactions it has already received as the receiver or third-party
      sender. NACHA rules do not prohibit a processor from holding funds before originating
      a payout, but they do impose obligations on timely return of unauthorized or
      erroneous entries and on the accuracy of ACH records. If the hold leads to a
      reversal of an ACH entry, NACHA’s return deadlines (e.g., 2 banking days for
      most returns, 60 days for unauthorized consumer debits) must be respected. NACHA
      also requires that originators and third-party senders have agreements covering
      fraud monitoring and risk management, which supports the legitimacy of the hold.

      ### 5. Card network rules (Visa/Mastercard)

      Card network rules generally allow acquirers and processors to hold funds for
      fraud or risk review, but they impose limits on hold duration and require prompt
      investigation. Visa’s Core Rules and Mastercard’s Rules permit reserves and
      holds for risk management, but indefinite or excessive holds can violate network
      standards and lead to fines or termination. The networks also require that merchants
      be given notice and an opportunity to provide evidence, which aligns with the
      proposed UX.

      ### 6. Consumer protection and unfair/deceptive practices

      Holding merchant proceeds without clear contractual authority or notice could
      be challenged as an unfair or deceptive practice under state UDAP statutes or
      the FTC Act. The banner notice and evidence-submission feature mitigate this
      risk, but the hold must be reasonable in duration and scope.

      - **Pros:** Reduces initial legal exposure.

      - **Cons:** May not meet business needs; inconsistent treatment could confuse
