    The single most decision-changing fact is **whether the screening results are
    used to make eligibility decisions about sellers**. If the reports include criminal-record
    or adverse-media data and the marketplace can deny, restrict, or deactivate a
    seller based on them, the reports are likely **consumer reports under the FCRA**,
    and the marketplace (as user) and the vendor (as consumer reporting agency) take
    on disclosure/authorization, adverse-action-notice, and dispute duties — plus
    state background-check restrictions. If the package is limited to identity verification
    and sanctions screening for fraud/risk purposes, the FCRA consumer-report framework
    may not attach at all, and the analysis is much lighter. That fork drives everything
    else.

    I''ve saved the reported facts and flagged the key assumptions (U.S.-primary scope,
    sellers as individuals, vendor as CRA). To move this forward, I need answers on
    the following, in priority order:

    1. **Use of results** — Will the marketplace use results to make employment-like/eligibility
    decisions (deny, restrict, or deactivate a seller on a criminal or adverse-media
    hit)? This determines whether FCRA adverse-action duties and state background-check
    restrictions apply.

    2. **Seller type** — Are sellers individuals, businesses, or both? (FCRA consumer-report
    treatment differs sharply.)

    3. **Screening package** — Identity + sanctions only, or also criminal-record
    and adverse-media checks? (The latter two are what trigger FCRA/background-check
    treatment.)

    4. **Jurisdictions** — Which countries are in scope? (Non-U.S. requires separate
    review.)

    5. **Retention & disputes** — How long reports are kept and how disputes/false
    matches are handled.

    KYB/KYC/sanctions precedent but does **not** address consumer-report or background-check
    treatment, so this is new ground.

    the FCRA consumer-report analysis largely drops away and the matter becomes a

  ## What you''re asking

  A Mosaic Relay customer runs a marketplace for home-service providers and wants
  to screen sellers before enabling payouts. The proposed flow collects consent, legal
  name, DOB, address, and government ID; sends it to an identity/background-check
  vendor; returns a status ("cleared," "review needed," "not eligible"); and lets
  the marketplace make the final activation call. You want to know whether Mosaic
  Relay may provide this workflow, what notices/consents are required, what restrictions
  apply to criminal or adverse-media data, and how to allocate compliance duties —
  with an eight-week target.

  ## The central legal question

  The single most decision-changing fact is **whether the screening results are used
  to make eligibility decisions about sellers**. If the reports include criminal-record
  or adverse-media data and the marketplace can deny, restrict, or deactivate a seller
  based on them, the reports are likely **consumer reports under the FCRA**, and the
  marketplace (as user) and the vendor (as consumer reporting agency) take on disclosure/authorization,
  adverse-action-notice, and dispute duties — plus state background-check restrictions.
  If the package is limited to identity verification and sanctions screening for fraud/risk
  purposes, the FCRA consumer-report framework may not attach at all, and the analysis
  is much lighter. That fork drives everything else.

  I''ve saved the reported facts and flagged the key assumptions (U.S.-primary scope,
  sellers as individuals, vendor as CRA). To move this forward, I need answers on
  the following, in priority order:

  1. **Use of results** — Will the marketplace use results to make employment-like/eligibility
  decisions (deny, restrict, or deactivate a seller on a criminal or adverse-media
  hit)? This determines whether FCRA adverse-action duties and state background-check
  restrictions apply.

  2. **Seller type** — Are sellers individuals, businesses, or both? (FCRA consumer-report
  treatment differs sharply.)

  3. **Screening package** — Identity + sanctions only, or also criminal-record and
  adverse-media checks? (The latter two are what trigger FCRA/background-check treatment.)

  4. **Jurisdictions** — Which countries are in scope? (Non-U.S. requires separate
  review.)

  5. **Retention & disputes** — How long reports are kept and how disputes/false matches

  exercise closer to UX Test 01.

  - **Working assumption — U.S.-primary.** Any non-U.S. country changes data-protection
  and background-check obligations materially.

  - **Open fork — eligibility vs. fraud-only use.** If results only flag fraud/identity
  risk and never drive a denial, the FCRA adverse-action and state ban-the-box framework
