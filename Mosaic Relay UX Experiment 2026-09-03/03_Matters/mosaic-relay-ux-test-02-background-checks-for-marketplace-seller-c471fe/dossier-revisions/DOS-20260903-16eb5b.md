# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

FCRA consumer-report framework applies because the marketplace may make eligibility decisions based on criminal-record and adverse-media results. Key open items: jurisdiction scope, adverse-media source (investigative consumer report risk), and retention/dispute handling.

## Decision question

Advise whether Mosaic Relay may provide a seller-screening workflow for a marketplace of home-service providers, and which notices and consents are required, what restrictions apply to criminal-record and adverse-media data, and how the parties should allocate compliance duties. The marketplace may use criminal-record or adverse-media results to deny, restrict, or deactivate a seller (eligibility decisions). Sellers are individuals. The package includes identity verification, sanctions screening, criminal-record, and adverse-media checks. Mosaic Relay contracts with the screening vendor and provides the workflow; the marketplace makes the final adverse eligibility decision. Legal answer needed before a planned launch (customer wants the operation live in eight weeks).

## Issues and workstreams

- Whether the screening reports constitute 'consumer reports' under the FCRA, triggering permissible-purpose, disclosure/authorization, and adverse-action notice duties.
- Whether criminal-record and adverse-media data are subject to FCRA and state background-check restrictions (e.g., ban-the-box, adverse action timing).
- Duty allocation: Mosaic Relay contracts with the vendor and provides the workflow; the marketplace is the FCRA 'user' making adverse decisions and owning adverse-action and dispute duties.
- Whether adverse media gathered through interviews creates an investigative consumer report with additional FCRA duties.
- Privacy and data minimization for collecting DOB, address, and government ID, and retention limits.
- Whether the marketplace's use of results for eligibility decisions creates employment-like decision duties.
