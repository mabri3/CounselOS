# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

FCRA consumer-report framework applies because the marketplace may use criminal-record and adverse-media results to deny, restrict, or deactivate sellers. Key open items: vendor/decision ownership (FCRA 'user'), jurisdiction scope, and whether adverse media is interview-based (investigative consumer report).

## Decision question

Advise whether Mosaic Relay may provide a seller-screening workflow for a marketplace of home-service providers (identity verification, sanctions screening, criminal-record, and adverse-media checks), which notices and consents are required, what restrictions apply to criminal or adverse-media data, and how the parties should allocate compliance duties. The marketplace may use results to deny, restrict, or deactivate sellers (eligibility decisions). Answer needed before a planned launch; customer wants the operation live in eight weeks.

## Material facts

- A Mosaic Relay customer operates a marketplace for home-service providers and wants to screen sellers before enabling payouts.
- The proposed experience asks each seller for consent, legal name, date of birth, address, and government identification.

## Issues and workstreams

- Whether the screening reports constitute 'consumer reports' under the FCRA, triggering permissible-purpose, disclosure/authorization, and adverse-action notice duties.
- Whether criminal-record and adverse-media data are subject to FCRA and state background-check restrictions (e.g., ban-the-box, adverse action timing).
- Whether Mosaic Relay or the marketplace is the 'user' of consumer reports and who bears FCRA compliance duties.
- Whether adverse media gathered through interviews creates an investigative consumer report with additional FCRA duties.
- Privacy and data minimization for collecting DOB, address, and government ID, and retention limits.
- Whether the marketplace's use of results for eligibility decisions creates employment-like decision duties.

## Open questions

- Which countries are in scope for the screening — any non-U.S. country materially changes data-protection and background-check obligations.
- Whether the screening vendor is a consumer reporting agency (CRA) and its own compliance posture — central to duty allocation; no vendor agreement is in the vault.
- Whether adverse media is gathered through interviews (investigative consumer report) or only public-record/automated sources.
