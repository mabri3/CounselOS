# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Whether Mosaic Relay may provide a seller-screening workflow for a marketplace customer (identity verification, sanctions screening, and possibly criminal-record/adverse-media checks) before enabling payouts, and which notices/consents are required, what restrictions apply to criminal or adverse-media data, and how compliance duties should be allocated among Mosaic Relay, the marketplace, and the screening vendor. Target: available in eight weeks.

## Decision question

Whether Mosaic Relay may provide a seller-screening workflow for a marketplace customer (identity verification, sanctions screening, and possibly criminal-record/adverse-media checks) before enabling payouts, and which notices/consents are required, what restrictions apply to criminal or adverse-media data, and how compliance duties should be allocated among Mosaic Relay, the marketplace, and the screening vendor. Target: available in eight weeks.

## Material facts

- A Mosaic Relay customer operates a marketplace for home-service providers and wants to screen sellers before enabling payouts.
- The proposed experience asks each seller for consent, legal name, date of birth, address, and government identification.
- Mosaic Relay would send the information to an identity and background-check vendor and display a status such as 'cleared,' 'review needed,' or 'not eligible.'
- The marketplace makes the final activation decision.
- Actors are sellers, the marketplace, Mosaic Relay, the screening vendor, and Mosaic Relay support staff.
- The customer wants this operation available in eight weeks.
- Business goal is to reduce fraud and protect marketplace customers.
- The check may include identity verification, sanctions screening, criminal-record information, and adverse media, but the exact package is unsettled.
- It is not known whether the marketplace will use results for employment-like decisions.
- It is not known whether sellers are independent businesses or individuals.
- It is not known which countries will be included.
- Report retention period is not decided.
- Dispute and false-match handling is not decided.
- Will the marketplace use the screening results to make employment-like or eligibility decisions about sellers — for example, deny, restrict, or deactivate a seller based on a criminal-record or adverse-media hit? — Not yet decided
- Are the sellers individuals, businesses, or both? — Individuals (natural persons)

## Assumptions

- U.S.-primary scope for this fictional scenario; any non-U.S. countries require separate review.
- Sellers are individuals (natural persons) for at least some checks, making FCRA consumer-report analysis relevant.
- The screening vendor is a consumer reporting agency (CRA) if it assembles criminal/adverse-media data for eligibility decisions.

## Issues and workstreams

- Whether the screening reports constitute 'consumer reports' under the FCRA, triggering permissible-purpose, disclosure/authorization, and adverse-action notice duties.
- Whether criminal-record and adverse-media data are subject to FCRA and state background-check restrictions (e.g., ban-the-box, adverse action timing).
- Whether Mosaic Relay or the marketplace is the 'user' of consumer reports and who bears FCRA compliance duties.
- Whether identity verification and sanctions screening alone (without consumer-report data) can be structured to avoid FCRA consumer-report treatment.
- Privacy and data minimization for collecting DOB, address, and government ID, and retention limits.
- Whether the marketplace's use of results for eligibility decisions creates employment-like decision duties.

## Open questions

- Whether sellers are independent businesses or individuals.
- The exact screening package (identity + sanctions only, or also criminal-record and adverse-media checks).
- Which countries are in scope.
- Report retention period and dispute/false-match handling.
- How long will reports be retained, and how will disputes and false matches be handled?
