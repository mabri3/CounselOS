      ## Beneficial-owner — what''s at stake in this design

      The proposed flow takes the **W-9 signer''s name only** for business sellers
      and collects **no owners**. That''s the gap. Here''s the analysis.

      ### The core problem: the W-9 signer is not the beneficial owner

      A W-9 captures the entity''s tax identification and the person signing the form
      — typically an authorized representative. It does **not** identify who actually
      owns or controls the entity. So "we take the signer''s name" gives you a name
      that may have nothing to do with the person whose identity matters for AML/sanctions
      purposes. That''s the fundamental flaw.

      ### What the law requires (US)

      **FinCEN CDD Rule (31 CFR 1010.230)** — if Mosaic Relay is a covered financial
      institution (which it likely is as a money transmitter/MSB), it must identify
      and verify beneficial owners of legal entity customers **at account opening**:

      - **Ownership prong:** any individual owning 25% or more of the entity''s equity.

      - **Control prong:** a single individual with significant responsibility to
      control/manage/direct the entity (CEO, CFO, managing member, etc.).

      - Requires collecting name, DOB, address, and ID number, and verifying identity.

      **Corporate Transparency Act (CTA, 31 USC 5336)** — a separate, entity-level
      reporting obligation to FinCEN for BOI. Note: the CTA''s reporting requirement
      has been subject to litigation and injunctions; its status has shifted over
      time, so treat the CTA piece as needing current verification rather than settled.

      ### The key tension with this design

      The CDD Rule applies at **account opening** — which here means when Mosaic Relay
      enables payout services for a seller. The design defers *all* identity to a
      $3,000 threshold, and even then only collects the signer''s name, not owners.
      So the beneficial-owner gap is not just a "nice to have" — it''s the same deferred-identity
      problem as the $3,000 threshold, applied to business entities. **Collecting
      the signer''s name at $3,000 does not satisfy the ownership/control prongs.**

      ### The "UI is already 11 screens" argument doesn''t carry

      The design rationale is that adding owner collection is too much UI. But the
      CDD Rule doesn''t have a UI-cost exemption. The practical answer is a **risk-based
      approach**: collect beneficial-owner information at onboarding (name, DOB, address,
      ID), run automated database/sanctions checks, and enable limited activity while
      completing enhanced verification within a short window — rather than collecting
      nothing and deferring everything to $3,000.

      ### What would change this

      - **Working assumption:** Mosaic Relay is a covered financial institution subject
      to the CDD Rule. If it''s structured as a pure technology provider with no money
      transmission (which the licensing question hasn''t resolved), the CDD Rule may
      not apply directly — but the sanctions and reputational risk would still demand
      owner collection, and the bank partner''s program may impose it anyway.

      - **Open fork:** Whether Mosaic Relay can rely on the marketplace to collect/verify
      owners (delegated model) or must do it directly. If delegated, Mosaic Relay
      stays legally responsible and needs strong contractual indemnities and audit
      rights.

      acquiring bank/processor, which may impose owner/control-person disclosure requirements
      beyond the CDD Rule. Those contracts weren''t provided. Also, the CTA''s current
      enforceability status needs verification given the litigation history.

    # What are the virtual-asset money transmission and sanctions requirements for


    ## Question


    What are the virtual-asset money transmission and sanctions requirements for USDC
    payouts to self-hosted wallets in Mexico?


    ## Working Analysis


    **No external authority retrieved**


    ## Matter summary

    Mosaic Relay, a payment infrastructure company, is launching Instant Payouts for
    marketplace sellers, with Product (Priya) targeting a Q1 ship date. The plan includes
    allowing international sellers in Mexico to elect payout in USDC through Circuit,
    a licensed exchange, with sellers providing wallet addresses that may be exchange
    accounts or self-hosted wallets. Counsel is involved because the current design
    defers identity collection until $3,000 in cumulative payouts, runs OFAC screening
    in nightly batch after payouts release, and does not collect beneficial owners
    for business sellers. The Q1 deadline creates pressure to resolve whether the
    USDC payout path can proceed without triggering unlicensed money transmission
    or sanctions violations.

    ## Decision question

    Can Mosaic Relay ship Instant Payouts in Q1 with USDC payouts to self-hosted wallets
    in Mexico, given that sellers currently onboard with only email and bank account,
    identity is not collected until $3,000 in cumulative payouts, OFAC screening runs
    nightly after funds release, and Mosaic Relay operates as an agent under its bank
    partner''s license rather than holding its own money transmitter licenses?

    ## Open questions

    - Does Mosaic Relay''s agent relationship with its bank partner extend to virtual-asset
    payouts, or does initiating USDC transfers to self-hosted wallets require separate
    money transmitter licenses in the US and Mexico?

    - What is the legal status of USDC payouts to self-hosted wallets under Mexican
    law, and does Mexico require specific licensing or registration for virtual-asset
    service providers?

    - Can OFAC screening be performed pre-release for USDC payouts without undermining
    the "instant" promise, given that self-hosted wallets lack the account-freeze
    controls available with exchange accounts?

    - Does the $3,000 identity-collection threshold create a window during which sanctioned
    parties could receive USDC payouts before screening occurs?

    - What are the beneficial-ownership and KYB requirements for business sellers
    receiving USDC payouts, and does the current W-9-only approach satisfy them?

    ## Likely rules/issues

    **US money transmission:** Mosaic Relay''s initiation of USDC payouts likely constitutes
    money transmission under FinCEN guidance, as virtual currency is treated as "value
    that substitutes for currency." Operating as an agent under a bank partner''s
    license may not cover virtual-asset activities, as most bank partner agreements
    address fiat rails only. State money transmitter licenses may be required unless
    an exemption applies.

    **Mexico virtual-asset regulation:** Mexico''s Fintech Law (Ley Fintech) requires
    registration with the CNBV for virtual-asset service providers. USDC payouts to
    Mexican residents may trigger local licensing or registration obligations, particularly
    if Mosaic Relay is deemed to be operating a virtual-asset exchange or transfer
    service.

    **Sanctions compliance:** OFAC''s sanctions apply to US persons and transactions
    with a US nexus. USDC is a US-dollar-denominated stablecoin issued by Circle,
    a US company, creating a clear US nexus. Nightly batch screening after payout
    release violates OFAC''s requirement that screening occur before funds are released
    or blocked. Self-hosted wallets present heightened risk because they lack the
    account controls of exchange accounts.

    **BSA/AML:** The $3,000 threshold for identity collection has no basis in BSA
    requirements. FinCEN''s customer identification program (CIP) rules require identity
    verification before establishing the account relationship, not after a payout
    threshold. For money transmitters, the threshold is $1,000 for certain recordkeeping,
    but CIP applies at account opening.

    **Beneficial ownership:** FinCEN''s beneficial-ownership rule requires collection
    of beneficial owners for legal-entity customers. The W-9-only approach does not
    satisfy this requirement.

    ## Viable paths

    **Path 1: Delay USDC payouts until compliance infrastructure is built.** Postpone
    the USDC payout option until Mosaic Relay obtains necessary licenses, implements
    pre-release OFAC screening, and collects full identity and beneficial-ownership
    information at onboarding. This is the lowest-risk path but may miss the Q1 deadline.

    **Path 2: Restrict USDC payouts to exchange accounts only.** Limit USDC payouts
    to wallets held at licensed exchanges where Circuit or the exchange performs sanctions
    screening and has account-freeze capabilities. This reduces sanctions risk but
