The EU Data Act termination for convenience regime is not a future obligation, it is current law, and every SaaS, PaaS and IaaS contract touching Irish customers or vendors must comply. The two-month notice cap, 30-day minimum data retrieval window, proportionate fee limits and machine-readable export requirements are non-negotiable statutory floors. Irish businesses that have not yet audited and updated their cloud contracts should treat this as an urgent compliance priority. A structured contract review, using the model clauses and checklists set out above, is the most efficient route to closing the gap before Irish enforcement arrangements are finalised.

Specialist information technology lawyers in Ireland can assist with bespoke clause drafting and vendor negotiations tailored to your specific workloads and commercial context.

FAQs

What are the rights of termination in the EU Data Act?

Customers can initiate switching and terminate certain data processing service contracts on notice. Contracts must not prevent switching and must enable migration within statutory notice windows, a maximum of two months, with a minimum 30-day data retrieval period.

How long is notice under the Data Act for switching a cloud provider?

The EU Data Act limits the contractual notice period for initiating a switch to a maximum of two calendar months. Contracts may set a shorter period but cannot require longer notice.

Can vendors charge egress or early termination fees under the Data Act?

Vendors may recover proportionate, documented costs associated with the switching process. However, punitive lock-in fees and disproportionate exit charges are prohibited. Any fee must be objectively justifiable and, in practice, should be itemised and subject to audit.

How is Data Act portability different from GDPR portability?

GDPR Article 20 portability applies to personal data and is exercised by the data subject. Data Act portability is broader: it covers all customer data, including non-personal data and metadata, and is exercised by the contracting entity. The Data Act also imposes active migration-assistance obligations on providers.

What should a SaaS termination clause include after the Data Act?

At minimum: an express switching right, a notice period not exceeding two months, migration assistance obligations, data export format specifications, a retrieval window of at least 30 calendar days, acceptance-testing rights and caps on any exit fees.

Who enforces Data Act obligations in Ireland?

Each Member State must designate one or more competent authorities. As of mid-2026, Ireland’s formal designation is pending. The substantive obligations are directly applicable nonetheless, and customers may invoke the regulation in Irish courts irrespective of the enforcement body’s operational status.

When did the EU Data Act become effective?

The Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) entered into force on 11 January 2024 and became applicable across all EU Member States on 12 September 2025. Irish implementing measures and regulator designations are expected to continue through 2026.
