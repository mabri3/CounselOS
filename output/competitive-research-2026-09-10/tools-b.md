# Legal AI tools and AI-native legal services

Research cut-off: 10 September 2026. Dated claims are limited to information available by that date. Current product pages without a publication date were accessed on 10 September 2026; roadmap statements are labelled separately. “Evidence” below means an observable shipped feature, a product description, a customer workflow, or a dated funding announcement. Marketing claims are labelled as such.

## Executive view

The six main companies split into three useful patterns:

1. **Matter or contract systems of action:** Ironclad and Wordsmith receive work, attach context, run repeatable steps, route approvals, and record the result. They are the closest competitive pressure on a CounselOS workflow from intake to lawyer decision.
2. **Grounded knowledge and drafting:** Lexroom and DeepJudge make an organisation’s legal sources and past work searchable, then turn retrieval into summaries, analysis, or drafts. Their strongest lesson is persistent context plus source traceability.
3. **Vertical, outcome-focused work:** Luminance focuses on business contracts and investigations; Eve focuses on plaintiff-firm cases. Both package domain workflows rather than selling a generic chatbot.

AI-native law firms and legal-service providers are different from ordinary software. They compete through delivered legal work, speed, and predictable pricing. That model is a launch threat if CounselOS remains only an internal tool, and an opportunity if it becomes the matter record and workbench that supports human legal delivery.

Across the market, the adoptable pattern is consistent: capture the request, preserve the source material and prior positions, produce a structured first work product, expose the reasoning and citations, and return the final judgment to a named lawyer. CounselOS should test whether a neutral, persistent matter record that follows work from intake through research, editable work product, and an explicit decision is valuable across practices; this is a product hypothesis, not an established market gap.

## Luminance

### Actual workflow and output

Luminance is a software platform for contract generation, negotiation, post-execution analysis, investigations, arbitration and litigation. Its current product description says it automates contract activity end to end, and its corporate product sheet places AI in generation, negotiation and post-execution analysis ([Luminance](https://www.luminance.com/), [Corporate Product Sheet](https://www.luminance.com/files/brochures/Corporate%20Product%20Sheet%202025%20%28UK%29.pdf)).

The observable output is structured contract intelligence: extracted clauses and obligations, risk or issue identification, review suggestions, negotiation support, and searchable post-signature information. Luminance’s diligence material describes contract review and consolidation work, with review acceleration “as much as 90%”; that percentage is a company claim, not an independent benchmark ([Diligence AI brochure](https://www.luminance.com/files/brochures/Diligence-AI%20for%20Automated%20Contract%20Review-2024.pdf)).

The practical workflow is: ingest a contract set; classify and identify clauses; review against legal or business positions; negotiate or amend; then retain the extracted terms for later obligations, investigations or disputes. That makes Luminance stronger in contract portfolio work than in open-ended legal research.

### Buyers, jurisdictions and practice areas

Luminance reports more than 1,000 organisations in 70 countries, including Big Four firms, more than a quarter of the Global Top 100 law firms, and enterprises such as AMD, BBC Studios, Hitachi and Liberty Mutual ([company](https://www.luminance.com/about/)). Buyers are enterprise legal departments, legal operations teams, procurement-facing contract groups and law firms.

Its main practices are commercial and transactional contracting, corporate diligence, investigations, arbitration and litigation support. Product/privacy/regulatory work appears mainly as contract and compliance review rather than a dedicated regulatory research product.

### Software versus delivered service

Luminance is a SaaS/software platform. It sells the product and enterprise implementation rather than acting as outside counsel. The lawyer or legal team remains responsible for the legal position and the final negotiation.

### Funding

The latest confirmed round is a $75m Series C led by Point72 Private Investments, announced in February 2025. The company said the round brought the amount raised in the preceding 12 months to more than $115m; TechCrunch reported total funding of $165m. This is equity financing, not debt, and no public valuation was identified ([Luminance](https://www.luminance.com/press/luminance-raises-75-million-in-series-c-funding-round-led-by-point72-private-investments/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/18/legal-ai-startup-luminance-backed-by-the-late-mike-lynch-raises-75m/)).

### Shipped features versus marketing

Evidence-backed shipped capabilities include contract generation, negotiation support, post-execution analysis, automated review/diligence and workflows for investigations/arbitration/litigation ([Luminance](https://www.luminance.com/)). The “Legal-Grade” and “Panel of Judges” language is product positioning. Treat the 90% time-saving figure and claims of accuracy as vendor-reported until validated on CounselOS matters.

### CounselOS threat and opportunity

Threat: a well-funded platform already owns a contract corpus, review workflow and enterprise procurement buyer. A matter workspace that only stores documents and produces a memo will look weaker on high-volume contract work.

Opportunity: CounselOS can own the cross-practice matter record. It can link the intake question, source documents, research notes, clauses, assumptions, editable draft, and explicit lawyer decision even when one step uses Luminance-like contract extraction. A small adoptable pattern is a “contract issue packet”: request, affected clause, source and precedent links, proposed language, unresolved question, owner and decision status.

## Ironclad

### Actual workflow and output

Ironclad is a contract lifecycle management (CLM) platform. Its public product description covers a no-code Workflow Designer, contract repository, AI Assist, conversational search, e-signature, dashboards and AI agents ([Ironclad CLM product](https://ironcladapp.com/product/ai-based-contract-management), [FY2026 product descriptions](https://legal.ironcladapp.com/fy2026-product-descriptions)).

The workflow starts with an intake form or third-party contract, routes it through conditional review and approvals, supports drafting and redlining, sends for signature, archives the final agreement, extracts metadata and tracks obligations or renewals. The Intake Agent can read an uploaded third-party contract and suggest answers for launch fields such as counterparty, value, term, auto-renewal, personal-data processing and DPA requirement; the user reviews and confirms those answers ([Intake Agent](https://support.ironcladapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/39616157653911-Intake-Agent-Overview)).

Outputs are a completed contract workflow, redlines, approval and signature history, structured metadata, repository records, renewal briefs and obligation notifications. Ironclad’s product terms explicitly describe source citations and reasoning for archive metadata suggestions subject to human review ([FY2026 product descriptions](https://legal.ironcladapp.com/fy2026-product-descriptions)).

### Buyers, jurisdictions and practice areas

The buyers are enterprise legal operations, commercial lawyers, procurement, sales/deal desk and finance stakeholders. Ironclad describes more than 2,000 customers and more than 2 billion contracts processed on its AI page ([Ironclad AI](https://ironcladapp.com/product/ironclad-ai)). It is a global enterprise product; public materials do not limit it to one jurisdiction.

The core practice is commercial and transactional contracting. Privacy and regulatory work appears in contract intake fields, DPAs, playbooks and obligations. It is not a litigation or general legal-research system.

### Software versus delivered service

Ironclad is software. Customers configure workflows, playbooks, permissions, integrations and approval chains. Implementation and customer success may help, but the legal service is delivered by the customer’s team.

### Funding

Ironclad’s latest publicly reported financing is a $150m Series E announced January 18, 2022, bringing total investment to $333m. Its reported valuation was $3.2bn; valuation is separate from the $333m invested ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ironclad-raises-150-million-series-e-funding-led-by-franklin-templeton-301462915.html), [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/ironclad/)). No later equity or debt round was found by the cut-off.

### Shipped features versus marketing

The Workflow Designer, repository, AI Assist, Smart Import, Intake Agent, Renewal Agent, conversational search, approval routing and e-signature are documented product features. “2+ billion processed contracts” and claims of being the only or deepest CLM are company marketing; use them as scale signals, not proof of outcome. Product descriptions also state that AI suggestions depend on configuration, document quality, permissions, available data, human review and usage limits ([FY2026 product descriptions](https://legal.ironcladapp.com/fy2026-product-descriptions)).

### CounselOS threat and opportunity

Threat: Ironclad defines the category expectation for intake-to-signature, structured state, role routing and repository visibility. A CounselOS matter without a clear owner, status, next step and audit trail will feel unfinished.

Opportunity: CounselOS should borrow the state machine without becoming a CLM clone. Add a light matter lifecycle: request received → context confirmed → research in progress → draft ready → lawyer decision → sent/closed. Keep the record portable across contract, privacy, product, regulatory and litigation work. Ironclad’s Intake Agent also demonstrates the value of pre-filling matter metadata from source documents while showing the lawyer exactly what was inferred.

## Lexroom

### Actual workflow and output

Lexroom is an AI platform for civil-law legal research, document analysis and drafting. Its product page says users can search official, current and certified sources, upload contracts/deeds/case files for summaries and actionable suggestions, and draft in Word while retaining control ([Lexroom product](https://www.lexroom.ai/en)).

The platform supports two explicit paths: research → drafting, or drafting → analysis. Its private library lets a team upload its own acts, contracts, legal theory and case law; users can query those documents, compare clauses, summarize them and obtain answers based only on the private corpus ([Lexroom FAQ](https://www.lexroom.ai/en/faq)).

Outputs are cited research answers with links to official sources, clause comparisons, contract summaries, risk/ambiguity lists, generated clauses, opinions and draft legal acts. A customer example describes using uploaded precedents to prepare an opinion and filing, then asking Lexroom to analyse the opponent’s writ and identify weaknesses and counterarguments ([Casavo customer story](https://www.lexroom.ai/en/customers/casavo)).

### Buyers, jurisdictions and practice areas

Lexroom targets lawyers, law firms, in-house legal teams, public administration, notaries, accountants and labour consultants. Its initial strength is civil-law Europe: Italy, Spain and Germany, with expansion to other European jurisdictions. The company says it has more than 8,000 law-firm and corporate users/customers in its 2026 press materials ([Lexroom press](https://www.lexroom.ai/en/press)).

Practice modules include banking and insurance, labour, civil, corporate, family, tax, IP, administrative, privacy/data protection, criminal, consumer, real estate, business crisis and compliance ([Lexroom FAQ](https://www.lexroom.ai/en/faq)). It therefore spans product/privacy/regulatory, corporate/transactional and litigation research more broadly than most contract tools.

### Software versus delivered service

Lexroom is software, with optional training and partner-firm-developed domain modules. It does not present itself as the lawyer of record. Its own customer stories stress that lawyers still review and apply critical judgment ([LM Avvocati customer story](https://www.lexroom.ai/en/customers/lm-law-firm)).

### Funding

Lexroom announced a $50m Series B in May 2026, bringing total raised to $73m. The round was led by Left Lane Capital, with Base10, Eurazeo, Entourage, View, DiHerent and Acurio Ventures named in the company announcement ([Lexroom Series B](https://www.lexroom.ai/en/blog/lexroom-raises-50-million-series-b-funding-to-redesign-europes-legal-ai-infrastructure)). This is reported equity funding; no debt facility or public valuation was identified. A January 2026 report cited an €80m valuation before the Series B, but that is a secondary report and should not be treated as a current post-money valuation ([Cinco Días](https://cincodias.elpais.com/companias/2026-01-23/lexroom-ultima-el-lanzamiento-en-espana-de-su-ia-para-bufetes-de-abogados.html)).

### Shipped features versus marketing

The product page, FAQ and customer stories provide concrete evidence of natural-language research, official-source links, private libraries, document analysis, clause comparison, Word drafting and linked workflows. “No risk of hallucinations” is a marketing claim; the defensible feature is that answers cite and link to the source set, with a private-corpus mode. The product’s current source coverage is civil-law Europe, so do not infer US coverage from generic “legal AI” wording.

### CounselOS threat and opportunity

Threat: Lexroom can move from question to cited research to editable draft in one workflow, and it has a broad practice taxonomy. CounselOS will lose users if research output is detached from the matter record or if sources are not easy to inspect.

Opportunity: adopt a source ledger and dual-path workflow. Every research output should retain source URL/document, jurisdiction, date accessed, quoted proposition, confidence or verification status, and the resulting draft section. Let lawyers begin with research or with a draft, then preserve the transition. This directly supports CounselOS’s persistent-context and source-change goals.

## Eve

### Actual workflow and output

Eve is a vertical AI platform for plaintiff law firms. Its AI Intake product answers calls 24/7, transcribes communications, collects lead details, scores and qualifies cases, and integrates with CRMs such as Clio and MyCase ([Eve AI Intake](https://www.eve.legal/intake)). Its platform pages list case intake and evaluation, medical overviews, demand-letter drafting, propounding discovery and responding to discovery.

The workflow is case-centric: capture a prospective client; qualify and score the lead; organize communications and medical information; prepare litigation documents; and support discovery. The product company says Eve processes more than 200,000 legal cases annually and has helped firms recover more than $3.5bn in settlements and judgments; those are vendor-reported outcome claims ([Eve company blog](https://www.eve.legal/blogs/the-next-chapter-in-ai-transformation-for-law-firms)).

Outputs include an intake record, transcript, lead score, case evaluation, medical overview, demand letter and discovery documents. The output is operational legal work for a plaintiff firm, rather than a general memo or source-grounded legal research answer.

### Buyers, jurisdictions and practice areas

The buyer is a plaintiff law firm, especially a personal-injury practice. Eve also lists labor and employment as a practice area. The public product is primarily US plaintiff litigation; its sources do not establish broad multi-jurisdiction capability.

### Software versus delivered service

Eve is software sold to firms. The firm’s lawyers remain responsible for client representation, strategy, settlement and filing decisions. Eve’s value comes from vertical workflows, not from acting as outside counsel.

### Funding

Eve announced a $103m Series B on September 30, 2025 at a valuation above $1bn, led by Spark Capital with existing investors participating. The disclosed financing history is $14m seed in 2023, $47m Series A on January 16, 2025, and $103m Series B, for $164m total equity reported by Sacra. No debt facility was identified ([Eve/PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/eve-raises-103-million-at-1-billion-valuation-to-help-plaintiff-firms-deliver-justice-through-ai-transformation-302570807.html), [Series A/Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/01/16/exclusive-eve-ai-legal-platform-raises-47-million-series-a-led-by-andreessen-horowitz/), [funding history](https://sacra.com/c/eve/)).

### Shipped features versus marketing

AI Intake’s voice answering, transcription, forms, lead scoring, case qualification and CRM integration are concrete features. The named workflows for medical overviews, demand letters and discovery are concrete product scope. “Deliver justice” and settlement totals are positioning or vendor-reported outcomes; validate them with customer references before relying on them.

### CounselOS threat and opportunity

Threat: Eve shows how quickly a focused vertical can own the entire workflow and measure value in cases handled, leads converted and documents produced. A generic matter workspace may feel too abstract for a lawyer with a repeatable case type.

Opportunity: CounselOS can support vertical “matter recipes” without hard-coding one practice. A recipe should define intake fields, source bundles, work-product templates, decision checkpoints and deadlines. Start with one product-counsel recipe and one privacy recipe, then let teams add practice-specific fields. Eve’s lead-score and case-evaluation pattern also suggests a useful “triage summary” that explains why a matter is urgent, risky or ready for lawyer review.

## DeepJudge

### Actual workflow and output

DeepJudge begins with permission-aware enterprise search across a firm’s document management, Microsoft 365 and other systems. Its Knowledge Search classifies documents, detects near duplicates and redlines, searches across clients, matters, documents and people, and respects existing access permissions and ethical walls ([DeepJudge product](https://www.deepjudge.ai/product)).

AI Workflows sit on top of the index. Built-in workflows can produce narrative reports, answer questions over documents, summarize matters and identify prior positions. Firms can build low-code/no-code multi-step workflows and agents that use internal data, choose their own commercial or open-source models, and keep outputs within the firm’s governance model ([AI Workflows launch](https://www.deepjudge.ai/news/deepjudge-unveils-legal-ai-workflows----the-first-to-seamlessly-connect-ai-to-the-full-scale-of-firms-internal-data)).

Recent shipped capabilities include SuperSearch, which analyses patterns across matters, documents and systems, and Agent Skills, which package a repeatable legal method such as an M&A summary into a reusable workflow that retrieves the right documents and produces a consistent output ([SuperSearch](https://www.deepjudge.ai/news/deepjudge-launches-supersearch), [Agent Skills](https://www.deepjudge.ai/news/agent-skills-are-now-available-in-deepjudge)). The platform also advertises an Agent Handoff Protocol so objective, materials, history and work product can move between AI experiences ([DeepJudge ecosystem](https://www.deepjudge.ai/ecosystem)).

### Buyers, jurisdictions and practice areas

DeepJudge sells to large law firms and legal departments. Customers named in product material include Freshfields, Gunderson Dettmer, CMS Switzerland, Wenger Vieli, Homburger, Lenz & Staehelin and others ([DeepJudge product](https://www.deepjudge.ai/product)). Because it searches the customer’s own internal corpus, the practice range is broad: M&A and transactional precedent, litigation and opposing-counsel history, commercial contracting, regulatory and compliance knowledge, client/matter overviews and business development.

Its jurisdiction is determined by the customer’s corpus and deployment. It supports cloud, private-cloud and on-premises deployment, which matters for firms with data-residency or confidentiality constraints ([DeepJudge security](https://www.deepjudge.ai/security)).

### Software versus delivered service

DeepJudge is enterprise software, with integrations and implementation support. It is not outside counsel and does not take responsibility for the legal advice produced by a customer’s firm.

### Funding

DeepJudge’s own announcement confirms a $10.7m seed round led by Coatue, announced in June 2024, and its own Series A announcement confirms a $41.2m Series A led by Felicis in 2025. Disclosed equity funding is therefore at least $51.9m. No debt or valuation was identified ([seed](https://www.deepjudge.ai/news/deepjudge-raises-10-7m-seed-round-led-by-coatue-and-announces-knowledge-assistant), [Series A](https://www.deepjudge.ai/news/future-of-legal-ai-is-here-it-lives-in-your-knowledge)).

### Shipped features versus marketing

Search across connected repositories, permissions/ethical walls, automatic classification, near-duplicate/redline detection, Knowledge Assistant, AI Workflows, Workflow Builder, SuperSearch, Agent Skills, connectors and deployment choices are concrete product claims with documentation. “Only” and “most powerful” descriptions are marketing. The key technical risk is retrieval quality and source completeness; DeepJudge itself explains that generative output quality depends on the underlying search and recommends human verification ([seed announcement](https://www.deepjudge.ai/news/deepjudge-raises-10-7m-seed-round-led-by-coatue-and-announces-knowledge-assistant)).

### CounselOS threat and opportunity

Threat: DeepJudge is a direct answer to the problem of fragmented institutional knowledge. If CounselOS stores matter context but cannot bring prior advice, source documents and precedents into the current matter, it will duplicate the problem.

Opportunity: make source continuity a first-class object. A CounselOS matter should have connected sources, permissions, a source snapshot, a change log and reusable skills. DeepJudge’s Agent Skills are a strong pattern for turning a lawyer’s method into a visible, editable recipe. Its handoff concept maps closely to CounselOS: preserve the objective, materials, history and work product when moving from intake to research to drafting.

## Wordsmith

### Actual workflow and output

Wordsmith is built exclusively for in-house legal teams. Its agents receive requests from Slack, email, Teams or other business channels; retrieve the company’s policies, playbooks, precedents and entities; perform multi-step work; draft a response or redline; route exceptions for approval; and log each step ([Wordsmith agents](https://www.wordsmith.ai/products/agents)).

Named workflows include sales-contract review and counter-position drafting, NDA triage and drafting, procurement/vendor assessment, privacy reviews and DPIAs, employment paperwork, legal question answering, matter triage, board materials and scheduled compliance checks. The output is a ready-to-send response, redline, draft, risk triage, board summary or compliance result, with escalation reasoning when the request crosses a rule.

Wordsmith’s 2026 product announcements make the matter layer explicit: each job is intended to become a matter with owner, SLA, stakeholders and context. The roadmap says the inbox was targeted for Q2 2026 and the matter layer for Q3 2026; treat roadmap timing as a disclosed plan unless a later shipped notice confirms availability ([June 3, 2026 announcement](https://www.wordsmith.ai/blog/wordsmith-ai-raises-70-million-series-b)). The same announcement describes reconciliation between playbooks, obligations, spend and decisions, which is a strong operating-model signal but not proof that every element was live at cut-off.

### Buyers, jurisdictions and practice areas

Buyers are GCs, heads of legal operations and in-house lawyers. Wordsmith states it is designed for in-house teams rather than law firms, and its customers include BT, Financial Times, Sage, Starling, Canva and Safelite. Its research product combines hundreds of trusted sources across 130+ jurisdictions with the company’s entities, risk profile and prior positions ([Wordsmith company](https://www.wordsmith.ai/company), [Microsoft customer feature](https://ukstories.microsoft.com/features/wordsmith-ai-helping-in-house-legal-teams-beat-the-clock/)).

Practice areas include commercial contracting, procurement, privacy/data protection and DPIAs, employment, regulatory/compliance and general legal operations. It also supports cross-functional intake from sales, HR, security and procurement.

### Software versus delivered service

Wordsmith is software. It may support initial configuration and pre-built agents, but the customer’s legal team sets the playbook and decides the level of autonomy. Its product explicitly returns exceptions to the lawyer and records the reason for escalation. Its Consilio partnership also shows that the platform can sit inside a legal-delivery model supporting legal operations delivery (a managed service/LOD relationship), rather than being limited to self-operated software ([Consilio partnership](https://www.wordsmith.ai/blog/consilio-wordsmith)).

### Funding

Wordsmith announced a $70m Series B on June 3, 2026, taking total funding to $100m. On August 5, 2026 it announced a further $14m Series B extension led by Intact Private Capital, with FT Ventures joining as an investor. That gives at least $114m of disclosed equity funding by the cut-off. No debt facility or valuation was reported in these announcements ([Series B](https://www.wordsmith.ai/blog/wordsmith-ai-raises-70-million-series-b), [extension](https://www.wordsmith.ai/blog/wordsmith-extends-series-b-with-investment-from-intact-private-capital-and-ft-ventures)).

### Shipped features versus marketing

Agents, playbooks, intake, contract review/redlining, repositories, research, Word/Slack/Microsoft 365 integration, logging, escalation and multi-jurisdiction research are documented product capabilities. Claims such as seven-figure savings, 14x revenue growth and 500+ companies are company-reported performance signals, not independent validation. The roadmap language is especially important: “matter layer” and cost reconciliation were described as planned milestones, so do not represent every roadmap item as shipped.

### CounselOS threat and opportunity

Threat: Wordsmith is the closest product narrative to CounselOS. It treats legal as an operating function, captures requests from wherever the business works, creates a matter, applies institutional judgment and escalates only where a lawyer must decide. The overlap is direct in product/privacy/regulatory work: Wordsmith lists privacy reviews and DPIAs, scheduled compliance checks, board-material preparation and matter triage as agent use cases ([Wordsmith agents](https://www.wordsmith.ai/products/agents)). Its Starling customer announcement also positions the platform for regulatory and data-privacy work in an in-house team ([Starling](https://www.wordsmith.ai/blog/starling-selects-wordsmith-as-the-ai-platform-for-its-in-house-legal-team)). Wordsmith is therefore a direct launch threat, not only a contract-review competitor. Consilio’s partnership shows a second route: the same workflow layer can support legal-service delivery as well as internal legal teams ([Consilio](https://www.wordsmith.ai/blog/consilio-wordsmith)).

Opportunity: differentiate on lawyer-controlled matter depth and source integrity. CounselOS can be the neutral workbench for product counsel and other specialist matters, with explicit decisions, editable work product, persistent source history and local ownership. Adopt Wordsmith’s simple pattern: every request gets an owner, SLA, stakeholders, context and next step. Make the decision object more visible and portable than an agent’s hidden escalation. CounselOS should test whether a portable, lawyer-owned matter record that connects the business request, jurisdiction-specific sources, editable work, decision and source changes across product, privacy, regulatory and commercial matters creates a durable advantage.

## What CounselOS should adopt

The market supports five focused product patterns:

1. **One matter object across the workflow.** Borrow Wordsmith’s owner/SLA/stakeholder/context model and Ironclad’s visible workflow state. A matter should not disappear into a chat or an agent run.
2. **Source-backed work as a default.** Borrow Lexroom’s official-source links and DeepJudge’s permission-aware retrieval. Store source identity, access date, jurisdiction, proposition and verification state beside the generated analysis.
3. **Editable footholds, not final-answer theatre.** Produce a structured first draft, clause comparison, issue map, research memo or decision brief that a lawyer can edit. Preserve the original generated output and the lawyer’s changes.
4. **Explicit decision checkpoints.** Borrow the human handoff from Wordsmith, DeepJudge and Ironclad. Record who decided, what was decided, which assumptions were accepted, what remains open and when the decision may need review.
5. **Practice recipes with shared infrastructure.** Borrow Eve’s vertical workflow depth and DeepJudge’s reusable Agent Skills. Keep the core matter model stable while recipes define intake fields, source bundles, work-product templates, deadlines and decision points for product, privacy, regulatory, commercial, corporate and litigation work.

The best initial wedge is a product-counsel matter recipe: request intake from Slack/email or a form; automatic context packet; source and precedent ledger; issue map; editable recommendation or draft; explicit lawyer decision; and persistent change record. It is small enough to ship, and it tests whether a durable bridge from a raw legal request to a defensible human decision creates measurable lawyer value.
