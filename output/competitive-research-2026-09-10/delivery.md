# Legal-work delivery companies

Research cut-off: 10 September 2026. “Delivered” means the company supplies legal work, a regulated law firm, or an embedded legal-services team. “Software” means a product sold for a customer’s own legal team or law firm. Funding figures separate disclosed equity rounds from acquisitions, debt, and conditional commitments. Where a company does not publish a total, this report says so.

## Executive findings

The nine names split into four delivery patterns:

1. **AI-native firms for company work:** Crosby, Moritz, and General Legal sell completed commercial/corporate work with attorneys supervising agents. They are closest to the CounselOS product-counsel workflow, but they deliver the last mile themselves.
2. **A vertical firm or network plus operating software:** Lawhive serves consumer and small-business matters through partner firms; Carta Law serves private-capital legal/compliance work after acquiring Avantia; Eudia combines enterprise software, managed services, and an Arizona alternative-business-structure law firm.
3. **Regulatory/compliance infrastructure plus an affiliated firm:** Norm Ai sells reusable regulatory agents and Norm Law sells institutional legal services.
4. **Narrow, high-volume delivery:** Garfield automates English/Welsh small-debt claims; LegalOS automates U.S. employment-visa petitions. Both have a clear intake-to-filing path, but neither is a general product-counsel system.

The strongest reusable product patterns are: matter intake that starts from business records; persistent, client-specific memory; agent work that produces editable, cited artifacts; visible status and audit trails; human approval at irreversible steps; and outcome or fixed pricing. Public materials provide uneven detail on how each company records the lawyer’s final decision, so CounselOS should make that state explicit and inspectable.

## Crosby

**Identity and model.** Crosby is a U.S. Professional Limited Liability Company and “AI-native law firm,” not a stand-alone copilot. It advertises 50+ licensed attorneys, malpractice insurance, fixed rates by document, and “completed work” as the buying unit ([Crosby home](https://crosby.ai/)).

**Buyer and jurisdictions.** The buyer is a fast-growing company’s head of legal, commercial counsel, procurement, sales, or operations. The public site names work for Cursor, Ramp, Cognition, and Fortune 500 customers. The firm is U.S.-based; its site lists lawyers and work with international transaction experience, but does not publish a complete jurisdiction map. Treat global coverage as unverified.

**Practice and work product.** The clear focus is commercial contracting: NDAs, MSAs, SOWs, BAAs, vendor, distribution, SaaS, licensing, and data-rights terms. The attorney roster and site also show private-equity transactions, financings, acquisitions, divestitures, and IP/technology transactions ([Crosby services and team](https://crosby.ai/)). Public evidence is strongest for review and negotiation through signature; it is weaker for product counseling, privacy programs, standalone regulatory advice, and litigation.

**Funding.** Crosby announced a **$60 million Series B on 31 March 2026**, led by Lux and Index with Sequoia, Elad Gil, 01 Advisors, and Bain Capital Ventures ([Series B announcement](https://crosby.ai/blog/series-b-planting-our-flag)). Axios separately reported a **$20 million Series A** and a prior **$5.8 million seed** ([Series A](https://www.axios.com/newsletters/axios-pro-rata-46ff2b15-12ab-43b0-a71b-5d7f86dd53dc); [seed](https://www.axios.com/newsletters/axios-pro-rata-143fd942-6084-40da-9c54-36c23645c95a)). Publicly disclosed equity totals are therefore **at least $85.8 million**. No debt, acquisition, or conditional commitment was found.

**Shipped differentiators.** Human attorneys direct agents; the system uses negotiated market data, custom persistent memory, and a client platform to oversee work. The announced roadmap includes counterparty-redline simulation and voice agents that negotiate for clients ([Series B announcement](https://crosby.ai/blog/series-b-planting-our-flag)). The evidence is company-reported; public customer performance examples are testimonials rather than independent outcome studies.

**CounselOS relevance.** Copy the “buy completed work” framing, fixed document pricing, persistent company memory, and lawyer escalation for edge cases. CounselOS should stay upstream: intake, issue map, research, editable work, and an explicit lawyer decision record. Crosby’s public product does not show that decision record as a first-class artifact.

## Carta / Carta Law

**Identity and model.** “Carta” is primarily the private-capital software company. The relevant legal-work delivery business is **Carta Law, formerly Avantia Law**, acquired by Carta in May 2026. Carta Law is an AI-native law firm and legal/compliance service for private-capital managers, not merely Carta’s cap-table software ([Carta acquisition announcement](https://www.avantialaw.com/news/carta-launches-carta-law-with-acquisition-of-avantia)).

**Buyer and jurisdictions.** Buyers are venture and private-equity funds, asset managers, and fund operations/legal teams. Carta says Avantia served 200+ global asset managers, including 30% of the world’s largest funds ([Carta Law overview](https://carta.com/uk/en/events/carta-law/)). The word “global” is used publicly; exact attorney-admission and matter-by-matter jurisdiction coverage is not published.

**Practice and work product.** The focus is fund and private-capital legal/compliance: routine contracts and NDAs, KYC/AML, LP transfers, diligence/data-room workflows, fund formation and administration support, and regulatory operations. Carta positions the offering as a unified legal, compliance, and fund-accounting workflow ([Carta Law overview](https://carta.com/uk/en/events/carta-law/)). This is transactional and regulatory work for funds, not general product counsel, privacy, or litigation.

**Funding and transaction.** The **Avantia acquisition price was undisclosed**; it is an acquisition, not a funding round ([acquisition announcement](https://www.avantialaw.com/news/carta-launches-carta-law-with-acquisition-of-avantia)). Carta parent’s latest widely reported equity round is **$500 million Series G in August 2021** at a $7.4 billion valuation ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2021/08/13/carta-valuation-fundraising)). CB Insights reports approximately **$1.159 billion across 14 rounds**, but this is a third-party estimate rather than a current company disclosure ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/eshares/financials)). Do not add the acquisition value to equity funding.

**Shipped differentiators.** The distinctive feature is workflow adjacency: legal/compliance work is connected to fund accounting, CRM, LP engagement, and portfolio analytics. Carta markets firm playbooks and AI workflows that preserve legal defensibility ([Carta Law overview](https://carta.com/uk/en/events/carta-law/)). Evidence is largely company-provided; independent details on delivered legal documents are limited.

**CounselOS relevance.** The lesson is to bind legal work to the operating system where the business already acts. CounselOS can connect matter context to product, sales, procurement, and risk records, while preserving a lawyer-owned decision log. Carta’s narrow fund specialization also shows why practice-area playbooks matter.

## Lawhive

**Identity and model.** Lawhive is a legal-services firm and management-services organization (MSO) with an AI operating system. Licensed attorneys at Lawhive partner firms deliver the legal service; the platform handles intake, drafting, research, case management, and payments ([Lawhive partner model](https://partners.lawhive.com/); [Lawhive home](https://lawhive.com/)).

**Buyer and jurisdictions.** Its core buyers are individuals, small businesses, and the small “Main Street” firms that serve them. It began in the U.K. and is expanding in the U.S.; Fortune reported about 500 lawyers across three regulated firms, two U.K. and one Arizona ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/05/lawhive-ai-law-firm-startup-series-b-venture-funding/)). U.S. state-law matters outside Arizona are co-counseled with local firms ([Lawhive home](https://lawhive.com/)).

**Practice and work product.** Publicly listed areas include family, immigration, plaintiff personal injury, employment, trust and estate planning, small business, intellectual property, landlord/tenant, civil litigation, consumer rights, and property transactions ([Lawhive partner page](https://partners.lawhive.com/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/05/lawhive-ai-law-firm-startup-series-b-venture-funding/)). Work products include consultations, demand letters, pleadings and other court documents, contracts, property documents, and case updates. Product counsel, privacy, regulatory, and complex corporate transactions are not the center of the public proposition.

**Funding.** Lawhive raised **$11.9 million seed in April 2024** and **$40 million Series A on 4 December 2024** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/04/lawhive-raises-40m-to-go-after-main-street-us-lawyers/)), then **$60 million Series B on 5 February 2026**, led by Mitch Rales ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/05/lawhive-ai-law-firm-startup-series-b-venture-funding/)). Publicly disclosed dollar-denominated equity rounds total **at least $111.9 million**. TechCrunch also reports a prior **£1.3 million pre-seed**, kept separate because it was reported in GBP ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/15/lawhive-raises-12m-to-expand-its-legaltech-ai-platform-for-small-firms/)). No debt, acquisition, or conditional commitment was found in the cited reports.

**Shipped differentiators.** The platform’s advantage is an AI paralegal/operating system used by a network of human firms, with flat fees and real-time digital case updates. It compresses routine consumer-law delivery rather than selling software alone ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/05/lawhive-ai-law-firm-startup-series-b-venture-funding/)).

**CounselOS relevance.** The intake-to-status experience and local-law routing are useful patterns. CounselOS should add a stronger matter-context layer and explicit lawyer decision capture for product/legal questions. Lawhive’s consumer/general-practice focus is evidence against starting with an undifferentiated all-practice marketplace.

## Garfield AI / Garfield.Law

**Identity and model.** Garfield.Law is an SRA-authorised English law firm that delivers legal services through an AI-powered litigation assistant. The SRA calls it the first purely AI-based firm authorised to provide regulated legal services in England and Wales ([SRA announcement](https://www.sra.org.uk/garfield-ai)).

**Buyer and jurisdiction.** Buyers are sole traders, SMEs, and enterprises recovering unpaid invoices. The scope is England and Wales small claims, generally debts up to £10,000 ([Garfield product page](https://www.garfield.law/)).

**Practice and work product.** This is narrow commercial debt-recovery litigation: invoice and contract intake; polite chasers; letters before action; Claim Form and Particulars of Claim; default-judgment applications; settlement correspondence; defence handling; and trial preparation ([Garfield launch](https://www.garfield.law/press/launch)). The SRA states that a client must approve each step, human solicitors remain accountable, and the system does not propose relevant case law because that is high risk ([SRA announcement](https://www.sra.org.uk/garfield-ai)).

**Funding.** No publicly disclosed equity round, debt, acquisition, or committed financing was found. Garfield’s own material stresses cautious funding; absence of evidence is not evidence of zero funding.

**Shipped differentiators.** Accounting integrations (Xero, Sage, QuickBooks, FreeAgent), bulk claims, team review/approve/edit/pause, and highly granular low fees are shipped features ([multiple claims](https://www.garfield.law/multiple-claims); [product page](https://www.garfield.law/)). Its regulated approval and approval-at-each-step model are unusually concrete. Public evidence covers a narrow claim type and should not be generalized to broad legal reasoning.

**CounselOS relevance.** Garfield is the clearest example of bounded autonomy with an explicit approval gate and visible matter state. CounselOS can use the same pattern for sending, filing, or recording a lawyer decision, while keeping research and editable work broader than Garfield’s template-like path.

## Moritz (moritzlegal.com)

**Identity and ambiguity.** This profile refers to **Moritz, the Y Combinator-backed AI-native law firm at moritzlegal.com**, not unrelated lawyers or researchers named Moritz. It is a California law firm with an MSO for non-legal operations ([Moritz terms](https://www.moritzlegal.com/terms)).

**Buyer and jurisdictions.** Buyers are in-house legal and operations teams at fast-moving companies. Moritz claims work across 44 countries and 200+ teams; the engagement letter confirms a California firm and says matter coverage is transactional and commercial. Exact local-law staffing by country is not fully published, so treat the 44-country figure as a company claim ([Moritz home](https://www.moritzlegal.com/)).

**Practice and work product.** Commercial: procurement, MSAs, SOWs, BAAs. Privacy: DPAs, DPIAs, outsourced DPO services. Corporate/M&A: diligence, term sheets, engagement letters, NDAs. Litigation support: discovery, small claims, and diligence. It also lists real-estate leases/subleases/assignments and employment contracts, terminations, and option plans ([Moritz home](https://www.moritzlegal.com/)). The engagement letter excludes in-court representation, tax, IP prosecution, and corporate-finance advice ([Moritz engagement letter](https://www.moritzlegal.com/engagement-letter)).

**Funding.** Moritz's **$9 million seed round was reported in May 2026**, with YC, 20VC, Urban Innovation Fund, and Inception among the backers ([Nordic9, May 1, 2026](https://nordic9.com/news/moritz-legal-raised-9-million-seed-with-20vc/); [YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/moritz)). Total disclosed equity located is **$9 million**. The exact closing date was not established. No debt, acquisition, or conditional commitment was found.

**Shipped differentiators and limits.** Same-day attorney-reviewed work, fixed pricing generally $250–$2,500, persistent legal workflows/database, and enterprise security certifications are public features ([Moritz home](https://www.moritzlegal.com/)). The engagement letter grants a broad, continuing de-identified-data license for AI development with no opt-out and discloses the MSO’s financial interest ([Moritz engagement letter](https://www.moritzlegal.com/engagement-letter)); this is a material diligence issue for confidential product matters.

**CounselOS relevance.** Moritz is a close comparator for product-counsel throughput: broad commercial/privacy/corporate artifacts, fixed prices, and senior judgment at the edge. CounselOS should make data-use choices and decision provenance clearer and user-controlled. Moritz’s exclusion of corporate finance and court representation shows the value of an explicit scope map.

## Norm Ai / Norm Law

**Identity and model.** Norm Ai is a legal-technology company; Norm Law, LLP is its independent affiliated law firm. Norm Ai sells regulatory/compliance AI and legal engineering. Norm Law supplies legal advice through licensed attorneys ([Norm technology](https://www.norm.ai/technology)).

**Buyer and jurisdictions.** Buyers are global institutional clients, especially financial-services, insurance, and other highly regulated enterprises. Norm reports client institutions managing more than $35 trillion in combined assets and identifies Blackstone as a client/example ([Norm home](https://www.norm.ai/); [Norm Law](https://www.norm.ai/resources/norm-law)). The public material does not provide a complete jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction service list; regulatory coverage is evidently multi-jurisdictional but should be verified per matter.

**Practice and work product.** Regulatory/compliance is the center: regulated-content review, compliance determinations, contract/NDAs/side-letter review, disclosures, substantiation, DDQ/RFP completion, and supervisory AI. The platform turns law, regulation, policy, and handbooks into explainable agents; outputs include audit-ready questionnaire answers, citations, version history, exports to original format/PDF/Excel, and contract flags ([Norm technology](https://www.norm.ai/technology); [DDQ/RFP](https://www.norm.ai/resources/introducing-norm-ai-ddq-and-rfp-completion)). Norm Law is described as a full-service AI-native law firm, but public specific delivered matters are sparse.

**Funding.** Norm’s official announcement on **7 July 2026** reports a **$120 million Series C at a $1.2 billion valuation**, led by Khosla Ventures, and says Norm has raised **more than $260 million since founding** ([Series C announcement](https://www.norm.ai/resources/norm-ai-raises-20-million-at-a-1-2-billion-valuation)). Earlier company material reports $48 million from financial institutions in January 2025 and an additional $50 million Blackstone investment in November 2025 ([Norm timeline](https://www.norm.ai/)). The company’s stated “more than $260 million” is the best current total; no debt or acquisition was found.

**Shipped differentiators.** Legal Engineering Automation Platform (LEAP) lets attorneys encode regulations into no-code agents; every action, edit, approval, reassignment, citation, and version change can be captured. Norm embeds review in Microsoft Word and PowerPoint and maintains institutional memory ([Microsoft integration](https://www.norm.ai/resources/norm-ai-and-microsoft); [Legal Engineering](https://www.norm.ai/resources/legal-engineering-a-paradigm-shift-in-law)).

**CounselOS relevance.** Norm is the strongest model for turning research and policy into reusable, explainable checks and audit trails. CounselOS should borrow the structured precedent, citations, versioning, and embedded work-product review, but keep recommendations separate from the lawyer’s recorded decision. Norm’s enterprise-regulatory orientation is broader and more automated than a local product-counsel MVP needs.

**Shipped differentiators and limits.** Published review and negotiation price points, Slack-native interaction, and direct attorney/AI-firm delivery are the clear differentiators. Evidence is primarily company/YC and founder-reported. The narrow public work catalog and no-litigation boundary limit claims of a full-service firm.

**CounselOS relevance.** General Legal validates fast intake through a familiar channel and a simple fixed-price artifact. CounselOS can combine that delivery surface with persistent matter context, grounded research, editable drafts, and a clear record of recommendation versus lawyer decision. Slack delivery is a useful notification/output surface; the durable matter record remains central.

It acquired Johnson Hana, a managed legal-services provider with 300+ consultants across the UK, U.S., and EU, and opened Eudia Counsel under Arizona’s alternative-business-structure program ([Eudia announcement](https://www.eudia.com/blog/the-next-chapter-of-human-ai-collaboration-in-legal-has-begun); [Arizona Supreme Court order](https://www.azcourts.gov/Portals/0/22/admorder/Orders25/2025-132.pdf?ver=oBRGA7p83fIYqM6x4h_8CA%3D%3D)).

**Buyer and jurisdictions.** Buyers are Fortune 500 chief legal officers, legal operations, compliance, procurement, and business teams. Delivery spans the U.S., UK, and EU through the Johnson Hana organization; the exact attorney coverage of Eudia Counsel varies by matter.

**Practice and work product.** Eudia’s platform applications cover contracts, compliance, M&A, litigation, and insights. Johnson Hana’s delivery capabilities include managed legal services, contracting/compliance, data privacy, litigation support, and secondments embedded in in-house teams ([Johnson Hana acquisition announcement](https://www.eudia.com/blog/the-next-chapter-of-human-ai-collaboration-in-legal-has-begun)). Work products include contract review, reasoned redlines, precedent-based first drafts, amended-and-restated agreements, compliance analysis, M&A support, litigation strategy, and institutional insights ([contracting](https://www.eudia.com/blog/contract-review-redlining-drafting-in-eudia); [Company Brain](https://www.eudia.com/blog/the-company-brain-how-eudia-turns-enterprise-legal-data-into-institutional-intelligence)).

**Funding and transaction.** Eudia announced **up to $105 million Series A in February 2025**, led by General Catalyst ([funding announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/eudia-secures-up-to-105m-in-series-a-funding-led-by-general-catalyst-to-transform-legal-work-through-ai-powered-augmented-intelligence-302375331.html)). “Up to” is a conditional/maximum commitment, so report **up to $105 million equity Series A**, not necessarily $105 million cash funded; no reliable total beyond that was found. The Johnson Hana acquisition is a separate transaction with no public price. No debt was found.

**Shipped differentiators.** The Company Brain structures clauses, obligations, entities, relationships, negotiation history, and decisions into institutional memory. Its API-first architecture works in Word, Outlook, ServiceNow, and enterprise repositories; edits, approvals, overrides, and decisions feed the learning loop ([Company Brain](https://www.eudia.com/blog/the-company-brain-how-eudia-turns-enterprise-legal-data-into-institutional-intelligence)). Eudia’s security center lists SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, and ISO 42001 ([trust center](https://trust.eudia.com/)).

**CounselOS relevance.** Eudia is the strongest comparator for persistent matter context and decision memory at enterprise scale. CounselOS can use the same “connect, structure, reason, learn” shape on a smaller local workspace. The risk is overbuilding a broad enterprise graph before proving the single-matter product-counsel loop.

## LegalOS (legalos.ai)

**Identity and ambiguity.** This profile refers to **LegalOS.ai, the YC Winter 2026 AI-native U.S. immigration law firm**, not LawVu’s June 2026 “LegalOS” operating-system announcement and not the South African legal directory at legalos.co.za. LegalOS.ai explicitly says LegalOS Inc. is not itself a law firm; licensed immigration attorneys review and sign work ([LegalOS about](https://www.legalos.ai/about-us); [YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/legalos)).

**Buyer and jurisdiction.** Buyers are startup founders, companies hiring global talent, and managers sponsoring U.S. workers. The service is U.S. immigration; no non-U.S. immigration practice is evidenced.

**Practice and work product.** O-1A/O-1B, H-1B, L-1A, L-1B, TN, EB-1A, EB-1C, and EB-2 NIW petitions. The AI agents intake documents and answers, draft petition narratives, compile evidence, anticipate USCIS objections, and produce filing-ready petitions; attorneys review and sign ([YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/legalos); [company story](https://www.legalos.ai/post/legalos-yc-w26)). LegalOS claims to have studied 12,000 successful petitions and to deliver in as little as 48 hours, with dozens filed and a 100% approval rate so far; these are early company claims, not an independent outcome study.

**Funding.** LegalOS joined **YC Winter 2026**, announced 12 March 2026 ([company announcement](https://www.legalos.ai/post/legalos-yc-w26)). No separate round amount, total, debt, acquisition, or conditional commitment was publicly disclosed in the cited sources. Do not infer YC’s standard deal amount without a company-specific disclosure.

**Shipped differentiators.** Narrow specialization, 24 specialized agents, training data from the founders’ family immigration practice, rapid document intake, status transparency, and attorney sign-off. The service is a full immigration filing workflow, not generic legal software.

**CounselOS relevance.** LegalOS shows how a narrow matter type can make intake, evidence collection, draft generation, objection anticipation, and attorney review concrete. CounselOS should use this depth for product-counsel matter templates, but avoid claiming a universal legal agent before it has practice-specific evidence and decision points.

## Comparative takeaways for CounselOS

| Capability | Strongest evidence | CounselOS implication |
|---|---|---|
| Intake from business records | Garfield accounting imports; Lawhive consumer intake; LegalOS document intake | Start matters from contracts, tickets, product specs, and policy records, not a blank chat. |
| Persistent context | Crosby custom memory; Eudia Company Brain; Norm institutional memory | Keep matter facts, prior decisions, playbooks, and citations reusable over time. |
| Editable deliverables | Crosby contract work; Eudia redlines/drafts; LegalOS petitions | Produce files a lawyer can revise and send, with source links and assumptions. |
| Explicit approval | Garfield requires approval at each step; LegalOS attorney sign-off; Norm audit trail | Treat send/file/sign/record-decision as distinct approval events. |
| Practice-specific scope | Carta fund workflows; Garfield debt claims; LegalOS visas; Moritz exclusions | Use clear issue maps and matter templates; do not imply unsupported jurisdiction or practice coverage. |
| Outcome pricing | Crosby fixed document rates; Moritz $250–$2,500; General Legal $500 contracts; Garfield per action | Price or measure completed work units, while preserving the lawyer’s choice and review. |
| Delivery channel | General Legal Slack; Eudia Word/Outlook/ServiceNow; Carta private-capital stack | Meet lawyers where work starts, but retain one durable matter record. |

The market evidence supports a CounselOS wedge around the “organized, researched, editable foothold” before the lawyer’s decision. The companies differ in how much of that workflow they expose publicly; CounselOS can make known facts, uncertainty, recommendations, editable work product, and the lawyer’s decision visible in one durable matter workspace.
