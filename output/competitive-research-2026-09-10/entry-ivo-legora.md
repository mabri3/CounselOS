# Market entry lessons: Ivo and Legora

**Cutoff:** 10 September 2026. Historical facts and current facts are separated. Where the conclusion is an inference, it is marked as such.

## Ivo: narrow in-house contract wedge

### Original wedge and launch path

Ivo was founded in 2022 as Latch and rebranded in 2024. Its founder, Min-Kyu Jung, describes the original pain as a corporate lawyer: line-by-line contract review and searching iManage for precedents. The wedge was therefore specific: help in-house lawyers review third-party contracts against their own standards, directly in Microsoft Word, with risks and redlines ([Ivo company facts](https://www.ivo.ai/llms-info); [founder account and Series B announcement](https://www.ivo.ai/blog/ivo-raises-55m-to-bring-ai-contract-intelligence-to-every-in-house-legal-team)).

The public record does not provide a precise first-product general-availability date or a named first customer. By February 2025, Ivo announced a $16M Series A and described customers including Canva, Fonterra, Pipedrive, WeightWatchers, Eventbrite, Blue Cross Blue Shield Kansas City, and Fortune 500 companies. That release also launched Ivo Search, expanding the wedge from review into portfolio search and reporting ([Series A and Search launch](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/02/05/3021329/0/en/ivo-raises-16m-series-a-to-deliver-reliable-ai-contract-review-at-scale-as-it-launches-ivo-search-agent.html)). The careful conclusion is that review came first and repository intelligence followed; the exact first customer is unconfirmed in public sources.

### Buyer, champion, and early distribution

The buyer was an enterprise in-house legal team, with procurement and legal operations as adjacent stakeholders. The champion was likely the commercial counsel or legal-operations leader who felt the review bottleneck directly; Ivo’s public customer stories name commercial-legal and legal-operations leaders at Canva, CDW, Atlassian, Vantage Data Centers, Hootsuite, and SharkNinja ([Ivo customer stories](https://www.ivo.ai/customer-stories)). This champion profile is an inference from the named roles, not a disclosed sales playbook.

The early distribution approach was product-led inside an existing work surface: a Microsoft Word add-in, playbooks built from company templates and guidance, and low-friction repository connection. Ivo later added Google Docs support, Intelligence, and Search. The company explicitly offers “white-glove onboarding,” with legal engineers digitizing playbooks, configuring fallback positions, and integrating existing legal technology ([Ivo canonical company facts](https://www.ivo.ai/llms-info)). This is a hybrid motion: a narrow product that can be trialed on live contracts, followed by enterprise implementation and expansion.

### Current expansion as of the cutoff

Ivo now presents Review, Assistant, and Intelligence as one platform. Review uses the full contract, playbook, and deal context; Assistant handles research, drafting, and Q&A; Intelligence extracts contract data and relationships across agreements. Review 2.0, launched May 4, 2026, added deal context, negotiation history, benchmark comparisons, and a multi-agent review architecture ([Review 2.0 announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ivo-updates-ivo-review-delivering-contract-intelligence-and-real-legal-judgment-that-performs-on-par-with-experienced-human-lawyer-in-a-head-to-head-study-302761041.html)). Ivo says its customers span Fortune 500 enterprises and fast-growing technology companies and that it is expanding sales, customer success, London, and New York operations ([Series B announcement](https://www.ivo.ai/blog/ivo-raises-55m-to-bring-ai-contract-intelligence-to-every-in-house-legal-team)).

The expansion is coherent: review is the entry product; historical contracts become a repository; repository data improves benchmarking and intelligence; research and drafting increase daily usage. The public record supports this product sequence. It does not prove that every customer expands in this order.

## Legora: law-firm co-building, then workflow platform

### Original wedge and launch path

Legora began as Leya in 2023. The company says the initial observation was a junior associate spending an entire summer manually summarizing court cases. After entering Y Combinator, it partnered with Mannheimer Swartling, one of the largest Nordic firms, and worked inside the firm for roughly nine months. The firm provided a dedicated Stockholm space through MSA Innovation; Legora used it as a product laboratory before commercial launch ([Leya is now Legora](https://legora.com/newsroom/leya-is-now-legora); [April 2026 company history](https://legora.com/newsroom/legal-teams-adoption-of-ai-propels-legora-past-100-million-in-annual-recurring-revenue)).

The original wedge was broad enough to attract lawyers but concrete enough to prove quickly: summarization, research, document review, and drafting connected to a firm’s documents, playbooks, templates, and public legal sources. Leya’s July 2024 Series A release described this connected platform and the $25M round ([Series A](https://legora.com/blog/announcing-25m-in-series-a)). General availability began in October 2024, according to the company ([April 2026 milestone](https://legora.com/newsroom/legal-teams-adoption-of-ai-propels-legora-past-100-million-in-annual-recurring-revenue)).

### Buyer, champion, and early distribution

The first strong champion was the innovative law firm willing to embed product builders with lawyers. Mannheimer Swartling was the earliest named institutional partner. Legora then used specialist knowledge partnerships and design customers to build trust: in July 2024, it announced a FromCounsel pilot with twelve major UK and US law firms, combining FromCounsel corporate-law content with the Leya platform ([FromCounsel pilot](https://legora.com/blog/leya-partners-with-fromcounsel)). This shows an early distribution pattern based on reference firms, content partners, and co-built pilots rather than self-serve acquisition.

The US launch in March 2025 made the motion explicit: a New York office and strategic partnership with Goodwin. At launch, Legora said it had more than 250 clients in nearly 20 countries and more than $35M raised ([US launch](https://legora.com/newsroom/legora-launches-in-the-us)). The likely champion was the innovation, knowledge-management, or operations leader who could sponsor firm-wide rollout while practice lawyers supplied workflows. That role assignment is an inference from the partnership structure and product description.

### Current expansion as of the cutoff

Legora has expanded from discrete tasks into Workflows that compose review, extraction, research, translation, drafting, and database search. It now reports more than 1,000 customers and $100M ARR, and says its platform supports multi-step agentic workflows and structured reports ([April 2026 milestone](https://legora.com/newsroom/legal-teams-adoption-of-ai-propels-legora-past-100-million-in-annual-recurring-revenue)). The March 2026 Series D release reported 800 customers in more than 50 markets, tens of thousands of lawyers, and US offices in New York, Denver, Houston, and Chicago ([Series D](https://legora.com/newsroom/legora-raises-550-million-series-d-to-fuel-us-growth)). The different customer counts reflect different announcement dates and should not be treated as a contradiction.

Portal is the current expansion beyond internal productivity: firms can publish branded workflows, share documents, let clients query a controlled corpus, and collaborate in real time ([official Portal page](https://legora.com/product/portal); [Portal launch](https://legora.com/newsroom/portal-announcement)). This turns firm know-how into a client-delivery surface. It also raises a harder product problem—permissions, audit trails, and which work product a client can see—that Legora addresses with role-based access and host-controlled sharing.

## Brief contrast: Harvey and Clio

Harvey’s wedge was a broad, high-end legal AI assistant sold into elite law firms and large enterprises, with early credibility from OpenAI and major-firm deployments. It expanded through Vault, reusable Workflow Agents, legal-source grounding, embedded legal engineering, and firm-wide spaces. Harvey’s current distribution is enterprise direct sales plus implementation support; it has the scale and capital to sell a platform before a narrow practice wedge is complete ([Harvey Series D](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-series-d); [Workflow Agents](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/legal-workflow-automation)).

Clio entered from the opposite direction: long-standing practice-management software with intake, matter, document, billing, and operations data. It added AI through Clio Manage, Grow, Draft, and Work, then acquired vLex for legal content and research. Its distribution advantage is an installed base and workflow data, while its current expansion targets enterprise firms and legal departments ([Clio intelligent platform](https://www.clio.com/about/press/clio-introduces-the-legal-industrys-first-intelligent-legal-work-platform/); [Clio Work](https://www.clio.com/work/?cta=callout)).

## What CounselOS must match for a product-counsel pilot

CounselOS does not need Harvey’s capital, Clio’s broad practice-management suite, or Legora’s global firm-sales footprint. It must match the minimum trust and workflow bar those products make visible:

- A fast path from a real request to a useful, editable artifact.
- Matter context that persists across sessions and is easy to inspect.
- A small library of repeatable workflows with clear inputs and outputs.
- Source-linked research and visible assumptions.
- A decision record that distinguishes recommendation, approval, rejection, and follow-up.
- A simple way to reuse approved policies, prior decisions, templates, and examples.

CounselOS can defer multi-jurisdiction content licensing, Word/Google Docs add-ins, client portals, broad DMS integrations, multi-agent architectures, and enterprise sales controls until a product-counsel pilot proves repeated use. The entry strategy should be a narrow set of end-to-end product-counsel jobs—launch intake, privacy/data review, regulatory scan, commercial contract issue map, and decision follow-up—using the existing stages, matter memory, workflows, editable work, and explicit decisions. This is an inference about sequencing, based on the observed wedges: Ivo won with one painful contract workflow; Legora won by co-building with one demanding firm and then composing workflows.
