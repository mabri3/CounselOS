# Legal tools competitive research

**Cutoff:** 10 September 2026. Facts dated after this cutoff are excluded. Vendor claims are identified as claims; reported product behavior is treated as evidence only when it is supported by a product page, help documentation, release, filing, or credible reporting.

## Executive view

The seven companies occupy different layers of legal work:

| Company | Core buyer | Concrete work product | Primary fit | Software or service |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Harvey | Large law firms; large in-house and professional-services teams | Research answers, contract/diligence analysis, litigation work, reusable agents and workflows, document sets | Broad legal, compliance and professional services | SaaS plus embedded legal-engineering support; not a law firm |
| Legora | Law firms and in-house teams, especially large and international teams | Research, tabular review, extraction, drafting, translation, workflow outputs, client portal answers | Broad legal; strong transactions, diligence, contracts, litigation | SaaS; customer co-building and implementation support |
| Ivo | Enterprise in-house legal and contracting teams | Contract redlines, playbook-based reviews, clause/source-linked answers, contract intelligence and research | Commercial, procurement, privacy/data, technology transactions | SaaS |
| Spellbook | Small/midsize law firms and in-house commercial teams | Word-native redlines, clause explanations, playbook findings, tracked-change actions | Commercial contracts and transactions | SaaS |
| Clio | Solo, small/midsize and increasingly enterprise firms; legal departments | Intake, matter records, billing/operations, templates, document sets, matter research, drafts | Broad practice coverage; historically practice management | SaaS platform; no delivered legal service |
| EvenUp | Plaintiff personal-injury firms | Medical chronologies, demand packages, intake/treatment workflows, case and negotiation prep, settlement intelligence | Personal injury litigation | SaaS plus expert-reviewed document production in some products; not legal representation |
| Thomson Reuters | Law firms, legal departments, government and regulated professionals | Westlaw-grounded research, document analysis, drafting, guided workflows, citation-linked answers | Litigation, transactions, privacy/regulatory, commercial | Enterprise software/content subscriptions; no delivered legal service |

The common product lesson for CounselOS is to make the “next lawyer decision” visible. The best examples do this through structured intake, matter context, a reusable playbook or workflow, an editable output, and traceability back to a clause, source, or record. CounselOS already has stages, decisions, workflows, and matter memory; the opportunity is to make those elements more reusable and more tightly linked to sources and editable work.

## Harvey

### What it actually does and produces

Harvey is a domain-specific legal and professional-services AI platform. Its current product surface includes Assistant, Vault, Workflow Agents, History, Library, knowledge sources, integrations, and contracting tools. Harvey’s support documentation describes Vault as secure data storage and organization, Workflow Agents as reusable automation, and knowledge sources as jurisdiction-specific legal research content ([Harvey Support Hub](https://help.harvey.ai/)). The company says its products cover contract analysis, due diligence, compliance, and litigation ([Harvey company page](https://www.harvey.ai/company)).

The useful output is therefore broader than chat: answers grounded in matter documents or legal sources; extracted issues and comparisons from document collections; draft clauses, memos, briefs, and correspondence; redline or contract-review results; and repeatable agents that run a defined multi-step task. Harvey’s Workflow Agents are designed so a firm can encode templates and expertise in natural language, then run the workflow repeatedly ([workflow automation guide](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/legal-workflow-automation)). Harvey reports more than 25,000 customer-run custom agents as of its March 2026 funding announcement ([March 2026 funding release](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-growth-round-at-dollar11-billion-valuation-co-led-by-gic-and-sequoia)).

The current buyer is primarily the Am Law/global law-firm segment, with expansion into Fortune 500 in-house, compliance, risk, government affairs, and other professional services. Harvey stated in February 2025 that it had 235 customers in 42 countries and the majority of the top 10 US law firms ([Series D announcement](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-series-d)); by September 2026, the company reported 80% of the Am Law 100 and half of the Fortune 10 among its customers ([September 2026 announcement](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-dollar550m-at-a-dollar155b-valuation-to-help-legal-teams-own-their-intelligence)). Jurisdiction coverage is product- and source-dependent; the platform is global, but the strongest public evidence is US and cross-border large-firm use.

### Practice areas and delivery model

Harvey spans product counseling and privacy/compliance (policy and regulatory analysis), commercial and corporate transactions (contract review, diligence, drafting), and litigation (case analysis, discovery and litigation documents). Its public materials also mention compliance and professional-services work. It is software. The embedded legal-engineering teams that help customers design agents are an implementation/service layer, not outsourced legal advice. Users remain the legal decision makers.

### Funding and company status

The latest disclosed round by the cutoff was a **$550 million round announced 9 September 2026**, co-led by Diffusion and Lightspeed, at a **$15.5 billion valuation** ([Harvey announcement](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-dollar550m-at-a-dollar155b-valuation-to-help-legal-teams-own-their-intelligence); [TechCrunch report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/harvey-hits-15-5b-valuation-months-after-reaching-11b/)). TechCrunch reports total funding of **more than $1.55 billion**. Harvey’s announcement does not state a cumulative total, so this is a reported figure rather than an independently reconstructed sum. The prior round was **$200 million announced 25 March 2026** at an **$11 billion valuation**, co-led by GIC and Sequoia ([Harvey release](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-growth-round-at-dollar11-billion-valuation-co-led-by-gic-and-sequoia)). These are equity/growth financings; the valuation is not capital raised, and no debt component was disclosed in the cited releases. Harvey is private, not publicly listed.

### Novel shipped features: evidence versus marketing

Evidence-backed shipped or publicly documented features include Vault document collections; Workflow Agents; a workflow library; knowledge-source grounding; spaces for controlled firm or cross-functional access; and integration into legal work environments ([Spaces](https://www.harvey.ai/platform/spaces), [support hub](https://help.harvey.ai/)). The 25,000-agent figure is a company-reported adoption measure, not an independent quality test. “Agentic,” “lawyer-like reasoning,” and “professional-class AI” are positioning language. A concrete differentiator is the combination of firm-specific reusable workflows, large document collections, and implementation help; the public record does not establish that outputs are legally correct without review.

### CounselOS implications

Adopt the pattern of a **workflow/agent library** where each workflow names inputs, required context, steps, output schema, and owner. Add a **matter-local source set** and a visible history of which sources and instructions produced each output. CounselOS should borrow Harvey’s embedded legal-engineering idea as lightweight “workflow configuration help,” while reusing its existing decision record, assumptions, and source-change impact view.

## Legora (formerly Leya)

### What it actually does and produces

Legora is a collaborative legal AI platform for research, review, drafting, extraction, translation, database search, and multi-step workflows. Its Workflows product lets a knowledge manager, associate, or partner compose tasks from natural-language instructions, firm standards, and native tools. The public examples include internal compliance review, supplier agreement analysis, timelines, court-case analysis and translation, employment-agreement review, lease red-flag reports, and S-1 risk-factor drafting ([Legora Workflows](https://legora.com/product/workflows)). Outputs include structured tables, reports, draft language, translations, extracted lists, and client-facing answers.

Legora’s platform also includes Tabular Review, legal research, a Word add-in, a database/search layer, a client Portal, and DMS/case-law/legislation search. The company describes use by 800 customers in more than 50 markets and tens of thousands of legal professionals; named customers include Bird & Bird, Cleary Gottlieb, White & Case, Linklaters, Deloitte, Dentons, and Goodwin ([March 2026 company release](https://legora.com/newsroom/legora-raises-550-million-series-d-to-fuel-us-growth)). This supports a buyer profile of large and international law firms plus sophisticated in-house teams. The product is designed for multi-jurisdictional work, but exact legal-source availability varies by market.

### Practice areas and delivery model

Legora is broad horizontal legal software. Its strongest public workflows cover commercial contracting, supplier and employment agreements, due diligence/data-room review, corporate/transactional drafting, compliance, regulatory alignment, and litigation/court-case analysis. Its Portal extends the same system to client-facing routine questions and standard-document review ([Portal](https://legora.com/product/portal)). It is SaaS, with customer co-development, onboarding, and workflow optimization; it does not sell legal representation.

### Funding and company status

The latest primary-source announcement before the cutoff was a **$550 million Series D announced 10 March 2026**, led by Accel at a **$5.55 billion valuation** ([Legora release](https://legora.com/newsroom/legora-raises-550-million-series-d-to-fuel-us-growth)). A credible funding database reports a **$50 million Series D extension in April 2026**, bringing the round to $600 million and total disclosed funding to **more than $850 million**; treat the extension and total as reported rather than an official Legora release ([Sacra funding profile](https://sacra.com/c/legora/)). Earlier disclosed rounds included $150 million in October 2025, $80 million Series B in May 2025, $25 million Series A, and a $10.5 million seed, according to the same source. These are equity financings; no debt or acquisition is included in the cited totals. Legora is private.

### Novel shipped features: evidence versus marketing

The strongest evidence is the published workflow builder and its concrete examples. The product composes review, extraction, research, translation, drafting, and databases into a repeatable sequence, and can publish workflows into a Portal. The “fully agentic” and “lawyer-like reasoning” descriptions are marketing claims; the shipped evidence is the builder, tool composition, workflow library, and output formats. The public pages do not provide independent accuracy or time-savings testing.

### CounselOS implications

Borrow **workflow composition from existing CounselOS primitives**: intake fields, matter memory, research, editable work, and explicit decisions should be callable as steps. Make each step produce a typed artifact (for example, “issue table,” “source memo,” “draft clause,” “decision request”). Legora’s Portal suggests a controlled external intake or client knowledge view; CounselOS can reuse its existing matter-scoped sharing, provenance, and source-change mechanisms to make that pattern safe and reviewable.

## Ivo

### What it actually does and produces

Ivo is an AI contract-intelligence platform aimed at in-house legal teams. Its three integrated products are **Review**, **Assistant**, and **Intelligence**, sharing a playbook system and dataset ([Ivo product overview](https://www.ivo.ai/llms-info)). Review analyzes an agreement using the full contract, the company playbook, and deal context, then produces issue findings, explanations, and redlines. Ivo’s August 2026 Review 2.0 documentation shows a concrete workflow: auto-detect review parameters; let the user confirm or adjust them; add deal context; choose a review method; and run the review ([Review 2.0 help](https://support.ivo.ai/articles/5162323750-running-a-review-2-0?lang=en)).

Assistant handles contract Q&A, drafting, and legal research. Intelligence extracts contract data, relationships among agreements and amendments, and business insights; it can combine the repository, trusted legal sources, and web sources, with links to the exact clause or source ([Ivo Intelligence](https://www.ivo.ai/product/intelligence)). Ivo supports Word and Google Docs add-ins and research against official sources such as EDGAR, the US Code, Companies House, and the Court of Justice ([Ivo FAQ](https://www.ivo.ai/faq)).

The buyer is enterprise in-house legal/procurement and contracting teams, including Fortune 500 companies. The public product is designed primarily for US and UK/EU commercial contracting and corporate legal work, with privacy/data and technology-transactions use cases. It is SaaS, not a law firm or delivered legal service.

### Practice areas and delivery model

Ivo is strongest in commercial contracts, procurement, technology transactions, corporate contracting, privacy/data terms, and regulatory research connected to agreements. It is a software platform. Its playbooks encode company positions; they are a customer control mechanism, not legal advice.

### Funding and company status

Ivo announced a **$55 million Series B on 20 January 2026**, led by Blackbird. Reuters reported a valuation of about **$355 million** and the company says the round brought total funding to approximately **$75 million** ([Ivo announcement](https://www.ivo.ai/blog/ivo-raises-55m-to-bring-ai-contract-intelligence-to-every-in-house-legal-team); [Reuters report](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/legal-ai-startup-ivo-raises-55-million-in-latest-funding-round-4455010); [Ivo company facts](https://www.ivo.ai/llms-info)). This is equity; no debt, acquisition, or separate valuation capital is included. Ivo is private.

### Novel shipped features: evidence versus marketing

Review 2.0 is a concrete product change from checklist reviews to context-aware review using the whole agreement, playbook, and deal context. Playbook Builder can generate a structured playbook from contracts, templates, and internal guidance ([Playbook Builder](https://support.ivo.ai/articles/9340856771-creating-a-playbook?lang=en)). Clause/source links and relationship extraction are observable feature claims. Ivo’s “97% accuracy” is a company-reported score against the CUAD benchmark, and its 2026 independent benchmark is company released; these are useful signals but not a neutral, broad legal-quality evaluation ([Ivo benchmark](https://www.ivo.ai/news/ivo-outperforms-claude-for-word-in-independent-contract-review-benchmark)).

### CounselOS implications

Refine the existing review setup before generation: identify parties, matter type, governing jurisdictions, playbook, and deal context, and let the lawyer confirm or edit the detected setup. Store those parameters with the resulting work. Borrow Ivo’s clause-relationship model for CounselOS source-change impact: amendments, superseding agreements, related policies, and prior decisions should be first-class links. The product should make the output useful even when the playbook is incomplete by labeling “playbook rule,” “source rule,” “assumption,” and “open decision.”

## Spellbook

### What it actually does and produces

Spellbook is a Word-centered AI contract review and drafting product. Its public product page says it applies standard playbooks across contract reviews, identifies risks, and adds redlines directly in Word ([Spellbook](https://spellbook.com/)). The Playbooks feature lets teams define what to review and use company documents or instructions as guidance. The output includes AI reasoning and references, suggested clause text, tracked changes, comments, and actions to accept, reject, or revise ([Playbooks overview](https://help.spellbook.legal/en/articles/9926250-playbooks-overview); [redline-aware playbooks](https://help.spellbook.legal/en/articles/13875622-first-party-playbooks-redline-review)).

Spellbook says it serves commercial legal work and had 4,000 law firms and in-house legal teams in 80 countries by its October 2025 financing announcement ([Series B announcement](https://spellbook.com/blog/series-b)). The buyer is typically a commercial lawyer, legal operations lead, or small/midsize firm that wants immediate Word-native review without a large implementation. It is software, not legal services. Jurisdiction coverage is dependent on the user’s playbook and the model/source configuration; public evidence is strongest for English-language commercial agreements.

### Practice areas and delivery model

Spellbook is focused on commercial contracts and transactional work: NDAs, supplier/customer agreements, data-use and privacy terms, liability and indemnity, insurance, governing law, and other common redline topics. It is not positioned as a litigation case-management or delivered legal service product.

### Funding and company status

Spellbook announced a **$50 million Series B on 9 October 2025**, led by Khosla Ventures, at a reported **$350 million valuation** ([company announcement](https://spellbook.com/blog/series-b); [Axios funding report](https://www.axios.com/2025/10/09/spellbook-legal-ai-funding)). Disclosed equity before that included a $20 million Series A and earlier seed financing. Spellbook separately announced a **$40 million debt facility from RBCx on 4 March 2026**, intended to support strategic acquisitions ([RBCx announcement](https://www.rbcx.com/news/press-releases/spellbook-secures-40m-usd-debt-financing-from-rbcx-to-support-strategic-acquisitions-in-consolidating-legal-ai-market/)). A funding database estimates at least **$80.9 million equity** through the Series B; do not describe the RBCx facility as equity raised. Spellbook is private.

### Novel shipped features: evidence versus marketing

The strongest shipped feature is redline-aware playbook review: the system evaluates tracked changes and proposes accept/reject/revise actions with comments. This is a concrete workflow improvement over a blank chat box. “Gold standard,” “game changer,” and “state-of-the-art” are vendor positioning; there is no neutral evidence in the cited materials that it is universally reliable. The Word add-in is an adoption advantage because the output lands where the contract already lives.

### CounselOS implications

Borrow the **review-in-place interaction**: let a lawyer select a clause or issue in an editable work product, see the relevant rule/source, and apply a suggested revision without losing the original. CounselOS can improve on Spellbook by preserving the issue as a structured matter object, linking the decision to a source and playbook version, and tracking later source changes. A small “playbook starter” flow could turn a few approved examples into an initial checklist while clearly marking it as provisional.

## Clio

### What it actually does and produces

Clio is a broad cloud legal-practice platform. Its traditional core includes client intake, matter and document management, calendaring, timekeeping, billing, payments, and firm operations. In 2025 it introduced an Intelligent Legal Work Platform spanning **Clio Manage, Clio Grow, Clio Draft, and Clio Work**, integrating practice management with legal intelligence ([Clio platform announcement](https://www.clio.com/about/press/clio-introduces-the-legal-industrys-first-intelligent-legal-work-platform/)).

Concrete outputs include intake records and follow-up tasks; matter templates, calendars, bills, and client updates; reusable document templates and populated document sets; court forms; matter summaries, timelines, issue/risk views, research, arguments, and drafts. Clio Work’s public product page says it reads matter files, surfaces facts, risks and gaps, finds supporting authority, and drafts in Word; it separately describes litigation and transactional workflows ([Clio Work](https://www.clio.com/work/?cta=callout)). Clio Draft turns Word files into reusable templates and can populate multiple documents from case data ([Clio Draft](https://www.clio.com/draft/)). Clio Manage automated workflows can generate documents, assign task lists, and apply matter templates when intake moves into matter management ([workflow help](https://help.clio.com/hc/en-150/articles/35132279298843-Clio-Manage-Automated-Workflows)).

The buyer base ranges from solo and small/midsize firms to enterprise firms, in-house legal, and government teams. Clio says it is used by more than 150,000 legal professionals and endorsed by more than 100 legal associations; its platform is international, while court-form and legal-source availability is jurisdiction-specific. Clio is software, not a delivered legal service.

### Practice areas and delivery model

Clio spans broad practice management. Clio Work publicly names litigation, transactions, research, contract review, and drafting; Clio Draft and intake support practice-specific templates such as immigration and state/county court forms. It is therefore a horizontal operating system with practice modules, rather than a single legal specialty.

### Funding and company status

Clio announced a **$500 million Series G in November 2025** at a **$5 billion valuation**, alongside a **$1 billion acquisition of vLex** and a **$350 million debt facility**; the release identifies $500 million as primary capital and $350 million as debt ([Clio announcement](https://www.clio.com/about/press/clio-completes-landmark-1b-vlex-acquisition-series-g-5b-valuation/)). It previously raised **$900 million Series F in July 2024** at a $3 billion valuation ([Series F](https://www.clio.com/uk/about/press/series-f/)). Sacra reports **over $1.7 billion total funding**, including more than $1.2 billion before Series G; this is a secondary-source cumulative figure and is not a simple sum of only the two latest rounds ([Sacra summary](https://sacra.com/c/clio/)). Do not add the $1 billion vLex acquisition to capital raised: it is M&A consideration, not an equity round. Clio is a private company; the valuation is a private-market value, not public-market capitalization.

### Novel shipped features: evidence versus marketing

Clio’s real differentiation is the combination of system-of-record data and system-of-action workflows: intake can trigger matter setup, matter records can trigger tasks/documents, and Clio Work can use the same context for research and drafts. Clio’s “first and only” category claims are marketing. The product evidence is the four-product integration, automated workflows, document-set population, and matter-aware research/drafting. Clio’s vLex acquisition is strategically important because it adds licensed/global legal content and Vincent AI, but the public materials do not prove output quality across every jurisdiction.

### CounselOS implications

Borrow Clio’s **matter lifecycle automations**: intake should create a matter, assign an owner, create a task list, and seed matter memory. Keep legal work and operational work connected, but show the transition clearly so an operational trigger does not silently become a legal recommendation. CounselOS can differentiate with a compact “matter brief” that always shows status, remaining unknowns, sources, decisions, and editable work in one place. Clio’s system-of-action direction validates that the workspace should advance work, not only store it.

## EvenUp

### What it actually does and produces

EvenUp is a vertical AI platform for plaintiff personal-injury law. Its product suite covers intake, treatment, demands, negotiation, discovery, and trial. Concrete products include **Express Demands** for demand packages, **MedChrons** for medical chronologies, **Companion** for PI case questions, **Medical Management**, **Communication Agents**, **AI Drafts**, **Case & Negotiation Prep**, **Settlement Repository**, **Workflow Automation**, and **Executive Analytics** ([EvenUp product suite](https://www.evenuplaw.com/products/)). MedChrons is described as AI-assembled and expert-reviewed; its output is a treatment chronology suitable for case evaluation and settlement work ([MedChrons](https://www.evenuplaw.com/products/medchrons/)). The platform’s value is end-to-end case preparation: organize records, identify missing treatment or liability facts, generate a demand, compare settlement priors, and prepare for negotiation or litigation.

The buyer is the plaintiff personal-injury firm, from growing boutiques to larger firms. The jurisdiction is principally US personal-injury practice; public materials do not support a claim of broad multi-jurisdiction legal research. EvenUp is software, with expert review in selected document workflows. It is not a law firm and does not represent plaintiffs.

### Practice areas and delivery model

EvenUp is specialized litigation software for personal injury: medical records, damages, liability, demand packages, discovery/trial preparation, negotiation, and settlement intelligence. Its narrow focus is a strength: it can encode the recurring records and outputs of one practice area instead of trying to be a general legal assistant.

### Funding and company status

EvenUp announced a **$135 million Series D on 8 October 2024**, led by Bain Capital Ventures, bringing total funding to **$235 million** and valuation above $1 billion ([company release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/evenup-raises-135m-in-series-d-funding-and-launches-new-products-to-help-level-the-playing-field-in-personal-injury-cases-302270003.html)). It then announced a **$150 million Series E on 7 October 2025**, led by Bessemer, at a valuation above **$2 billion**, bringing total capital raised to **$385 million** ([EvenUp newsroom](https://www.evenuplaw.com/newsroom/); [Fortune report](https://fortune.com/2025/10/07/exclusive-evenup-raises-150-million-series-e-at-2-billion-valuation-as-ai-reshapes-personal-injury-law/)). These are equity financings; no debt or acquisition is included in the cited total. EvenUp is private.

### Novel shipped features: evidence versus marketing

The credible product evidence is the practice-specific suite and expert-reviewed MedChrons. The company has reported large document and records volumes processed, but those are company metrics. “Level the playing field,” “high-quality demands,” and “proactive AI” are positioning. The meaningful innovation is workflow coverage across the case lifecycle, including treatment follow-up and negotiation priors, rather than generic drafting.

### CounselOS implications

Borrow the **vertical case-stage model**. CounselOS could define matter stages for product counseling—intake, facts/context, research, drafting, review, decision, follow-up—and surface missing inputs at each stage. EvenUp’s treatment-gap pattern maps directly to product legal: identify missing facts, owners, dates, and evidence before a recommendation is finalized. A “decision prep” output should show the best current path, counterarguments, missing facts, and the next action, with every item linked to its source.

## Thomson Reuters

### What it actually does and produces

Thomson Reuters is a public, diversified content and technology company, not a startup. Its legal AI product is **CoCounsel Legal**, integrated with Westlaw and Practical Law. The August 2025 launch combined legal research, document search, workflow automation, and AI assistance, with Deep Research and guided workflows ([CoCounsel Legal launch](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2025/august/thomson-reuters-launches-cocounsel-legal-transforming-legal-work-with-agentic-ai-and-deep-research)).

Concrete outputs include Westlaw-grounded research plans and answers; linked authorities; lease abstracts; document-to-standard comparisons; contract analysis; privacy and employee policies; complaints; discovery requests/responses; deposition transcript review; correspondence, briefs, and other drafts. CoCounsel’s guided workflow documentation gives the output shape: structured abstracts with key terms, obligations and dates; deviations and missing provisions against a user standard; and suggested language ([guided workflows](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/innovation/introducing-our-first-cocounsel-guided-legal-workflows/)). It is embedded in Microsoft Word, Teams, Outlook, and existing Thomson Reuters products. Thomson Reuters reports more than one million CoCounsel users across professional workflows in a 2025 investor release ([one million professionals](https://ir.thomsonreuters.com/static-files/251a6aea-1795-4744-bb13-dafffd47ce43)).

The buyers are law firms, corporate legal departments, government, and other regulated professionals. Jurisdiction coverage is strongest where Westlaw and Practical Law have licensed content; the product has US, UK, and other regional offerings. It is enterprise software and subscription content, not delivered legal advice.

### Practice areas and delivery model

CoCounsel covers litigation, transactions, commercial contracts, privacy and regulatory analysis, employment, and general legal research/drafting. The Westlaw/Practical Law corpus is the differentiator for authority and standard-setting. Thomson Reuters also sells non-legal tax, audit, risk, and compliance products, but those are outside this legal-tools comparison.

### Funding and company status

Thomson Reuters is publicly listed (TSX/Nasdaq: TRI), so “latest funding round” and “total raised” are not meaningful startup metrics. ### Novel shipped features: evidence versus marketing

The strongest evidence-backed feature set is the combination of licensed authority, guided multi-step workflows, document-standard benchmarking, Microsoft 365 embedding, and citation-linked research. “Fiduciary-Grade AI” and “professional-grade” are brand claims. Thomson Reuters has the most defensible source moat in this set, but source access does not prove that every generated answer is correct; the lawyer still reviews the linked authority and draft.

### CounselOS implications

Borrow **source-linked workflow outputs** and **benchmark-against-standard** review. CounselOS should allow a matter owner to select a prior decision, policy, template, or approved work product as the standard, then return deviations, missing provisions, and proposed language. Every research statement should point to the source passage, not only a bibliography. CounselOS can differentiate by linking that output to explicit decisions and to a source-change monitor, so the system can tell the lawyer when a prior recommendation or policy may need review.

## Cross-company patterns CounselOS should adopt

1. **Reusable context in analysis.** Ivo’s review setup, Clio’s intake-to-matter workflow, and EvenUp’s case-stage model all reduce missing context. CounselOS can reuse its existing matter fields to carry business objective, jurisdictions, product or matter stage, parties, deadlines, owners, source set, and open questions into each workflow without adding a new completeness gate.

2. **A first-class matter memory.** Harvey Vault, Legora’s connected platform, Clio Work’s matter context, and Ivo’s repository show that useful output depends on a durable context layer. CounselOS should keep Markdown as the readable source of truth and expose a compact, editable matter brief rather than hide context in prompts.

3. **Reusable workflows with typed outputs.** Harvey Agents, Legora Workflows, Thomson Reuters guided workflows, and EvenUp’s case stages all turn know-how into repeatable steps. A CounselOS workflow should declare inputs, steps, output type, owner, and review state. Examples: “privacy intake → issue map,” “new product launch → research memo,” “contract request → redline plus decision log.”

4. **Editable work product inside the flow.** Spellbook’s Word redlines and Clio Draft’s document sets prove that lawyers need to edit the result where the work already lives. CounselOS should make every generated memo, issue table, clause, and recommendation editable, with a direct link to the supporting source and decision.

5. **Explicit review and decision records.** Most competitors emphasize generated work; fewer make the final lawyer decision a durable object. CounselOS should separate “recommended,” “accepted,” “rejected,” “deferred,” and “superseded,” with decision owner, date, rationale, assumptions, and affected artifacts.

6. **Source and change traceability.** Ivo’s clause/source links and Thomson Reuters’ Westlaw-linked answers are strong patterns. CounselOS should preserve citations at paragraph or issue level, label source status (supplied, verified, unverified lead, assumption, generated analysis), and calculate which decisions and work products are affected when a source changes.

7. **Targeted vertical workflows before a general agent.** EvenUp’s narrow PI focus demonstrates the advantage of encoding one practice’s records, stages, and outputs deeply. CounselOS should start with product counsel: launch intake, privacy/data review, regulatory scan, commercial contract issue map, and decision log. General agent behavior can come later after these end-to-end flows produce observable lawyer value.
