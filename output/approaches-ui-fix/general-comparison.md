The company wants to reduce customer-support response times within six weeks. It must not increase incorrect refunds. The current facts do not support a clear winner because neither option has measured speed or accuracy.

**Option A — Internal assistant with staff approval**

The existing support team uses an internal tool to find relevant knowledge. A staff member approves every response before it goes to the customer.

This option makes quality control easier. Staff can identify an incorrect answer before it causes an incorrect refund. The company also keeps direct control over response standards, escalation, and policy interpretation.

However, staff approval can remain a bottleneck. The option will not meet the six-week objective unless faster knowledge search saves more time than review adds. Workload, staffing levels, and tool integration will affect that result.

Option A may also be easier to introduce within six weeks because it does not depend on negotiating and implementing a new vendor arrangement. That conclusion is provisional because the readiness of the internal assistant is unknown.

**Option B — Vendor AI sends responses directly**

The vendor receives customer tickets that contain personal information. Its AI sends responses without company staff approval.

This option could remove the staff-review bottleneck. It may therefore produce the larger reduction in response time. Direct sending also makes scaling easier if ticket volume increases.

The same feature makes incorrect refunds harder to prevent. An incorrect interpretation can reach a customer before the company reviews it. Correction work, customer dissatisfaction, and inconsistent refund treatment could remove some of the expected time savings.

The contract prohibits provider training with company data. That term reduces one stated data-use concern, but it does not answer how long ticket data is kept, which subcontractors receive it, where it is processed, or how it is deleted. Applicable privacy and data-protection duties cannot be identified from the supplied facts because the locations, customers, and governing law are unknown.

Option B may also be harder to implement safely within six weeks. The company must review the missing data terms, configure the service, test its responses, and create controls for errors and unusual cases. If acceptable terms or controls cannot be completed in time, the expected speed benefit may not be available by the deadline.

**Problems that remain under both options**

- **Contradictory refund policy:** Neither option fixes the source rule. An internal assistant can find conflicting instructions, and a vendor AI can automate one of the conflicting interpretations. The contradiction can therefore cause incorrect refunds under either option.
- **No accuracy evidence:** There is no measured baseline or test result showing whether either system gives correct answers.
- **No time-saving evidence:** There is no proof that knowledge search will shorten staff handling time or that vendor automation will reduce total time after escalations and corrections.
- **Unclear exception handling:** The facts do not state how either option handles unusual tickets, uncertain policy questions, suspected fraud, or high-value refunds.
- **Implementation risk:** Both options need configuration, testing, and staff preparation within the same six-week period.

**Tradeoff**

Option A currently offers stronger control over incorrect refunds, but its staff-review step may limit the response-time improvement. Option B may offer greater speed, but it creates a higher risk that errors reach customers and leaves important data-handling and implementation questions open.

The refund-policy contradictions should be resolved for both options. Without that step, the company risks making wrong decisions faster rather than improving support.

**Evidence that would change the choice**

A short test using the same representative ticket set would be most useful. It should measure:

- Time from ticket receipt to final response.
- Correct answer rate.
- Incorrect refund rate and value.
- Staff time required for review, escalation, and correction.
- Performance on conflicting or unclear refund instructions.
- Performance on unusual and high-value cases.

For Option B, the choice also depends on reviewing its retention, deletion, security, processing-location, and subcontractor terms. The company would need evidence that the vendor can implement agreed controls within six weeks.

If Option A meets the required time improvement in the test, it is the better-supported choice because it retains human approval. If only Option B meets the speed target, the company must decide whether testing, restricted use cases, escalation rules, and acceptable data terms can keep refund errors from increasing. If neither passes both measures, neither meets the stated objective.
