The Current approach better supports the planned close and day-one migration. The Bank-held funds structure could reduce some state licensing risk, but only if the bank takes the legally important roles. It adds work and does not solve the existing financial-crime issues.

Objective

Complete the acquisition and migration on schedule. Avoid operating without required state authority. Prevent gaps in identity checks, transaction monitoring, and alert handling.

Current approach

Mosaic becomes the operating provider after the asset purchase.

How it helps

It keeps the current product and migration design.
It avoids new bank agreements, user terms, and system connections.
It gives the best chance of meeting the day-one migration target.

How it hinders

Mosaic plans to rely on Harbor’s licenses in four new markets. Their continued use after an asset purchase is not confirmed.
Mosaic would take the full effect of the unresolved identity, monitoring, and alert issues.
A single day-one migration increases the impact of a failure. One unresolved issue could affect all transferred users.

This approach is practical only with launch gates. Mosaic should exclude a state or user group if the required review for that part is incomplete.

Bank-held funds structure

A partner bank holds customer funds. The remaining operating roles are not yet defined.

How it helps

It could reduce Mosaic’s state money-transmission obligations.
That benefit is more likely if the bank contracts with users, controls transfers and settlement, records balances, and owes repayment to users.
It could support access to a market where Mosaic cannot obtain authority before launch.

How it hinders

Holding funds is only one part of the legal analysis. Mosaic could still need state authority if it controls payment activity or has the main customer obligation.
The structure requires bank agreements, customer terms, funds-flow changes, technical integration, and operating procedures.
These changes could delay the migration.
The benefit cannot be measured until the bank’s role and Mosaic’s role are defined.

This structure is therefore a possible licensing solution, not a complete compliance solution.

Issues that remain under both

Open alerts and suspicious activity reports. The parties must review the alerts and filing history. Bank custody does not resolve possible past problems or determine who must handle unresolved cases.

Identity records. Mosaic must decide whether its compliance program permits use of Harbor’s earlier identity results. It also needs a plan for users whose underlying records are missing.

Monitoring continuity. Harbor’s monitoring contract ends at close. The transaction feed must move to Mosaic’s system and be tested without a monitoring gap.

State-by-state authority. The legal question changes under the bank structure, but it does not disappear. Each state must assess the activities performed by Mosaic, Harbor, and the bank.

Division of duties. The operating documents must allocate identity checks, sanctions screening, monitoring, alert review, regulatory filings, privacy duties, and customer support.

Customer migration and data use. Both approaches require review of the authority to transfer customer records, change terms, and give users access to Mosaic’s full product set.

Welcome bonus. The offer terms, eligibility rules, disclosures, and funds flow need review under either structure.

Recommendation

Keep the Current approach as the planning baseline because it best supports the planned timing. Apply firm launch gates for state authority, identity records, alert review, and monitoring readiness.

Continue the Bank-held funds structure only as a fallback or targeted market solution. Use it only if a detailed funds-flow and responsibility map shows a clear legal benefit that is greater than the added work.

If the gates are not complete, the safest practical alternative is a narrower migration. Mosaic could delay the four new markets or affected user groups without delaying the whole acquisition.

No external authority retrieved. State licensing and federal financial-crime conclusions remain provisional.
