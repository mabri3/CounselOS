    ## Summary of the request

    Mosaic Relay wants to require background checks for employees and contractors
    who can view payment data, change payout controls, approve refunds, or investigate
    disputes. The program would collect consent, send identifying info to a background-check
    vendor, and block access until the check completes, with results categorized as
    clear/review/ineligible. Checks would run before access, every three years, and
    after certain role changes. You''re asking for legal advice on which checks are
    lawful and necessary per role, required notices and authorizations, review standards,
    and the dispute process — across employees in California, New York, Texas, Ireland,
    and the UK, under one global policy.

    I''ve recorded the intake state. The key tension I see up front: a single uniform
    global policy will collide with US state-law variation (California and New York
    restrict credit checks and criminal-history use in ways Texas does not) and with
    GDPR/UK GDPR rules on processing criminal-record data. That''s manageable, but
    it shapes the whole design.

    To give you the strongest advice, I need a few decisions from you (ordered by
    how much they change the analysis):

    **1. Which searches does the business intend to run?** (criminal, identity, sanctions,
    credit, employment-history — or not yet decided)

    This drives the lawfulness analysis most directly. Credit checks in particular
    are restricted in CA and NY, so whether credit is in scope is a major fork.

    **2. Which roles/access levels trigger a check?** (all four categories as stated,
    or a narrower/broader set)

    **3. Do contractor agencies already run their own checks, and will Mosaic Relay
    rely on those or run its own?** This affects duplication and who owns consent/notice
    for contractors.

    **4. What lookback periods are intended for each search type?** State limits apply
    (e.g., CA generally caps at 7 years).

    **5. Is the "one global policy" a hard requirement, or can it include jurisdiction-specific
    carve-outs where law requires?** This determines whether we design a truly uniform
    policy or a global framework with local overrides.

    Answer these and I''ll move into the legal analysis.'
