    ## Summary of the request

    Mosaic Relay (Product) wants to launch a **self-service consumer refund portal**
    for card and ACH payments. A payer would enter a transaction reference, verify
    a phone number or email, select a reason, and request a refund. The system would
    **auto-approve requests within 30 days** for defined reasons, **deny requests
    outside that window**, and notify the merchant. Funds return to the original payment
    method when possible. The stated goals are lower support volume and faster consumer
    outcomes; merchants stay responsible for their goods/services, and the portal
    won''t resolve card-network disputes.

    You''re asking for legal advice on the portal''s **role**, **eligibility and notice
    wording**, **authentication and fraud controls**, **timing requirements**, **recordkeeping**,
    and **when a denied request must route to the merchant or another dispute process**.

    I''ve recorded the reported facts, surfaced the key issues, and noted two working
    assumptions (Mosaic Relay isn''t a bank/holds no deposits; the portal spans North
    America and Europe). No launch date or jurisdiction scope is set yet.

    ## Prioritized questions

    The most decision-changing question first:

    1. **Mosaic Relay''s role** — Is Mosaic Relay acting as the merchant''s agent
    (authorized to approve and execute refunds on the merchant''s behalf), or as an
    independent decision-maker? This drives the authority, liability, and authorization
    structure for auto-approving and moving funds.

    2. **Jurisdiction and product scope** — Which jurisdictions and product types
    will the portal serve first? Refund/cancellation obligations vary materially by
    location and product (digital vs. physical, subscriptions).

    3. **Target launch date** — This affects how much legal review and build-out can
    happen before launch.

    4. **Authentication method** — What''s planned to verify a payer''s identity before
    releasing funds (phone OTP, email link, bank login)? This is central to the fraud-control
    and liability analysis.

    I''ll hold the remaining items (partial refunds, recurring payments, ACH return
    rules, escalation after denial, recordkeeping) as workstreams to develop once
    these are answered.'

The working recommendation routes a denied request down one of two paths. Here's how they compare:
**Path 1 — Route to the merchant**
- **When it applies:** The request needs merchant judgment or data the portal can't resolve — goods/services disputes, partial or recurring charges, merchant approval, or missing transaction data.
- **What the payer gets:** A clear merchant-support route with a response target, plus the reason, transaction date/window, and refund options.

- **Why it matters legally:** Merchants remain responsible for their goods/services, so this path keeps that responsibility with the merchant and avoids Mosaic Relay taking on liability for the underlying transaction.
**Path 2 — Route to another dispute process**
- **When it applies:** The claim is really a payment-rail dispute, not a merchant refund — card-network disputes go to the payer's issuer/network process; ACH unauthorized/error claims go to the bank/processor return process.
- **What the payer gets:** Direction to the correct external process; the portal must not imply a refund request replaces those rights.

- **Why it matters legally:** These are statutory/network rights (e.g., card chargeback rights, ACH error-resolution rules) that the portal can't override or extinguish by offering a refund instead.
**Key differences at a glance**
| | Merchant path | Dispute-process path |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Goods/services, partial/recurring, merchant approval, missing data | Card-network or ACH unauthorized/error claim |
| Decision-maker | Merchant | Issuer/network or bank/processor |
| Portal's role | Facilitate handoff + notice | Refer out; preserve rights |
| Legal basis | Merchant's responsibility for goods/services | Network rules / statutory error-resolution rights |
**Bottom line:** The merchant path is for disputes about the *transaction's substance* (did the payer get what they paid for), while the dispute-process path is for claims about the *payment itself* (was it authorized/valid).
