# What authentication and fraud-control standards are expected for releasing funds

## Question

What authentication and fraud-control standards are expected for releasing funds to a verified payer in a self-service refund context?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a B2B payment-infrastructure provider, is building a self-service refund portal for card and ACH payments. Payers would enter a transaction reference, verify a phone number or email, select a reason, and receive an automated refund decision within a 30-day window. Counsel is involved because the portal releases funds to consumers, creating authentication, fraud, and compliance obligations across North America and Europe. A limited pilot is targeted in 60 days, subject to legal review and control testing.
## Decision question
What authentication strength and fraud-control stack must Mosaic Relay implement before releasing funds to a verified payer through the self-service refund portal, given that the method is undecided, the portal covers card and ACH rails across North America and Europe, and a 60-day pilot is planned?
## Open questions
- What specific authentication method will be used (e.g., SMS OTP, email link, knowledge-based, or stronger)? This is undecided and drives the fraud-control design.
- Will Mosaic Relay act as the merchant's agent (authorized to approve and execute refunds) or as an independent decision-maker? This affects liability for fraudulent or erroneous releases.
- What transaction-risk scoring and velocity controls will apply to refund requests, and how will they interact with existing fraud monitoring?
- How will the portal handle account-takeover risk when the payer's contact details (phone/email) may have been compromised?
- What step-up authentication or manual review triggers will apply to high-value or high-risk refund requests?

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.**
Based on model-only analysis and the matter context, the following legal and regulatory frameworks are likely to govern authentication and fraud controls for this portal:
**United States**
- **Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA) and Regulation E**: Apply to ACH refunds and any electronic fund transfers from a consumer's account. If a refund is initiated through the portal and the payer claims it was unauthorized, Mosaic Relay or its partners could face liability if authentication was inadequate. Regulation E error-resolution procedures may be triggered.
- **NACHA Operating Rules**: Govern ACH entries and returns. Unauthorized ACH debit returns (R07, R10) and the warranties around authorization are relevant. The portal must not create a path that bypasses or conflicts with NACHA return processes.
- **State money-transmitter and consumer-protection laws**: May impose authentication or safeguarding requirements depending on whether Mosaic Relay is deemed to be in the flow of funds.
- **FTC Act Section 5 (unfair or deceptive practices)**: Releasing funds based on weak authentication could be deemed an unfair practice if it causes consumer harm.
- **Card-network rules (Visa/Mastercard)**: While the portal does not resolve chargebacks, card-network rules around refund processing, merchant of record, and fraud liability still apply. Weak authentication that leads to fraudulent refunds could create network compliance exposure.
**European Union / United Kingdom**
- **Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2) and Strong Customer Authentication (SCA)**: SCA requirements apply to electronic payment transactions. While refunds are typically treated differently from payments, if the portal initiates a credit transfer or card refund, SCA or equivalent authentication may be expected. The UK Payment Services Regulations 2017 mirror these requirements post-Brexit.
- **GDPR / UK GDPR**: Authentication processes involve processing personal data (phone numbers, email addresses, device identifiers). Lawful basis, data minimization, and security obligations apply. Fraud signals and risk scoring must comply with automated-decision safeguards (Article 22) if they produce legal or similarly significant effects.
- **Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU) and national implementations**: Refund rights for distance contracts (14-day withdrawal for many goods/services) interact with the 30-day auto-approve window. Authentication must not create barriers to exercising statutory rights.
- **ePrivacy Directive**: SMS and email verification may implicate consent and confidentiality requirements.
**Cross-cutting issues**
- **Account takeover (ATO) risk**: Verifying possession of a phone number or email is weak if those channels are compromised. Layered controls (device fingerprinting, behavioral analytics, transaction-history matching) are expected.
- **Agent vs. independent decision-maker**: If Mosaic Relay acts as the merchant's agent, the merchant bears primary liability for the refund decision, but Mosaic Relay still has a duty of care in execution. If independent, Mosaic Relay assumes more direct liability for fraudulent or erroneous releases.
- **Recordkeeping**: Authentication events, fraud signals, and decision logs must be retained to support dispute resolution, regulator requests, and audit trails.

## Viable paths
**Path 1: Minimal viable controls (fastest to pilot)**
- SMS or email OTP verification only.
- Transaction-reference matching (amount, date, merchant).
- Basic velocity limits (e.g., one refund per transaction, daily caps).
- Manual review for high-value refunds (threshold TBD).
*Pros*: Fast to implement; meets 60-day pilot timeline. *Cons*: Higher ATO and fraud risk; may not satisfy PSD2 SCA expectations or card-network fraud standards; could create Reg E liability if unauthorized refunds occur.
**Path 2: Layered risk-based authentication (recommended starting point)**
- SMS or email OTP plus device fingerprinting and behavioral signals.
- Transaction-risk scoring that considers refund amount, merchant history, payer tenure, and velocity.
- Step-up authentication (e.g., knowledge-based questions, bank-account verification, or manual review) for high-risk or high-value requests.
- Clear audit trail of all authentication events and fraud signals.
*Pros*: Balances speed and security; aligns with industry practice for payment infrastructure; more defensible under Reg E, PSD2, and card-network rules. *Cons*: Requires integration with fraud vendors; may add friction to user experience.
**Path 3: Strong authentication for all refunds (most conservative)**
- Multi-factor authentication (e.g., OTP plus authenticator app or hardware token).
- Bank-account verification (micro-deposits or instant account verification) for ACH refunds.
- Mandatory manual review for all refunds above a low threshold.
*Pros*: Strongest fraud protection; lowest legal risk. *Cons*: High friction; may not be feasible for a consumer self-service portal; could delay pilot beyond 60 days.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Authentication method chosen**: If Product selects a stronger method (e.g., authenticator app, bank verification), the fraud-control stack can be lighter. If they choose SMS OTP only, layered controls become essential.
- **Agent vs. independent role**: If Mosaic Relay is the merchant's agent, the merchant's fraud liability and chargeback exposure may allow lighter Mosaic Relay controls. If independent, Mosaic Relay needs stronger controls to protect itself.
- **Transaction values and volumes**: High-value refunds or high-volume merchants may require step-up authentication or manual review thresholds.
- **Fraud-loss tolerance**: If Mosaic Relay or its partners are willing to absorb higher fraud losses for speed, controls can be lighter. If not, stronger controls are needed.
- **Jurisdictional scope**: If the pilot is limited to the US initially, PSD2/SCA concerns are deferred. If EU/UK is included at launch, SCA and GDPR compliance become immediate.
- **Existing fraud infrastructure**: If Mosaic Relay already has transaction-risk scoring and device intelligence, incremental controls may be minimal. If not, build vs. buy decisions affect timeline.

## Recommended last-mile verification
Before finalizing the authentication and fraud-control design, counsel should:
1. **Confirm the authentication method** with Product and Engineering, and document the decision in the matter record.
2. **Review card-network rules** (Visa, Mastercard) and NACHA Operating Rules for any specific authentication or fraud-control requirements applicable to refund processing.
3. **Consult with acquiring bank and processor partners** to confirm their expectations for refund authentication and any contractual requirements.
4. **Validate PSD2/SCA applicability** with EU/UK counsel if the pilot includes European markets, particularly whether refunds are treated as payment transactions requiring SCA.
5. **Test the fraud-control stack** against known ATO and refund-fraud scenarios, and document the results.
6. **Draft the payer-facing authentication notice** to ensure it meets plain-language and accessibility requirements (WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA, payer's language).
7. **Confirm recordkeeping requirements** with Compliance, including retention periods for authentication logs and fraud signals by jurisdiction.
