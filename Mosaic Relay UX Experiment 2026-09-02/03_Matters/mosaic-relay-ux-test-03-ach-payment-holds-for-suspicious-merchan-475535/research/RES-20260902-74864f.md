# When does pausing or holding funds create money-transmission licensing exposure

## Question

When does pausing or holding funds create money-transmission licensing exposure for a non-bank payment infrastructure provider?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a non-bank payment infrastructure provider, wants to implement an automated control that pauses ACH debits, credits, and seller payouts when transaction-risk signals exceed a threshold. The system would place a 24-hour hold (with risk-manager-approved extensions), show merchants a generic explanation, and create an investigation task for Payment Operations. Counsel is involved because pausing or holding funds may create money-transmission licensing exposure, NACHA compliance obligations, and consumer-protection risks. The control would operate continuously, affecting merchants, payers, recipients, and acquiring/ACH partners.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay implement automated 24-hour holds (with extensions) on ACH transactions and payouts without triggering state money-transmission licensing requirements, and what contractual, notice, and operational safeguards must be in place to avoid custody-based licensing exposure while remaining compliant with NACHA rules and consumer-protection laws?
## Open questions
- Which jurisdictions do the merchants, payers, and recipients sit in? This determines which state money-transmission laws and NACHA rules apply.
- When a hold is placed, do the funds stay with the acquiring/ACH partner, or does Mosaic Relay take custody of them? Custody is the key factor in money-transmission analysis.
- Are any of these consumer-originated payments, or is this B2B only? Consumer payments trigger Reg E and additional consumer-protection obligations.
- What is the maximum hold period and how are extensions documented? Indefinite or extended holds increase licensing and UDAAP risk.
- Does Mosaic Relay's contract with acquiring/ACH partners permit holds, and what escalation obligations exist?
---
## Likely rules and issues
### Money-transmission licensing perimeter
The core issue is whether pausing or holding funds causes Mosaic Relay to "receive money for transmission" or "hold funds incidental to transmission," which would trigger state money-transmitter licensing requirements. Key factors:
1. **Custody and control**: If Mosaic Relay takes possession or control of funds (even temporarily), it may be deemed to be engaged in money transmission. If funds remain with the acquiring bank or ACH partner and Mosaic Relay merely instructs the partner to delay settlement, licensing risk is lower.
2. **Duration of hold**: A brief administrative hold (e.g., 24 hours) for fraud review is less likely to be characterized as transmission than an extended or indefinite hold. Longer holds begin to resemble stored value or deposit-taking.
3. **Who bears the risk**: If Mosaic Relay is liable to the merchant or recipient for the funds during the hold period, that suggests custody. If the acquiring partner retains risk and Mosaic Relay is only a technology layer, the analysis shifts.
4. **State-by-state variation**: Money-transmission laws vary. Some states (e.g., New York, California) have broad definitions; others exempt certain payment-processing activities. The "agent of the payee" exemption may apply if Mosaic Relay is acting on behalf of the merchant, but this is fact-specific.
5. **NACHA Operating Rules**: NACHA rules govern ACH transactions and impose obligations on Originators, Third-Party Senders, and ODFIs/RDFIs. Holds must comply with NACHA return and notification timelines. Unauthorized-debit risk arises if a hold delays a consumer's ability to dispute a transaction.
### Consumer-protection and UDAAP risk
- **Reg E**: If any payments are consumer-originated, Regulation E (Electronic Fund Transfer Act) applies. Holds that delay error-resolution or investigation timelines may violate Reg E.
- **UDAAP**: A generic explanation to merchants may be deemed deceptive if it misleads them about the reason for the hold or their rights. Unfair or abusive practices risk arises if holds are applied arbitrarily, without clear criteria, or without a path to resolution.
- **Complaint handling**: Failure to provide a mechanism for merchants to dispute or escalate holds may create UDAAP exposure.
### Contractual and operational issues
- **Partner agreements**: Mosaic Relay's contracts with acquiring banks and ACH partners must permit holds and allocate responsibility for compliance, notice, and escalation.
- **Recordkeeping**: Holds must be documented with clear audit trails to demonstrate compliance with NACHA, state law, and internal policies.
- **Segregated accounts**: If Mosaic Relay does take custody of funds, segregation and safeguarding requirements may apply under state money-transmission laws.

## Viable paths
### Path 1: Partner-implemented holds (lowest licensing risk)
Mosaic Relay instructs the acquiring bank or ACH partner to delay settlement or payout. Funds remain with the partner. Mosaic Relay provides the risk-scoring and decisioning layer but does not take custody.
**Advantages**: Minimizes money-transmission licensing exposure; aligns with existing partner agreements; simpler compliance posture.
**Requirements**: Partner contracts must permit holds; Mosaic Relay must ensure partner complies with NACHA and consumer-protection obligations; clear escalation and release criteria.
### Path 2: Mosaic Relay holds funds with safeguarding (higher licensing risk)
Mosaic Relay takes custody of funds during the hold period, places them in a segregated account, and implements safeguarding measures.
**Advantages**: Greater operational control; faster decisioning.
**Requirements**: State money-transmitter licenses (or reliance on exemptions); compliance with safeguarding, bonding, and net-worth requirements; robust recordkeeping and audit trails; clear consumer and merchant notices.
### Path 3: Hybrid model with contractual allocation
Mosaic Relay and partners share responsibility. Mosaic Relay provides risk signals and recommends holds; partners implement and custody funds; Mosaic Relay handles merchant communication and investigation.
**Advantages**: Balances control and licensing risk; leverages partner infrastructure.
**Requirements**: Clear contractual allocation of roles, responsibilities, and liabilities; coordinated compliance and escalation workflows.

## Facts that could change the answer
1. **Custody**: If Mosaic Relay takes custody of funds (even briefly), money-transmission licensing risk increases significantly. If funds remain with partners, risk is lower.
2. **Jurisdictions**: If merchants, payers, or recipients are in states with broad money-transmission definitions (e.g., NY, CA, TX), licensing exposure is higher. If all parties are in states with narrow definitions or exemptions, risk is lower.
3. **Consumer vs. B2B**: If any payments are consumer-originated, Reg E and state consumer-protection laws apply, imposing stricter notice, investigation, and error-resolution requirements.
4. **Hold duration**: A 24-hour hold is defensible as a fraud-review measure. Extended or indefinite holds (e.g., >72 hours without resolution) increase licensing and UDAAP risk.
5. **Partner agreements**: If existing contracts with acquiring/ACH partners do not permit holds or do not allocate compliance responsibility, Mosaic Relay may need to renegotiate or implement holds unilaterally (increasing risk).
6. **Segregation**: If Mosaic Relay holds funds in a segregated account, safeguarding and licensing obligations are triggered. If funds are commingled or not segregated, additional risk arises.
---
## Recommended last-mile verification
Before proceeding, counsel should:
1. **Confirm custody flow**: Obtain a clear diagram or description of where funds sit during the hold period. If Mosaic Relay takes custody, licensing analysis is required. If funds remain with partners, confirm that partner agreements permit holds.
2. **Identify jurisdictions**: Determine which states' money-transmission laws apply based on merchant, payer, and recipient locations. Conduct a 50-state survey or rely on counsel with money-transmission expertise.
3. **Review partner contracts**: Confirm that acquiring and ACH partner agreements permit holds, allocate compliance responsibility, and address notice, escalation, and liability.
4. **Assess consumer exposure**: Determine whether any payments are consumer-originated. If so, ensure compliance with Reg E, including error-resolution and investigation timelines.
5. **Draft hold policy**: Document hold criteria, duration, extension approval, release conditions, merchant notice, and complaint-handling procedures. Ensure the policy is consistent with NACHA rules and state law.
6. **Consult NACHA rules**: Review NACHA Operating Rules on holds, returns, and notifications. Ensure the control complies with return timelines and unauthorized-debit provisions.
7. **Consider UDAAP review**: Have counsel review the generic merchant explanation and hold mechanics for UDAAP risk. Ensure merchants have a clear path to dispute or escalate holds.

## Recommendation (for lawyer's decision)

Mosaic Relay should instruct acquiring/ACH partners to delay settlement or payout when risk signals exceed a threshold. Funds remain with partners. Mosaic Relay provides risk-scoring and decisioning but does not take custody.
**Key safeguards**:
- Confirm partner agreements permit holds and allocate compliance responsibility.
- Implement a written hold policy with clear criteria, duration limits, extension approval, release conditions, and merchant notice.
- Ensure the generic merchant explanation is accurate and not misleading (avoid UDAAP risk).
- Provide a mechanism for merchants to dispute or escalate holds.
- Document all holds with audit trails to demonstrate compliance with NACHA and state law.
- If any payments are consumer-originated, ensure compliance with Reg E error-resolution and investigation timelines.
