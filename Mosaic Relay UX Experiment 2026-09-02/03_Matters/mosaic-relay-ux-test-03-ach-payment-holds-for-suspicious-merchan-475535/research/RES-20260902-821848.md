# What Reg E and UDAAP obligations apply to holds on

## Question

What Reg E and UDAAP obligations apply to holds on consumer-originated ACH payments?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a B2B payment-infrastructure provider, wants to implement an automated control that pauses ACH debits, credits, and seller payouts when transaction-risk signals exceed a threshold. The system would place a temporary 24-hour hold (with risk-manager-approved extensions), show merchants a generic explanation, and create an investigation task for Payment Operations. Counsel is involved because the control implicates NACHA Operating Rules, potential Regulation E exposure for consumer-originated payments, UDAAP risks from generic explanations, and money-transmission licensing perimeter questions. The company is fictional and the launch timing is not yet fixed.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay implement automated 24-hour holds (with extensions) on ACH transactions and seller payouts without violating Regulation E, NACHA rules, or UDAAP prohibitions, and without triggering money-transmission licensing obligations, given that the company does not take custody of funds and the consumer/B2B mix is not yet confirmed?
## Open questions
- **Consumer vs. B2B scope**: Are any of the ACH transactions consumer-originated (e.g., consumer bill payments, P2P transfers), or is this strictly B2B? This determines whether Regulation E applies.
- **Fund custody and control**: When a hold is placed, do the funds remain with the acquiring/ACH partner, or does Mosaic Relay take custody or control? This drives money-transmission analysis.
- **Maximum hold period and extension criteria**: What is the longest permissible hold, and what standards govern risk-manager extensions? Indefinite or open-ended holds create higher legal risk.
- **Notice content and timing**: What specific information will the generic explanation contain, and when will it be provided? Vague or misleading notices create UDAAP exposure.
- **Segregated accounts**: Are held funds segregated or commingled? This affects both money-transmission and consumer-protection analysis.

## Likely rules and issues
### Regulation E (Electronic Fund Transfer Act)
**Applicability**: Regulation E applies to electronic fund transfers (EFTs) involving consumer accounts. If any of the ACH transactions Mosaic Relay processes are consumer-originated (e.g., a consumer authorizes a merchant to debit their account, or a consumer receives a payout), Reg E likely applies to those transactions.
**Key obligations if Reg E applies**:
1. **Unauthorized transfers and error resolution (12 C.F.R. § 1005.11)**: Consumers have the right to report unauthorized EFTs and errors. Financial institutions must investigate promptly (generally within 10 business days, or 20 days for new accounts) and provisionally credit the consumer's account if the investigation takes longer. If Mosaic Relay's hold delays a consumer's access to funds or prevents a consumer from receiving a credit, and the consumer disputes the underlying transaction, the hold could interfere with error-resolution timelines.
2. **Stop-payment rights (12 C.F.R. § 1005.10(c))**: Consumers have the right to stop payment on preauthorized EFTs by notifying their financial institution at least three business days before the scheduled transfer. If Mosaic Relay's hold is placed after a consumer has initiated a stop-payment request, the hold could conflict with the consumer's stop-payment rights.
3. **Disclosure requirements (12 C.F.R. § 1005.7)**: Financial institutions must provide initial disclosures of terms and conditions, including error-resolution procedures and liability limits. If Mosaic Relay is considered a "financial institution" under Reg E (which is unlikely if it does not hold consumer accounts), or if it is acting as a service provider to a financial institution, the hold mechanism and consumer notice must be consistent with the account-holding institution's Reg E disclosures.
**Practical impact**: If the control applies to consumer-originated ACH transactions, Mosaic Relay must ensure that holds do not delay or prevent consumers from exercising Reg E rights, and that the hold and investigation process aligns with the account-holding financial institution's error-resolution obligations. The 24-hour initial hold is likely defensible as a fraud-prevention measure, but extensions beyond 24–48 hours without clear consumer notice and a path to resolution could create Reg E risk.
### UDAAP (Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices)
**Applicability**: UDAAP prohibitions under the Dodd-Frank Act (12 U.S.C. § 5531, § 5536) apply to any covered person or service provider offering consumer financial products or services. Even if Mosaic Relay is B2B, if its actions affect consumers (e.g., delaying consumer payouts or preventing consumer-authorized debits), UDAAP risk exists. The CFPB and FTC have authority to enforce UDAAP.
**Key UDAAP risks**:
1. **Deceptive practices**: A "generic explanation" for the hold could be deceptive if it misleads merchants or consumers about the reason for the hold, the expected duration, or the process for release. For example, if the notice says "your transaction is under review" but does not explain that funds will be held for up to 24 hours (or longer with extensions), or if it implies a legal determination when none has been made, that could be deceptive.
2. **Unfair practices**: A practice is unfair if it causes or is likely to cause substantial injury to consumers, the injury is not reasonably avoidable, and the injury is not outweighed by countervailing benefits. Holding funds for extended periods without clear notice, a defined process for resolution, or a way for merchants/consumers to expedite review could be unfair, especially if the hold causes overdrafts, missed payments, or other downstream harm.
3. **Abusive practices**: A practice is abusive if it materially interferes with a consumer's ability to understand a term or condition, or takes unreasonable advantage of a consumer's lack of understanding or inability to protect their interests. Open-ended holds with vague explanations and no clear escalation path could be abusive.
**Practical impact**: The generic explanation must be accurate, timely, and provide enough information for the merchant (and any affected consumers) to understand the reason for the hold, the expected duration, and the process for resolution. Mosaic Relay should avoid language that implies a legal or regulatory determination, and should provide a clear path for merchants to contact support and expedite review. Holds should have a defined maximum duration, and extensions should be rare and based on documented risk criteria.
### NACHA Operating Rules
**Applicability**: NACHA rules govern ACH transactions in the US. Mosaic Relay's ACH partners (ODFIs and RDFIs) are bound by NACHA rules, and Mosaic Relay's contracts with those partners likely require compliance.
**Key NACHA issues**:
1. **Return timeframes**: NACHA rules impose strict return timeframes (e.g., 2 banking days for most returns, 60 days for unauthorized consumer debits). If Mosaic Relay's hold delays the processing of a return or the settlement of a transaction beyond these timeframes, it could create liability for the ODFI/RDFI and breach Mosaic Relay's partner agreements.
2. **Unauthorized debit returns**: Consumers can return ACH debits as unauthorized within 60 days of the settlement date. If Mosaic Relay's hold prevents a merchant from receiving funds that are later returned as unauthorized, the hold may reduce Mosaic Relay's exposure, but it does not eliminate the consumer's right to return the transaction.
3. **Notification of change (NOC) and return reason codes**: NACHA rules require specific return reason codes and NOC procedures. Mosaic Relay's hold and investigation process must align with these codes and procedures.
**Practical impact**: The hold mechanism must be designed to comply with NACHA return timeframes and must not prevent the ODFI/RDFI from meeting their NACHA obligations. Mosaic Relay should coordinate with its ACH partners to ensure the hold process is consistent with NACHA rules and partner agreements.
### Money-transmission licensing
**Applicability**: Money-transmission laws vary by state, but generally require a license if a person receives money for transmission or holds funds incident to transmission. If Mosaic Relay takes custody or control of held funds, or if it holds funds in a segregated account for later disbursement, it could trigger money-transmission licensing obligations in some states.
**Practical impact**: The current assumption is that Mosaic Relay does not take custody of funds and holds are implemented through partner arrangements. If that assumption is correct, money-transmission risk is lower. However, if Mosaic Relay has any discretion over the timing or release of held funds, or if it holds funds in its own accounts, the money-transmission analysis could change. This is a fact-specific question that requires confirmation of the fund-flow mechanics.

## Viable paths
### Path 1: Limit the control to B2B transactions only
**Description**: Restrict the automated hold to ACH transactions where both the originator and the receiver are businesses, and exclude consumer-originated or consumer-received transactions.
**Advantages**:
- Avoids Regulation E entirely.
- Reduces UDAAP risk (though does not eliminate it, as UDAAP can still apply to practices affecting small businesses in some states).
- Simplifies compliance with NACHA rules, as B2B transactions have fewer consumer-protection overlays.
**Disadvantages**:
- May not address all fraud risks if consumer-originated transactions are a significant fraud vector.
- Requires clear transaction-type identification and filtering logic.
**Next step**: Confirm with Product whether the control can be scoped to B2B only, and if so, implement transaction-type filters.
### Path 2: Apply the control to all transactions, but design for Reg E and UDAAP compliance
**Description**: Implement the hold for all ACH transactions, but build in consumer-protection safeguards.
**Key safeguards**:
- **Clear, accurate notice**: Provide a notice that explains the hold is a fraud-prevention measure, states the expected duration (24 hours with possible extensions), and provides a contact for questions and escalation.
- **Defined maximum hold period**: Set a maximum hold period (e.g., 72 hours) after which funds must be released or the transaction must be returned, unless there is a documented legal or regulatory basis for a longer hold.
- **Reg E alignment**: Ensure the hold does not delay or prevent consumers from exercising Reg E rights (e.g., stop-payment, error resolution). Coordinate with account-holding financial institutions to ensure the hold process aligns with their Reg E obligations.
- **Complaint and escalation process**: Provide a clear path for merchants and consumers to contact support, escalate concerns, and request expedited review.
- **Recordkeeping**: Maintain records of all holds, including the reason for the hold, the duration, the outcome of the investigation, and any consumer or merchant communications.
**Advantages**:
- Addresses fraud risk across all transaction types.
- Reduces UDAAP and Reg E risk through proactive compliance design.
**Disadvantages**:
- More complex to implement and monitor.
- Requires ongoing coordination with ACH partners and account-holding institutions.
**Next step**: Draft the consumer and merchant notice language, define the maximum hold period and extension criteria, and coordinate with ACH partners to confirm NACHA and Reg E alignment.
### Path 3: Implement the hold through partner arrangements without taking custody
**Description**: Structure the hold so that funds remain with the acquiring/ACH partner, and Mosaic Relay does not take custody or control.
**Advantages**:
- Reduces money-transmission licensing risk.
- Aligns with Mosaic Relay's business model (not a bank, does not hold deposits).
**Disadvantages**:
- Requires partner cooperation and may limit Mosaic Relay's control over the hold process.
- May not be feasible if partners are unwilling or unable to implement holds on Mosaic Relay's behalf.
**Next step**: Confirm with ACH partners whether they can implement holds on Mosaic Relay's behalf, and if so, document the arrangement in the partner agreement.

## Facts that could change the answer
1. **Consumer vs. B2B scope**: If the control applies to consumer-originated transactions, Regulation E applies and the compliance burden increases significantly. If it is B2B only, Reg E does not apply.
2. **Fund custody**: If Mosaic Relay takes custody or control of held funds, money-transmission licensing obligations could be triggered in some states. If funds remain with partners, that risk is lower.
3. **Maximum hold period**: If the hold can extend beyond 72 hours without a clear legal or regulatory basis, UDAAP and Reg E risk increases. A defined maximum hold period with documented extension criteria reduces risk.
4. **Notice content**: If the generic explanation is vague, misleading, or implies a legal determination, UDAAP risk increases. Clear, accurate, and timely notice reduces risk.
5. **Segregated accounts**: If held funds are segregated, money-transmission and consumer-protection analysis could change. If funds are commingled, the analysis is different.

2. **Confirm fund-flow mechanics**: Confirm with Treasury/Operations whether Mosaic Relay takes custody of held funds, or whether funds remain with acquiring/ACH partners. This drives the money-transmission analysis.
3. **Review NACHA rules and partner agreements**: Review the NACHA Operating Rules and Mosaic Relay's agreements with ACH partners to confirm that the hold mechanism complies with return timeframes, notification requirements, and any partner-specific hold or reserve provisions.
4. **Draft and review notice language**: Draft the merchant and consumer notice language and review it for UDAAP compliance. The notice should be clear, accurate, and provide a path for questions and escalation.
5. **Define maximum hold period and extension criteria**: Work with Risk and Product to define a maximum hold period (e.g., 72 hours) and documented criteria for extensions. Extensions should be rare and based on objective risk factors.
6. **Consult with ACH partners**: Coordinate with acquiring and ACH partners to confirm that the hold process aligns with their NACHA obligations and does not create liability for them.
7. **Consider state money-transmission laws**: If Mosaic Relay takes custody of funds or has discretion over the timing or release of held funds, consult with counsel in relevant states to confirm whether money-transmission licensing is required.
