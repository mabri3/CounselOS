      Can Mosaic Relay implement a 24-hour automated hold on ACH debits, credits,

      unfair-practice or unauthorized-debit exposure, and what notice, recordkeeping,

      - Do the acquiring/ACH partner agreements impose additional hold, escalation,

      ## Likely rules and issues

      Based on general knowledge of NACHA Operating Rules and payment regulation (model-only
      analysis, not retrieved authority):

      ### NACHA Operating Rules — holds and returns

      NACHA Operating Rules govern ACH transactions in the US. Key issues for the
      proposed control:

      - **Holds on ACH credits and debits**: NACHA rules generally do not prohibit
      a participant (such as an ODFI, RDFI, or third-party sender) from placing a
      temporary hold on funds for risk or fraud review, but the rules impose strict
      timing requirements for returns and notifications of change (NOCs). If Mosaic
      Relay or its partner is the ODFI or RDFI, holding funds beyond certain windows
      may conflict with return deadlines or settlement obligations.

      - **Return timeframes**: Under NACHA rules, ACH debit returns for unauthorized
      transactions (R10, R07, etc.) must generally be initiated within specific windows
      (e.g., 24 hours for certain return reasons, up to 60 days for consumer unauthorized
      debits under Reg E). If Mosaic Relay''s hold delays the merchant''s ability
      to return a debit or respond to a return, it could create liability or violate
      NACHA timing rules.

      - **Notification requirements**: NACHA rules require certain notifications to
      originators and receivers in specific circumstances (e.g., NOCs for incorrect
      account information, return notifications). A generic merchant-facing explanation
      may not satisfy these requirements if the hold affects the merchant''s ability
      to meet its own obligations.

      - **Third-party sender registration and risk management**: If Mosaic Relay is
      acting as a third-party sender or third-party service provider under NACHA rules,
      it must comply with registration, risk management, and monitoring obligations.
      The proposed control aligns with these obligations, but the implementation must
      not interfere with the ODFI''s ability to meet its own NACHA obligations.

      ### Money-transmission licensing

      If Mosaic Relay takes custody of funds during the hold period (i.e., funds are
      moved to a Mosaic Relay-controlled account rather than remaining with the acquiring/ACH
      partner), it may trigger state money-transmission licensing requirements. Most
      states define money transmission to include receiving money for transmission
      or holding funds incident to transmission. If funds remain with the regulated
      partner and Mosaic Relay merely instructs the partner to delay release, licensing
      risk is lower. This is a critical fact-dependent question.

      ### Reg E and unauthorized-debit risk

      If any of the ACH debits are consumer-originated (i.e., debits to consumer accounts),
      Regulation E (12 C.F.R. § 1005) applies. Reg E imposes error-resolution and
      liability rules for unauthorized electronic fund transfers. If Mosaic Relay''s
      hold delays a consumer''s ability to dispute an unauthorized debit or receive
      a refund, it could create Reg E liability for the financial institution and
      reputational/contractual risk for Mosaic Relay. The control must not interfere
      with the consumer''s Reg E rights or the bank''s ability to meet Reg E timelines.

      ### UDAAP (Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices)

      A generic explanation to merchants ("your transaction is under review") may
      be acceptable if it is accurate and not misleading, but if the hold is lengthy,
      funds are not released promptly after review, or merchants are not given a meaningful
      opportunity to resolve the issue, it could be characterized as unfair or abusive
      under CFPB or FTC standards. Transparency, prompt resolution, and clear escalation
      paths mitigate this risk.

      ### Contractual obligations to partners

      Mosaic Relay''s agreements with acquiring banks, ACH operators, and payment
      processors likely impose additional hold, notice, and escalation requirements.
      These contracts may specify maximum hold periods, required notices, and liability
      allocation. The control must be consistent with these agreements.

      ## Viable paths

      ### Path 1: Implement the control with partner-mediated holds (lower risk)

      Structure the control so that funds remain with the acquiring/ACH partner and
      Mosaic Relay merely instructs the partner to delay release. This minimizes money-transmission
      licensing risk and keeps Mosaic Relay out of the flow of funds. Ensure the partner
      agreement permits the hold and specifies notice, maximum duration, and release
      conditions. Provide merchants with a clear, accurate explanation and a defined
      escalation path. Limit initial holds to 24 hours and require risk-manager approval
      for extensions, with a defined maximum hold period (e.g., 5 business days) to
      avoid indefinite holds.

      ### Path 2: Implement the control with Mosaic Relay custody (higher risk, requires
      licensing analysis)

      If Mosaic Relay takes custody of funds during the hold, conduct a state-by-state
      money-transmission licensing analysis before launch. This path is viable only
      if Mosaic Relay obtains necessary licenses or confirms an exemption applies
      (e.g., agent-of-the-payee, payment processor exemption). This path requires
      significant legal and compliance work and is not recommended unless the business
      model requires custody.

      ### Path 3: Limit the control to B2B transactions only (reduces Reg E risk)

      If the control applies only to B2B ACH transactions (no consumer-originated
      debits or credits), Reg E does not apply, and the analysis simplifies. Confirm
      with the business whether consumer payments are in scope. If they are, implement
      additional safeguards to ensure Reg E compliance (e.g., carve-outs for consumer
      disputes, expedited review for consumer-originated transactions).

      ### Path 4: Pilot the control with a subset of merchants and enhanced monitoring

      Launch the control as a pilot with a small group of merchants, enhanced fraud-analyst
      staffing, and daily monitoring of hold durations, release times, and merchant
      complaints. Use pilot data to refine hold thresholds, notice language, and escalation
      procedures before full rollout. This path allows the business to validate the
      control''s effectiveness while identifying and mitigating legal and operational
      risks.
