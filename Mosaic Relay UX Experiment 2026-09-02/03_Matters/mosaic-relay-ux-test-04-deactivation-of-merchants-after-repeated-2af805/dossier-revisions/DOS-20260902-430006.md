# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Product change to deactivate merchants with chargeback rates above threshold for three consecutive months; metric now defined (network + internal thresholds, excluding rebutted friendly-fraud, pending authorizations, and consumer-law complaints); marketplace override limited to documented exception approval. Remaining open items: cure/appeal process and launch timing/scope.

## Decision question

Mosaic Relay wants to deactivate merchants whose chargeback rate stays above a defined threshold for three consecutive months. The proposed experience warns the merchant, requires a remediation plan, restricts new payment methods, and disables processing after month three unless a documented exception is approved. Refunds and dispute responses remain available for 90 days after deactivation. Legal advice is requested on the metric, warning/termination notices, cure and appeal rights, post-deactivation refund/dispute obligations, and any required reporting or customer communications.

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay wants to deactivate merchants whose chargeback rate remains above a defined threshold for three consecutive months.
- The proposed experience would warn the merchant, require a remediation plan, restrict new payment methods, and then disable processing if performance does not improve.
- Warnings would begin after the first month above threshold, and deactivation would occur after the third month unless a documented exception is approved.
- Existing refunds and dispute responses would remain available for 90 days after deactivation.
- The merchant agreement permits suspension for excessive disputes but does not define the notice content, cure period, or appeal process.
- Mosaic Relay has network monitoring obligations and wants a consistent process.
- Some merchants sell digital goods, while others sell physical goods or recurring services.
- Actors include merchants, their customers, Mosaic Relay Risk and Support, card networks, acquiring banks, and marketplace customers.
- How should the chargeback metric be defined for triggering warnings and deactivation? — Use network-specific thresholds (Visa/Mastercard monitoring program levels): Use an internal Mosaic Relay-defined threshold: Exclude false/friendly-fraud disputes that are successfully rebutted: Exclude pending authorizations from the metric
- exclude them (metric tracks chargebacks only).
- Can marketplace customers override a Mosaic Relay deactivation decision? — Override only with documented exception approval

## Assumptions

- The defined chargeback threshold and three-month window are treated as business-set parameters pending confirmation of network-specific thresholds.
- Deactivation is a contractual suspension/termination right under the merchant agreement, not a new regulatory obligation.
- Network-specific thresholds (Visa/Mastercard monitoring program levels) and an internal Mosaic Relay-defined threshold are both used, with the internal threshold treated as the operative trigger pending confirmation of how the two interact.

## Issues and workstreams

- Notice and cure: merchant agreement does not define notice content, cure period, or appeal process for suspension/deactivation.
- Post-deactivation obligations: refund and dispute response obligations after deactivation (90-day window) need legal framing.
- Reporting and customer communications: any required reporting to networks/banks or customer communications after deactivation is undefined.
- Timing and scope: target launch date and affected jurisdictions/networks are undefined.
- Cure period and appeal process before deactivation are undefined.

## Open questions

- Cure period and appeal process before deactivation
- Target launch date and affected jurisdictions/networks
