# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a B2B payment-infrastructure provider, plans to deactivate merchants whose chargeback rate exceeds a defined threshold for three consecutive months. The proposed flow includes warnings after the first month, a required remediation plan, restrictions on new payment methods, and eventual processing disablement, with refunds and dispute responses continuing for 90 days post-deactivation. Counsel is involved because the merchant agreement permits suspension for excessive disputes but lacks defined notice content, cure periods, or appeal processes, creating contractual and network-compliance uncertainty. The initial launch is assumed to cover U.S. Visa and Mastercard programs through acquiring-bank partners, with no set launch date.

## Decision question

What specific notice content, cure-period length, and appeal rights must Mosaic Relay build into its merchant deactivation flow to satisfy Visa and Mastercard monitoring-program requirements and avoid acquirer penalties or network sanctions when terminating merchants for excessive chargebacks?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay wants to deactivate merchants whose chargeback rate remains above a defined threshold for three consecutive months.
- The proposed experience would warn the merchant, require a remediation plan, restrict new payment methods, and then disable processing if performance does not improve.
- Warnings would begin after the first month above threshold, and deactivation would occur after the third month unless a documented exception is approved.
- Existing refunds and dispute responses would remain available for 90 days after deactivation.
- The merchant agreement permits suspension for excessive disputes but does not define the notice content, cure period, or appeal process.
- Mosaic Relay has network monitoring obligations and wants a consistent process.
- Some merchants sell digital goods, while others sell physical goods or recurring services.
- Actors include merchants, their customers, Mosaic Relay Risk and Support, card networks, acquiring banks, and marketplace customers.

- exclude them (metric tracks chargebacks only).
- Can marketplace customers override a Mosaic Relay deactivation decision? — Override only with documented exception approval
- What cure period and appeal process should merchants have before deactivation? — 30-day cure with written appeal
- What is the target launch date and which jurisdictions/networks apply first? — No launch date is set. Assume an initial U.S. launch covering Visa and Mastercard programs through Mosaic Relay's acquiring-bank partners; confirm applicable jurisdictions, network programs, and local notice rules with Risk and Compliance before rollout.

## Assumptions

- The defined chargeback threshold and three-month window are treated as business-set parameters pending confirmation of network-specific thresholds.
- Deactivation is a contractual suspension/termination right under the merchant agreement, not a new regulatory obligation.
- Network-specific thresholds (Visa/Mastercard monitoring program levels) and an internal Mosaic Relay-defined threshold are both used, with the internal threshold treated as the operative trigger pending confirmation of how the two interact.

## Issues and workstreams

- Notice and cure: merchant agreement does not define notice content, cure period, or appeal process for suspension/deactivation.
- Post-deactivation obligations: refund and dispute response obligations after deactivation (90-day window) need legal framing.
- Reporting and customer communications: any required reporting to networks/banks or customer communications after deactivation is undefined.
- Timing and scope: target launch date and affected jurisdictions/networks are undefined; confirm with Risk and Compliance before rollout.

## Open questions

- What are the exact current Visa and Mastercard chargeback-monitoring thresholds (e.g., Visa’s VDMP tiers, Mastercard’s ECM/ECM+ levels) that would trigger network-mandated action, and how do they interact with Mosaic Relay’s internal threshold?
- Does the merchant agreement’s existing suspension clause provide sufficient contractual basis for the proposed 30-day cure and written-appeal process, or is an amendment required before launch?
- How should successfully rebutted friendly-fraud disputes be treated in the metric to avoid penalizing merchants for chargebacks that are later reversed?
- What post-deactivation reporting, if any, do acquiring-bank partners require to the networks (e.g., MATCH listing or equivalent), and what customer communications are mandated during the 90-day refund/dispute window?
- Can marketplace customers override a deactivation only through a documented exception, and what approval level and audit trail does that exception require?
- **Network monitoring programs**: Visa and Mastercard operate chargeback-monitoring programs that impose escalating obligations on acquirers (and, by extension, their payment-service-provider partners like Mosaic Relay) when merchant chargeback rates exceed defined thresholds. These programs typically require the acquirer to notify the merchant, impose remediation requirements, and ultimately terminate processing if performance does not improve. The exact thresholds and timelines are set by the networks and flow down through acquiring-bank agreements.
- **Contractual gap**: The merchant agreement permits suspension for “excessive disputes” but does not define notice content, cure period, or appeal process. This creates a risk that a deactivation could be challenged as procedurally unfair or in breach of implied contractual duties, especially if the merchant can show it was not given a meaningful opportunity to cure.
- **Metric definition**: The decision to exclude successfully rebutted friendly-fraud disputes and pending authorizations from the metric reduces false positives but must be documented clearly to avoid disputes about how the rate is calculated.
- **Post-deactivation obligations**: The 90-day window for refunds and dispute responses is a business choice that likely aligns with network expectations, but the legal basis for continuing to process refunds after termination should be confirmed in the merchant agreement.
- **Marketplace override**: Allowing marketplace customers to override deactivation only with documented exception approval creates a control point, but the exception process must be auditable and consistently applied to avoid arbitrary-treatment claims.
- **Adopt network-aligned thresholds and timelines**: Use Visa/Mastercard monitoring-program levels as the operative trigger, with the internal threshold set at or slightly below network levels to provide early warning. This minimizes the risk of acquirer penalties and ensures consistency with partner expectations.
- **Layer contractual notice and cure onto existing suspension right**: Build the 30-day cure and written-appeal process into the product flow as a matter of policy, while simultaneously amending the merchant agreement (or adding a policy incorporated by reference) to define notice content, cure rights, and appeal procedures. This reduces contractual challenge risk without delaying launch.
- **Phased rollout with exception governance**: Launch with the assumed U.S. Visa/Mastercard scope, require documented exception approval for any marketplace override, and assign exception authority to a designated Risk or Legal owner. Track exceptions to identify patterns that may require policy adjustment.
- Confirmation that Visa/Mastercard thresholds or timelines have changed, or that acquiring-bank partners impose stricter requirements than the networks’ baseline.
- Discovery that the merchant agreement’s suspension clause is narrower than described, or that applicable state law (e.g., California) imposes additional notice or cure requirements for certain merchant categories.
- Evidence that a material volume of chargebacks are friendly fraud that is later rebutted, which could make the metric definition outcome-determinative for borderline merchants.
- Acquiring-bank partner requirements for MATCH listing or equivalent reporting after deactivation, which could create additional compliance steps.
- Confirm current Visa and Mastercard chargeback-monitoring thresholds, timelines, and acquirer obligations directly with acquiring-bank partners or network program documentation.
- Review the merchant agreement’s suspension and termination clauses to confirm they support the proposed notice, cure, and appeal flow, or draft a targeted amendment.
- Validate with Risk and Compliance that the 90-day post-deactivation refund/dispute window satisfies any network or partner requirements for wind-down operations.
- Document the metric-calculation methodology (including treatment of rebutted disputes and pending authorizations) in a policy that can be referenced in merchant notices.
