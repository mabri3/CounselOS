# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay (a fictional test company) plans to deactivate merchants whose chargeback rate exceeds a defined threshold for three consecutive months, with warnings starting after month one, a required remediation plan, restrictions on new payment methods, and processing disabled after month three absent a documented exception. The merchant agreement permits suspension for excessive disputes but does not define notice content, cure period, or appeal rights, and existing refunds and dispute responses would remain available for 90 days post-deactivation. Counsel is involved because the program touches card-network monitoring obligations, acquiring-bank relationships, contractual suspension rights, and merchant/consumer communications. Intake answers set a 30-day cure with written appeal, marketplace override only via documented exception, and an assumed initial U.S. launch covering Visa and Mastercard programs through acquiring-bank partners.

## Decision question

Can Mosaic Relay launch a U.S. three-strikes chargeback deactivation program — internal threshold plus Visa/Mastercard monitoring levels, warnings after month one, 30-day cure with written appeal, deactivation after month three, and 90-day post-termination refund/dispute access — under its current merchant agreement, or must it amend notice, cure, and appeal terms and align thresholds with network program rules before rollout?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay wants to deactivate merchants whose chargeback rate remains above a defined threshold for three consecutive months.
- The proposed experience would warn the merchant, require a remediation plan, restrict new payment methods, and then disable processing if performance does not improve.
- Warnings would begin after the first month above threshold, and deactivation would occur after the third month unless a documented exception is approved.
- Existing refunds and dispute responses would remain available for 90 days after deactivation.
- The merchant agreement permits suspension for excessive disputes but does not define the notice content, cure period, or appeal process.
- Mosaic Relay has network monitoring obligations and wants a consistent process.
- Some merchants sell digital goods, while others sell physical goods or recurring services.
- Actors include merchants, their customers, Mosaic Relay Risk and Support, card networks, acquiring banks, and marketplace customers.
- How should the chargeback metric be defined for triggering warnings and deactivation? — Use network-specific thresholds (Visa/Mastercard monitoring program levels): Use an internal Mosaic Relay-defined threshold: Exclude false/friendly-fraud disputes that are successfully rebutted: Exclude pending authorizations from the metric
- exclude them (metric tracks chargebacks only).
- Can marketplace customers override a Mosaic Relay deactivation decision? — Override only with documented exception approval
- What cure period and appeal process should merchants have before deactivation? — 30-day cure with written appeal
- What is the target launch date and which jurisdictions/networks apply first? — No launch date is set. Assume an initial U.S. launch covering Visa and Mastercard programs through Mosaic Relay's acquiring-bank partners; confirm applicable jurisdictions, network programs, and local notice rules with Risk and Compliance before rollout.

## Assumptions

- The defined chargeback threshold and three-month window are treated as business-set parameters pending confirmation of network-specific thresholds.
- Deactivation is a contractual suspension/termination right under the merchant agreement, not a new regulatory obligation.
- Network-specific thresholds (Visa/Mastercard monitoring program levels) and an internal Mosaic Relay-defined threshold are both used, with the internal threshold treated as the operative trigger pending confirmation of how the two interact.

## Issues and workstreams

- Notice and cure: merchant agreement does not define notice content, cure period, or appeal process for suspension/deactivation.
- Post-deactivation obligations: refund and dispute response obligations after deactivation (90-day window) need legal framing.
- Reporting and customer communications: any required reporting to networks/banks or customer communications after deactivation is undefined.
- Timing and scope: target launch date and affected jurisdictions/networks are undefined; confirm with Risk and Compliance before rollout.

## Open questions

- What are the current operative Visa (VAMP/VFMP) and Mastercard (ECM/HECM) thresholds and timelines as passed through by Mosaic Relay's acquiring banks, and do the internal thresholds sit below them with enough buffer?
- Does the merchant agreement's suspension clause require amendment (or a policy incorporated by reference) to support defined notice content, a 30-day cure, and a written appeal process?
- How are successfully rebutted friendly-fraud disputes, pending authorizations, and consumer-law complaints counted in the metric, and is that counting defensible if a merchant challenges deactivation?
- What post-deactivation obligations run to cardholders (refunds, dispute responses within the 90-day window) and to networks/acquirers (closure reporting, MATCH or equivalent listing), and who funds residual chargeback exposure?
- Can marketplace customers contractually override a deactivation, and does any consumer-facing communication duty arise when a merchant with recurring services is deactivated?
