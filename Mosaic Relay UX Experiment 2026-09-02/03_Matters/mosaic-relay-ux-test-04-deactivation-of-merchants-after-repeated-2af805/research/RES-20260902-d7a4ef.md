# What are the current Visa and Mastercard chargeback monitoring program

## Question

What are the current Visa and Mastercard chargeback monitoring program thresholds and timelines (e.g., VAMP, VFMP, MCM) that would apply to a U.S. merchant?

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a B2B payment infrastructure provider, wants to deactivate merchants whose chargeback rate exceeds a defined threshold for three consecutive months. The proposed process would warn merchants after the first month, require a remediation plan, restrict new payment methods, and disable processing after the third month unless a documented exception is approved. Existing refunds and dispute responses would remain available for 90 days post-deactivation. Counsel is involved because the merchant agreement permits suspension for excessive disputes but does not define notice content, cure period, or appeal process, and the company has network monitoring obligations that require a consistent, legally defensible process.
## Decision question
What chargeback ratio thresholds, minimum monthly counts, measurement periods, and program timelines under Visa's VAMP/VFMP and Mastercard's MCM programs should govern Mosaic Relay's three-month deactivation trigger for U.S. merchants, given that the merchant agreement lacks defined notice, cure, and appeal terms?
## Open questions
- What are the current numerical thresholds (chargeback-to-transaction ratios and minimum monthly counts) for VAMP, VFMP, and MCM/ECM as of September 2026, and have any program consolidations or supersessions occurred?
- Should the internal Mosaic Relay threshold be set at, below, or above network program thresholds to provide an early-warning buffer before network-mandated action?
- How should false or friendly-fraud disputes that are successfully rebutted be treated in the metric—excluded entirely or counted until resolution?
- What is the interaction between the proposed 30-day cure period and network program timelines, which may impose shorter remediation windows once a merchant is placed in a monitoring program?
- Are there state or federal consumer-protection notice requirements that apply when a payment processor restricts or terminates merchant services, beyond contractual obligations?

## Likely rules and issues
**Card-network monitoring programs.** Visa and Mastercard each operate merchant monitoring programs that track chargeback and fraud ratios. Visa's programs include the Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program (VAMP) and the Visa Fraud Monitoring Program (VFMP). Mastercard's programs include the Chargeback Monitored Merchant (MCM) program and the Excessive Chargeback Merchant (ECM) program. These programs typically define:
- A chargeback-to-transaction ratio threshold (often expressed as a percentage)
- A minimum monthly chargeback or fraud count to trigger program placement
- A monthly measurement and review cycle
- Escalation tiers with increasing consequences (warnings, fines, mandated remediation, acquirer liability shifts, and ultimately processing termination)
The specific numerical thresholds, counts, and timelines are set by network operating rules and program guides that are not publicly available in full and are updated periodically. The supplied public research explicitly declined to provide these numbers, noting that no Visa or Mastercard rule documents were in the research context.
**Contractual gap.** The merchant agreement permits suspension for excessive disputes but does not define notice content, cure period, or appeal process. This creates a risk that deactivation could be challenged as procedurally unfair or in breach of implied covenant of good faith, particularly if the merchant was not given adequate opportunity to cure.
**Consumer-law overlay.** Federal consumer-protection statutes (Regulation Z billing-error provisions, FCRA) govern cardholder disputes and refund rights but do not directly regulate network merchant monitoring. However, state unfair-and-deceptive-practices statutes and general contract law may impose procedural fairness obligations on Mosaic Relay's deactivation process.
**Post-deactivation obligations.** The 90-day window for refunds and dispute responses after deactivation needs legal framing to ensure compliance with network rules (which may require longer or indefinite dispute-response capability) and consumer-protection law (which may impose refund obligations independent of the merchant relationship).
## Viable paths
**Path 1: Align internal thresholds with network programs.** Set Mosaic Relay's deactivation threshold at or slightly below the network program thresholds (once confirmed) to provide merchants with an early-warning buffer before network-mandated consequences attach. This path minimizes network compliance risk but requires obtaining current network program parameters.
**Path 2: Set a conservative internal threshold independent of network programs.** Establish a Mosaic Relay-defined threshold (e.g., a fixed chargeback ratio) that is intentionally more conservative than network thresholds, providing a wider buffer. This path is simpler to administer but may deactivate merchants who would not trigger network action, creating commercial friction.
**Path 3: Tiered approach with network alignment.** Use network thresholds as the outer boundary but implement a tiered internal process: warning at a lower internal threshold, remediation plan requirement at a mid-level, and deactivation only when network thresholds are approached or breached. This balances risk management with merchant relationship preservation.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Current network thresholds.** The specific VAMP, VFMP, MCM, and ECM thresholds and timelines as of September 2026 are not in the record. If network thresholds have changed (e.g., VAMP consolidation of prior Visa programs), the internal threshold alignment would shift.
- **Merchant volume distribution.** If Mosaic Relay's merchant base includes high-volume merchants, even a small number of chargebacks could trigger network minimum-count thresholds, affecting how the internal metric should be calibrated.
- **Friendly-fraud treatment.** If successfully rebutted friendly-fraud disputes are excluded from the metric, the effective threshold may be higher in practice, reducing false-positive deactivations.
