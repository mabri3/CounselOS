# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a payment-infrastructure provider, wants to combine transaction metadata from multiple merchants to train a fraud-detection model. The data includes device signals, payment details, and behavioral events, with direct identifiers removed but retained for seven years. Counsel is involved because the business needs to launch this model and must determine whether the de-identification approach satisfies GDPR and US state privacy laws before processing begins.

## Decision question

Can Mosaic Relay lawfully train fraud models on cross-merchant transaction data using pseudonymization and aggregation, or must it achieve true anonymization under GDPR and CCPA/CPRA standards, given that device identifiers remain personal data in some jurisdictions and the seven-year retention period may exceed storage-limitation requirements?

## Material facts

- Data is collected while Mosaic Relay provides payment and fraud services to merchants.
- Mosaic Relay would not sell the data.
- The proposed operation would remove direct identifiers and combine data across customers.
- The training set would be retained for seven years.
- Model training would occur monthly, with outputs used in real-time authorization and payout decisions.
- Data categories include device signals, payment method details, transaction amounts, merchant category, broad regional location, dispute outcomes, and account events.
- Actors include Mosaic Relay, merchants, payers, recipients, fraud vendors, cloud providers, and model-governance staff.
- Mosaic Relay believes aggregation and pseudonymization reduce privacy risk.

## Issues and workstreams

- Lawful basis for cross-merchant aggregation and model training
- Controller vs processor role allocation across Mosaic Relay, merchants, and vendors
- Whether de-identification/pseudonymization is sufficient to avoid personal-data obligations, given device data is personal in some jurisdictions
- Seven-year retention justification and deletion procedures

- Cross-border transfer compliance
- Contract language with merchants and vendors

## Open questions

- Which specific identifiers (device IDs, IP addresses, account hashes) remain in the training set after de-identification, and can they be linked back to individuals using Mosaic Relay’s internal systems or vendor tools?
- Do current merchant contracts and DPAs expressly permit cross-merchant aggregation and secondary use for fraud-model training, or is this use currently prohibited?
- In which jurisdictions will the data subjects (payers/recipients) reside, and where will the training data be stored and processed (cross-border transfer implications)?
- Will the model’s real-time authorization and payout decisions be fully automated without human review, triggering GDPR Article 22 or CCPA/CPRA automated-decision requirements?
- Can Mosaic Relay implement true anonymization (irreversible de-identification) for the training set, or is pseudonymization the only feasible technical approach given the need for model updates?
