# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a payment-infrastructure provider, plans to use transaction metadata from multiple merchants to train a fraud-detection model. The data includes device signals, payment method details, transaction amounts, merchant category, broad regional location, dispute outcomes, and account events. The model would make real-time authorization and payout decisions affecting payers and recipients. Counsel is involved because the business needs legal advice before a planned launch on lawful purposes, controller/processor roles, contract language, notice requirements, retention, de-identification standards, and automated-decision-making obligations.

## Decision question

Can Mosaic Relay lawfully deploy a fraud-detection model trained on cross-merchant transaction data to make real-time authorization and payout decisions without human review, and what contractual, notice, and technical safeguards must be in place before launch to comply with GDPR Article 22 and comparable automated-decision-making requirements across multiple jurisdictions?

## Material facts

- Data is collected while Mosaic Relay provides payment and fraud services to merchants.
- Mosaic Relay would not sell the data.
- The proposed operation would remove direct identifiers and combine data across customers.
- The training set would be retained for seven years.
- Model training would occur monthly, with outputs used in real-time authorization and payout decisions.
- Data categories include device signals, payment method details, transaction amounts, merchant category, broad regional location, dispute outcomes, and account events.
- Actors include Mosaic Relay, merchants, payers, recipients, fraud vendors, cloud providers, and model-governance staff.
- Mosaic Relay believes aggregation and pseudonymization reduce privacy risk.
