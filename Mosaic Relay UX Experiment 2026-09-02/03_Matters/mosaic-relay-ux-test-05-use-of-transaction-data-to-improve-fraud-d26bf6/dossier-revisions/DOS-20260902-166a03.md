# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a payment-infrastructure provider, wants to combine transaction metadata from multiple merchants to train a fraud-detection model. The data includes device signals, payment details, transaction amounts, merchant category, broad regional location, dispute outcomes, and account events. The company plans to remove direct identifiers, retain the training set for seven years, and use model outputs in real-time authorization and payout decisions. Counsel is involved because the cross-border scope is unmapped, the exact identifiers retained after de-identification are unknown, and the legal basis for cross-merchant aggregation and automated decision-making requires validation before a planned launch.

## Decision question

Can Mosaic Relay lawfully transfer and store transaction data across borders for fraud-model training given that the jurisdictions are unmapped, the exact identifiers retained after de-identification are unconfirmed, and the data will be used for real-time automated decisions affecting payers and recipients?

## Material facts

- Data is collected while Mosaic Relay provides payment and fraud services to merchants.
- Mosaic Relay would not sell the data.

- The training set would be retained for seven years.

- Data categories include device signals, payment method details, transaction amounts, merchant category, broad regional location, dispute outcomes, and account events.
