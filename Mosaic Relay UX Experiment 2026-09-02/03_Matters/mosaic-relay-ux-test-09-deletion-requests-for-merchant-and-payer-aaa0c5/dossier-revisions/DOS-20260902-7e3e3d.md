# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Design a privacy-center deletion workflow that lets merchants and payers request deletion of their personal information. The workflow must verify the requester (tiered by risk/data sensitivity), identify linked accounts and transactions, delete or de-identify eligible records, and explain any data retained for fraud prevention, accounting, disputes, legal obligations, or audit. Requests are acknowledged immediately and completed within the applicable legal deadline, anchored to multiple US state laws as the global default. Legal advice is sought on scope and exemptions, response deadlines, required notices, data minimization, retention exceptions, and the technical evidence needed to show the request was handled correctly.

## Decision question

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay wants a privacy-center workflow letting merchants and payers request deletion of their personal information.
- The workflow would verify the requester, identify linked accounts and transactions, delete or de-identify eligible records, and explain retained data.
- Retained data is explained for fraud prevention, accounting, disputes, legal obligations, or audit.
- Requests would be acknowledged immediately and completed within the applicable legal deadline.
- Payment records, sanctions-screening results, dispute evidence, support tickets, and ledger entries are stored in separate systems.
- Some data is supplied by merchants, while other data is generated by Mosaic Relay.
- The company wants one global response template and a centralized deletion job.
- Actors include data subjects, merchant administrators, Mosaic Relay Privacy, Compliance, Support, processors, and cloud providers.
- Which jurisdictions and privacy laws must this deletion workflow satisfy first? — Global default / all major regimes: Multiple US state laws
- What is the target launch date for the deletion workflow, and is there a hard deadline? — No launch date or hard deadline is confirmed. Assume design and control mapping start now, with interim manual intake and hold controls until the workflow is approved and tested.
- For merchant-supplied data, does Mosaic Relay act as a processor, joint controller, or independent controller? — Not yet determined
- How should requesters be verified before deletion is processed? — Tiered by risk / data sensitivity
- Could deletion impair ongoing suspicious-activity or AML investigations, requiring a legal-hold/retention exception? — Not sure
- What are the retention schedules and exemption categories for each record type (payment records, sanctions-screening results, dispute evidence, support tickets, ledger entries)? — No approved schedules or exemptions are confirmed for any record type. Treat payment records and ledger entries as potentially subject to accounting and legal retention; sanctions-screening results and dispute evidence as potentially subject to fraud, AML, dispute, and audit holds; support tickets as case-by-case. Build an interim retention matrix, legal-hold flag, owner, retention end date, and documented exception reason before automation.
- deletion must propagate to backups: Neither household nor employee in scope.

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay acts as a controller for some data (e.g., its own generated records) and a processor for merchant-supplied data; allocation to be confirmed.
- A single global response template and centralized deletion job are feasible across the separate systems.
- The workflow must meet the most restrictive applicable US state deadline (45 days with possible 45-day extension) as the global default.
- A legal-hold/retention exception should be built into the deletion job by default given the unresolved AML/suspicious-activity impact.
- Interim manual intake and hold controls will be stood up now since no launch date is confirmed.

## Issues and workstreams

- Controller/processor allocation between Mosaic Relay and merchants is unresolved, which determines who must honor a deletion request and how obligations flow.
- Retention schedules and which records are exempt (fraud, accounting, disputes, legal, audit) are not defined; interim retention matrix needed before automation.
- Whether deletion could impair suspicious-activity/AML investigations is unresolved; a legal-hold/retention exception should be designed in by default.
- Backup deletion capability and household/employee request handling are not addressed.
- Appeal rights (required under several US state laws) are not addressed.

## Open questions

- Backup deletion capability and whether deletion must propagate to backups
- Appeal rights process for denied requests
- Approved retention schedules and exemption categories per record type
- Should the workflow include an appeals process for denied requests (required under several US state laws)?
