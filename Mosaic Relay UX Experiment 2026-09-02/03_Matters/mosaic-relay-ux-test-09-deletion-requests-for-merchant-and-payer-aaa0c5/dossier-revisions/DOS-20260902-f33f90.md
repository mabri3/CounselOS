# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

US state privacy law deletion workflow for merchant and payer personal data; controller/processor allocation unresolved; tiered verification; no confirmed launch date with interim manual intake and hold controls.

## Decision question

Design a privacy-center deletion workflow letting merchants and payers request deletion of their personal information. The workflow must verify the requester (tiered by risk/data sensitivity), identify linked accounts and transactions, delete or de-identify eligible records, and explain retained data (fraud prevention, accounting, disputes, legal obligations, audit). Requests acknowledged immediately and completed within the applicable legal deadline. Anchor: multiple US state laws (global default). No confirmed launch date; design and control mapping start now with interim manual intake and hold controls. Legal advice sought on scope and exemptions, response deadlines, required notices, data minimization, retention exceptions, and technical evidence of correct handling.

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay wants a privacy-center workflow letting merchants and payers request deletion of their personal information.
- The workflow would verify the requester, identify linked accounts and transactions, delete or de-identify eligible records, and explain retained data.
- Retained data is explained for fraud prevention, accounting, disputes, legal obligations, or audit.
- Requests would be acknowledged immediately and completed within the applicable legal deadline.
- Payment records, sanctions-screening results, dispute evidence, support tickets, and ledger entries are stored in separate systems.
- Some data is supplied by merchants, while other data is generated by Mosaic Relay.
- The company wants one global response template and a centralized deletion job.
- Actors include data subjects, merchant administrators, Mosaic Relay Privacy, Compliance, Support, processors, and cloud providers.
- Which jurisdictions and privacy laws must this deletion workflow satisfy first? — Global default / all major regimes: Multiple US state laws
- What is the target launch date for the deletion workflow, and is there a hard deadline? — No launch date or hard deadline is confirmed. Assume design and control mapping start now, with interim manual intake and hold controls until the workflow is approved and tested.
- For merchant-supplied data, does Mosaic Relay act as a processor, joint controller, or independent controller? — Not yet determined
- How should requesters be verified before deletion is processed? — Tiered by risk / data sensitivity

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay acts as a controller for some data (e.g., its own generated records) and a processor for merchant-supplied data; allocation to be confirmed.
- A single global response template and centralized deletion job are feasible across the separate systems.
- The workflow must meet the most restrictive applicable US state deadline (45 days with possible 45-day extension) as the global default.

## Issues and workstreams

- Controller/processor allocation between Mosaic Relay and merchants is unresolved, which determines who must honor a deletion request and how.
- Retention schedules and which records are exempt (fraud, accounting, disputes, legal, audit) are not defined.
- Whether deletion could impair suspicious-activity/AML investigations is unresolved.
- Backup deletion capability and household/employee request handling are not addressed.
- Appeal rights (required under several US state laws) are not addressed.

## Open questions

- Retention schedules and exemption categories
- Whether deletion could impair suspicious-activity/AML investigations
- Backup deletion capability
- Household/employee request handling
- Appeal rights process
- What retention schedules and exemption categories apply to each record type (payment, sanctions, disputes, support, ledger)?
- Must deletion propagate to backups, and how should household or employee requests be handled?
- Should an appeals process be built in for denied requests (required under several US state laws)?
