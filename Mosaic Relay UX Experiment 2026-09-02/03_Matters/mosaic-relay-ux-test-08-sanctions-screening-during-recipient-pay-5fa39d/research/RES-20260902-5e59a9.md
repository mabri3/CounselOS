# What are the regulatory expectations for false-positive correction and appeal

## Question

What are the regulatory expectations for false-positive correction and appeal procedures in sanctions screening?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a B2B payment-infrastructure provider, plans to screen every payout recipient against sanctions and restricted-party lists at onboarding, before each payout, and when lists update. Potential matches route to manual review; confirmed matches stop the payout and restrict the account. Counsel is involved because the program implicates sanctions blocking and rejection duties, reporting and record-retention obligations, tipping-off constraints on recipient communications, and fair-treatment controls for legitimate recipients. Several scoping facts — jurisdictions, lists, thresholds, and blocked-funds handling — remain undefined, and the matter is rated high risk.
## Decision question
What false-positive correction and appeal procedures must Mosaic Relay build into its pre-release payout screening so that legitimate recipients flagged by name, address, business-detail, or bank-data matching can be cleared quickly and consistently — given incomplete data, transliterated names, and cross-border payouts — without violating sanctions blocking duties or tipping-off constraints?
## Open questions
- Which jurisdictions and currencies will the screening cover? This determines which regimes' delisting, unblocking, and due-process expectations apply (e.g., OFAC SDN reconsideration vs. EU/UK delisting and licensing routes).
- Which sanctions and restricted-party lists will be used, and will any non-government vendor lists drive account restrictions beyond legal blocking duties?
- What match-scoring thresholds and escalation deadlines are planned? These set the volume of false positives the correction procedure must absorb and the service-level expectations for clearing them.
- How will funds be held while a payout is in review or blocked, and does that handling differ for a false positive versus a confirmed match?
- Can merchants receive match explanations for their recipients' payouts, or is match information restricted to Compliance? This shapes what the appeal channel can disclose.

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.** The following is model-only analysis based on general sanctions-compliance practice; it is not retrieved authority and should be verified against current primary sources before reliance.
Likely applicable frameworks and expectations, by issue:
1. **No general legal duty to offer a formal "appeal," but strong regulatory expectation of a correction path.** Sanctions regimes (e.g., US OFAC-style, EU, UK) generally do not require private screening programs to provide a formal appeals process to flagged counterparties. However, examiners and enforcement guidance consistently expect compliance programs to be risk-based, calibrated, and effective — which in practice means documented procedures for investigating potential matches, clearing false positives, and tuning matching logic. A program that blocks legitimate recipients indefinitely with no correction path looks unreasonable and creates discrimination, contract, and reputational exposure.
2. **Administrative reconsideration / delisting channels sit with the government, not Mosaic Relay.** A truly listed person typically must petition the listing authority (e.g., an SDN reconsideration process or EU/UK delisting request). Mosaic Relay's obligation is narrower: distinguish *false positives* (recipient is not the listed person) from *true matches*, and route true matches into blocking/rejection and reporting workflows. The correction procedure should make this distinction explicit.
3. **Blocking vs. rejection discipline.** For a confirmed match, some regimes require blocking (freeze and report) while others permit or require rejection (turn the transaction away). A false positive, once cleared, must result in prompt release of the payout and removal of any account restriction — with an audit trail showing why the hold was lifted. Holding funds after clearance, or restricting an account on a stale or weak match, is a compliance and contract risk.
4. **Tipping-off and communication constraints.** The product team's plan to show only "additional review is required" is directionally consistent with anti-tipping-off norms, but the correction procedure needs a lawful channel for the flagged recipient (or the merchant, if permitted) to supply clarifying information — full legal name, date of birth, address history, identifiers — without Mosaic Relay disclosing the specific list, match logic, or whether a true match was confirmed. What may be said, to whom, and when is jurisdiction-dependent and remains an open question here.
5. **Consistency and anti-discrimination controls.** Because names may be transliterated and data incomplete, name-only matching will disproportionately flag certain ethnic and national-origin groups. Regulators and internal audit will expect: documented match-scoring thresholds, consistent evidence standards for clearing vs. escalating, human review before any account restriction, logging of decisions, periodic tuning and false-positive-rate measurement, and QA sampling for inconsistent outcomes.
6. **Record retention.** Screening decisions, alerts, dispositions, and supporting evidence generally must be retained for defined periods (commonly multi-year, varying by regime) and be producible to authorities. The correction procedure's records are part of that retention set.
## Viable paths
- **Path A — Tiered disposition with a documented correction lane (recommended starting point).** Auto-clear below a low threshold; manual review in the middle with a defined SLA; confirmed-match escalation to Compliance with blocking/reporting workflow. Add a structured false-positive intake (via merchant or recipient support channel) that accepts clarifying evidence, applies a written evidence standard, requires human sign-off to release funds, and logs every step. Communicate only status ("additional review is required") plus a request for specific information, not match details.
- **Path B — Minimal manual review only.** Operationally cheaper, but without a documented correction path and evidence standards, the program is exposed on consistency, discrimination, and examiner expectations. Not advisable at this risk level.
- **Path C — Vendor-managed adjudication.** Outsource match review to the screening vendor. Feasible for first-pass clearing, but Mosaic Relay retains regulatory responsibility; vendor decisions still need Mosaic Relay's evidence standards, audit access, and override capability.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Jurisdictions/currencies:** determines which regimes' delisting, licensing, unblocking, and reporting rules the correction procedure must interface with, and whether tipping-off statutes restrict communications.
- **Lists used:** government lists trigger legal blocking duties; vendor or consortium lists trigger only contractual/policy decisions, which changes what "confirmed match" and "restrict the account" may lawfully mean.
- **Blocked-funds handling:** whether funds sit in a Mosaic Relay-controlled flow or a partner bank's account changes who must file blocked-property or rejected-transaction reports and who can release funds after clearance.
- **Merchant visibility:** if merchants can receive explanations, the appeal channel can be merchant-mediated; if not, Mosaic Relay needs a direct recipient channel, which has product and tipping-off implications.
- **Thresholds and volumes:** very low thresholds could generate false-positive volumes that make SLA-bound human review impractical, forcing design changes.
