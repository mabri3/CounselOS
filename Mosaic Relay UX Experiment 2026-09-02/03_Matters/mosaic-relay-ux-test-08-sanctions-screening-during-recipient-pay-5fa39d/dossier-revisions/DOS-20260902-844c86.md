# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a B2B payment-infrastructure provider, wants to screen every payout recipient against sanctions and restricted-party lists immediately before releasing funds. Potential matches would route payouts to manual review; confirmed matches would stop the payout and restrict the account. Product has asked counsel to define the legal guardrails—screening scope, blocking duties, reporting, record retention, and recipient communications—before launch. No launch date has been set, but the control design is underway now.

## Decision question

When Mosaic Relay's pre-release screening confirms a sanctions match on a payout recipient, must the company block (freeze) the funds, reject the transaction, or simply delay it—and what reports to government authorities and record-retention periods apply to each outcome across the jurisdictions and currencies the product will support?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay proposes to screen every payout recipient immediately before release against sanctions and restricted-party lists.
- Screening would compare recipient names, addresses, business details, and bank information against sanctions and restricted-party lists.
- Potential matches would place the payout into a manual-review queue.
- A confirmed match would stop the payout and restrict the account.
- Screening would occur at onboarding, before each payout, and when list updates are received.
- Recipient information can be incomplete, names may be transliterated, and some payouts cross borders.
- The product team wants to show recipients only that 'additional review is required' to reduce evasion.
- Actors include merchants, sellers, contractors, recipients, Mosaic Relay Compliance, screening vendors, banks, and government authorities.

- What is the target launch timing for the payout screening control? — Not yet determined
- How will Mosaic Relay handle funds while a payout is in review or blocked? — Not yet defined
- Can merchants receive match explanations for their recipients' payouts, or is match information restricted to Compliance? — Not yet decided

## Assumptions

- Jurisdiction scope is not yet defined; analysis will need to be scoped to specific countries/currencies once known
- The specific sanctions/restricted-party lists used are not yet identified
- Match-scoring thresholds, escalation deadlines, and blocked-funds handling are not yet defined

## Issues and workstreams

- Required screening scope and whether pre-release screening of every recipient is legally required or best practice across the jurisdictions served
- Blocking and rejection duties when a match is confirmed, including whether funds must be blocked, rejected, or reported
- Reporting and record-retention obligations to government authorities (e.g., OFAC/SDN-style reporting) and how long records must be kept
- Communications to affected parties, including whether the 'additional review is required' message is permissible or risks tipping off / evasion concerns
- Controls to prevent discriminatory or inconsistent treatment of legitimate recipients, including false-positive correction procedures and match-scoring thresholds

## Open questions

- Which countries and currencies will the payout product support at launch? This determines which sanctions regimes (e.g., US OFAC, EU, UK OFSI, UN) apply and whether blocking or rejection duties differ.
- Which specific sanctions and restricted-party lists will the screening vendor check? List coverage drives both match logic and downstream reporting obligations.
- How will Mosaic Relay handle funds while a payout is in review or after a confirmed block—segregated account, return to sender, or hold in place?
- What match-scoring thresholds and escalation deadlines are planned? These affect both false-positive rates and the timeliness of any required reports.
- Can merchants receive match explanations, or is that information restricted to Compliance? This shapes the tipping-off and evasion-risk analysis.
