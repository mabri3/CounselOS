    - Most conservative and most aligned with OFAC-style practice for blocked persons

    retention.

    - Funds stay tied up indefinitely until a license or delisting — a real operational
    burden.

    ### Path 2 — Reject-and-return

    **What it is:** On a confirmed match, reject the payout and return the funds to
    the sender, filing any required rejected-transaction report.

    **Strengths:**

    - Operationally simpler — no ongoing blocked-account administration.

    - Appropriate where the transaction is prohibited but the party is not a "blocked
    person" (e.g., certain sectoral-sanctions scenarios), or where the funds never
    sit with Mosaic Relay.

    **Costs / requirements:**

    - Not available for all match types — a true blocked-person match generally must
    be *blocked*, not returned.

    - Still carries reporting duties (rejected-transaction reports) and retention.

    - Returning funds to a sender who may itself be implicated can create its own
    risk.

    ### The key nuance that decides between them

    The research is consistent on one decisive point: **where the funds sit in the
    payment chain.** Because Mosaic Relay is not a bank and does not hold deposits,
    the actual freeze may have to be executed by the **partner bank** holding the
    funds. That makes the realistic path a **hybrid**:

    on reporting; the bank executes the freeze. This requires a clear contractual
    allocation of blocking/reporting duties with each partner.

    ### Bottom line

    - **Block** is the correct default for a confirmed match against a blocked person
    — it''s the conservative, OFAC-aligned baseline.

    - **Reject-and-return** is the fallback for match types where blocking isn''t
    the required response.

    - **Which one actually applies in practice depends on the funds flow and the partner-bank
