## Question

What minimum KYC/KYB, beneficial-ownership, and sanctions-screening data do US card-network and acquiring-bank rules require before a merchant can process its first payment?

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment-infrastructure provider, wants to reduce marketplace seller activation from two business days to five minutes by running automated KYB, identity verification, sanctions screening, and risk scoring during signup. Low-risk sellers would receive immediate limited processing access (capped at $10,000 monthly volume with delayed payouts), while higher-risk sellers remain pending. Counsel is involved because the model must satisfy card-network rules, acquiring-bank requirements, and US AML/KYC obligations before the first payment is processed. The business needs the legal answer before a planned launch, though no fixed date has been set.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay activate marketplace sellers for immediate limited payment processing (up to $10,000/month with delayed payouts) based solely on automated KYB, beneficial-ownership collection, and sanctions screening completed during a five-minute signup, or must manual review or additional verification be completed before the first payment?

## Likely rules and issues
**Card-network rules (Visa/Mastercard):** Both networks require acquirers to maintain merchant onboarding programs that verify business identity, beneficial ownership, and sanctions status before settlement. The networks do not mandate a specific waiting period, but they require "reasonable procedures" to verify merchant identity and legitimacy. The $10,000 monthly cap and delayed payouts may qualify as risk-mitigating factors, but the acquirer remains liable for fraud losses if onboarding is deficient.
**Bank Secrecy Act / FinCEN CDD Rule:** Mosaic Relay is not a bank, but its acquiring partners are subject to the CDD Rule, which requires identification and verification of beneficial owners of legal entity customers. The rule applies at account opening; for payment processing, the "account" is typically the merchant agreement. FinCEN has not explicitly addressed five-minute automated onboarding, but the rule requires "risk-based procedures" for verification. The 25% ownership threshold and control prong must be captured.
**OFAC sanctions screening:** Screening must occur before the first transaction. Real-time API screening is standard, but the model must account for false positives and have a manual review path for potential matches. The five-minute timeline is feasible only if the screening vendor provides sub-second response times and the risk-scoring logic auto-approves only clear non-matches.
**State money transmission:** If Mosaic Relay is deemed to be "receiving money for transmission" or "selling payment instruments," state licensing may be required. The delayed-payout structure may help argue against money transmission, but this varies by state. The marketplace model may also trigger agent-of-the-payee exemptions in some states, but this depends on the contractual flow of funds.
**Consumer protection (UDAAP):** The CFPB and state attorneys general scrutinize instant-onboarding models for inadequate disclosure of fees, payout delays, and reserve requirements. Sellers must receive clear disclosures about the $10,000 cap, payout timing, and any holds before they accept the first payment.
## Viable paths
**Path A: Automated-only with enhanced monitoring**
Proceed with five-minute automated onboarding for sellers scoring below a defined risk threshold. Require manual review for any sanctions hits, beneficial-ownership discrepancies, or high-risk business categories. This path is fastest but requires robust vendor SLAs and acquirer buy-in.
**Path B: Two-tier activation**
Activate sellers immediately for payment acceptance but hold settlement until manual review is completed within 24 hours. This satisfies the "before first payment" timing for screening while allowing the business to meet the five-minute signup goal. The delayed payout already contemplated supports this structure.
**Path C: Marketplace-led CDD with Mosaic Relay verification**
Contractually shift primary CDD responsibility to the marketplace (which has the seller relationship), with Mosaic Relay performing verification and sanctions screening. This may reduce Mosaic Relay's regulatory exposure but requires the marketplace to have its own compliance program and may not satisfy acquirer requirements.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Acquirer requirements:** If partners mandate manual review or specific document verification (e.g., Articles of Incorporation, bank statements), the five-minute model may need modification.
- **Seller locations:** If sellers are in states with strict money-transmission laws (e.g., New York, California), additional licensing or bonding may be required.
- **Business categories:** High-risk categories (e.g., adult, gambling, crypto) may be excluded from instant onboarding regardless of automation.
- **Vendor capabilities:** If the KYB vendor cannot verify beneficial ownership in real time against authoritative sources, manual fallback is necessary.
- **Volume trajectory:** If sellers routinely exceed $10,000/month, the model may need to trigger enhanced due diligence (EDD) automatically.
## Recommended last-mile verification
Before launch, counsel should:
1. **Obtain written acquirer confirmation** that the automated onboarding flow satisfies their merchant-onboarding requirements, specifically addressing the five-minute timeline and $10,000 cap.
2. **Run a pilot with 50–100 sellers** across risk tiers to validate that the automated screening catches sanctions hits and beneficial-ownership discrepancies without excessive false positives.
3. **Draft and test seller disclosures** for the $10,000 cap, payout delays, and reserve rights to ensure they are clear and conspicuous before the first payment.
4. **Map the funds flow** to confirm whether any state money-transmission licenses are triggered, particularly if Mosaic Relay ever takes possession of funds.
5. **Document the CDD allocation** in the marketplace agreement, specifying which party is responsible for beneficial-ownership verification and ongoing monitoring.
