# US money-transmission licensing perimeter for a payment-infrastructure provider

## Question

US money-transmission licensing perimeter for a payment-infrastructure provider that does not hold deposits, plus minimum KYC/KYB and beneficial-owner records, sanctions screening, acquiring-bank and card-network dependencies, consumer-protection disclosures and adverse-action notices, privacy and retention, and structuring shared CDD ownership for a marketplace instant-onboarding model.

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a payment-infrastructure provider that is not a bank and does not hold deposits, wants to cut marketplace-seller activation from two business days to five minutes by running automated KYB, identity verification, sanctions screening, and risk scoring during signup. Low-risk sellers would get immediate limited processing access (capped at $10,000 monthly volume with delayed payouts); higher-risk sellers would stay pending. Counsel is involved because Product needs a legal answer before a planned launch on whether this limited-access model is permissible and what must be verified before a seller's first payment. The decision is gated on acquiring-partner requirements that are still under discussion and on an unconfirmed state licensing path.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay launch five-minute instant onboarding for low-risk marketplace sellers — capped at $10,000/month with delayed payouts — before full KYB completes, and if so, what minimum verification, records, disclosures, and licensing conditions must be satisfied before a seller's first payment, given that acquiring-partner requirements, fund-flow/custody structure, and the state licensing path are not yet confirmed?
## Open questions
- **Flow of funds / custody:** Does Mosaic Relay ever take custody of funds at any point? This is the single most decisive fact for the money-transmission licensing trigger and is not yet confirmed.
- **Acquiring-partner minimums:** What do the acquiring partners require to be verified before a seller processes its first payment? If they require full KYB before any processing, the five-minute model fails as designed. (Under discussion.)
- **State licensing path:** Which states will sellers be in, and does the agent-of-payee or processor exemption apply in each? No 50-state survey has been done; the $10,000 cap is a risk control, not a licensing exemption.
- **Vendor decision logic / FCRA:** Is the risk-scoring and identity data a "consumer report" under FCRA? This determines whether adverse-action notices are legally required for rejected sellers, and the notice process is not yet designed.
- **CDD allocation:** How will the shared customer-due-diligence obligation be divided between the marketplace and Mosaic Relay, with a single accountable owner per control? (To be allocated.)

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.** The following is generated analysis based on the matter record and general regulatory framework; it is not retrieved or verified authority and must be confirmed against current statutes and rules before reliance.
**Money transmission (state).** The controlling question is whether Mosaic Relay "receives" or "transmits" money. Because it does not hold deposits, the perimeter turns on custody and flow of funds. Two common exemptions are relevant: (1) the **agent-of-payee** exemption, where payment to Mosaic Relay (or the partner) satisfies the buyer's obligation to the seller; and (2) the **processor / payment-processing** exemption, where Mosaic Relay acts purely as a technical conduit under contract with the merchant or acquiring bank. Both are state-specific and must be tested state by state against the confirmed flow-of-funds diagram. The $10,000 cap and delayed payouts reduce risk but do **not** create a licensing exemption.
**Federal MSB / FinCEN.** Federal money-services-business status is independent of state licensing and must be analyzed separately. If Mosaic Relay is a money transmitter under FinCEN rules, MSB registration and a full BSA/AML program follow regardless of state exemptions.
**KYC/KYB and beneficial ownership.** For legal-entity sellers, the CDD rule framework requires identifying and verifying beneficial owners (25%+ owners and at least one control person). The minimum-before-activation floor is: verified legal entity identity (name, business type, tax ID, address), verified beneficial owners, government ID for controlling individuals, completed sanctions screening with no unresolved hit, completed risk scoring classifying the seller low-risk, validated payout destination, and captured expected volume and payment methods.
**Sanctions (OFAC).** Screening must genuinely complete — not merely be initiated — before the first payment, with no unresolved hit. This is a hard gate that cannot be risk-tiered away.
**Acquiring-bank and card-network dependencies.** Partner requirements may set a higher floor than Mosaic Relay's own tiering and may require full KYB before any processing. Card-network payfac registration and underwriting for the instant tier must be confirmed. These are contractual/network dependencies, not just regulatory ones.
**Consumer protection and adverse action.** If the vendor identity/risk data is a "consumer report" under FCRA, rejected sellers may be entitled to adverse-action notices; ECOA may also apply to the extent sellers are individuals/sole props. The notice process depends on the vendor decision logic, which is still open.
**Privacy and retention.** A retention schedule should be set at least to the five-year BSA floor; the exact period is an open fact.
**Shared CDD ownership.** Regulatory accountability cannot be contracted away. The marketplace agreement must document allocation with a single accountable owner for each control (identity verification, sanctions screening, risk scoring, activation decision).
## Viable paths
- **Path A — Agent-of-payee structuring:** Structure the marketplace–Mosaic Relay–seller agreements so payment to the partner satisfies the buyer's obligation to the seller and Mosaic Relay never holds funds for its own account. Preserves the no-money-transmitter position but requires careful contract and flow-of-funds engineering.
- **Path B — Processor exemption:** Operate purely as a technical processor under contract with the acquiring bank / merchant, never touching custody. Narrower product flexibility.
- **Path C — Launch-state gating (recommended in combination with A):** Launch instant onboarding only in states confirmed exempt or low-risk after a 50-state survey; keep the two-day flow elsewhere. No state should be green-lit until the survey and flow-of-funds confirmation are complete.
These paths are generated analysis, not verified law.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Custody of funds:** If Mosaic Relay ever holds funds, the no-money-transmitter position likely collapses and licensing is triggered.
- **Acquiring-partner requirements:** If partners require full KYB before any processing, the five-minute model is not permissible as designed.
- **Seller locations:** Which states sellers are in determines which licensing, consumer-protection, and disclosure regimes apply.
- **Vendor data classification:** Whether the data is a "consumer report" determines FCRA adverse-action obligations.
- **Payfac status:** Whether Mosaic Relay operates as a payment facilitator changes card-network registration and underwriting duties.
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. Confirm the **flow-of-funds / custody structure in writing** with engineering and acquiring partners — who holds what, when, under which contract.
2. Obtain the **acquiring partners' minimum pre-activation verification requirements in writing**.
3. Commission a **50-state money-transmission survey** against the confirmed flow-of-funds diagram, testing agent-of-payee and processor exemptions state by state.
4. Analyze **FinCEN MSB registration** separately from state licensing.
5. Confirm **card-network payfac registration/underwriting** for the instant tier.
6. Determine whether vendor data is a **consumer report under FCRA** and design the adverse-action/notice process accordingly.
7. Document **CDD allocation** in the marketplace agreement with a single accountable owner per control.
8. Set the **retention schedule** (at least the five-year BSA floor).
