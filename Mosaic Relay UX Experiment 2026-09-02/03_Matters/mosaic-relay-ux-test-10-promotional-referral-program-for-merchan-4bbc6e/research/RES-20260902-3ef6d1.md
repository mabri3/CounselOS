## Question

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a fictional B2B payment-infrastructure provider, wants to launch a six-month promotional referral program offering merchants a $250 account credit (fee offset) for each new business that processes $5,000 in eligible volume. The program would use referral links, email invitations, in-product banners, customer testimonials, and sales-staff offers during onboarding calls across all supported markets. Counsel is involved because the request raises unresolved questions about FTC endorsement disclosures, marketing-consent requirements, consumer-protection risks, tax treatment, and geographic restrictions. The program has no fixed launch date, and the company has not yet confirmed whether the credit changes pricing disclosures or how referral relationships will be disclosed.

## Likely rules and issues
**FTC endorsement and material-connection rules.** The FTC's Endorsement Guides (16 C.F.R. Part 255) require clear and conspicuous disclosure of material connections between endorsers and advertisers. A $250 account credit is a material connection that must be disclosed at each touchpoint: referral links, email invitations, in-product banners, sales scripts, and testimonials. The disclosure must state that the referring merchant may receive a credit after the referred business processes $5,000 in eligible volume.
**Testimonial substantiation.** Testimonials must be genuine, current, and representative. If testimonials make savings, volume, or performance claims, Mosaic Relay must have substantiation. If results are not typical, a clear qualification is required. Displaying the number of successful referrals is permissible if accurate and not misleading.
**Marketing-consent requirements.** Email invitations must comply with CAN-SPAM (accurate header information, non-deceptive subject lines, identification as an ad, valid physical postal address, clear opt-out mechanism, and honoring opt-outs within 10 business days). Promotional SMS/text messages require prior express written consent under the TCPA and must include opt-out controls. In-product banners and sales scripts are not subject to CAN-SPAM or TCPA but must still comply with FTC disclosure rules.
**Consumer-protection risks.** The program must avoid deceptive or unfair practices. The $5,000 eligible-volume test, six-month window, non-cash credit, caps, exclusions, and fraud/abuse rules must be clearly disclosed in plain-language terms. The credit must not be presented as cash or as a discount on the referred business's pricing unless it actually changes the referred business's pricing.
**Tax and accounting treatment.** The account credit is likely a marketing expense for Mosaic Relay and may be taxable income to the referring merchant. If the aggregate credits to a merchant exceed $600 in a calendar year, Mosaic Relay may need to issue a 1099-MISC or 1099-NEC. The credit's classification (reduction of revenue vs. marketing expense) and any W-9 collection process must be confirmed with tax counsel and accounting.
**Geographic restrictions.** If any supported markets are outside the United States, local rules on referral incentives, endorsements, marketing consent, and tax treatment must be screened. Some jurisdictions may restrict or prohibit referral incentives in regulated industries or require additional disclosures.
**Eligibility exclusions and regulated industries.** The program should exclude businesses in regulated industries (e.g., money services, cannabis, adult, gambling) if Mosaic Relay's payment partners restrict them. Partner restrictions must be confirmed before launch.
## Viable paths
**Path 1: Account credit (fee offset) — recommended.** The $250 account credit is simpler to administer, avoids cash-payment complexities, and aligns with Mosaic Relay's B2B infrastructure model. It is less likely to be treated as income to the referring merchant (though tax counsel must confirm). It does not require changes to the referred business's pricing disclosures unless the credit is passed through. The main risks are FTC disclosure compliance and tax classification.
**Path 2: Cash reward — not recommended at this stage.** A cash reward would require more complex tax reporting (W-9/1099), potential state money-transmitter licensing analysis, and higher consumer-protection risk. It would also require clearer disclosure of the cash incentive and could be seen as a more aggressive inducement. The business has not requested this path, and the account credit is sufficient for the stated objective.
