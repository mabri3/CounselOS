# CAN-SPAM and TCPA consent requirements for referral email and text

## Question

CAN-SPAM and TCPA consent requirements for referral email and text invitations.

## Working Analysis

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay (a fictional B2B payment-infrastructure provider) wants to launch a six-month promotional referral program offering existing merchants a $250 account credit (fee offset, not cash) when a referred business processes $5,000 in eligible volume. The program would use referral links, email invitations, in-product banners, testimonials, and sales outreach, and could run in all supported markets. Counsel is involved because the incentive structure raises endorsement-disclosure, marketing-consent, consumer-protection, tax, and geographic-compliance questions before launch. Timing is flexible (no fixed launch date), but the business needs a legal answer on whether and how to proceed.
## Decision question
Should Mosaic Relay launch the referral program using the proposed $250 account-credit structure, and if so, what disclosure, consent, eligibility, and approval controls must be in place before the six-month pilot begins—particularly for email and text invitations under CAN-SPAM and TCPA?

Based on general legal knowledge (model-generated analysis, not retrieved authority):
### CAN-SPAM Act (email)
- **Applicability**: Referral email invitations sent by Mosaic Relay or by referring merchants using Mosaic Relay's platform are likely "commercial electronic mail messages" under CAN-SPAM because their primary purpose is to promote a commercial product or service (Mosaic Relay's payment platform).
- **Key requirements**:
- No false or misleading header information (From, To, Reply-To must be accurate).
- No deceptive subject lines.
- Clear identification that the message is an advertisement.
- Valid physical postal address of the sender (Mosaic Relay).
- Clear and conspicuous opt-out mechanism that remains functional for at least 30 days.
- Honor opt-out requests within 10 business days.
- Monitor third-party compliance (if referring merchants send emails using Mosaic Relay's tools, Mosaic Relay may be liable for their violations).
- **Referral-specific issue**: If the referring merchant sends the invitation email through Mosaic Relay's system, both the merchant and Mosaic Relay may be considered "senders" or "initiators" under CAN-SPAM, creating shared compliance obligations.
### TCPA (text messages and calls)
- **Applicability**: The TCPA restricts unsolicited calls and text messages to wireless numbers using an automatic telephone dialing system (ATDS) or prerecorded/artificial voice. After *Facebook v. Duguid* (2021), the ATDS definition is narrower, but many marketing platforms still qualify.
- **Key requirements**:
- **Prior express written consent** is required for telemarketing texts to wireless numbers. Consent must be unambiguous, in writing, and disclose that consent is not a condition of purchase.
- **Prior express consent** (oral or written) may suffice for non-telemarketing informational texts, but referral invitations are likely telemarketing.
- Opt-out mechanism must be provided and honored immediately.
- National Do-Not-Call Registry compliance for calls (though B2B calls may be exempt in some circumstances).
- **Referral-specific issue**: If referring merchants send text invitations using Mosaic Relay's platform, Mosaic Relay could face vicarious liability if it provides the means and the merchants lack proper consent. Sales staff offering the incentive during onboarding calls must also comply with TCPA if using an ATDS or prerecorded voice.
### FTC Endorsement Guides (testimonials and material connections)
- **Material connection disclosure**: The $250 credit is a material connection between Mosaic Relay and the referring merchant. Any testimonial, review, or endorsement by a referring merchant must clearly and conspicuously disclose this connection.
- **Genuine and representative**: Testimonials must reflect the honest opinions of actual customers. If results are not typical (e.g., savings, volume, ease of use), the ad must clearly disclose generally expected results or have substantiation.
- **"Earn rewards for growing the Mosaic Relay community"**: This tagline is acceptable if the reward structure is clearly disclosed, but displaying the number of successful referrals could create an implied performance claim that requires substantiation.
### State consumer-protection laws
- Many states have "mini-FTC" acts that prohibit unfair or deceptive acts or practices (UDAP). Referral programs must not mislead about the nature of the incentive, eligibility, or the relationship between the parties.
- Some states (e.g., California, New York) have specific disclosure or consent requirements for referral marketing.
### Tax and accounting
- **For Mosaic Relay**: The $250 credit is likely a marketing expense or reduction in revenue, not a cash payment. Accounting treatment should be confirmed.
- **For referring merchants**: The credit may be taxable income (or a reduction in deductible expenses). If aggregate credits exceed $600 in a calendar year, Mosaic Relay may need to issue a Form 1099-MISC or 1099-NEC. W-9 collection may be required.
- **For referred businesses**: The credit does not change their pricing unless it affects the fee schedule they see.
### Geographic restrictions
- **Non-U.S. markets**: If the program runs outside the U.S., local marketing-consent, consumer-protection, privacy (e.g., GDPR, CASL in Canada), and tax rules apply. Canada’s Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) is stricter than CAN-SPAM and requires express or implied consent for commercial electronic messages. GDPR requires lawful basis for processing personal data (e.g., email addresses) for marketing.
- **Regulated industries**: Some payment partners or acquiring banks may restrict referral incentives for merchants in regulated or high-risk industries (e.g., gambling, cannabis, adult, financial services).

## Viable paths
### Path 1: Launch U.S.-only pilot with account-credit structure (recommended)
- **Scope**: Limit the six-month pilot to U.S.-based referring merchants and referred businesses.
- **Email**: Use CAN-SPAM-compliant referral emails. Ensure accurate header info, clear ad identification, valid postal address, functional opt-out, and 10-day opt-out honoring. Monitor referring-merchant compliance if they send emails via Mosaic Relay's platform.
- **Text**: Exclude promotional SMS unless prior express written consent is confirmed. If SMS is used, implement a compliant consent flow (e.g., checkbox with clear disclosure, not pre-checked, not a condition of using the platform).
- **Testimonials**: Use only genuine, current testimonials. Disclose the material connection (e.g., "This merchant received a $250 account credit for referring new businesses"). Substantiate any performance claims or add a "results not typical" qualifier.
- **Terms**: Publish plain-language program terms covering the $5,000 eligible-volume test, six-month window, non-cash credit, caps, exclusions, fraud/abuse rules, and when the credit is applied.
- **Tax**: Confirm with tax counsel whether the credit is taxable to referring merchants and whether W-9/1099 processes are needed.
- **Approval**: Require Marketing, Sales, Product, Tax/Accounting, and Legal sign-off before launch.
### Path 2: Launch with cash-reward structure (not recommended at this stage)
- **Difference**: Cash rewards are more likely to be treated as taxable income to the recipient and may trigger additional consumer-protection scrutiny (e.g., state gift-card or rebate laws).
- **Complexity**: Cash payments require payment-processing infrastructure, tax withholding/reporting, and potentially money-transmitter licensing in some states.
- **Recommendation**: Defer cash rewards until the account-credit pilot is validated and tax/legal review is complete.
### Path 3: Launch in all supported markets (defer)
- **Risk**: Non-U.S. markets have stricter marketing-consent (e.g., CASL, GDPR), consumer-protection, and tax rules. Launching globally without market-by-market review creates significant compliance risk.
- **Recommendation**: Treat non-U.S. expansion as a separate launch decision after identifying supported markets and screening local rules.
