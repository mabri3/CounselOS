# Tax treatment of referral account credits vs. cash rewards for

## Question

Tax treatment of referral account credits vs. cash rewards for the issuer and recipients.

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a fictional B2B payment-infrastructure provider, wants to launch a six-month promotional referral program offering merchants a $250 account credit (not cash) when a referred business processes $5,000 in eligible volume. The program would be promoted through referral links, email invitations, in-product banners, customer testimonials, and sales calls, potentially across all supported markets. Counsel is involved because the tax treatment of the referral credit—both for Mosaic Relay as issuer and for referring merchants as recipients—remains unresolved, and the answer affects pricing disclosures, reporting obligations, and whether the credit structure should be confirmed or changed before launch.
## Decision question
Should Mosaic Relay proceed with the proposed $250 account-credit (fee-offset) referral structure, or switch to a cash-reward structure, given that the tax characterization, reporting obligations, and recipient-side income treatment for each path are unresolved and could affect pricing disclosures, merchant tax liability, and Mosaic Relay's own deduction and information-reporting obligations?
## Open questions
- What is the correct federal income tax characterization of the $250 account credit for the referring merchant (gross income, rebate, purchase-price adjustment, or something else), and does the answer change if the reward were cash instead?
- What are Mosaic Relay's obligations as issuer: is the credit deductible as a marketing expense, when is it deductible, and does Mosaic Relay have Form 1099 or other information-reporting obligations to referring merchants?
- Does the $5,000 eligible-volume condition or the non-cash form of the credit change the tax analysis for either party?
- Are there state tax considerations (income, sales/use, or gross-receipts) that differ from the federal treatment, particularly if the program expands beyond the United States?
- What is the aggregate annual credit amount a single referring merchant could earn, and does that affect reporting thresholds or withholding obligations?

## Viable paths
**Path A: Proceed with the $250 account-credit structure and obtain tax advice before launch.**
- Mosaic Relay would treat the credit as a marketing or promotional expense.
- The referring merchant's tax treatment would depend on whether the credit is characterized as income, a rebate, or a purchase-price adjustment.
- Information-reporting obligations (e.g., Form 1099-MISC or 1099-NEC) would depend on the characterization and aggregate annual amounts.
- This path preserves the business objective of driving platform engagement and fee offset.
**Path B: Switch to a cash-reward structure.**
- Cash payments are more clearly taxable income to the recipient and more clearly deductible to the issuer.
- Cash rewards would likely trigger Form 1099 reporting obligations if aggregate annual payments to a single recipient exceed $600.
- This path may be simpler from a tax-compliance perspective but changes the business model and may reduce the incentive's effectiveness at driving platform usage.
**Path C: Restructure the credit as a discount or rebate on future fees.**
- If the credit is framed as a reduction in the price of Mosaic Relay's services rather than a separate reward, it might be treated as a purchase-price adjustment rather than income to the recipient.
- This could simplify recipient-side tax treatment but requires careful drafting of terms and conditions and may not align with the marketing message "Earn rewards for growing the Mosaic Relay community."
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Form of the credit:** Whether the credit is a statement credit, a reduction in future invoices, or a separate account balance affects characterization.
- **Conditions attached:** The $5,000 eligible-volume requirement may support treatment as a rebate or discount rather than a gift or prize.
- **Aggregate annual amounts:** If a referring merchant could earn more than $600 in a year, Form 1099 reporting is more likely required.
- **Mosaic Relay's line of business:** Whether Mosaic Relay is treated as a service provider, a marketplace, or something else affects the issuer-side deduction analysis.
- **State and non-U.S. jurisdictions:** Tax treatment varies by state and country; some jurisdictions may treat the credit as taxable income even if federal law does not.
- **Whether the referred business is a related party:** If the referring merchant and referred business are under common ownership, the IRS may scrutinize the arrangement more closely.
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Obtain a formal tax opinion or memorandum from tax counsel** addressing:
- Federal income tax characterization of the $250 account credit for both Mosaic Relay and referring merchants.
- Whether the credit is deductible to Mosaic Relay and, if so, when and under what theory (marketing expense, cost of goods sold, etc.).
- Whether Mosaic Relay has Form 1099 or other information-reporting obligations, and at what threshold.
- Whether the answer changes if the reward were cash instead of a credit.
2. **Confirm state tax treatment** in the states where Mosaic Relay operates and where referring merchants are located, particularly if any state has a gross-receipts tax or unique income-tax rules.
3. **If the program will expand outside the United States,** obtain local tax advice in each target market before launch.
4. **Coordinate with accounting** to ensure the credit is properly recorded in Mosaic Relay's financial statements and that any required tax reporting is operationalized before launch.
5. **Review the program terms and conditions** to ensure they align with the intended tax characterization (e.g., if the credit is intended to be a rebate, the terms should not describe it as a "reward" or "prize").
---
**Recommendation (separate from lawyer's decision):** Do not launch the referral program until tax counsel has confirmed the federal income tax treatment of the $250 account credit for both Mosaic Relay and referring merchants, and until any required information-reporting processes are in place. If tax counsel confirms that the credit is taxable income to the recipient and that Mosaic Relay has Form 1099 obligations, ensure that the program terms, onboarding flow, and merchant communications clearly disclose this. If tax counsel confirms that the credit can be treated as a rebate or purchase-price adjustment, ensure that the program terms and marketing copy align with that characterization.
