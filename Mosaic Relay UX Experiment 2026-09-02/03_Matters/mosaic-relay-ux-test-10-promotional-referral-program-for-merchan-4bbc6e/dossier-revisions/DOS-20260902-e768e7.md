## Decision question

Provide legal review of Mosaic Relay's proposed six-month merchant referral program (referring merchant receives a $250 account credit after the referred business processes $5,000 in eligible volume), covering incentive/endorsement disclosures, marketing-consent requirements, consumer-protection risks, tax and accounting treatment, geographic restrictions, and final terms and approval process. The lawyer has asked to compare both paths.

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay proposes a referral program where a referring merchant receives a $250 account credit after the referred new business processes $5,000 in eligible volume.
- The program would run for six months and could be promoted in all supported markets.
- The proposed experience uses referral links, email invitations, in-product banners, and customer testimonials.
- Sales staff could also offer the incentive during onboarding calls.
- The credit would reduce future platform fees and would not be paid as cash.
- Mosaic Relay wants to say 'Earn rewards for growing the Mosaic Relay community' and may display the number of successful referrals.
- Actors include referring merchants, referred businesses, Mosaic Relay Sales and Marketing, payment partners, and referred businesses' owners and beneficial owners.
- Legal advice is requested on incentive/endorsement disclosures, marketing consent, consumer-protection risks, tax/accounting treatment, geographic restrictions, and final terms and approval process.
- Which two paths should I compare? The request describes one structure (a $250 account credit offsetting future platform fees). I want to confirm the comparison before drafting. — Account-credit (fee-offset) path vs. cash-reward path

## Assumptions

- The 'both paths' the lawyer wants compared most likely refers to the account-credit (fee-offset) structure versus a cash-reward structure, but this needs confirmation.
- The program is primarily U.S.-focused for initial analysis, with non-U.S. rules flagged for follow-up.
- The referral credit is a marketing incentive rather than a lending or deposit product.

## Issues and workstreams

- Which two paths the lawyer wants compared is not yet defined in the request.
- Whether the account-credit (fee-offset) structure vs. a cash-reward structure changes tax, disclosure, and consumer-protection treatment.
- Whether the credit changes pricing disclosures to referred businesses.
- How referral relationships and the incentive are disclosed to referred businesses and in testimonials.
- Whether testimonials are genuine and representative.
- Email and text marketing-consent requirements (e.g., CAN-SPAM, TCPA).
- Tax treatment of the credit for Mosaic Relay and for referring/referred merchants.
- Eligibility exclusions and treatment of regulated industries.
- Rules outside the United States given promotion in all supported markets.

## Open questions

- Whether testimonials are genuine, representative, and disclosed as incentivized.
