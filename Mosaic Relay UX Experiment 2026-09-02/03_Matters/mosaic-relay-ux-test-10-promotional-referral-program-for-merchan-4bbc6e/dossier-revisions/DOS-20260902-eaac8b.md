# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay, a fictional B2B payment-infrastructure provider, wants to launch a six-month promotional referral program offering merchants a $250 account credit (not cash) when a referred business processes $5,000 in eligible volume. The program would be promoted through referral links, email invitations, in-product banners, customer testimonials, and sales calls, potentially across all supported markets. Counsel is involved because the tax treatment of the referral credit—both for Mosaic Relay as issuer and for referring merchants as recipients—remains unresolved, and the answer affects pricing disclosures, reporting obligations, and whether the credit structure should be confirmed or changed before launch.

## Decision question

Should Mosaic Relay proceed with the proposed $250 account-credit (fee-offset) referral structure, or switch to a cash-reward structure, given that the tax characterization, reporting obligations, and recipient-side income treatment for each path are unresolved and could affect pricing disclosures, merchant tax liability, and Mosaic Relay's own deduction and information-reporting obligations?

## Material facts

- Mosaic Relay proposes a referral program where a referring merchant receives a $250 account credit after the referred new business processes $5,000 in eligible volume.
- The program would run for six months and could be promoted in all supported markets.
- The proposed experience uses referral links, email invitations, in-product banners, and customer testimonials.
- Sales staff could also offer the incentive during onboarding calls.
- The credit would reduce future platform fees and would not be paid as cash.
- Mosaic Relay wants to say 'Earn rewards for growing the Mosaic Relay community' and may display the number of successful referrals.
- Actors include referring merchants, referred businesses, Mosaic Relay Sales and Marketing, payment partners, and referred businesses' owners and beneficial owners.
- Legal advice is requested on incentive/endorsement disclosures, marketing consent, consumer-protection risks, tax/accounting treatment, geographic restrictions, and final terms and approval process.

## Assumptions

- The 'both paths' the lawyer wants compared most likely refers to the account-credit (fee-offset) structure versus a cash-reward structure, but this needs confirmation.
- The program is primarily U.S.-focused for initial analysis, with non-U.S. rules flagged for follow-up.
- The referral credit is a marketing incentive rather than a lending or deposit product.
- The account-credit and cash-reward paths are the two structures to be compared.

## Issues and workstreams

- Whether the account-credit (fee-offset) structure vs. a cash-reward structure changes tax, disclosure, and consumer-protection treatment.
- Whether the credit changes pricing disclosures to referred businesses.
- How referral relationships and the incentive are disclosed to referred businesses and in testimonials.
- Whether testimonials are genuine and representative.
- Email and text marketing-consent requirements (e.g., CAN-SPAM, TCPA).
- Tax treatment of the credit for Mosaic Relay and for referring/referred merchants.
- Eligibility exclusions and treatment of regulated industries.
- Rules outside the United States given promotion in all supported markets.

## Open questions

- What is the correct federal income tax characterization of the $250 account credit for the referring merchant (gross income, rebate, purchase-price adjustment, or something else), and does the answer change if the reward were cash instead?
- What are Mosaic Relay's obligations as issuer: is the credit deductible as a marketing expense, when is it deductible, and does Mosaic Relay have Form 1099 or other information-reporting obligations to referring merchants?
- Does the $5,000 eligible-volume condition or the non-cash form of the credit change the tax analysis for either party?
- Are there state tax considerations (income, sales/use, or gross-receipts) that differ from the federal treatment, particularly if the program expands beyond the United States?
- What is the aggregate annual credit amount a single referring merchant could earn, and does that affect reporting thresholds or withholding obligations?
