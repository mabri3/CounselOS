# FTC substantiation and 'reasonable basis' standard for advertising claims, inclu

## Question

FTC substantiation and 'reasonable basis' standard for advertising claims, including superlative and comparative claims

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay, a fictional B2B payment-infrastructure provider, plans a US and Canada campaign next quarter for a new payout feature. Marketing's draft copy claims "Get your sellers paid instantly, with no hidden holds" and "The fastest way to move money to contractors," but the product supports same-day payouts only for eligible recipients, and payouts can be delayed by risk reviews, bank outages, weekends, cutoff times, sanctions screening, or recipient verification. Counsel is involved because the business has no tested payout-time data, no fee schedule, no testimonial consents, and no decided disclosure, approval, or Canadian language/consent approach. The business needs the legal answer before the planned launch.
## Decision question
Which headline and supporting claims can Mosaic Relay run in the US and Canada next quarter — given that "instantly," "no hidden holds," and "fastest" are currently unsubstantiated — and what qualifiers, fee/eligibility disclosures, and evidence must be in place before launch across email, paid search, social, and customer presentations?
## Open questions
- What does the actual tested payout-time distribution look like (median, percentage same-day, tail delays)? This determines whether any speed claim beyond "same-day for eligible recipients" is supportable.
- What is the expedited-payout fee schedule (amount or range, who pays, when charged)? Needed to draft compliant fee disclosures.
- Will the campaign run in French for Canada, and does the business hold CASL-compliant consent for Canadian marketing emails?
- Who owns final approval of campaign copy before launch?
- Is there any competitor benchmarking that could support a comparative claim, or should "fastest" be dropped entirely?

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.** The following is model-only analysis based on general knowledge of US and Canadian advertising law; it has not been verified against current sources and should be confirmed before reliance.
**United States — FTC substantiation framework (analysis, not retrieved authority):**
- Under FTC policy, an advertiser must have a **reasonable basis** for all express and implied objective claims *before* dissemination. For most product-performance claims, that means "competent and reliable evidence" — generally tests, analyses, or studies conducted in an objective manner by qualified persons, using procedures generally accepted in the field.
- **"Instantly"** is an absolute performance claim. The product delivers same-day at best, and only for eligible recipients, with multiple known delay causes. Without tested data showing near-universal immediate receipt, "instantly" is very likely deceptive. The known delay factors (risk reviews, bank outages, weekends, cutoffs, sanctions screening, verification) directly contradict the claim.
- **"No hidden holds"** is an absolute claim contradicted by the fact that risk reviews and screening can hold or delay payouts. Even if holds are disclosed somewhere, the word "hidden" implies none occur — a claim the product cannot support.
- **"Fastest"** is a comparative/superlative claim requiring substantiation against the relevant competitive set (e.g., reliable comparative testing of payout times across providers). No such benchmarking exists. Unsubstantiated superiority claims are a classic FTC and NAD (National Advertising Division) target, and competitors can also bring Lanham Act § 43(a) false-advertising claims.
- **Qualifiers must be clear, conspicuous, and proximate** — fine print cannot contradict the headline. A headline saying "instantly" with a footnote saying "same-day for eligible recipients, delays may apply" does not cure the deception; the headline itself must be truthful.
- **Case-study statistics** implicate the FTC Endorsement Guides: testimonials must reflect honest experience, results claimed must be typical or accompanied by clear disclosure of generally expected results, material connections must be disclosed, and the advertiser needs the customer's permission plus substantiation for any performance numbers used.
- **Channel-specific:** paid search and social have limited space, which does not excuse disclosure — the FTC's .com Disclosures guidance applies (proximity, prominence, unavoidable hyperlinks for material terms). Email marketing implicates CAN-SPAM (accurate header/subject, opt-out, physical address).
**Canada (analysis, not retrieved authority):**
- The **Competition Act** prohibits materially false or misleading representations and requires that performance claims be based on "adequate and proper testing" done *before* the claim is made — the testing burden sits on the advertiser. "Fastest" and "instantly" would face the same or stricter scrutiny.
- **CASL** requires express or valid implied consent for commercial electronic messages to Canadian recipients, plus sender identification and a functioning unsubscribe. Consent status is undecided — this is a launch blocker for the email channel in Canada.
- **Quebec's Charter of the French Language** requires French versions of commercial advertising for Quebec audiences; whether the campaign will run in French is undecided.
- Quebec's Consumer Protection Act and privacy law (Law 25) may also bear on targeting and disclosures.
## Viable paths
1. **Softened, substantiable headline (recommended starting point).** Anchor on what is true today: e.g., "Same-day payouts for eligible recipients" or "Pay sellers and contractors as fast as the same day." Pair with clear, proximate qualifiers: eligibility requirements, that fees may apply to expedited payouts, that timing depends on the recipient's bank and payment rail, and that verification or review may delay payment. This can launch on the current record.
2. **Substantiate, then upgrade.** Commission a payout-time study (distribution across recipients, rails, banks, days) and, if "fastest" is desired, credible third-party comparative benchmarking. If data supports it, claims like "9X% of payouts arrive same day" become available — specific, verifiable, and far stronger than "instantly."
3. **Drop the superlatives permanently; differentiate on transparency.** "No hidden holds" could be replaced with an accurate transparency claim (e.g., "See exactly when and why a payout is held") — turning a compliance problem into a positioning asset, consistent with Mosaic Relay's explainable-decisions principle.
4. **Staggered launch.** Launch in the US with softened claims now; gate the Canadian email program on CASL consent confirmation and French-language assets.
## Facts that could change the answer
- Tested payout-time data showing near-instant receipt for a defined, disclosed recipient set could support a carefully qualified speed claim (though likely still not unqualified "instantly").
- A fee schedule showing expedited payouts are free would remove the fee-disclosure burden and open "no extra cost" claims.
- Existing customer contracts or marketing consents might already cover case-study use — worth checking before treating consent as a gap.
- If the campaign excludes Quebec and uses only CASL-compliant implied/express consent lists, the Canadian workstream simplifies materially.
- Competitor benchmarking, if it exists or is commissioned, could revive a comparative claim in qualified form.

## Recommendation (for the lawyer's decision — not a recorded decision)
Advise Marketing that "instantly," "no hidden holds," and "fastest" cannot run as drafted: there is no reasonable basis, and the known delay factors affirmatively contradict two of the three claims. Offer the softened-headline path (Path 1) as launch-ready now, with qualifiers drafted into the headline's immediate visual field, a linked fee/eligibility disclosure once the fee schedule is supplied, and written customer consent plus substantiation files before any case-study statistic is used. Gate the Canadian email channel on CASL consent and a French-language decision. Recommend assigning a named final approval owner (suggest Legal + Marketing joint sign-off) before launch.
