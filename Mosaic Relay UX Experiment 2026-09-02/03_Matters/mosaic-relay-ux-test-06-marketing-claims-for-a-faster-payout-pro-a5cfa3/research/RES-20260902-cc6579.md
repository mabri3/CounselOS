# CASL consent requirements for commercial electronic messages to Canadian recipie

## Question

CASL consent requirements for commercial electronic messages to Canadian recipients

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay (a fictional test company) plans a US–Canada marketing campaign next quarter for a new payout feature, with draft copy claiming "Get your sellers paid instantly, with no hidden holds" and "The fastest way to move money to contractors." The product actually supports same-day payouts only for eligible recipients, and payouts can be delayed by risk reviews, bank outages, weekends, cutoff times, sanctions screening, or recipient verification. Counsel is involved because the claims are currently unsubstantiated (no tested payout-time data exists), fee and eligibility disclosures are undecided, no written customer consent exists for case-study testimonials, and the Canadian consent/language approach is unresolved. The business needs the legal answer before the planned launch.
## Decision question
Before next quarter's US–Canada launch across email, paid search, social, and customer presentations, which replacement headline claims and qualifier/disclosure package can Mosaic Relay run without tested payout-time data, and what consent, French-language, and testimonial-approval steps must Marketing complete first so the campaign does not create deceptive-advertising or CASL exposure?
## Open questions
- What consent basis (express or implied) does Mosaic Relay actually hold for Canadian email recipients, and do existing records capture consent date, source, and the identification/unsubscribe elements CASL requires? This determines whether the email channel can launch in Canada at all.
- What is the expedited-payout fee schedule (amount/range, who pays, when charged)? Needed to draft compliant fee disclosure language.
- Will the campaign run in Québec, and if so, who owns French-language adaptation of copy and disclosures?
- Who is the final approval owner for campaign copy before launch? Currently unassigned.
- Will any case-study statistics be attributed to named customers, and can written consent plus substantiation be obtained before launch?

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.** The following is model-only analysis based on general knowledge of the governing frameworks; it has not been verified against current primary sources and should be confirmed before reliance.
**Canada — CASL (the research question):**
- CASL applies to "commercial electronic messages" (CEMs) sent to electronic addresses, and marketing email to Canadian recipients squarely qualifies. Unlike US CAN-SPAM (opt-out), CASL is fundamentally **opt-in**: a CEM may only be sent with express consent or a qualifying form of implied consent.
- **Express consent** requires a clear, affirmative opt-in request that identifies the sender (and any third party on whose behalf the message is sent), provides contact information, and states the purpose. Consent records (date, method, wording shown) must be retained — the sender bears the evidentiary burden.
- **Implied consent** exists in defined circumstances, notably an "existing business relationship" (e.g., a purchase or contract within the prior 24 months, or an inquiry within the prior 6 months) and conspicuously published business addresses relevant to the sender's role (with no "do not contact" statement). Implied consent is time-limited and fact-specific; it cannot be assumed for purchased lists or scraped addresses.
- **Content requirements:** every CEM must identify the sender (and any person on whose behalf it is sent), include valid contact information, and contain a functioning, no-cost unsubscribe mechanism honored within 10 business days.
- **Penalties are material:** administrative monetary penalties up to CAD $10 million for organizations per violation, with potential director/officer and vicarious liability. (A private right of action was legislated but its coming-into-force was suspended; treat enforcement as regulator-led.)
- **Practical consequence for this campaign:** the email channel in Canada is the highest-risk element. Paid search, social ads, and presentations are generally not CEMs (they are not sent to an electronic address), so CASL's consent regime principally gates the email program — though platform messages (e.g., LinkedIn InMail-type direct messages) can be CEMs and need care.
**US — email and general advertising:**
- CAN-SPAM: accurate header/subject lines, identification as an ad where required, valid physical postal address, functioning opt-out honored within 10 business days, and monitoring of any creative agency sending on Mosaic Relay's behalf (both parties can be liable).
- FTC Act § 5 / FTC advertising substantiation policy: objective claims — especially "instantly," "fastest," and "no hidden holds" — require a reasonable basis (competent and reliable evidence) **before** dissemination. "Fastest" is a comparative superiority claim requiring head-to-head substantiation against competitors; none exists here.
- FTC Endorsement Guides: customer case-study statistics are endorsements; they must reflect honest experience, typical results (or clear disclosure of atypical results), and any material connection must be disclosed. Written consent and substantiation for each statistic are needed.
**Canada — other advertising law:**
- Competition Act false/misleading representations provisions (civil and criminal tracks): claims must be literally true and not misleading in general impression; performance claims ("fastest," speed statistics) must be supported by adequate and proper testing conducted **before** the claim is made. This independently kills the current headline even if CASL is solved.
- Québec Charter of the French Language (as amended by Bill 96): commercial advertising directed at Québec consumers generally must be in French (or bilingual with French at least equivalent). If the campaign targets Québec, French versions of copy, disclosures, and landing pages are required.
- Canadian privacy law (PIPEDA / Québec Law 25) governs collection and use of personal information for targeting and list-building, separate from CASL consent.
**Claim-specific issues:**
- "Instantly" — false on the facts (same-day at best, eligibility-gated, multiple delay causes).
- "No hidden holds" — contradicted by risk reviews, sanctions screening, and verification holds; also arguably deceptive given expedited fees are undisclosed.
- "Fastest" — unsubstantiated comparative claim; no testing exists.
- Fee/eligibility disclosure — expedited fees and verification requirements are material conditions that must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously, proximate to the speed claim, not buried in linked terms.
## Viable paths
1. **Softened-claim launch (recommended baseline):** Replace the headline with a truthful, qualified claim — e.g., "Same-day payouts for eligible recipients" — paired with proximate disclosures: eligibility conditions, that fees may apply to expedited payouts, that timing depends on the recipient's bank and payment rail, and that verification or review may delay funds. This path requires no new data and can meet the launch date.
2. **Substantiation-first launch:** Commission a tested payout-time distribution study (and competitor benchmarking if any comparative claim is desired) before launch; keep superlatives out until data supports them. Likely incompatible with next quarter's date unless scoped narrowly.
3. **Channel-split Canada approach:** Launch paid search, social, and presentations in Canada on schedule (with Competition Act-compliant claims and French versions if Québec is in scope), and gate Canadian **email** behind a CASL consent audit — suppressing recipients without documented express or valid implied consent, or running a consent-confirmation campaign first.
4. **Case studies deferred or conditioned:** Hold customer statistics out of launch copy until written consent, substantiation, and typicality disclosures are in place; or run them only in later-stage materials (presentations) with approvals documented.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Consent inventory:** If Mosaic Relay's CRM already captures CASL-compliant express consent (or all Canadian recipients are current customers within the 24-month implied window), the email channel may proceed with minimal delay; if lists include prospects, purchased contacts, or stale relationships, large segments may need suppression.
- **Québec targeting:** If Québec is excluded, French-language obligations narrow substantially; if included, translation and Law 25 privacy requirements add lead time.
- **Fee structure:** A flat, disclosed expedited fee is easy to disclose; variable or recipient-dependent fees may require more prominent, specific disclosure.
- **Whether any recipients are consumers:** Consumer-facing recipients (individual contractors) raise consumer-protection overlay in both countries and strengthen the case for plain-language disclosures.
- **Agency role:** If the creative agency sends email on Mosaic Relay's behalf, CASL and CAN-SPAM liability allocation and consent-flow mechanics must be contractually fixed.
