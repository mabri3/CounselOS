# FTC endorsement/testimonial guides and evidence standards for customer performan

## Question

FTC endorsement/testimonial guides and evidence standards for customer performance statistics in case studies

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay (a fictional test company) plans a US and Canada marketing campaign next quarter for a new payout feature, with draft copy claiming “Get your sellers paid instantly, with no hidden holds” and “The fastest way to move money to contractors.” The product actually supports same-day payouts only for eligible recipients, and payouts can be delayed by risk reviews, bank outages, weekends, cutoff times, sanctions screening, or identity verification; fees may apply to expedited payouts. Marketing, Product, Sales, and a creative agency are involved, and counsel is involved because the campaign includes absolute speed claims, fee/eligibility disclosures, and planned customer performance statistics in case studies. No tested payout-time data exists, no written customer consent for testimonials exists, and the final copy approval owner is unassigned — all before a planned launch.
## Decision question
Before next quarter's US/Canada launch across email, paid search, social, and customer presentations, which replacement claims and disclosure language can Mosaic Relay run for its same-day payout feature — given no tested payout-time data, undisclosed expedited fees, and verification-based delays — and what evidence and written consents must exist before using customer performance statistics in case studies?
## Open questions
- What does the actual payout-time distribution look like once tested (median, percentage same-day, delay causes)? This determines whether any speed claim beyond “same-day for eligible recipients” is supportable.
- What is the expedited-payout fee schedule (amount/range, who pays, when charged)? Needed to draft compliant fee disclosure language.
- Will the Canadian campaign run in French, and does Mosaic Relay hold CASL-compliant consent for Canadian marketing emails?
- Who is the final approval owner for campaign copy before launch?
- Which customers will appear in case studies, and will they give written consent covering the specific statistics and quotes used?

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.** The following is model-only analysis based on general knowledge of US and Canadian marketing law frameworks; it has not been verified against current sources and should be confirmed before reliance.
**US — FTC Act Section 5 and the FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255):**
- Claims must be truthful, not misleading, and substantiated before dissemination. “Instantly” and “fastest” are objective claims requiring a reasonable basis — typically competent and reliable evidence. No tested payout-time data exists, so neither claim is currently substantiated.
- “Fastest” is a comparative superiority claim; it requires substantiation against the relevant competitive set, not just internal performance.
- “No hidden holds” is contradicted by known facts: risk reviews, sanctions screening, and verification can delay or hold payouts. Even if disclosed elsewhere, the headline itself is likely deceptive because it denies a practice that occurs.
- Customer performance statistics in case studies are likely “endorsements” under the Endorsement Guides. Key implications: (1) the advertiser needs substantiation that the endorser's experience is representative of what consumers can generally expect, or must clearly and conspicuously disclose the generally expected results; (2) “results not typical” disclaimers are generally insufficient without disclosure of typical results; (3) any material connection (payment, free service, discounts) between Mosaic Relay and the featured customer must be disclosed; (4) the advertiser can be liable for unsubstantiated statements made through endorsements.
- Email channel: CAN-SPAM Act requirements (accurate header/subject lines, opt-out mechanism, physical address) apply to commercial email. Canadian email: CASL requires express or valid implied consent, identification, and functioning unsubscribe — a materially stricter regime.
**Canada — Competition Act and Quebec language law:**
- The Competition Act prohibits false or misleading representations; performance claims must be based on adequate and proper testing done before the claim is made. Drip pricing provisions require the total price including mandatory fees to be disclosed upfront.
- Quebec's Charter of the French Language (as amended by Bill 96) requires French versions of commercial advertising for campaigns reaching Quebec consumers.
- CASL consent for commercial electronic messages is undecided and is a gating item for the email channel in Canada.
**Internal issues (from matter files):**
- The two headline claims conflict with recorded facts about eligibility limits and delay causes.
- Fee and eligibility disclosure approach is undecided; fee schedule not yet provided.
- No written customer consent exists for case-study testimonials.
- Final approval owner unassigned; Canada French/CASL approach undecided.
## Viable paths
**Path A — Softened, substantiable claims (recommended starting point):** Replace absolute claims with qualified, accurate ones, e.g., “Same-day payouts for eligible recipients” with clear, conspicuous qualifiers: “Payout timing depends on the recipient's bank and payment rail. Fees may apply to expedited payouts. Identity verification may be required.” This path is launchable on the current record once fee and eligibility disclosures are drafted and the fee schedule is confirmed.
**Path B — Substantiated speed claim (deferred):** Commission a payout-time study (distribution across rails, banks, cutoff times, delay causes). If data supports it, a claim like “most payouts arrive same day” could run with the study on file. “Fastest” should remain off the table unless comparative testing against competitors is also done — a heavier lift unlikely to complete before launch.
**Path C — Case studies with controls:** Use customer statistics only after (1) written consent covering the specific quotes, statistics, name/logo use, and channels; (2) verification that the statistics are accurate and current; (3) either substantiation that results are typical or a clear disclosure of generally expected results; (4) disclosure of any material connection. Until then, strip performance statistics from launch materials.
## Facts that could change the answer
- Tested payout-time data showing a strong same-day rate could support a modest speed claim (Path B).
- The fee schedule's structure (flat vs. percentage, who bears it) changes disclosure wording and Canadian drip-pricing analysis.
- Whether the campaign targets Quebec determines French-language obligations.
- Whether featured customers received any incentive changes endorsement-disclosure requirements.
- Whether any recipients are consumers (vs. businesses) could pull in additional consumer-protection overlays.
