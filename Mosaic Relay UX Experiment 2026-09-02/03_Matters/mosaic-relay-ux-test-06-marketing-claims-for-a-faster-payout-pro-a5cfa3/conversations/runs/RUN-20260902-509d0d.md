    **Matter:** Mosaic Relay UX Test — 06 — Marketing Claims for a Faster Payout Product

    ## 1. Claims substantiation — "instantly" and "fastest"

    **Bottom line:** The draft headline claims ("instantly," "fastest") are **currently
    unsupportable** and must be softened. There is no tested payout-time data, and
    the product is same-day-only for eligible recipients, with multiple delay conditions.
    The claims cannot survive in their current form.

    ### The substantiation standard

    - US federal law prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting
    commerce. **[VERIFIED — 15 U.S.C. § 45, FTC Act]**

    - The FTC requires that advertisers possess a **"reasonable basis"** for objective
    claims — including performance, efficacy, and comparative claims — **before**
    the claim is made. The reasonable basis must be substantiated by competent and
    reliable evidence appropriate to the claim. **[VERIFIED — FTC Policy Statement
    on Advertising Substantiation (1983)]**

    - **Superlative claims** ("fastest," "best," "instant") are objective claims that
    require substantiation. An unqualified superlative implies the product outperforms
    all competitors, which requires comparative evidence. **[VERIFIED — FTC substantiation
    doctrine; UNVERIFIED LEAD — exact evidentiary standard varies by claim type]**

    - **"Instant"** is a temporal claim that implies immediate delivery. The product
    is same-day, not instant, and only for eligible recipients. **[VERIFIED — company
    fact: same-day only]**

    ### Why the current claims fail

    - **No tested data:** The business confirmed there is no tested payout-time distribution.
    Without data, there is no reasonable basis for "fastest" or "instant." **[VERIFIED
    — company fact]**

    - **Same-day, not instant:** The product supports same-day payouts, not instant
    payouts. "Instant" overstates the actual timing. **[VERIFIED — company fact]**

    - **Eligibility limitation:** Same-day applies to eligible recipients only; some
    must complete identity verification. An unqualified "instant" claim ignores this
    material limitation. **[VERIFIED — company fact]**

    - **Delay conditions:** Risk reviews, bank outages, weekends, cutoff times, sanctions
    screening, and recipient verification can delay payouts. These are material facts
    that an unqualified speed claim omits. **[VERIFIED — company fact]**

    ### Recommended softened claims

    Replace the absolute claims with accurate, qualifiable language:

    - **"Get your sellers paid faster"** (not "instantly") — a relative claim that
    can be supported by the same-day feature versus standard payout timing, **if**
    the comparison is accurate and disclosed.

    - **"Same-day payouts for eligible recipients"** — an accurate, specific claim
    that matches the product capability.

    - **"A faster way to move money to contractors"** (not "the fastest") — a relative
    claim that avoids the superlative and the need for competitor-comparative substantiation.

    **[VERIFIED — company facts; UNVERIFIED LEAD — the specific softened wording should
    be tested for accuracy against actual payout-time data once available]**

    ## 2. Qualifiers and disclosure placement

    **Bottom line:** Any speed claim must be qualified with a clear, prominent disclosure
    of eligibility, timing, and delay conditions. The disclosure must be **close and
    clear** to the claim, not buried.

    ### The "clear and conspicuous" standard

    - Disclosures must be **clear and conspicuous** — meaning consumers can notice,
    read, and understand them. **[VERIFIED — FTC Dot Com Disclosures (2013)]**

    - The disclosure must be placed **near the claim** it qualifies, in a format and
    location consumers will see. **[VERIFIED — FTC Dot Com Disclosures]**

    - On mobile and social, disclosures must be visible without requiring the consumer
    to scroll or click to find material limitations. **[VERIFIED — FTC Dot Com Disclosures;
    UNVERIFIED LEAD — platform-specific display rules]**

    ### Recommended qualifier language

    A short, prominent eligibility line adjacent to the claim:

    > "Same-day payouts for eligible recipients. Subject to identity verification,
    risk review, and bank/rail timing. Fees may apply to expedited payouts."

    - **Drop "no hidden holds" entirely.** It conflicts with the real delay conditions
    (risk reviews, sanctions screening, verification) and is affirmatively misleading.
    **[VERIFIED — company fact: delays exist]**

    - **Disclose fees at the point of the claim**, not just at checkout, with the
    full schedule linked. **[VERIFIED — FTC substantiation/disclosure doctrine; UNVERIFIED
    LEAD — exact placement depends on channel]**

    - **Disclose eligibility** (identity verification, eligible recipients) adjacent
    to the claim. **[VERIFIED — company fact: eligibility limitation]**

    **[VERIFIED — FTC disclosure doctrine; UNVERIFIED LEAD — final wording pending
    fee schedule and tested data]**

    ## 3. Comparative advertising — "fastest"

    **Bottom line:** The comparative superlative "fastest" requires substantiation
    against competitors, which is not available. It must be removed or replaced with
    a non-comparative relative claim.

    ### The comparative-claim standard

    - Comparative advertising is lawful, but **claims that compare to competitors
    must be truthful and substantiated**. **[VERIFIED — FTC Act § 5; general comparative-advertising
    doctrine]**

    - A **superlative** ("fastest," "best") is an implied comparative claim that the
    product outperforms all others in the category. This requires evidence of superiority
    over competitors. **[VERIFIED — FTC substantiation doctrine; UNVERIFIED LEAD —
    exact evidentiary standard]**

    - **No competitor data exists.** The business has no tested payout-time data,
    let alone comparative data against competitors. The "fastest" claim is unsupportable.
    **[VERIFIED — company fact]**

    ### Recommended approach

    - **Remove "fastest."** Replace with a non-comparative or accurately-qualified
    relative claim ("a faster way," "same-day payouts").

    - **If a comparative claim is desired later**, it requires: (a) tested payout-time
    data for Mosaic Relay, (b) comparable data for the named competitor(s), and (c)
    a methodology that is fair and representative. **[UNVERIFIED LEAD — methodology
    to be developed if comparative claims are pursued]**

    - **Avoid naming competitors** without substantiated comparative data. **[VERIFIED
    — FTC doctrine]**

    **[VERIFIED — FTC doctrine; UNVERIFIED LEAD — comparative methodology not yet
    developed]**

    ## 5. Customer case studies and testimonials

    **Bottom line:** Customer performance statistics in case studies require evidence
    standards and testimonial/endorsement approvals. **No written customer consent
    exists yet** — this is a material gap that blocks the case studies from running.

    ### The endorsement standard

    - The FTC requires that endorsements reflect the **honest opinions, findings,
    beliefs, or experiences** of the endorser, and that advertisers disclose material
    connections. **[VERIFIED — FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 C.F.R. Part 255]**

    - **Performance statistics** in case studies must be **substantiated** — the advertiser
    must have competent and reliable evidence supporting the claimed results. **[VERIFIED
    — FTC substantiation doctrine]**

    - **Representative results:** If the case study presents results that are not
    typical, the advertiser must disclose the generally expected performance, or clearly
    and conspicuously state what the generally expected results are. **[VERIFIED —
    FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 C.F.R. § 255.2]**

    - **Consent:** Using a customer''s name, quote, or performance data requires the
    customer''s **written consent** (and, for testimonials, the customer''s honest
    experience). **[VERIFIED — FTC Endorsement Guides; UNVERIFIED LEAD — exact consent
    form requirements]**

    ### Recommended approach

    - **Obtain written consent** from each customer before using their name, quote,
    or performance data in a case study. **[VERIFIED — required]**

    - **Substantiate the performance statistics** with actual, verifiable data. **[VERIFIED
    — required]**

    - **Disclose material connections** (e.g., if the customer is compensated or has
    a relationship with Mosaic Relay). **[VERIFIED — FTC Endorsement Guides]**

    - **Disclose typical results** if the featured results are not representative.
    **[VERIFIED — FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 C.F.R. § 255.2]**

    - **Retain the underlying data** supporting each statistic. **[VERIFIED — substantiation
    doctrine]**

    ### Material gap

    **No written customer consent exists** for case-study testimonials. The case studies
    cannot run until consent is obtained and the performance data is substantiated.
    **[VERIFIED — company fact]**

    ## 6. Channel-specific rules — email, paid search, social, customer presentations

    **Bottom line:** Each channel has specific rules that affect how claims and disclosures
    must be presented, and Canada adds CASL consent requirements for email.

    ### Email (US and Canada)

    - **US:** The CAN-SPAM Act requires accurate header information, a clear subject
    line, a valid physical postal address, a working opt-out mechanism, and honoring
    opt-outs. **[VERIFIED — 15 U.S.C. § 7701 et seq., CAN-SPAM]**

    - **Canada:** **CASL** requires **express or implied consent** before sending
    a commercial electronic message (CEM) to a Canadian recipient, plus identification
    and an unsubscribe mechanism. **[VERIFIED — CASL, S.C. 2010, c. 23; UNVERIFIED
    LEAD — consent status not confirmed]**

    - **Material gap:** The **CASL consent approach is not yet decided**. If the campaign
    emails Canadian recipients without CASL-compliant consent, it risks significant
    penalties. **[VERIFIED — company fact: consent undecided]**

    ### Paid search

    - Paid search ads are subject to the same substantiation and disclosure rules
    as other advertising. **[VERIFIED — FTC doctrine]**

    - **Disclosure placement:** On paid search, the claim and disclosure must fit
    within the ad''s character limits; material limitations must be disclosed in the
    ad itself or via a clear link. **[UNVERIFIED LEAD — platform-specific ad policies]**

    - **Platform policies:** Google and other platforms have their own ad policies
    on financial products and claims; these must be reviewed. **[UNVERIFIED LEAD —
    platform policies not reviewed]**

    ### Social media

    - Social posts are subject to the same substantiation and disclosure rules. **[VERIFIED
    — FTC doctrine]**

    - **Disclosure visibility:** On social, disclosures must be visible without requiring
    the consumer to click "more" or scroll to find material limitations. **[VERIFIED
    — FTC Dot Com Disclosures]**

    - **Endorsement disclosure:** If influencers or customers post about the product,
    material connections must be disclosed. **[VERIFIED — FTC Endorsement Guides]**

    ### Customer presentations

    - Customer presentations are **sales materials** and are subject to the same truth-in-advertising
    rules as other marketing. **[VERIFIED — FTC doctrine]**

    - **Oral claims:** Any speed or fee claims made orally in presentations must be
    accurate and qualified, consistent with the written disclosures. **[VERIFIED —
    FTC doctrine]**

    - **Case-study data:** Performance statistics presented in customer presentations
    must be substantiated and, if not typical, disclosed as such. **[VERIFIED — FTC
    Endorsement Guides]**

    **[VERIFIED — FTC/CAN-SPAM/CASL doctrine; UNVERIFIED LEAD — platform-specific
    policies and CASL consent status]**
