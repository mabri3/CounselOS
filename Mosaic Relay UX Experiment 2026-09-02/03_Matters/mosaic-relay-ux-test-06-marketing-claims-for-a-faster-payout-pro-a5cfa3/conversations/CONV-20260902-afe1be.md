### The substantiation standard
- US federal law prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce. **[VERIFIED — 15 U.S.C. § 45, FTC Act]**
- The FTC requires that advertisers possess a **"reasonable basis"** for objective claims — including performance, efficacy, and comparative claims — **before** the claim is made. - **Superlative claims** ("fastest," "best," "instant") are objective claims that require substantiation. An unqualified superlative implies the product outperforms all competitors, which requires comparative evidence. **[VERIFIED — FTC substantiation doctrine; UNVERIFIED LEAD — exact evidentiary standard varies by claim type]**
- **"Instant"** is a temporal claim that implies immediate delivery. The product is same-day, not instant, and only for eligible recipients. **[VERIFIED — company fact: same-day only]**

- **No tested data:** The business confirmed there is no tested payout-time distribution. Without data, there is no reasonable basis for "fastest" or "instant." **[VERIFIED — company fact]**
