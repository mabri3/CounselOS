# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Consumer dispute portal for card purchases; consumers only; English plus Spanish; initial release 2026-10-15; Payments Operations owns operating-model decisions; evidence rules/status labels mixed legal/policy; draft with assumptions for merchant of record, networks, seller terms, escalation.

## Decision question

Draft the legal requirements and operating model for a consumer dispute portal for card purchases. Buyers (consumers only) report non-receipt, unauthorized use, duplicate charges, or defective goods; the portal collects statements/documents, sends automated messages (English plus Spanish), and routes cases to the marketplace or seller. Initial release target 2026-10-15. Separate legal requirements from customer policy choices; Payments Operations owns operating-model decisions once legal sets requirements; evidence rules/status labels are mixed (some fixed, some policy). Draft with best-available assumptions for merchant of record, networks/regions, seller-terms allocation, and escalation behavior.

## Material facts

- A consumer-facing marketplace asks Mosaic Relay to provide a dispute portal for card purchases.
- Proposed dispute categories: non-receipt, unauthorized use, duplicate charges, defective goods.
- The portal would collect statements and documents, send automated messages, and route cases to the marketplace or seller.
- Actors include buyers, sellers, the marketplace, Mosaic Relay, card networks, issuers, and support agents.
- Product wants an initial release in six weeks (target date 2026-10-15).
- Known facts include the proposed categories, evidence upload flow, and response-time dashboard.
- Missing facts include network rules, customer-of-record status, required notices, language access, accessibility, evidence sharing, and escalation when the marketplace does not respond.
- Who is the merchant of record / customer of record for the card transactions the dispute portal will cover? — Not yet determined — this is being decided
- Which card networks and regions must the initial release support? — Not yet determined
- Are the buyers who will use the portal consumers, business purchasers, or both? — Consumers only
- Whether marketplace seller terms and marketplace terms allocate dispute/refund/chargeback responsibility — Not sure — need to check
- When the marketplace or seller does not respond within the network deadline, what should the portal do? — Not yet decided — need a recommendation
- What language(s) must the dispute portal and its notices support for the initial release? — English plus Spanish
- Who should own the operating-model decisions (notices, deadlines, status labels, escalation paths) once legal sets the requirements? — Payments Operations
- Should the portal's evidence rules and status labels be treated as legal requirements or as customer policy choices? — Mixed — some fixed, some policy
- draft with assumptions.

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay is not the merchant of record for the card transactions unless stated otherwise; the marketplace likely is, with Mosaic Relay as a payments-infrastructure service provider
- Card transactions are consumer card purchases subject to Visa/Mastercard chargeback rules and U.S. consumer-protection law
- The dispute portal is U.S.-focused for the initial release unless stated otherwise
- The marketplace is the merchant of record; Mosaic Relay is a payments-infrastructure service provider, not the merchant of record.
- Card transactions are U.S. consumer card purchases subject to Visa/Mastercard chargeback rules and U.S. consumer-protection law for the initial release.
- The dispute portal is U.S.-focused for the initial release.
- Seller/marketplace terms do not yet allocate dispute/refund/chargeback responsibility; this must be checked and addressed in the draft.
- When the marketplace or seller does not respond within the network deadline, the portal needs a defined fallback (to be recommended in the draft).

## Issues and workstreams

- Card network chargeback rules (Visa/Mastercard) and who bears them — depends on merchant-of-record status
- Consumer-protection obligations (FTC Mail/Telephone Order rules, state consumer laws, EFTA/Reg E for unauthorized use) and required notices
- Whether Mosaic Relay is a service provider to the marketplace or the merchant of record, which determines who must respond to disputes
- Evidence handling, sharing, and retention rules for dispute documentation
- Escalation path when the marketplace or seller does not respond within network deadlines
- Language access (English plus Spanish) and accessibility obligations for consumer-facing dispute communications
- Separating legal requirements from customer policy choices, and assigning owners
