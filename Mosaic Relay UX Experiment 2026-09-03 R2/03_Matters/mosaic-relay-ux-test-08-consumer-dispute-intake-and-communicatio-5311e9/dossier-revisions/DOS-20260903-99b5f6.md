# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Consumer dispute portal for card purchases; consumers only; six-week launch target. Pivotal open items: merchant-of-record status, card networks/regions, seller-terms allocation, and escalation path.

## Decision question

A consumer-facing marketplace asks Mosaic Relay to provide a dispute portal for card purchases. Buyers (consumers only) could report non-receipt, unauthorized use, duplicate charges, or defective goods. The portal would collect statements and documents, send automated messages, and route cases to the marketplace or seller. Actors include buyers, sellers, the marketplace, Mosaic Relay, card networks, issuers, and support agents. Product wants an initial release in six weeks (target 2026-10-15). Legal must specify the exact intake questions, notices, deadlines, evidence rules, status labels, communications, and escalation paths the portal should require, separating legal requirements from customer policy choices, assigning owners, and recording the approved operating model. Still open: merchant-of-record status, card networks/regions, seller-terms allocation, and escalation path when the marketplace/seller does not respond.

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay is not the merchant of record for the card transactions unless stated otherwise; the marketplace likely is, with Mosaic Relay as a payments-infrastructure service provider
- Card transactions are consumer card purchases subject to Visa/Mastercard chargeback rules and U.S. consumer-protection law
- The dispute portal is U.S.-focused for the initial release unless stated otherwise

## Issues and workstreams

- Card network chargeback rules (Visa/Mastercard) and who bears them — depends on merchant-of-record status
- Consumer-protection obligations (FTC Mail/Telephone Order rules, state consumer laws, EFTA/Reg E for unauthorized use) and required notices
- Whether Mosaic Relay is a service provider to the marketplace or the merchant of record, which determines who must respond to disputes
- Evidence handling, sharing, and retention rules for dispute documentation
- Escalation path when the marketplace or seller does not respond within network deadlines
- Language access and accessibility obligations for consumer-facing dispute communications
- Separating legal requirements from customer policy choices, and assigning owners
