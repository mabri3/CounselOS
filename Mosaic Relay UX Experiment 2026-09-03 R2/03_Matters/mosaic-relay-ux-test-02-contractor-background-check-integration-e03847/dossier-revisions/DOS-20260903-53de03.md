# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

A software platform wants to use Mosaic Relay to pay delivery contractors, with a background-check vendor screening contractors for identity, criminal history, sanctions, and work eligibility before the first payout. The platform would receive a “clear,” “review,” or “fail” result, while Mosaic Relay processes identity data and payout instructions. Counsel is involved because criminal-history screening of contractors creates disparate-impact discrimination exposure under Title VII, alongside FCRA, state fair-chance, privacy, and payments issues. Target launch is 2026-10-29, eight weeks out, and the contractors are independent contractors of the platform.

## Decision question

Before the 2026-10-29 launch, should legal permit the platform to use criminal-history screening results (clear/review/fail) to gate contractor payouts, and if so, what individualized-assessment, notice, and data-minimization controls must the platform and Mosaic Relay implement to avoid disparate-impact liability under EEOC guidance — given that the contractors are independent contractors, the final decision-maker is undecided, and Mosaic Relay would otherwise receive screening data it does not need?

## Open questions

- Who makes the final decision on a “review” or “fail” result — the platform, Mosaic Relay, or the vendor? This determines who bears Title VII disparate-impact exposure and who must run any individualized assessment.
- Are the background-check reports “consumer reports” under FCRA and is the vendor a consumer reporting agency? If yes, the full adverse-action apparatus applies; if the vendor is not a CRA, disparate-impact risk remains but the procedural overlay changes.
- Which states will screening and payouts cover at launch? State and local fair-chance laws (e.g., California, New York City) impose stricter limits on criminal-history use than federal guidance, and some apply explicitly to independent contractors.
- What criminal-history data does the vendor actually return (arrests vs. convictions, lookback period, offense categories)? EEOC guidance treats arrests and convictions differently, and blanket exclusions are the highest-risk pattern.
- Does the platform’s use of screening results create an employment relationship or joint-employer exposure with Mosaic Relay, given that Mosaic Relay processes identity data and payout instructions?
