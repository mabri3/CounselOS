# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

FCRA consumer-reporting characterization and decision-maker posture; adverse-action workflow; state coverage; contractor classification.

## Decision question

A software platform wants to use Mosaic Relay to pay contractors and proposes integrating a background-check vendor to screen contractors for identity, criminal history, sanctions, and work eligibility before the first payout. The platform receives a "clear"/"review"/"fail" result; Mosaic Relay processes identity data and payout instructions. Target launch is 2026-10-29 (eight weeks out). Legal must specify the product behavior, consent language, notices, dispute rights, access controls, and customer contract terms required, mapping privacy, consumer-reporting (FCRA), employment, discrimination, and payments risks.

## Material facts

- A software platform wants to use Mosaic Relay to pay contractors.
- The platform proposes adding a background-check vendor to screen contractors for identity, criminal history, sanctions, and work eligibility before the first payout.
- The platform would receive a 'clear', 'review', or 'fail' result from the screening.
- Mosaic Relay would process identity data and payout instructions.
- Actors are Product, the platform, the screening vendor, and contractors.
- Target launch is eight weeks away (2026-10-29).
- The vendor's API and stated coverage in the United States are known.
- The initial use case involves delivery contractors.
- Who makes the final decision when a contractor receives a 'review' or 'fail' screening result? — Not yet decided

## Assumptions

- The background-check reports are consumer reports under FCRA and the vendor is a consumer reporting agency (to be confirmed)
- Contractors are independent contractors, not employees, of the platform (to be confirmed)
- The screening is used for an employment/engagement decision, triggering adverse-action obligations
- Mosaic Relay's role is limited to processing identity data and payout instructions, not making the screening decision

## Issues and workstreams

- FCRA consumer-reporting characterization and who is the 'user' of the consumer report
- Adverse-action notice obligations (pre-adverse and adverse-action) and who provides them
- State background-check, ban-the-box, and pay-to-screen laws
- Disparate-impact discrimination risk from criminal-history screening
- Privacy and data-minimization for identity data collection and processing
- Money-transmission characterization and which party is the regulated actor
- Contractor dispute and reinvestigation rights

## Open questions

- Whether the background-check reports are 'consumer reports' under FCRA and whether the vendor is a consumer reporting agency
- The exact reports/products used (identity, criminal, sanctions, work eligibility) and their data sources
- The adverse-action workflow and who provides pre-adverse and adverse-action notices
- The dispute process and who handles contractor disputes of report accuracy
- State coverage and which state background-check, ban-the-box, and pay-to-screen laws apply
- Whether contractors are employees or independent contractors of the platform
- Consent language and notice flow for collecting and processing identity data
- Whether the platform or Mosaic Relay is the regulated actor for money-transmission purposes
- Who makes the final decision on a 'review' or 'fail' result — the platform, Mosaic Relay, or the vendor (determines FCRA user/employer/processor posture)
- Whether contractors are employees or independent contractors of the platform (employment vs. consumer-reporting framing)
