      ## The legal frame in brief

      Recurring ACH debits are governed primarily by the **NACHA Operating Rules**,
      and — if any accounts are **consumer** accounts — by **Regulation E / EFTA**
      (stop-payment rights, 60-day error resolution, and specific authorization language).
      The single biggest fork is the consumer/business mix, because it determines
      which of those two frameworks applies and reshapes almost every downstream requirement.

      ## Prioritized questions

      I''ve ordered these by how much each answer moves the legal analysis:

      1. **Consumer vs. business account mix** — This determines the entire regulatory
      framework. Consumer accounts trigger Reg E/EFTA stop-payment and error-resolution
      obligations plus stricter NACHA authorization language; business accounts are
      NACHA-only with far fewer protections.

      2. **Fixed vs. variable debit amount** — Variable-amount recurring debits require
      NACHA advance notice (10 days before the first debit, 7 days before each subsequent
      debit) and specific authorization language; fixed amounts do not.

      3. **Cancellation process and propagation** — How a user''s cancellation reaches
      Mosaic Relay''s operations to stop future debits, and who owns that stop obligation.

      4. **Authorization record format and retention** — NACHA requires the ODFI to
      retain the authorization (or a record of it) for 2 years; the format and retention
      need confirmation for recordkeeping approval.

      5. **Retry and unauthorized-return handling** — Retries may constitute new debits
      needing fresh authorization/notice, and unauthorized-return codes (R07/R10/R29)
      must be handled to avoid continued debits after revocation.

      I''ve saved the reported facts and these questions to the matter record. Answering
      the first question (consumer vs. business mix) will most change the shape of
      the requirements legal should approve.'
