# What are the Reg E/EFTA requirements for consumer recurring ACH

## Question

What are the Reg E/EFTA requirements for consumer recurring ACH debits, including stop-payment and error-resolution rights?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay plans to support recurring ACH debits for a software platform collecting monthly subscription fees, with users entering bank account details, agreeing to recurring debits, receiving confirmation, and canceling through the platform. The actors are the platform, its users, Mosaic Relay, the originating bank, and the ACH operator. Counsel is involved because Product wants a pilot in 30 days and needs legal approval of the authorization, recurring-debit, cancellation, notice, return, and recordkeeping requirements. The consumer versus business account mix remains unresolved and determines whether Reg E/EFTA applies at all.
## Decision question
Should legal approve the proposed recurring ACH debit flow for a 30-day pilot, and what specific authorization language, advance-notice timing, cancellation propagation, retry limits, return-code handling, and 2-year recordkeeping format must be locked in before launch to avoid unauthorized-debit liability and Reg E/EFTA violations if consumer accounts are included?
## Open questions
- What is the mix of consumer versus business accounts expected in the pilot? This determines whether Reg E/EFTA stop-payment and error-resolution rights apply or whether NACHA rules alone govern.
- Is the subscription debit amount fixed or variable? This determines whether NACHA requires 10-day advance notice before the first debit and 7-day notice before each subsequent variable debit.
- How does a user's cancellation propagate from the platform to Mosaic Relay's operations to stop future debits, and who owns the stop obligation?
- What is the authorization record format and retention period? NACHA requires the ODFI to retain the authorization (or a reproducible record) for 2 years.
- How should retries of returned debits be handled? Retries may constitute new debits requiring fresh authorization and notice, and returned-debit codes (R07, R10, R29) need defined handling to avoid continued debits after a user revokes authorization.
## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.**
The controlling framework is the NACHA Operating Rules for all ACH debits, plus Regulation E (12 C.F.R. Part 1005) and the Electronic Fund Transfer Act (15 U.S.C. § 1693 et seq.) if any consumer accounts are debited. The core issues are:
1. **Authorization requirements.** NACHA requires that recurring ACH debits be authorized by a written or similarly authenticated agreement that is readily identifiable as an authorization and that clearly and conspicuously states its terms. For consumer accounts, Reg E requires that preauthorized electronic fund transfers be authorized in writing or similarly authenticated, and that a copy of the authorization be provided to the consumer. The authorization must specify the amount (or the method for determining variable amounts), the frequency, and the account to be debited.
2. **Advance notice.** NACHA requires that the originator provide notice to the receiver at least 10 calendar days before the first debit and, for variable amounts, at least 7 calendar days before each subsequent debit. If the amount is fixed and the schedule is regular, the initial authorization may suffice if it clearly discloses the schedule. Reg E requires that the consumer receive notice of the terms of the preauthorized transfer, including the amount and frequency, at the time of authorization.
3. **Stop-payment and revocation rights.** Under Reg E, a consumer may stop payment of a preauthorized electronic fund transfer by notifying the financial institution at least 3 business days before the scheduled date of the transfer. The consumer may also revoke authorization by notifying the originator. NACHA requires that the originator honor a revocation and stop initiating debits promptly. The platform's cancellation process must propagate to Mosaic Relay's operations in time to prevent the next scheduled debit.
4. **Error resolution.** Reg E provides consumers with 60 days from the date of the periodic statement to report errors, including unauthorized electronic fund transfers. The financial institution must investigate and resolve errors within specified timeframes (10 business days for provisional credit, 45 days for final resolution). NACHA return codes R07 (authorization revoked), R10 (customer advises not authorized), and R29 (corporate customer advises not authorized) trigger specific obligations to stop further debits and, in some cases, to return funds.
5. **Retry logic.** NACHA limits the number of times a returned debit may be reinitiated. A debit returned for insufficient funds (R01) may be reinitiated up to two times within 180 days, but a debit returned for authorization revoked (R07) or not authorized (R10, R29) must not be reinitiated without new authorization. Retries may constitute new debits requiring fresh authorization and notice.
6. **Recordkeeping.** NACHA requires the ODFI to retain the authorization (or a record of it) for 2 years in a format that can be accurately reproduced. Reg E requires financial institutions to retain records of electronic fund transfers for at least 2 years. The authorization record format and retention period must be confirmed.
7. **Platform role.** The platform's role (originator, third-party sender, or service provider) affects NACHA obligations and potential money-transmission licensing requirements. If the platform is the originator, it bears primary responsibility for authorization and notice. If Mosaic Relay is a third-party sender, it has additional registration and compliance obligations under NACHA.
## Viable paths
**Path A: Approve pilot with conditions.** Legal approves the 30-day pilot subject to: (1) confirmation that the pilot will include only business accounts (NACHA-only, no Reg E), or (2) if consumer accounts are included, implementation of Reg E-compliant authorization language, stop-payment procedures, and error-resolution processes. This path requires the platform to confirm the consumer/business mix and to implement the required controls before launch.
**Path B: Delay pilot pending fact-gathering.** Legal declines to approve until the platform provides the missing facts: consumer/business mix, fixed versus variable amounts, cancellation propagation process, authorization record format, retry logic, and unauthorized-return handling. This path avoids the risk of unauthorized-debit liability but may miss the 30-day window.
**Path C: Approve pilot for business accounts only.** Legal approves the pilot on the condition that only business accounts are debited, deferring the Reg E/EFTA analysis to a later phase. This path allows the pilot to proceed while reducing regulatory risk, but may not meet the platform's business needs if consumer accounts are expected.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Consumer versus business account mix.** If any consumer accounts are included, Reg E/EFTA applies and requires specific authorization language, stop-payment rights, and error-resolution procedures. If only business accounts are included, NACHA rules alone govern.
- **Fixed versus variable debit amounts.** If amounts are variable, NACHA requires 7-day advance notice before each debit. If fixed, the initial authorization may suffice if it clearly discloses the schedule.
- **Cancellation propagation process.** If the platform's cancellation process does not propagate to Mosaic Relay's operations in time to stop the next scheduled debit, Mosaic Relay may be liable for unauthorized debits.
- **Authorization record format.** If the authorization record is not retained in a reproducible format for 2 years, Mosaic Relay may violate NACHA recordkeeping requirements.
- **Retry logic.** If retries are treated as new debits without fresh authorization and notice, Mosaic Relay may violate NACHA rules and Reg E.
- **Platform role.** If the platform is the originator, it bears primary responsibility for authorization and notice. If Mosaic Relay is a third-party sender, it has additional NACHA registration and compliance obligations.
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Confirm the consumer/business account mix** with the platform before approving the pilot. If consumer accounts are included, require Reg E-compliant authorization language and error-resolution procedures.
2. **Confirm whether debit amounts are fixed or variable** and ensure the authorization and notice processes comply with NACHA advance-notice requirements.
3. **Map the cancellation propagation process** from the user through the platform to Mosaic Relay's operations to ensure that cancellations are honored before the next scheduled debit.
4. **Confirm the authorization record format and retention period** to ensure compliance with NACHA's 2-year recordkeeping requirement.
5. **Define retry logic and unauthorized-return handling** to ensure that returned debits are not reinitiated without fresh authorization and that R07, R10, and R29 return codes trigger immediate cessation of further debits.
6. **Confirm the platform's role** (originator, third-party sender, or service provider) to determine NACHA and money-transmission obligations.
