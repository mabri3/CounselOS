# What are the current NACHA Operating Rules requirements for recurring

## Question

What are the current NACHA Operating Rules requirements for recurring ACH debit authorization, advance notice, and 2-year record retention?

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay Holdings, Inc. plans to support recurring ACH debits for a software platform that collects monthly subscription fees from end users. The proposed user journey involves a user entering bank account details, agreeing to recurring debits, receiving confirmation, and being able to cancel through the platform. Counsel is involved because Product wants to launch a pilot in 30 days, and the legal requirements for authorization, notice, cancellation, returns, and recordkeeping under NACHA Operating Rules and potentially Regulation E remain unconfirmed. The consumer versus business account mix is unresolved, which determines whether EFTA/Reg E protections apply in addition to NACHA rules.
## Decision question
Should legal approve Mosaic Relay’s proposed recurring ACH debit flow for a 30-day pilot, given unresolved questions about consumer vs. business account mix, fixed vs. variable debit amounts, cancellation propagation mechanics, authorization record format, retry logic for returned debits, and the platform’s role in the ACH flow (originator, third-party sender, or service provider)?
## Open questions
- What is the expected mix of consumer versus business bank accounts in the pilot? This determines whether Regulation E/EFTA stop-payment rights, 60-day error resolution, and specific authorization language requirements apply in addition to NACHA Operating Rules.
- Is the monthly subscription debit amount fixed or variable? Variable amounts trigger NACHA’s advance-notice requirement (10 days before the first debit and 7 days before each subsequent debit), while fixed amounts may only require notice of the schedule.
- How does a user’s cancellation request propagate from the platform through Mosaic Relay’s operations to stop future debits, and who owns the contractual and operational obligation to ensure no post-revocation debits occur?
- What is the authorization record format, and does it meet NACHA’s requirement that the ODFI retain the authorization (or a reproducible record) for 2 years from the date of the last debit?
- What role does the platform play in the ACH flow—originator, third-party sender, or service provider—and does Mosaic Relay’s role trigger money-transmission licensing or additional NACHA registration obligations?

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.**
Based on the matter files and general knowledge of the ACH regulatory framework, the following rules and issues are likely controlling:
### NACHA Operating Rules (apply to all ACH debits)
1. **Authorization requirements**: Recurring ACH debits require a valid authorization from the account holder. For consumer accounts, the authorization must be in writing or "similarly authenticated" (electronic signature compliant with E-SIGN). The authorization must clearly identify the account, the amount or method for determining the amount, the timing/frequency, and the account holder's agreement to recurring debits.
2. **Advance notice for variable amounts**: If the debit amount varies, NACHA requires the originator to provide notice of the amount and scheduled date at least 10 calendar days before the first debit and at least 7 calendar days before each subsequent debit. If the amount is fixed, notice of the schedule at authorization may suffice.
3. **Record retention**: The ODFI (originating bank) must retain the original authorization or a reproducible record for 2 years from the date of the last debit. The record must be capable of being reproduced accurately and provided to the RDFI or account holder upon request.
4. **Return and retry rules**: Returned debits (e.g., insufficient funds, closed account, unauthorized) may be reinitiated only under specific conditions. NACHA limits reinitiation to two attempts following the first return, and only if the return was for insufficient funds (R01) or uncollected funds (R09). Returns coded R07 (authorization revoked), R10 (customer advises not authorized), or R29 (corporate customer advises not authorized) must not be reinitiated without new authorization.
5. **Stop payment and revocation**: Account holders may revoke authorization at any time. The originator must have a process to receive and act on revocations promptly. Continued debits after revocation constitute unauthorized transactions and expose the originator and ODFI to return liability and NACHA enforcement.
### Regulation E / EFTA (applies if consumer accounts are involved)
1. **Preauthorized electronic fund transfers**: Recurring ACH debits from consumer accounts are "preauthorized EFTs" under Reg E. The consumer must authorize the transfers in writing or by electronic signature, and a copy of the authorization must be provided to the consumer.
2. **Stop-payment rights**: Consumers may stop payment of a preauthorized EFT by notifying their bank at least 3 business days before the scheduled debit. The bank must honor the stop request.
3. **Error resolution**: Consumers have 60 days from the date of the periodic statement to report unauthorized or incorrect EFTs. The bank must investigate and provisionally credit the account within 10 business days if it cannot resolve the error immediately.
4. **Unauthorized EFT liability**: Consumer liability for unauthorized EFTs is capped at $50 if reported within 2 business days, $500 if reported within 60 days, and unlimited thereafter. Continued debits after revocation are unauthorized.
### Money transmission and licensing
If Mosaic Relay or the platform is deemed to be "receiving money for transmission" or holding funds, state money-transmitter licensing laws may apply. The platform's role (originator, third-party sender, or service provider) and Mosaic Relay's role in the funds flow must be clarified to determine licensing obligations.

## Viable paths
### Path 1: Approve pilot with conditions (recommended if facts align)
Approve the 30-day pilot subject to the following conditions:
- **Confirm account mix**: If any consumer accounts are in scope, require Reg E-compliant authorization language, stop-payment procedures, and error-resolution processes.
- **Confirm debit amount structure**: If amounts are variable, implement the 10-day/7-day advance-notice process. If fixed, document the schedule in the authorization.
- **Document cancellation flow**: Map the cancellation process from user action through platform to Mosaic Relay operations to ODFI, with defined SLAs and a named owner for the stop obligation.
- **Define retry logic**: Prohibit reinitiation of debits returned R07, R10, or R29 without new authorization. Limit reinitiation of R01/R09 returns to two attempts.
- **Confirm authorization record format**: Ensure the authorization record is reproducible and retained for 2 years from the last debit.
- **Clarify platform role**: Confirm whether the platform is the originator, a third-party sender, or a service provider, and whether Mosaic Relay's role triggers money-transmission licensing.
### Path 2: Delay pilot pending fact-gathering
If the consumer/business mix, cancellation flow, or platform role cannot be confirmed within the 30-day window, recommend delaying the pilot until these facts are established. Launching without clarity on Reg E applicability or cancellation mechanics creates material legal and operational risk.
### Path 3: Business-accounts-only pilot
If the platform can restrict the pilot to business accounts only, Reg E/EFTA does not apply, and only NACHA rules govern. This simplifies compliance but requires contractual and operational controls to prevent consumer accounts from entering the flow.

## Facts that could change the answer
1. **Consumer vs. business account mix**: If any consumer accounts are in scope, Reg E/EFTA applies, triggering stop-payment rights, 60-day error resolution, and specific authorization language. If business-only, only NACHA rules apply.
2. **Fixed vs. variable debit amounts**: Variable amounts require 10-day advance notice before the first debit and 7-day notice before each subsequent debit. Fixed amounts may only require notice of the schedule at authorization.
3. **Cancellation propagation mechanics**: If the platform cannot guarantee that a user's cancellation will stop future debits before the next scheduled debit, the flow creates unauthorized-transaction risk and potential Reg E liability.
4. **Platform's role in ACH flow**: If the platform is the originator, it bears NACHA authorization and recordkeeping obligations. If it is a third-party sender, additional NACHA registration and risk-management obligations apply. If Mosaic Relay is the originator or holds funds, money-transmission licensing may be triggered.
5. **Retry logic for returned debits**: If the platform plans to retry debits returned as unauthorized (R07, R10, R29), this violates NACHA rules and creates liability. If retries are limited to insufficient-funds returns (R01, R09) and capped at two attempts, this is permissible.
6. **Authorization record format**: If the authorization is captured electronically but not in a reproducible format (e.g., no audit trail, no timestamp, no IP address), it may not satisfy NACHA's 2-year retention requirement.

## Recommended last-mile verification
Before legal approves the pilot, the following verification steps are recommended:
1. **Obtain and review the proposed authorization screen and flow**: Confirm that the authorization language meets NACHA and (if applicable) Reg E requirements, including clear identification of the account, amount or method for determining amount, frequency, and cancellation rights.
2. **Confirm the consumer/business account mix**: Obtain data or a representation from the platform on the expected account mix. If consumer accounts are in scope, confirm Reg E compliance processes are in place.
3. **Map the cancellation process end-to-end**: Document how a user's cancellation request flows from the platform UI to Mosaic Relay operations to the ODFI, with defined SLAs and a named owner. Confirm that cancellations are processed before the next scheduled debit.
4. **Review retry logic and return-handling procedures**: Confirm that returned debits are coded correctly, that R07/R10/R29 returns are not reinitiated without new authorization, and that R01/R09 retries are capped at two attempts.
5. **Confirm authorization record format and retention**: Verify that the authorization record is reproducible, includes an audit trail (timestamp, IP address, user action), and is retained for 2 years from the last debit.
6. **Clarify the platform's and Mosaic Relay's roles in the ACH flow**: Obtain a funds-flow diagram and confirm whether the platform is the originator, a third-party sender, or a service provider, and whether Mosaic Relay's role triggers money-transmission licensing or NACHA third-party sender registration.
7. **Review the ODFI agreement**: Confirm that the originating bank's agreement with Mosaic Relay or the platform addresses authorization retention, return handling, and liability allocation.
