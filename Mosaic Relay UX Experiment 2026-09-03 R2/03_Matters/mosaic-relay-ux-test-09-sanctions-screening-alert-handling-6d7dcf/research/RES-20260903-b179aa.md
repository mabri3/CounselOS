# Map the sanctions-screening change from manual to vendor-based screening across

## Question

Map the sanctions-screening change from manual to vendor-based screening across operational controls, sanctions compliance, privacy and cross-border data transfer, and recordkeeping. Identify exact screening events, matching rules, manual-review steps, access restrictions, notices, record retention, fallback procedures, and migration-readiness work for a two-month weekend cutover.

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay Holdings, Inc., a B2B payments infrastructure provider, is replacing manual sanctions screening with a vendor solution that returns potential matches and confidence signals. Product proposes automatically blocking exact matches, allowing low-confidence matches to proceed, and sending review cases to an offshore operations team, with a weekend migration planned in two months. Counsel is involved because the change implicates sanctions compliance (OFAC and global lists), cross-border data transfers, recordkeeping, and operational controls that must be validated before cutover. The timeline is aggressive given unresolved controls around evidence standards, escalation authority, and data transfer mechanisms.
## Decision question
Should legal approve the vendor-based sanctions screening design—including auto-blocking exact matches, holding low-confidence matches for two-person review, offshore data transfer, and daily vendor-managed list refresh—and authorize the weekend cutover in two months, or condition approval on completing specific migration-readiness gates?
## Open questions
- What is the defined scope of "selected payouts" that trigger pre-payment screening, and which actor events (onboarding, payout, list refresh) are in scope?
- What evidence standard and documentation will the maker/checker team use to distinguish true vs. false matches, and what is the escalation path when reviewers disagree or are unavailable?
- Which data transfer mechanism (adequacy decision, SCCs, derogation) will govern the offshore transfer of sanctions review data, and has the vendor DPA/sub-processor terms been reviewed?
- How will Mosaic Relay independently verify the vendor's daily list refresh and global/multi-jurisdiction coverage, given conflicting sanctions lists across jurisdictions?
- What customer communications and notice procedures will apply when payouts are held or blocked, and what record retention schedule will govern screening and review records?
---
## Likely rules and issues
**Sanctions compliance (OFAC and global lists):** Mosaic Relay must screen against applicable sanctions lists at onboarding and before selected payouts. The proposed design—auto-blocking exact matches and holding low-confidence matches for human review—aligns with regulatory expectations, but the program must reconcile conflicting or divergent lists across jurisdictions. Daily vendor-managed list refresh requires independent verification to ensure coverage and timeliness.
**Cross-border data transfer:** Sending review cases to an offshore operations team in a different region triggers data transfer restrictions. The transfer mechanism (adequacy decision, SCCs, or derogation) must be finalized, and the vendor DPA/sub-processor terms must be reviewed to ensure compliance with data protection laws.
**Recordkeeping and evidence standards:** The program lacks defined evidence standards for true vs. false match determinations, documentation requirements for maker/checker review, and a record retention schedule for screening and review records. These gaps must be resolved before cutover.
**Operational controls:** The two-person (maker/checker) review process is sound, but escalation authority and procedures for reviewer unavailability must be defined. Fallback to manual screening on vendor outage is appropriate, but the weekend migration timeline is aggressive given unresolved controls.
## Viable paths
1. **Conditional approval with hard gates:** Approve the design conditionally but do not authorize the weekend cutover until all migration-readiness gates are complete. This preserves the decision trail and ensures controls are validated before launch.
2. **Phased rollout:** Migrate a subset of customers first, validate controls, and expand gradually. This reduces risk but may delay the two-month timeline.
3. **Delay cutover:** Postpone the migration until all controls are fully defined and tested. This is the safest option but may not align with business objectives.
## Facts that could change the answer
- **Vendor list coverage:** If the vendor's global/multi-jurisdiction coverage is incomplete or unverified, the program may not meet regulatory expectations.
- **Data transfer mechanism:** If no valid transfer mechanism is available, the offshore review process may need to be redesigned or relocated.
- **Evidence standards:** If the maker/checker team cannot define clear evidence standards, the program may face challenges in distinguishing true vs. false matches.
- **Customer communications:** If notice procedures for held or blocked payouts are not defined, the program may face customer dissatisfaction or regulatory scrutiny.
## Recommended last-mile verification
- **Vendor list refresh:** Independently verify the vendor's daily list refresh and global/multi-jurisdiction coverage.
- **Data transfer mechanism:** Finalize the transfer mechanism (adequacy decision, SCCs, or derogation) and review the vendor DPA/sub-processor terms.
- **Evidence standards:** Define and document the evidence standard for true vs. false match determinations and the escalation path for reviewer disagreements.
- **Customer communications:** Develop notice procedures for held or blocked payouts and ensure they align with regulatory expectations.
- **Record retention:** Establish a record retention schedule for screening and review records.
