# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Sanctions screening program design and migration readiness for a global/multi-jurisdiction vendor-based screening workflow at onboarding and before selected payouts.

## Decision question

Compliance wants to replace manual sanctions screening at onboarding and before selected payouts with a vendor that returns potential matches and confidence signals. Product proposes automatically blocking exact matches, holding funds on low-confidence matches until human review clears them, and sending review cases to an offshore operations team. Legal must approve the exact screening events, matching rules, manual-review steps, access restrictions, notices, record retention, and fallback procedures, and track migration readiness for a weekend cutover in two months. Screening covers global/multi-jurisdiction sanctions lists. Legal answer needed before planned launch.

## Material facts

- Compliance wants to replace manual sanctions screening with a vendor that returns potential matches and confidence signals.
- Screening applies at onboarding and before selected payouts.
- Product proposes automatically blocking exact matches.
- Product proposes allowing low-confidence matches to proceed.
- Product proposes sending review cases to an offshore operations team.
- Product wants to migrate customers over a weekend in two months.
- Known facts include vendor coverage, API response times, and proposed matching thresholds.
- Missing facts include list-update frequency, jurisdictional scope, escalation authority, data transfer locations, evidence standards, customer communications, and procedures for true and false matches.
- For low-confidence matches, Product proposes letting them proceed automatically. What should happen to funds while a low-confidence match is pending? — Hold funds until a human review clears the match
- Which sanctions lists and jurisdictions should the screening program cover? — Global/multi-jurisdiction coverage
- When does the business need the legal answer? — Before a planned launch

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay is a payments infrastructure provider, not a bank, and coordinates regulated partners (per company profile).
- Sanctions screening obligations are driven by OFAC/regulatory expectations and partner (acquiring bank/processor) requirements.

## Issues and workstreams

- Offshore operations team handling review cases raises cross-border data transfer and privacy concerns, acute under global/multi-jurisdiction scope
- Weekend migration in two months may be aggressive given unresolved controls
- No defined escalation authority or evidence standards for true vs false matches
- Global coverage raises list-update frequency and conflicting-list handling questions
- No defined fallback procedure if the screening vendor is unavailable

## Open questions

- Offshore operations team location and whether review data crosses borders
- Escalation authority for true vs false match decisions and release of held payouts
- Fallback procedure if the screening vendor is unavailable
- Vendor list-update frequency and how refresh is verified
- Evidence standards for documenting true vs false match determinations
- Customer communications plan for blocked or held funds
- Record retention schedule for screening and review records
- Where is the offshore operations team located, and does review data cross borders?
- Who has authority to decide a true vs false match and release a held payout?
- What happens if the screening vendor is unavailable?
- How often does the vendor update its sanctions lists, and how is that verified?
