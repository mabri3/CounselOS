# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Sanctions screening alert handling: vendor replaces manual screening; legal must approve screening events, matching rules, manual review, access, notices, retention, and fallback, and track weekend migration readiness.

## Decision question

Compliance wants to replace manual sanctions screening at onboarding and before selected payouts with a vendor that returns potential matches and confidence signals. Product proposes automatically blocking exact matches, allowing low-confidence matches to proceed, and sending review cases to an offshore operations team. Legal must approve the exact screening events, matching rules, manual-review steps, access restrictions, notices, record retention, and fallback procedures, and track migration readiness for a weekend cutover in two months.

## Material facts

- Compliance wants to replace manual sanctions screening with a vendor that returns potential matches and confidence signals.
- Screening applies at onboarding and before selected payouts.
- Product proposes automatically blocking exact matches.
- Product proposes allowing low-confidence matches to proceed.
- Product proposes sending review cases to an offshore operations team.
- Product wants to migrate customers over a weekend in two months.
- Known facts include vendor coverage, API response times, and proposed matching thresholds.
- Missing facts include list-update frequency, jurisdictional scope, escalation authority, data transfer locations, evidence standards, customer communications, and procedures for true and false matches.

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay is a payments infrastructure provider, not a bank, and coordinates regulated partners (per company profile).
- Sanctions screening obligations are driven by OFAC/regulatory expectations and partner (acquiring bank/processor) requirements.

## Issues and workstreams

- Auto-release of low-confidence matches creates sanctions exposure on funds flow before human review
- Offshore operations team handling review cases raises cross-border data transfer and privacy concerns
- Weekend migration in two months may be aggressive given unresolved controls
- No defined escalation authority or evidence standards for true vs false matches

## Open questions

- Jurisdictional scope of the screening program (which sanctions lists and which countries' rules apply)
- List-update frequency and how the vendor's list refresh is verified
- Escalation authority for review cases (who decides true vs false match, and at what threshold)
- Data transfer locations for the offshore operations team and any cross-border privacy implications
- Evidence standards and record retention for match decisions
- Customer communications for blocked or held payouts
- Fallback procedures if the vendor is unavailable
