    ## What I understand you''re asking

    Mosaic Relay wants to combine six data sources — onboarding information, transaction
    history, device signals, sanctions results, dispute records, and payout behavior
    — into a shared risk model that scores fraud/risk to support **onboarding, transaction
    review, payout controls, and account deactivation**. Engineering proposes a shared
    data model and a **ten-week launch**. You''ve asked legal to approve the exact
    **purposes, notices, consent/opt-out steps, retention limits, access controls,
    rights workflows, and model-governance controls** for this program.

    This is a privacy review, and the highest-leverage questions are the ones that
    change which legal regime applies and what obligations attach. I''ve recorded
    the intake state and saved the reported facts.

    ## The questions that matter most, in priority order

    **1. Which jurisdictions will this cover at launch and near term?** This is the
    single most decision-changing fact. If EU/UK individuals are involved, GDPR Art.
    22 automated decision-making and profiling rules apply and reshape the whole design.
    If it''s US-only, the analysis centers on CCPA/CPRA, FCRA-adjacent consumer-reporting
    concerns, and state ADM laws. LATAM adds another layer.

    **2. Does the score drive automated decisions, or does a human review before action?**
    This determines whether automated-decision-making obligations (GDPR Art. 22, CCPA/CPRA
    access/opt-out rights, state ADM laws) attach at all, and whether adverse-action
    notice obligations arise. The answer likely differs per use case — onboarding
    auto-approval vs. account deactivation.

    **3. Do the current vendor contracts and data-source permissions permit combining
    this data into a shared model?** The request says vendor contracts are known,
    but whether they permit this *secondary* combination is the crux of the "permitted
    operational use vs. secondary use" question you flagged.

    **4. For the seven-year retention of risk events, are there deletion exceptions?**
    Seven years of risk data against data-minimization and deletion obligations is
    a live tension — some categories (cleared sanctions hits, resolved disputes) may
    need earlier deletion.

    I''ve saved these as the ordered question set. Answer them (or skip any) and I''ll
    refine the analysis and map the data flows, retention assumptions, and required
    contract/engineering changes.'
