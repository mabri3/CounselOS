## Likely rules / issues
**United States**
- **Fee discount vs. income:** If the credit is applied only against future platform fees and has no cash value, the IRS generally treats it as a reduction in the purchase price (a discount) rather than gross income to the referrer. The referrer simply pays less for services; no 1099 is required for a true discount.
- **Promotional consideration / income:** If the credit is transferable, redeemable for cash, or can be used outside the platform, it may be treated as taxable income to the referrer under IRC § 61. Mosaic Relay would then need to consider 1099‑MISC reporting if the aggregate value to a single referrer exceeds $600 in a calendar year.
- **Deductibility for Mosaic Relay:** The credit is likely a deductible marketing/promotional expense under IRC § 162, but the timing of the deduction depends on when the credit is issued versus when it is redeemed.
- **State tax:** Some states may treat the credit differently for sales/use tax purposes, but because Mosaic Relay provides services (not tangible goods), this is a lower‑risk area.
**Canada**
- **GST/HST:** A non‑cash credit applied against future fees is generally treated as a reduction in the consideration for the supply, not as a separate taxable benefit. Mosaic Relay would reduce the GST/HST collected on the net fee.
- **Income tax:** If the credit is a true discount (no cash value, non‑transferable), it is not income to the referrer under the Income Tax Act. If it has cash‑equivalent value, it could be a taxable benefit or business income.
- **Reporting:** Canada does not have a direct equivalent to the 1099‑MISC for most business‑to‑business promotional credits, but if the credit is treated as income, Mosaic Relay may need to issue a T4A (for individuals) or T5 (for corporations) if the value exceeds $500 in a calendar year.
- **Provincial variations:** Quebec’s QST and other provincial sales taxes generally follow the same “reduction in consideration” approach, but counsel should confirm no provincial‑specific rules apply.
**Cross‑border**
- Because the test is limited to the US and Canada, no withholding or treaty issues are expected for a pure fee discount. If the credit were treated as income, cross‑border withholding could become relevant for Canadian referrers receiving US‑source income, but that is unlikely here.
## Viable paths
1. **Treat as a fee discount (recommended baseline).** Draft terms to state the credit is non‑cash, non‑transferable, not redeemable for cash, and applies only to future platform fees. This supports the “reduction in consideration” position in both jurisdictions and avoids current income recognition for the referrer.
2. **Treat as promotional income with reporting.** If the business wants the credit to be more flexible (e.g., transferable or combinable), Mosaic Relay would need to track aggregate value per referrer and issue 1099‑MISC (US) or T4A/T5 (Canada) when thresholds are met. This adds administrative burden and may deter participation.
3. **Hybrid: discount with a cap.** Keep the credit as a discount but add a low annual cap (e.g., $500) to stay below reporting thresholds even if the IRS or CRA were to recharacterize it. This is a conservative middle ground.

- **Aggregate value per referrer:** The 10‑credit cap means a referrer could receive up to $1,000 in credits. If the IRS or CRA views the credit as income, that exceeds the $600 (US) and $500 (Canada) reporting thresholds.
- **Referred merchant benefit:** If the referred merchant also receives a credit or discount, its own tax treatment (and Mosaic Relay’s reporting) could change.
- **Provincial/state specifics:** Quebec’s QST rules or state‑level sales tax rules could impose additional obligations if the credit is treated as a reduction in consideration for a taxable supply.
- **Existing customer agreements:** If the platform agreement already addresses promotional credits, the new terms must align to avoid conflicting tax treatment.
