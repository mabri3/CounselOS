# CAN-SPAM and CASL requirements for promotional email to business customers

## Question

CAN-SPAM and CASL requirements for promotional email to business customers in the US and Canada

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay Holdings, Inc. plans to launch a three-month referral program in the United States and Canada, offering existing business customers a $100 platform credit when a referred merchant completes onboarding and processes its first eligible payment. Marketing intends to promote the offer through email, social media, and partner channels. Counsel is involved because the email-consent basis for promotional outreach is not yet confirmed, and the program must comply with CAN-SPAM (US) and CASL (Canada) before the requested launch date of 2026-10-03.
## Decision question
Can Mosaic Relay send promotional email about the referral program to existing business customers in the US and Canada starting 2026-10-03, and if so, what consent basis, content, identification, and unsubscribe mechanisms must be in place to satisfy CAN-SPAM and CASL?
## Open questions
- What is the actual email-consent status for existing business customers in the US and Canada? The matter record states consent is "not yet confirmed" and assumes "transactional/service consent only until Marketing and Compliance produce opt-in records."
- Do any recipients fall under CASL's implied-consent categories (e.g., existing business relationship, conspicuously published address), or is express consent required for all Canadian recipients?
- Will any promotional emails be sent by marketing partners on Mosaic Relay's behalf, and if so, who is the "sender" for CAN-SPAM/CASL compliance purposes?
- Are there any regional exclusions within the US or Canada (e.g., Quebec's additional language or privacy requirements) that would further restrict the email campaign?

## Likely rules and issues
### United States — CAN-SPAM Act (15 U.S.C. §§ 7701–7713)
**No external authority retrieved.** The supplied public research explicitly states that the CAN-SPAM Act is not in the assembled context and declines to reconstruct it from memory. The following is generated analysis based on general legal knowledge, not retrieved authority.
CAN-SPAM applies to "commercial electronic mail messages" — email whose primary purpose is commercial advertisement or promotion. A referral-program offer to existing business customers is almost certainly a commercial message. Key requirements likely include:
1. **No false or misleading header information** — the "From," "To," and routing information must be accurate and identify Mosaic Relay (or the partner, if partner-sent).
2. **No deceptive subject lines** — the subject must accurately reflect the content.
3. **Identification as an advertisement** — the message must clearly and conspicuously disclose that it is an advertisement, unless the recipient has given prior affirmative consent (opt-in). If Mosaic Relay has only transactional/service consent, this disclosure is required.
4. **Valid physical postal address** — the message must include Mosaic Relay's current street address, a PO box, or a private mailbox.
5. **Opt-out mechanism** — a clear, functioning unsubscribe link or reply mechanism that remains active for at least 30 days after sending. Opt-out requests must be honored within 10 business days.
6. **No sale or transfer of opted-out addresses** — once a recipient opts out, their address cannot be sold or transferred (except to a compliance vendor).
7. **Monitoring of third parties** — Mosaic Relay cannot contract away responsibility; it must monitor partners and vendors sending on its behalf.
**Business-to-business nuance:** CAN-SPAM does not exempt B2B email. The FTC has declined to create a B2B carve-out, so all requirements apply equally to messages sent to business addresses.
### Canada — CASL (S.C. 2010, c. 23)
**No external authority retrieved.** The supplied public research explicitly states that CASL is not in the assembled context and declines to reconstruct it from memory. The following is generated analysis based on general legal knowledge, not retrieved authority.
CASL is significantly stricter than CAN-SPAM. It applies to "commercial electronic messages" (CEMs) sent to or from Canadian computer systems. A referral-program promotion is a CEM. Key requirements likely include:
1. **Consent requirement** — CASL is opt-in. The sender must have either:
- **Express consent** — the recipient affirmatively agreed to receive CEMs (oral or written, with specific disclosure requirements), or
- **Implied consent** — which exists in limited categories, most relevantly:
- **Existing business relationship** — the recipient has purchased a product or service from Mosaic Relay within the past 24 months, or made an inquiry within the past 6 months.
- **Conspicuously published address** — the recipient's email is publicly available and relevant to their business role, and the message is relevant to that role (narrow and fact-specific).
2. **Identification requirements** — the message must identify the sender (and, if sent on behalf of another, that party), and provide contact information (mailing address and either phone, email, or web address) valid for at least 60 days.
3. **Unsubscribe mechanism** — a clear, no-cost, easily performed unsubscribe mechanism that remains functional for at least 60 days. Unsubscribe requests must be honored within 10 business days.
4. **Burden of proof** — the sender bears the burden of proving consent. Mosaic Relay must maintain records demonstrating the consent basis for each Canadian recipient.
**Critical gap:** The matter record states consent status is "not yet confirmed" and assumes "transactional/service consent only." Transactional consent (e.g., a customer agreed to receive service-related emails) is **not** sufficient under CASL for promotional messages. Mosaic Relay must establish either express consent or a valid implied-consent category (most likely existing business relationship) before sending promotional email to Canadian recipients.

## Viable paths
### Path 1: Launch on schedule with confirmed consent (recommended if consent records exist)
**Condition:** Marketing and Compliance produce documented opt-in or implied-consent records for all US and Canadian recipients before 2026-10-03.
**Actions:**
- **US recipients:** Confirm that recipients have not opted out. If recipients have only transactional consent, include clear advertisement identification in the email. Ensure accurate header information, non-deceptive subject line, valid postal address, and functioning unsubscribe mechanism.
- **Canadian recipients:** Confirm either express consent or implied consent via existing business relationship (purchase within 24 months or inquiry within 6 months). Maintain proof-of-consent records for each recipient. Ensure sender identification, contact information, and unsubscribe mechanism meet CASL requirements.
- **Partner-sent email:** If marketing partners send promotional email on Mosaic Relay's behalf, ensure the partner agreement assigns compliance responsibility, requires the partner to identify Mosaic Relay, and obligates the partner to honor opt-outs and maintain records. Mosaic Relay remains liable under both CAN-SPAM and CASL for messages sent on its behalf.
- **Suppression list:** Implement a shared suppression list across all channels and partners to ensure opted-out recipients are not re-contacted.
**Timeline:** Achievable by 2026-10-03 if consent records are produced and verified in the next two weeks.
### Path 2: Delay launch until consent is confirmed
**Condition:** Marketing and Compliance cannot produce consent records by 2026-10-03.
**Actions:**
- Delay the email component of the campaign. Launch social media and partner-channel components (which have different consent dynamics) on schedule.
- Use the delay period to collect express consent from existing customers via a transactional email or in-product prompt (e.g., "Would you like to hear about special offers and programs?").
- Relaunch email outreach once consent is documented.
**Timeline:** Email launch delayed by 2–4 weeks depending on consent-collection response rate.
### Path 3: Limit email to transactional/service messages only
**Condition:** Consent cannot be confirmed, but Product wants to inform existing customers about the referral program.
**Actions:**
- Send a transactional or service message (e.g., "New feature: Referral program now available in your dashboard") rather than a promotional message. Transactional messages are exempt from CAN-SPAM's advertisement-identification requirement and may fall outside CASL's CEM definition if the primary purpose is informational rather than promotional.
- **Risk:** The line between transactional and promotional is fact-specific. If the message emphasizes the $100 credit and encourages participation, it is likely promotional. This path requires careful copy review and may still require CASL consent if the message is deemed a CEM.
**Timeline:** Achievable by 2026-10-03 with careful copy drafting.
### Path 4: Exclude Canada from the email campaign
**Condition:** CASL consent cannot be confirmed, but Product wants to proceed with US email outreach.
**Actions:**
- Launch email campaign in the US only. Use social media, partner channels, and in-product notifications for Canadian customers.
- **Trade-off:** Reduces the addressable audience for the three-month test and may skew results if Canadian customers are a material segment.
**Timeline:** Achievable by 2026-10-03.

## Facts that could change the answer
1. **Consent records exist but have not been produced.** If Marketing and Compliance can produce documented opt-in records (express consent) or evidence of existing business relationships (implied consent under CASL), Path 1 becomes viable and the launch can proceed on schedule.
2. **The referral credit is recharacterized as a consumer credit product.** If the $100 platform credit is deemed a consumer credit or charge card solicitation (unlikely on current facts, but not impossible if end users are consumers), Regulation Z's application-and-solicitation disclosure rules would attach, adding a layer of complexity. The supplied public research flags this as unresolved.
3. **Marketing partners send email on Mosaic Relay's behalf.** If partners send promotional email, Mosaic Relay remains liable under CAN-SPAM and CASL. The partner agreement must assign compliance responsibility, require identification of Mosaic Relay, and obligate the partner to honor opt-outs. If partners send to their own lists without Mosaic Relay's involvement, the analysis changes.
4. **Quebec recipients are included.** If the campaign includes Quebec, additional French-language requirements under the Charter of the French Language and Quebec's Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector may apply. The matter record does not specify whether Quebec is in scope.
5. **The email is deemed transactional rather than promotional.** If the email copy is carefully drafted to emphasize the informational aspect (e.g., "New feature available") rather than the promotional aspect (e.g., "Earn $100"), it may fall outside CAN-SPAM's advertisement-identification requirement and CASL's CEM definition. This is a fact-specific determination that requires copy review.

## What would change this
- **Working assumption:** The analysis assumes that "transactional/service consent only" means recipients agreed to receive service-related emails but did not affirmatively opt in to promotional messages. If recipients actually provided broader consent (e.g., "I agree to receive marketing communications"), the CAN-SPAM advertisement-identification requirement may not apply, and CASL express consent may be satisfied. The answer would shift from "confirm consent before launch" to "verify consent records and proceed."
- **Open fork:** Whether Canadian recipients have implied consent via existing business relationship (purchase within 24 months or inquiry within 6 months) or require express consent. If implied consent exists, the launch can proceed with proper documentation. If not, Mosaic Relay must either collect express consent (delaying launch) or exclude Canada from the email campaign.
- **Not examined:** The specific email copy and templates. The distinction between transactional and promotional messages is fact-specific and depends on the primary purpose of the email. If the copy emphasizes the informational aspect ("New feature available") rather than the promotional aspect ("Earn $100"), it may fall outside CAN-SPAM's advertisement-identification requirement and CASL's CEM definition. Counsel should review the actual copy before launch.
- **Not examined:** Whether Quebec is in scope. The matter record states the test is in "the United States and Canada" but does not specify whether Quebec is included. If Quebec is in scope, additional French-language and privacy requirements may apply.
- **Not examined:** The supplied public research packet contains Regulation Z materials addressed to consumer-credit disclosures. The packet flags as unresolved whether these provisions reach promotional email sent to business customers. On current facts, the $100 platform credit is a B2B referral reward, not a consumer credit product, so Regulation Z likely does not apply. However, if the credit is recharacterized or if end users are consumers, this analysis would change.
