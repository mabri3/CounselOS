# State and provincial advertising/promotion disclosure requirements for referral

## Question

State and provincial advertising/promotion disclosure requirements for referral offers

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay Holdings, Inc. (a fictional B2B payments infrastructure provider) wants to launch a three-month referral test in the United States and Canada on 2026-10-03. Existing business customers would receive a $100 non-cash platform credit when a referred merchant completes onboarding and settles its first eligible payment. Marketing plans to promote the offer through email, social media, and partner channels, with partners compensated at a flat $25 per verified successful referral. Counsel is involved because the campaign implicates advertising disclosure rules, email-consent requirements (CAN-SPAM/CASL), tax characterization, and platform-risk controls before the launch date.
## Decision question
Should legal approve the referral program’s current terms—$100 non-credit, 90-day expiration, 10-credit cap, new-merchant-only eligibility, and flat-fee partner compensation—for a US/Canada launch on 2026-10-03, or must the program be modified (e.g., delayed, restructured, or re-scoped) to comply with state/provincial advertising disclosure, email-consent, and tax requirements?
## Open questions
- **Email-consent basis**: Marketing has not confirmed whether US and Canadian recipients have opted in to promotional messages; without documented consent, CASL (Canada) and CAN-SPAM (US) requirements may block email as a channel.
- **Tax treatment**: The $100 credit’s characterization (discount vs. taxable income to the referrer) remains undetermined and requires tax counsel review before launch.
- **Quebec inclusion**: It is unclear whether “Canada” includes Quebec, which has distinct French-language and consumer-protection disclosure rules that could require separate creative or exclusion.
- **Partner agreement execution**: No signed partner agreements currently exist; the flat-fee structure and compliance obligations need to be papered before partners begin promotion.
- **“Eligible payment” definition**: The specific transaction types or thresholds that trigger the credit are undefined, creating ambiguity in the eligibility trigger.

## Likely rules and issues
**Advertising and promotion disclosures**
US state UDAP laws (e.g., California Business & Professions Code § 17500, New York General Business Law § 349) prohibit deceptive acts and practices in commerce. For referral offers, this requires clear and conspicuous disclosure of material terms—eligibility restrictions, expiration dates, caps, and non-cash status—adjacent to the call to action, not buried in hyperlinked terms. The FTC Act § 5 and state “Little FTC Acts” apply the same standard to B2B offers where the referrer is a small business. In Canada, the federal Competition Act (ss. 74.01–74.06) prohibits false or misleading representations and requires disclosure of material conditions; Quebec’s Consumer Protection Act adds French-language requirements and specific rules for promotional contests or credits if Quebec is in scope.
**Email marketing consent**
CAN-SPAM (US) permits commercial email to business addresses without prior opt-in but requires accurate header information, non-deceptive subject lines, identification as an ad, and a functioning opt-out mechanism honored within 10 business days. CASL (Canada) is stricter: it generally requires express opt-in consent for commercial electronic messages (CEMs) unless an exception applies (e.g., existing business relationship with conspicuous publication of the address). Sending promotional referral emails to Canadian customers without documented express consent creates regulatory risk.
**Tax characterization**
The $100 platform credit is likely a reduction in future fees (a discount) rather than cash income, but if the credit is transferable or redeemable for cash equivalents, it could constitute taxable income to the referrer under IRC § 61 or trigger 1099-MISC reporting obligations if annual credits exceed $600. Canadian tax treatment may differ; GST/HST implications for the credit’s value should be confirmed.
**Partner marketing and FTC endorsement rules**
Paying partners a flat $25 per successful referral creates a material connection that must be disclosed under FTC Endorsement Guides (16 C.F.R. Part 255) and Canadian Competition Bureau guidelines. Partner posts must include clear disclosure (e.g., “#ad” or “Sponsored”) adjacent to the referral claim, not only in a bio or footer.
**Platform risk and fraud**
Self-referrals, synthetic merchant accounts, and duplicate referrals are foreseeable abuse vectors. The program terms must explicitly prohibit self-referrals, employee/sales-team participation, and referrals of existing merchants, with clawback rights for credits issued on prohibited referrals.

## Viable paths
**Path A: Launch on 2026-10-03 with modified scope**
Proceed with the three-month test but limit initial promotion to non-email channels (social media, in-product notifications, and partner channels) until email-consent records are verified. Exclude Quebec to avoid French-language and provincial consumer-protection complexity. Require tax counsel sign-off on credit characterization before launch. This preserves the timeline but narrows the channel mix.
**Path B: Delay launch to cure consent and tax gaps**
Postpone launch until Marketing produces documented opt-in records for US and Canadian email lists and tax counsel confirms the credit’s treatment. This ensures full channel availability and regulatory certainty but misses the 2026-10-03 target.
**Path C: Launch with email in the US only**
Proceed with email promotion to US customers under CAN-SPAM (which does not require opt-in for B2B) while suppressing all Canadian email addresses until CASL consent is documented. This allows immediate email testing in the US market while respecting Canadian consent requirements.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Quebec inclusion**: If the test includes Quebec, French-language disclosures and Quebec Consumer Protection Act compliance become mandatory, potentially requiring separate creative and terms.
- **Email list provenance**: If Marketing can demonstrate that Canadian recipients expressly opted in to promotional messages within the last 24 months, CASL concerns are mitigated; if not, email must be excluded for Canada.
- **Credit transferability**: If the $100 credit is transferable or redeemable for cash (rather than solely applicable to future platform fees), it likely constitutes taxable income to the referrer, requiring 1099-MISC reporting and potentially changing the reward structure.
- **Partner channel scope**: If partners include influencers or affiliates with consumer audiences (rather than B2B marketing agencies), FTC endorsement disclosure requirements become more stringent.
- **“Eligible payment” definition**: If the first eligible payment is defined as any transaction (including $0.01 test transactions), the fraud risk increases; if it requires a minimum settlement amount (e.g., $50), the economics and eligibility clarity change.

## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Confirm email-consent status**: Obtain from Marketing/Compliance a certification of opt-in status for US and Canadian lists, or implement a suppression list for Canadian addresses until CASL consent is documented.
2. **Tax counsel review**: Have tax counsel confirm whether the $100 credit is a non-taxable discount or taxable income, and whether 1099-MISC reporting applies for referrers earning >$600 in credits annually.
3. **State/provincial survey**: Verify whether any specific state (e.g., California, New York) or province (e.g., Quebec) has unique referral-program or credit-expiration disclosure requirements not covered by the general UDAP/Competition Act analysis.
4. **Partner agreement execution**: Ensure all marketing partners sign the written agreement incorporating the flat-fee structure, disclosure obligations, and clawback rights before any partner promotion begins.
5. **Terms finalization**: Confirm that the draft terms explicitly define “eligible payment,” prohibit self-referrals and employee participation, state the 90-day expiration clearly at sign-up and issuance, and include the 10-credit cap.
