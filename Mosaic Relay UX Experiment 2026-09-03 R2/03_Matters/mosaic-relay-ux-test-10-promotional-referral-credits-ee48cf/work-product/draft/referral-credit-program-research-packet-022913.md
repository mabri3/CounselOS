      **Matter:** Mosaic Relay UX Test — 10 — Promotional Referral Credits

      ## Program parameters (from the dossier)


      - **Reward:** Non-cash $100 Mosaic Relay platform credit, applied only to the
      referrer''s future platform fees.

      - **Eligibility trigger:** Referred merchant completes onboarding and settles
      its first eligible payment.

      - **Referrer eligibility:** Existing Mosaic Relay business customers only; employees/sales
      teams excluded; self-referrals prohibited.

      - **Referred merchant:** Must be new to Mosaic Relay.

      - **Credit terms:** One credit per referred merchant; not cash-redeemable, transferable,
      or combinable; expires 90 days after issuance; cap of 10 credits per referrer
      during the test.

      - **Channels:** Email, social media, partner channels.

      - **Partners:** Written agreement; flat $25 fee per verified successful referral;
      no payment for leads/self-referrals/duplicates/prohibited channels; approved
      copy; material-connection disclosure; clawback/holdback for abuse.

      - **Disclosure:** Short-form disclosure adjacent to every CTA; full terms linked
      and shown before sign-up and at issuance; partner material-connection disclosure
      next to the claim.

      - **Scope:** US and Canada only; no other regions approved.

      ## 1. Advertising and promotion rules (US and Canada)


      **Research question:** What federal/state (US) and federal/provincial (Canada)
      advertising and promotion rules apply to a referral offer promoted via email,
      social, and partner channels?


      ### Authorities

      - **FTC Act § 5 — unfair or deceptive acts or practices (US)** — [UNVERIFIED].
      Governs deceptive advertising; a referral offer must not misrepresent eligibility,
      value, or terms.

      - **FTC Guides Concerning Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising
      (16 CFR Part 255)** — [UNVERIFIED]. Governs material-connection disclosure for
      endorsements, including affiliate/partner promotions.

      - **Competition Act (Canada), false or misleading representations (ss. 52, 74.01)**
      — [UNVERIFIED]. Prohibits materially false or misleading representations to
      the public; applies to promotional claims.

      - **Provincial consumer-protection statutes (e.g., Ontario CPA, Quebec CPA)**
      — [UNVERIFIED]. May impose additional disclosure and promotional-competition
      requirements.

      - **State advertising statutes (US)** — [UNVERIFIED]. State UDAP statutes may
      impose additional requirements beyond the FTC Act.


      ### Key considerations

      - The short-form disclosure adjacent to every CTA is the right structural approach;
      the substance must be accurate and not misleading.

      - "First eligible payment" must be defined precisely so the claim is not misleading
      about when the credit is earned.

      - Quebec''s promotional-competition rules and French-language requirements may
      apply if any Quebec residents are targeted — confirm whether Quebec is in scope.


      ---


      ## 2. Privacy and email-consent basis (CAN-SPAM / CASL)


      **Research question:** What consent and disclosure obligations apply to promotional
      email to business customers in the US and Canada?


      ### Authorities

      - **CAN-SPAM Act (US, 15 U.S.C. § 7701 et seq.)** — [UNVERIFIED]. Applies to
      commercial email; requires accurate header/subject, a working unsubscribe mechanism,
      physical postal address, and opt-out honoring. Applies to B2B email; no prior
      opt-in required, but opt-out must be honored.

      - **CASL (Canada, S.C. 2010, c. 23)** — [UNVERIFIED]. Requires express or implied
      consent for commercial electronic messages (CEMs); requires identification and
      unsubscribe mechanisms; applies to B2B email. Implied consent can arise from
      an existing business relationship, but promotional outreach needs a documented
      basis.

      - **PIPEDA (Canada)** — [UNVERIFIED]. Governs collection, use, and disclosure
      of personal information; relevant to any customer-data handling in the campaign.


      ### Key considerations

      - **Consent status is not yet confirmed.** The dossier assumes transactional/service
      consent only until Marketing/Compliance produce opt-in records. **No promotional
      email should be sent to US or Canadian recipients without a documented basis.**

      - For Canada, CASL requires a documented consent basis (express or implied)
      and proof-of-consent records; suppression lists and unsubscribe handling are
      mandatory.

      - For the US, CAN-SPAM does not require prior opt-in but requires a functioning
      unsubscribe mechanism and honoring opt-outs within 10 business days.

      - This is a **pre-launch blocker**: the campaign cannot proceed on promotional
      email until the consent basis is documented.


      ---


      ## 3. Tax treatment of the platform credit


      **Research question:** How is the non-cash $100 platform credit (applied to
      future platform fees) treated for tax purposes for the referrer and the referred
      merchant?


      ### Authorities

      - **Internal Revenue Code — gross income (US, § 61)** — [UNVERIFIED]. Whether
      the credit is income to the referrer depends on whether it is a rebate/discount
      (reduces cost) or a payment for services (income).

      - **IRS treatment of rebates and discounts** — [UNVERIFIED]. A discount on goods/services
      the recipient purchases is generally not gross income; a payment for referring
      a customer may be income.

      - **Canada Revenue Agency — business income and promotional credits** — [UNVERIFIED].
      Similar characterization question under the Income Tax Act (Canada).

      - **Form 1099-MISC / 1099-NEC reporting (US)** — [UNVERIFIED]. Whether the credit
      or the partner fee triggers information reporting.


      ### Key considerations

      - **Tax treatment is undetermined and flagged for tax counsel review.** This
      is a pre-launch item.

      - The characterization turns on whether the credit is a **discount on the referrer''s
      own fees** (likely not income) versus **compensation for the referral service**
      (potentially income). The $25 partner fee is more clearly compensation for services.

      - The referred merchant receives no cash or credit under the current design,
      so the tax question centers on the referrer and the partner.

      - Information-reporting obligations (1099s) for the partner fee should be confirmed.


      ---


      ## 4. Partner disclosure obligations


      **Research question:** What disclosure obligations apply to marketing partners
      promoting the referral offer, and where must disclosures appear?


      ### Authorities

      - **FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255)** — [UNVERIFIED]. A partner with
      a material connection (the $25 fee) must disclose that connection clearly and
      conspicuously, adjacent to the endorsement/claim — not buried in a footer.

      - **FTC Act § 5** — [UNVERIFIED]. Deceptive-practice exposure for undisclosed
      material connections.

      - **Competition Act (Canada), false/misleading representations** — [UNVERIFIED].
      Applies to partner claims; partners must not make false or misleading representations.

      - **Advertising Standards Canada / Ad Standards** — [UNVERIFIED]. Industry self-regulatory
      guidance on disclosure.


      ### Key considerations

      - The dossier already requires the material-connection disclosure **next to
      the claim, not only in a footer** — this aligns with FTC guidance.

      - Partners must use approved copy and follow US/Canada marketing and privacy
      rules; the written partner agreement should make these obligations enforceable.

      - Partner posts on social media must carry the disclosure in the same medium
      where the claim appears.


      ---


      ## 5. Platform and fraud risk


      **Research question:** What fraud and abuse controls should govern the referral
      program, and what records should be kept?


      ### Authorities / frameworks

      - **Self-referral and synthetic-referral abuse** — [UNVERIFIED]. Controls should
      detect referrers referring their own entities or creating synthetic merchants
      to earn credits.

      - **Duplicate-referral detection** — [UNVERIFIED]. One credit per referred merchant;
      systems must prevent double-counting.

      - **Clawback / holdback mechanisms** — [UNVERIFIED]. The dossier requires clawback
      or payment holdback for abuse; terms must reserve the right to reverse credits/fees.

      - **Campaign record-keeping** — [UNVERIFIED]. Records of referrals, verification,
      credits issued, and partner payments support audit and dispute resolution.


      ### Key considerations

      - The dossier''s controls (new-merchant requirement, verified onboarding + first
      eligible payment, no self/employee/sales referrals, 10-credit cap, clawback/holdback)
      are the right baseline.

      - Verification of "first eligible payment" must be defined to prevent gaming
      (e.g., minimum payment amount, settled not just authorized).

      - Fraud-response terms should be in the customer terms and the partner agreement,
      with documented triggers and review paths per Mosaic Relay''s risk posture.


      ---


      ## Key assumptions


      1. **US and Canada only** — the program is limited to these regions for the
      three-month test; no other regions are approved. If Quebec is targeted, additional
      provincial rules may apply.

      2. **Consent basis is transactional/service only** until Marketing/Compliance
      produce opt-in records; promotional email must not be sent without a documented
      basis.

      3. **The credit is a non-cash platform-fee credit**, not a deposit, cash, or
      bank product — consistent with Mosaic Relay''s non-bank positioning.

      4. **Tax treatment is undetermined** and requires tax counsel; the characterization
      (discount vs. income) is unresolved.

      5. **Draft terms and fraud controls exist** but have not been uploaded to the
      matter; legal approval should be of the exact language, not the summary.


      ## Open questions


      1. **Email-consent opt-in records** — do they exist, and what is the documented
      basis (express vs. implied) for US and Canadian promotional email? (Pre-launch
      blocker.)

      2. **Tax treatment** — is the credit a fee discount or income to the referrer,
      and does the $25 partner fee trigger information reporting? (Pre-launch, pending
      tax counsel.)

      3. **Quebec scope** — are any Quebec residents targeted, triggering provincial
      promotional-competition and French-language rules?

      4. **"First eligible payment" definition** — what minimum amount/type of payment
      qualifies, to prevent gaming?

      5. **Draft terms and fraud-control documents** — need to be uploaded for legal
      review of exact language.


      ## Recommendation path (recommendations only — not decisions)


      1. **Block promotional email** until Marketing/Compliance document the consent
      basis and produce opt-in records (CAN-SPAM/CASL compliance).

      2. **Refer tax characterization to tax counsel** before launch; confirm 1099
      reporting for the partner fee.

      3. **Define "first eligible payment"** precisely in the terms to prevent gaming
      and support the disclosure claim.

      4. **Confirm Quebec scope** and apply provincial rules if targeted.

      5. **Upload draft terms and fraud-control documents** so legal can approve exact
      language, not the summary.

      6. **Finalize the short-form disclosure** and confirm it appears adjacent to
      every CTA and at sign-up/issuance, with full terms linked.

      7. **Finalize the partner agreement** with enforceable disclosure, approved-copy,
      privacy, record-keeping, and clawback obligations.

## Recommendation
**Approve the program as structured, subject to the three launch conditions in "Unresolved blockers" below.** The program is well-scoped: US/Canada only, non-cash platform-fee credit, written partner terms, and documented fraud controls. The two legal blockers (email-consent basis and tax characterization) and one documentation item (exact terms/fraud-control language) must land before launch. This recommendation is separate from any decision; the lawyer's approval is the decision.

## Approved terms
- **Eligibility:** Existing Mosaic Relay business customers only. Referred merchant must be new to Mosaic Relay. Reward triggers only after the referred merchant completes onboarding and its first eligible payment settles.
- **Exclusions:** No self-referrals, no employee or sales-team referrals, no duplicate referrals.
- **Scope:** **Quebec is excluded from this three-month test unless Product and Legal approve a Quebec-specific variant.** No Quebec residents may be targeted, and no Quebec-specific promotional-competition or French-language requirements apply, until such a variant is approved.
- **Reward:** One non-cash $100 platform-fee credit per verified successful referral, applied only to the referrer's future platform fees. Not redeemable for cash, not transferable, not combinable with other promotions.
- **Expiration:** Credit expires 90 days after issuance; stated clearly at sign-up and at issuance.
- **Cap:** Maximum 10 successful credits per referrer during the test.

## Disclosure copy
**Short-form disclosure (adjacent to every call to action in email, social, and partner creative):**
> Existing Mosaic Relay business customers only; referred merchant must be new; onboarding and first eligible payment must settle; one non-cash $100 platform-fee credit, not transferable or combinable, expires 90 days after issue; maximum 10 credits per referrer; no self-referrals or employee/sales referrals.
- Link the full terms from each placement.
- Show full eligibility and expiration terms before sign-up and repeat them when the credit is issued.
- Partner posts must show the material-connection disclosure next to the claim, not only in a footer.

## Consent basis
- **US (CAN-SPAM):** No promotional email without a documented basis; include accurate header info, a clear opt-out, and honor opt-outs promptly.
- **Canada (CASL):** Promotional email requires consent (express or implied) and a working unsubscribe mechanism; maintain proof-of-consent records.
- **Status:** Consent basis is **not yet confirmed**. Assume transactional/service consent only until Marketing/Compliance produce opt-in records. **No promotional email may launch until consent evidence is attached** — do not send promotional email to US or Canadian recipients without a documented basis.

## Partner requirements
- Written partner agreement with a **flat $25 fee per verified successful referral** (new merchant, completed onboarding, first eligible payment settled).
- No payment for leads, self-referrals, duplicate referrals, or prohibited channels.
- Partners must use approved copy, disclose the material connection, follow US/Canada marketing and privacy rules, avoid customer-data sharing outside approved flows, keep campaign records, cooperate with fraud reviews, and accept clawback or payment holdback for abuse.

## Reward restrictions
- Non-cash $100 platform-fee credit; not cash, not transferable, not combinable.
- 90-day expiration; 10-credit cap per referrer.
- No self-referrals or employee/sales referrals.

## Fraud responses
- Detect and reject self-referrals and synthetic referrals.
- Verify each referred merchant is new and that onboarding and first eligible payment actually settle before issuing credit.
- Clawback or hold payment for abuse; partners cooperate with fraud reviews.
- Document fraud-review triggers and outcomes.

## Monitoring records
- Campaign records: eligibility checks, credit issuance, expiration, cap tracking.
- Consent proof: opt-in records for promotional email (US/Canada).
- Partner records: agreements, campaign records, fraud-review cooperation.
- Retention per applicable US/Canada privacy and record-keeping rules.
