# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Marketing at Mosaic Relay Holdings, Inc. — a fictional payments-infrastructure provider that is not a bank and does not hold deposits — wants to advertise "bank-level security" and "institution-grade protection" across the website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search ads. Counsel is involved because the phrases risk implying bank status or fund protection the company cannot support, and because comparative security claims require substantiation under FTC Act §5 and state UDAP law. The campaign targets launch on 2026-10-03, leaving roughly one month to complete a substantiation file and approve final wording.

## Decision question

Should legal approve "bank-level security" and "institution-grade protection" for the 2026-10-03 launch across website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search — and if so, with what mandatory qualifiers, substantiation evidence, review controls, and prohibited language — given that Mosaic Relay is not a bank and does not hold customer funds?

## Material facts

- Marketing wants to advertise Mosaic Relay as providing 'bank-level security' and 'institution-grade protection' for online businesses.
- The campaign would appear on the website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search ads.
- The campaign is planned for launch next month (target date 2026-10-03).
- Mosaic Relay is a payments-infrastructure provider, not a bank, and does not hold deposits or operate as a bank.
- Known facts include current encryption, monitoring, incident response, and certification claims.
- Actors include Marketing, Sales, Product, Security, Compliance, customers, and prospective customers.

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay's existing encryption, monitoring, incident response, and certification claims are accurate and current as represented
- The campaign is primarily targeted at US audiences unless geographic scope is expanded
- No prior legal approval or approved wording exists for these phrases

## Issues and workstreams

- Whether 'bank-level security' / 'institution-grade protection' is substantiated by competent and reliable evidence (FTC Act s.5 / state UDAP) for the specific audience, given Mosaic Relay is not a bank
- Whether the security-strength comparison reads to the audience as a security claim rather than an implication of bank status or fund custody (perception risk even though intent is a comparison)
- Whether the claim's permissibility varies by geographic audience (US vs. selected LatAm and European markets)
- Whether use of customer logos and comparisons with competitors raise endorsement and comparative-advertising issues
- Whether limitations of coverage (what 'bank-level' does and does not cover) must be disclosed

## Open questions

- What specific substantiating evidence does Mosaic Relay currently hold (encryption standards, monitoring scope, incident-response metrics, certifications), and does it meet or exceed the security baseline of actual banks?
- What is the primary geographic audience — US only, or does the campaign reach EU/UK/LatAm markets where local advertising rules differ?
- Will the campaign use customer logos or name competitors, and if so, is there documented consent and substantiation for any comparative claim?
- What limitations of coverage exist (what "bank-level" does and does not protect) that would need disclosure to avoid a misleading omission?
- Do acquiring-bank or processor contracts restrict or prescribe security-related marketing language?
