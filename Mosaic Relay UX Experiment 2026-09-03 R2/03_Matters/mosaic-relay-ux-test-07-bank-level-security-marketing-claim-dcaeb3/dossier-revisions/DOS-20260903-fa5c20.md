# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay Holdings, Inc., a payments-infrastructure provider (not a bank), wants to launch a marketing campaign next month (target 2026-10-03) claiming it provides "bank-level security" and "institution-grade protection." The campaign would run across the website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search ads. Counsel is involved because the company is not a bank, does not hold deposits, and the phrases could imply bank status or fund custody while requiring substantiation under FTC Act § 5 and state UDAP laws. The business team has indicated the intent is purely a security-strength comparison, but the audience perception and evidentiary support remain unverified.

## Decision question

Should legal approve the specific phrases "bank-level security" and "institution-grade protection" for use across website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search ads launching 2026-10-03, and if so, what exact qualifiers, substantiation evidence, review controls, and prohibited language must accompany them to avoid deceptive-practices exposure given that Mosaic Relay is not a bank?

## Material facts

- Marketing wants to advertise Mosaic Relay as providing 'bank-level security' and 'institution-grade protection' for online businesses.
- The campaign would appear on the website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search ads.
- The campaign is planned for launch next month (target date 2026-10-03).
- Mosaic Relay is a payments-infrastructure provider, not a bank, and does not hold deposits or operate as a bank.

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay's existing encryption, monitoring, incident response, and certification claims are accurate and current as represented
- The campaign is primarily targeted at US audiences unless geographic scope is expanded
- No prior legal approval or approved wording exists for these phrases

## Issues and workstreams

- Whether 'bank-level security' / 'institution-grade protection' is substantiated by competent and reliable evidence (FTC Act s.5 / state UDAP) for the specific audience, given Mosaic Relay is not a bank
- Whether the security-strength comparison reads to the audience as a security claim rather than an implication of bank status or fund custody (perception risk even though intent is a comparison)
- Whether the claim's permissibility varies by geographic audience (US vs. selected LatAm and European markets)
- Whether use of customer logos and comparisons with competitors raise endorsement and comparative-advertising issues
- Whether limitations of coverage (what 'bank-level' does and does not cover) must be disclosed

## Open questions

- What specific encryption standards, monitoring scope, incident-response metrics, and certifications does Mosaic Relay currently hold, and do they meet or exceed the baseline security practices of actual banks?
- Which geographic markets will see the campaign (US only, or also selected LatAm and European markets), and do local advertising standards in those jurisdictions treat "bank-level" claims differently?
- Will the campaign use customer logos or make comparisons to named competitors, and if so, what consent and substantiation exists for those endorsements?
- What are the specific limitations of coverage (what "bank-level" does and does not protect) that must be disclosed to avoid misleading the audience?
- Does the current security program documentation include third-party audits, penetration-test results, or compliance certifications (e.g., SOC 2, PCI DSS) that can serve as competent and reliable evidence?
