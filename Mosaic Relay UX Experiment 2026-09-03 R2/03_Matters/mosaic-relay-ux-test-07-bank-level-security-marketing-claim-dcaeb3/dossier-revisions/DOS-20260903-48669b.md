# Matter dossier

## Matter summary

Mosaic Relay Holdings, Inc., a payments-infrastructure provider that is not a bank and does not hold deposits, wants to launch a marketing campaign claiming "bank-level security" and "institution-grade protection" across its website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search ads. The campaign is targeted for launch on 2026-10-03. Counsel is involved because the claims raise substantiation, consumer-perception, and regulatory-classification risks — particularly the risk that the language implies bank status or fund protection that Mosaic Relay does not provide. The geographic scope is unresolved; the company operates in the US plus selected Latin American and European markets, and the intake answer on geographic scope was "Continue with assumptions."

## Decision question

Should legal approve the phrases "bank-level security" and "institution-grade protection" for use in marketing across the US and potentially EU/UK and Latin American markets by 2026-10-03, and if so, what specific qualifiers, substantiation evidence, prohibited language, and review controls must accompany them — or should the campaign instead use only specific, verifiable descriptive security claims to avoid implying bank status or fund protection?

## Material facts

- Marketing wants to advertise Mosaic Relay as providing 'bank-level security' and 'institution-grade protection' for online businesses.
- The campaign would appear on the website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search ads.
- The campaign is planned for launch next month (target date 2026-10-03).
- Mosaic Relay is a payments-infrastructure provider, not a bank, and does not hold deposits or operate as a bank.
- Known facts include current encryption, monitoring, incident response, and certification claims.
- Actors include Marketing, Sales, Product, Security, Compliance, customers, and prospective customers.
- Does the campaign intend to convey that Mosaic Relay itself is a bank or that it holds/protects customer funds, or is the 'bank-level security' language meant purely as a security-strength comparison? — Purely a security-strength comparison; no implication of bank status or fund custody
- What substantiating evidence does Mosaic Relay currently hold for the security claims? (Select all that apply.) — Documented encryption standards (e.g., AES-256, TLS): Documented monitoring and incident-response program
- Which countries or regions are in scope? — Continue with assumptions

## Assumptions

- Mosaic Relay's existing encryption, monitoring, incident response, and certification claims are accurate and current as represented
- The campaign is primarily targeted at US audiences unless geographic scope is expanded
- No prior legal approval or approved wording exists for these phrases

## Issues and workstreams

- Whether 'bank-level security' / 'institution-grade protection' is substantiated by competent and reliable evidence (FTC Act s.5 / state UDAP) for the specific audience, given Mosaic Relay is not a bank
- Whether the security-strength comparison reads to the audience as a security claim rather than an implication of bank status or fund custody (perception risk even though intent is a comparison)
- Whether the claim's permissibility varies by geographic audience (US vs. selected LatAm and European markets)
- Whether use of customer logos and comparisons with competitors raise endorsement and comparative-advertising issues
- Whether limitations of coverage (what 'bank-level' does and does not cover) must be disclosed

## Open questions

- **Which specific countries in Latin America and Europe will the campaign reach?** The intake answer deferred this ("Continue with assumptions"). Financial-promotion and misleading-advertising rules differ materially by jurisdiction, and several EU member states plus the UK impose specific restrictions on claims that could imply regulated-entity status.
- **What certifications does Mosaic Relay actually hold?** The intake response listed "documented encryption standards" and "documented monitoring and incident-response program" but did not confirm any specific certification (e.g., PCI DSS, SOC 2, ISO 27001). The substantiation file cannot be completed without this.
- **Will the campaign use customer logos or name competitors?** This was flagged as a missing fact in the original request and remains unresolved. Logo use raises endorsement/consent issues; competitor comparisons trigger comparative-advertising rules that vary by jurisdiction.
- **What are the specific limitations of coverage?** The request asks what "bank-level" does and does not cover, but no one has yet defined the boundaries of the security claim — what threats, what layers of the stack, what exclusions.
- **Do Mosaic Relay's acquiring-bank and processor contracts restrict or require pre-approval of marketing claims that reference banking or security?** Partner contracts in payments commonly include marketing-review clauses, and a claim referencing "bank-level" anything may trigger those provisions.
