# FTC and state UDAP treatment of 'bank-level security' claims by

## Question

FTC and state UDAP treatment of 'bank-level security' claims by non-bank payment providers

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay Holdings, Inc., a payments-infrastructure provider (not a bank), wants to launch a marketing campaign next month (target 2026-10-03) claiming it provides "bank-level security" and "institution-grade protection." The campaign would run across the website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search ads. Counsel is involved because the company is not a bank, does not hold deposits, and the phrases could imply bank status or fund custody while requiring substantiation under FTC Act § 5 and state UDAP laws. The business team has indicated the intent is purely a security-strength comparison, but the audience perception and evidentiary support remain unverified.
## Decision question
Should legal approve the specific phrases "bank-level security" and "institution-grade protection" for use across website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search ads launching 2026-10-03, and if so, what exact qualifiers, substantiation evidence, review controls, and prohibited language must accompany them to avoid deceptive-practices exposure given that Mosaic Relay is not a bank?
## Open questions
- What specific encryption standards, monitoring scope, incident-response metrics, and certifications does Mosaic Relay currently hold, and do they meet or exceed the baseline security practices of actual banks?
- Which geographic markets will see the campaign (US only, or also selected LatAm and European markets), and do local advertising standards in those jurisdictions treat "bank-level" claims differently?
- Will the campaign use customer logos or make comparisons to named competitors, and if so, what consent and substantiation exists for those endorsements?
- What are the specific limitations of coverage (what "bank-level" does and does not protect) that must be disclosed to avoid misleading the audience?
- Does the current security program documentation include third-party audits, penetration-test results, or compliance certifications (e.g., SOC 2, PCI DSS) that can serve as competent and reliable evidence?

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.**
Based on model knowledge of FTC Act § 5 and state UDAP frameworks:
1. **Substantiation requirement**: Claims must be truthful, non-misleading, and substantiated by competent and reliable evidence before dissemination. "Bank-level security" is an objective claim that implies a specific, measurable standard comparable to what regulated banks maintain.
2. **Implication risk**: Even if intended as a security-strength comparison, the phrase "bank-level" could imply to reasonable consumers that Mosaic Relay is a bank, is regulated like a bank, or protects funds in deposit accounts. This creates deception risk under FTC policy on implied claims.
3. **Audience perception**: The net impression on the target audience (online businesses, marketplaces, platforms) matters. If the audience includes entities that handle consumer funds, the claim could be read as a promise about fund safety, not just data security.
4. **State UDAP overlay**: State unfair-and-deceptive-practices statutes (e.g., California Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200, New York Gen. Bus. Law § 349) mirror FTC standards but may have lower thresholds for consumer harm and allow private rights of action.
5. **Comparative advertising**: If the campaign compares Mosaic Relay to competitors or uses customer logos, additional substantiation and consent requirements apply under FTC endorsement guides and comparative-advertising doctrine.
## Viable paths
**Path A — Approve with heavy qualification and substantiation**
- Permit "bank-level security" only if paired with an immediate, clear qualifier such as "Mosaic Relay is not a bank and does not hold deposits" and a specific description of what the claim covers (e.g., "AES-256 encryption, 24/7 monitoring, SOC 2 Type II certified").
- Require documented evidence for each element: encryption standards, monitoring scope, incident-response SLAs, and current certifications.
- Prohibit standalone use of "bank-level" or "institution-grade" without the qualifier.
- **Risk**: Medium. The qualifier reduces but does not eliminate implication risk.
**Path B — Replace with descriptive, substantiated claims**
- Drop "bank-level" and "institution-grade" entirely.
- Use specific, verifiable statements: "AES-256 encryption at rest and in transit," "SOC 2 Type II certified," "24/7 security monitoring with <15-minute incident-response SLA."
- **Risk**: Low. Eliminates implication and substantiation gaps.
**Path C — Delay launch pending substantiation audit**
- Postpone the 2026-10-03 launch until Security and Compliance produce a complete evidence file for each claim element.
- **Risk**: Low legal risk, but high business cost (missed launch window).
## Facts that could change the answer
- If Mosaic Relay holds a current SOC 2 Type II report, PCI DSS Level 1 certification, or equivalent third-party audit that explicitly benchmarks against banking-industry security standards, Path A becomes more viable.
- If the campaign will run in jurisdictions with stricter advertising standards (e.g., certain EU member states under the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive), Path B becomes strongly preferable.
- If customer logos or competitor comparisons are planned, additional consent and substantiation requirements could make Path A impractical within the timeline.
- If the sales team has already used "bank-level" in customer contracts or pitches, there may be existing exposure that requires remediation regardless of the path chosen.
## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Evidence file**: Security and Compliance should produce a one-page substantiation memo listing each security control, the evidence supporting it (audit reports, penetration-test summaries, certification letters), and the date of last verification.
2. **Audience-perception check**: Run a quick internal review with 3–5 non-legal employees from Sales and Support: show them the proposed claim and ask what they think it means. If any interpret it as "Mosaic Relay is a bank" or "your funds are protected like a bank deposit," the implication risk is confirmed.
3. **Geographic scope**: Confirm with Marketing whether the campaign will be geo-targeted or globally visible. If global, flag for local counsel review in target LatAm and European markets.
4. **Competitor and logo use**: Obtain written confirmation from Marketing that no customer logos or competitor names will be used without separate legal review.
5. **Record approved wording**: Once a path is chosen, save the exact approved phrases, required qualifiers, and prohibited language in the matter workspace under `recommendations.md` and link to the substantiation memo.
