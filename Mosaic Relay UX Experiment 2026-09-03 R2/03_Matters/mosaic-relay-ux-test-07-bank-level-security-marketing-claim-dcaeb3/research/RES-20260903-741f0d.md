# Regulatory guidance on language implying bank status or deposit protection

## Question

Regulatory guidance on language implying bank status or deposit protection by non-banks

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
Mosaic Relay Holdings, Inc. — a payments-infrastructure provider that is not a bank and does not hold deposits — wants to launch a marketing campaign next month (target 2026-10-03) claiming it provides "bank-level security" and "institution-grade protection." The campaign would run across the website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search ads, reaching customers and prospective customers. Counsel is involved because the phrases may imply bank status or fund protection that Mosaic Relay cannot support, and because each phrase needs competent and reliable substantiation before publication. The business team has confirmed the intent is a security-strength comparison only, not an implication of bank status or fund custody.
## Decision question
Should legal approve "bank-level security" and "institution-grade protection" for the 2026-10-03 launch across website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search — and if so, with what qualifiers, substantiation evidence, and prohibited-language list — given that Mosaic Relay is not a bank, does not hold deposits, and currently holds documented encryption standards and a documented monitoring/incident-response program but no completed substantiation file for either phrase?
## Open questions
- What specific substantiating evidence exists for each phrase — encryption standards, monitoring scope, incident-response metrics, and any certifications actually held — and is it current and verified?
- What is the primary geographic audience? If the campaign reaches EU/UK or Latin American markets, local advertising-standards and financial-promotion rules may impose different or stricter requirements.
- Will customer logos be used, and with what consent? Will the campaign compare Mosaic Relay to named competitors?
- What limitations of coverage exist (what "bank-level" does and does not cover) that would need disclosure?
- Do Mosaic Relay's acquiring-bank and processor contracts restrict or require approval for security-related marketing claims?

## Likely rules and issues
**No external authority retrieved.**
The analysis below is model-only analysis based on the matter file and general legal knowledge. It is not retrieved authority.
### 1. FTC Act § 5 — deceptive acts or practices (US)
The core federal issue. A representation is deceptive if it is likely to mislead a reasonable consumer and is material. Two distinct deception risks:
- **Unsubstantiated objective claim.** "Bank-level security" is an objective claim that requires a reasonable basis — competent and reliable evidence — before dissemination. If Mosaic Relay cannot show its controls meet or exceed the security baseline of actual banks, the claim is deceptive regardless of intent.
- **Implied claim of bank status or fund protection.** Even if the intended meaning is a security-strength comparison, the phrase "institution-grade protection" and the word "bank" can convey an implied message that Mosaic Relay is a bank, is regulated as one, or protects customer funds. The FTC evaluates net impression, not intent. Because Mosaic Relay does not hold deposits and is not a bank, this implied claim is likely false and material.
### 2. State UDAP statutes
Every state has an unfair-and-deceptive-acts-and-practices statute. State attorneys general and private plaintiffs can challenge unsubstantiated or misleading security claims. Some states (e.g., California, New York) are particularly active on fintech marketing that blurs the bank/non-bank line.
### 3. Banking-regulator guidance on non-bank use of "bank"
Federal and state banking regulators (FDIC, state banking departments) have taken the position that non-banks may not use the word "bank" or related terminology in a way that suggests they are a bank or that deposits are FDIC-insured. The FDIC has issued specific guidance and taken enforcement action against non-bank fintechs for implying deposit-insurance coverage. "Bank-level security" is a comparison, not a direct claim of bank status, but "institution-grade protection" comes closer to implying regulated-institution status and fund safety.
### 4. FTC Endorsement Guides and comparative advertising
If the campaign uses customer logos, that is an endorsement requiring consent and reflecting the honest experience of the endorser. If it compares Mosaic Relay to named competitors, comparative-advertising rules require substantiation for the comparison and prohibit disparagement that is false or misleading.
### 5. Geographic variation
If the campaign reaches EU/UK audiences, the UK Financial Conduct Authority's financial-promotions regime and EU consumer-protection directives impose stricter rules on claims by non-bank payment firms. Several EU member states restrict the use of banking terminology by non-banks. Latin American markets vary. The current assumption is a US-primary audience, but this needs confirmation.
### 6. Contractual restrictions
Acquiring-bank and processor agreements often contain marketing-approval clauses, brand guidelines, and restrictions on security claims. Card-network rules (Visa, Mastercard) also impose requirements on how partners describe security and compliance. These are contractual, not regulatory, but breach can trigger termination or fines.

## Viable paths
### Path A — Approve "bank-level security" with mandatory qualifiers (medium risk)
Approve the phrase only as a qualified security-strength comparison, contingent on:
1. **Completed substantiation file** showing Mosaic Relay's controls meet or exceed the security baseline of actual banks — encryption, monitoring, incident response, and any certification — verified current by Security and Compliance.
2. **Mandatory qualifier** in every use: "Mosaic Relay is not a bank and does not hold deposits or customer funds," plus a specific description of what the claim covers and a limitations-of-coverage statement.
3. **Prohibited language**: no "institution-grade protection," no fund-protection or deposit-insurance implication, no standalone "bank-level" without the qualifier, no unheld certification, no competitor comparison or customer logo without consent and substantiation.
4. **Review controls**: audience-perception check, geographic-scope confirmation (local review if EU/UK reached), acquiring-bank/processor contract review, approved wording recorded and distributed, re-verification trigger on any security change or annually.
### Path B — Replace with specific, verifiable descriptive claims (low risk; recommended default)
Drop both phrases. Use specific, verifiable claims instead: "AES-256 encryption at rest and in transit," "24/7 monitoring," "[certification held]." This eliminates the implication risk and the substantiation gap. It is the lowest-risk path and the recommended default.
### Path C — Delay launch pending substantiation audit (low legal risk, high business cost)
Hold the campaign until Security and Compliance complete a full substantiation audit and the lawyer approves final wording. This protects against launching with unsubstantiated claims but misses the 2026-10-03 target.

## Facts that could change the answer
- **Substantiation file contents.** If the evidence shows Mosaic Relay's controls do not meet or exceed actual bank baselines, Path A fails and Path B or C is required.
- **Geographic scope.** If the campaign reaches EU/UK or Latin America, additional local rules apply and may prohibit the phrases entirely or require local legal review.
- **Customer logos or competitor comparisons.** If either is planned, additional consent, endorsement, and comparative-advertising requirements attach.
- **Acquiring-bank/processor contract terms.** If contracts restrict security claims or require partner approval, Path A may be contractually barred even if legally permissible.
- **Certifications actually held.** If Mosaic Relay holds a recognized certification (e.g., PCI DSS Level 1, SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001), that strengthens substantiation for specific claims but does not cure the implied-bank-status problem of "institution-grade protection."

3. **Geographic-scope confirmation** from Marketing on where the campaign will run, with local counsel review if EU/UK or Latin America is in scope.
4. **Contract review** of acquiring-bank, processor, and card-network agreements for marketing-approval or security-claim restrictions.
