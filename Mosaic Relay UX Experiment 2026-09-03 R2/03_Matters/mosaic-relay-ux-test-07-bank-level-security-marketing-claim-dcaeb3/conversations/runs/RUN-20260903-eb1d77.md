    ## Substantive assessment

    **Bottom line:** "Bank-level security" is potentially supportable **only as a
    qualified security-strength comparison** backed by competent and reliable evidence.
    "Institution-grade protection" should **not** be approved — it reads as fund protection
    and regulated-institution status, which Mosaic Relay (a non-bank that doesn''t
    hold deposits) cannot support. The safer default is to replace both phrases with
    specific, verifiable descriptive claims.

    ### Why the claim is risky

    - **Substantiation.** "Bank-level security" is an objective, measurable claim.
    Under FTC Act § 5 and state UDAP, it must be substantiated by competent and reliable
    evidence *before* dissemination — and the evidence must show Mosaic Relay''s controls
    **meet or exceed the security baseline of actual banks**, not merely that it has
    "good" security. You currently hold documented encryption standards and a monitoring/incident-response
    program, but **no certification (SOC 2, ISO 27001, PCI DSS) is confirmed**. That''s
    the gap that decides whether the claim is supportable at all.

    - **Net-impression risk.** Even though the intent is a pure security comparison,
    the FTC evaluates what the audience actually understands. "Bank-level" can imply
    Mosaic Relay is a bank, is regulated like a bank, or protects customer funds in
    deposit accounts — which would be affirmatively misleading. This must be tested,
    not assumed away.

    - **"Institution-grade protection" is worse.** "Protection" in a payments context
    reads as protection of funds, and "institution" implies a regulated financial
    institution. It''s harder to qualify into a safe security-only claim.

    ### Publication surfaces identified

    Website, sales materials, partner presentations, and paid search ads (confirmed),
    plus potential social, email, case studies/testimonials, in-product messaging,
    and PR. Each has different qualifier and control requirements — paid search is
    character-limited, so the qualifier may need to live on the landing page; partner
    presentations need contractual control over republication.

    ### Approved wording (conditional on substantiation)

    > "Mosaic Relay provides bank-level security for your payment data — AES-256 encryption
    at rest and in transit, 24/7 monitoring, and [certification]. Mosaic Relay is
    a payments-infrastructure provider, not a bank, and does not hold deposits or
    customer funds."

    **Required qualifiers:** the non-bank/no-deposits statement, a specific description
    of what the claim covers, and a limitations-of-coverage disclosure.

    **Prohibited language:** "institution-grade protection," any fund-protection or
    deposit-insurance implication, standalone "bank-level" without the qualifier,
    any unheld certification, and any competitor comparison or customer logo without
    consent/substantiation.
