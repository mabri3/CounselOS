# US payments, consumer-protection, contract, and operational risks for a split-pa

## Question

US payments, consumer-protection, contract, and operational risks for a split-payment partial-refund launch. Cover card versus ACH refund timing, refund disclosures, seller notices, allocation and fee reversal rules, negative balances, chargeback interaction, ledger controls, support overrides, tax treatment, and the source of truth for disputed allocations. State what must be verified before launch and do not invent citations.

## Working Analysis

**No external authority retrieved**

## Matter summary
A marketplace customer of Mosaic Relay wants to launch a tool that lets its operators issue partial refunds on orders paid with multiple payment methods (card and ACH) and split among multiple sellers, with automated allocation, platform-fee reversal, and status updates to buyers and sellers. Mosaic Relay would provide the refund API and ledger infrastructure; the marketplace operator makes refund decisions; sellers receive adjusted payouts. Counsel is involved because the tool touches funds flow, consumer refund timing, seller contract terms (currently silent), chargeback interaction, and ledger integrity — and seller terms must be amended before the behavior is contractually supported. Product wants to ship in one quarter.
## Decision question
What refund rules, buyer-facing disclosures, timing commitments, ledger controls, seller notice requirements, and support-override approval gates must legal require before Mosaic Relay ships a US-only split-payment partial-refund tool in one quarter — given that seller terms are currently silent on refunds and fee reversal, and that a wrong allocation or double-recovery between refunds and chargebacks creates direct financial exposure for Mosaic Relay and its marketplace customer?
## Open questions
- **Tax treatment of refunded amounts and reversed platform fees** — no intake answer addresses whether refund events trigger corrected tax documents (1099-K adjustments for sellers, sales-tax refund mechanics). This could change ledger design and seller notice content.
- **Whether Mosaic Relay's money-transmission characterization shifts** when it calculates and directs multi-seller refund allocations rather than merely passing through a single merchant refund instruction. This is a perimeter question that could change licensing analysis.
- **ACH return windows versus refund windows** — the intake answer says "5–7 business days" for refunds, but ACH returns (unauthorized debit claims) operate on a different timeline (60 days from statement for consumers under NACHA rules). Whether the tool must distinguish between a voluntary refund and an ACH return is unresolved.
- **What happens when a partial refund is issued but the chargeback was already filed for the full amount** — the intake answer says "a refund reduces or closes the dispute amount," but the operational mechanics of notifying the card network and preventing double-recovery are not yet specified.
- **Whether the marketplace's buyer-facing refund policy** (deadlines, conditions, method-of-refund rules) already exists and complies with applicable state refund-disclosure statutes, or whether legal must draft it as part of this launch.

## Likely rules and issues
### Payments and funds-flow
**Card refunds.** Card network rules (Visa, Mastercard) require that refunds be processed to the original card and generally prohibit refunding to a different payment method. For split-tender orders (part card, part ACH), the tool must route each refund component back to the originating payment method. Network rules also impose timing expectations — issuers typically must post credits within a few business days of receiving the refund, but the marketplace's promise to the buyer ("5–7 business days") is a customer-service commitment, not a network mandate. The risk is that a disclosed timeline becomes a deceptive-practice issue if the tool cannot consistently meet it.
**ACH refunds.** ACH "refunds" are not a native NACHA concept the way card credits are. Reversing an ACH debit requires either a reversal (within 5 banking days of settlement, limited to erroneous entries) or a new ACH credit transaction. If the tool treats ACH refunds as simple reversals beyond the 5-day window, that is a NACHA rules violation. The tool likely needs to originate a separate ACH credit for the buyer's refunded portion, which means Mosaic Relay or its partner must have origination capability and the buyer's account details must be retained or re-collected.
**Money transmission perimeter.** Mosaic Relay's assumed position is that it is a technology provider, not a money transmitter. When Mosaic Relay's software calculates each seller's share of a refund and directs funds movement across multiple parties, the analysis of whether it is "receiving money for transmission" or acting as an agent of a payee/exempt entity becomes more fact-intensive. This is a material assumption that must be verified against the actual funds-flow architecture (who holds funds at each step, whose account debits and credits hit, and what the partner bank agreements say).
### Consumer protection
**Refund disclosures.** Several states (e.g., California, New York, Florida) have statutes requiring sellers to disclose refund/return policies or post conspicuous notices. If the marketplace's buyer-facing terms do not already address partial refunds on multi-seller orders, the tool's launch should be gated on updated buyer-facing disclosures. The FTC Act's prohibition on deceptive practices applies if the marketplace promises a refund timeline it cannot meet or if the allocation logic produces outcomes buyers would not expect (e.g., refunding to a different payment method than the one used).
**Timing promises.** The intake answer commits to "standard card-network/ACH refund windows (e.g., 5–7 business days)." Legal should require that this promise be (a) disclosed clearly at the point of refund initiation, (b) operationally achievable given partner processing times, and (c) tracked with exception reporting so that missed timelines trigger proactive buyer communication.
### Contract
**Seller terms are silent.** This is the most concrete launch blocker. The tool will debit seller balances, reverse platform fees, and potentially hold future payouts — none of which is currently authorized by the seller agreement. Required amendments:
- Authorization to debit seller balances (including future payouts) for refund allocations
- Fee reversal mechanics (prorated to refunded amount, per intake answer)
- Notice requirements (timing, content, and method of refund notifications to sellers)
- Negative-balance handling (seller obligation to restore a negative balance, Mosaic Relay's right to offset against future payouts, and any cap or time limit on recovery)
- Dispute-resolution process for allocation disagreements
**Marketplace operator terms.** The marketplace's agreement with Mosaic Relay should specify that the marketplace operator (not Mosaic Relay) is responsible for refund decisions, buyer communications, and compliance with consumer-protection disclosure requirements. Mosaic Relay's role should be defined as infrastructure provider executing instructions.
### Operational and ledger controls
**Source of truth.** The intake answer establishes the immutable order ledger with a versioned allocation snapshot at capture as the source of truth. This is the right architecture. Legal should require:
- Every refund event references the original allocation snapshot by version identifier
- No support edit may overwrite the original allocation — adjustments only via approved reconciliation records
- Append-only event log with actor, timestamp, and approval chain for every refund, adjustment, and override
**Negative seller balances.** The intake answer allows negative balances with future-payout holds. Legal should require:
- A disclosed cap or threshold beyond which the marketplace (not Mosaic Relay) bears the credit risk
- A time limit after which unrecovered negative balances are written off or escalated
- Seller notice when a balance goes negative due to a refund
**Support overrides.** The intake answer allows overrides only with a named approver. Legal should require:
- A defined approver role (not just "manager" — a specific title or team)
- Written rationale recorded at the time of override
- Periodic audit of override frequency and patterns
- Overrides cannot modify the original allocation snapshot — they create a new versioned event
**Chargeback interaction.** The intake answer says a refund reduces or closes the dispute amount. Legal should require:
- Automated notification to the card network when a partial refund is issued against a disputed transaction
- A control preventing the buyer from receiving both a full chargeback credit and a partial refund for the same line items (double-recovery)
- Clear rules for which event takes precedence when a chargeback and a refund are in flight simultaneously
### Tax
Unaddressed in intake. Partial refunds and fee reversals may affect 1099-K reporting for sellers (gross amount vs. net), sales-tax refund obligations (if the marketplace is the seller of record or a marketplace facilitator under state law), and Mosaic Relay's own revenue recognition. This needs a tax workstream before launch.
---
## Viable paths
**Path A — Launch with contract amendments and controls in place (recommended).** Amend seller terms, build the ledger controls described above, disclose refund timing to buyers, and gate launch on the tax analysis. This is achievable in one quarter if the seller-term amendment and the ledger-control build start immediately.
**Path B — Pilot with a limited seller cohort.** Launch with a small group of sellers who sign the amended terms, limiting blast radius while the full seller base is migrated. This reduces contractual risk but does not eliminate the consumer-protection or tax questions.
**Path C — Delay launch until all open questions are resolved.** Safest legally but likely misses the one-quarter target. Appropriate only if the money-transmission perimeter analysis or the tax analysis reveals a structural problem.

## Facts that could change the answer
- If Mosaic Relay (not the partner bank) touches funds at any point in the refund flow, the money-transmission analysis changes materially.
- If the marketplace is a "marketplace facilitator" for sales-tax purposes in any state, the refund tool must handle tax-adjustment events, which adds build scope.
- If any seller agreements already contain refund or setoff language (even if the intake answer says "silent"), the amendment scope changes.
- If the ACH refund mechanism requires originating new credits (rather than reversing debits), Mosaic Relay needs ODFI relationship confirmation and buyer account-detail retention, which adds compliance scope.
- If the marketplace's buyer base includes consumers in states with specific refund-disclosure statutes (e.g., California's § 1723), the disclosure requirements are more prescriptive.

## Recommended last-mile verification
1. **Funds-flow mapping** — trace every dollar in a partial-refund scenario from buyer payment method through Mosaic Relay, partner bank, and seller payout accounts. Confirm who holds funds at each step and whether Mosaic Relay's role stays within the technology-provider characterization.
2. **NACHA rules check** — confirm with the ACH partner whether the refund mechanism is a reversal, a return, or a new credit origination, and what timing and authorization rules apply to each.
3. **Card network refund rules** — confirm with the acquiring partner the specific Visa/Mastercard requirements for partial refunds on split-tender transactions, including any prohibition on refunding to a different payment method.
4. **State refund-disclosure statutes** — identify which states' statutes apply based on the marketplace's buyer base and confirm the buyer-facing refund policy meets the most restrictive applicable standard.
5. **Tax analysis** — engage tax counsel or the finance team to confirm 1099-K adjustment mechanics, sales-tax refund handling, and revenue-recognition treatment of reversed platform fees.
6. **Seller agreement audit** — pull the actual seller agreement text to confirm it is truly silent on refunds, fee reversal, setoff, and negative balances, and to identify the amendment mechanism (unilateral notice vs. affirmative consent).

## What would change this
- **Working assumption — Mosaic Relay never takes possession or control of funds.** The entire money-transmission and regulatory-perimeter analysis leans on this. If the funds-flow mapping (verification item 1) shows Mosaic Relay's name on any account that holds settlement funds, even briefly, the licensing analysis must be redone and Path A's timeline is at risk.
- **Open fork — ACH refund mechanism.** If the ACH partner confirms that refunds beyond the 5-day reversal window require new credit originations (likely), the tool needs buyer account-detail retention and ODFI origination capability. If the partner instead offers a proprietary "refund" rail that handles this, the build scope shrinks but the partner contract must be reviewed for liability allocation. Both paths are viable; they lead to different build timelines and different compliance checklists.
- **Open fork — Marketplace facilitator status.** If the marketplace is a facilitator for sales-tax purposes in any state, the refund tool must generate tax-adjustment events and the seller notice must include tax-line detail. If it is not, the tax workstream is limited to 1099-K and revenue recognition. This fork changes both build scope and seller-notice content.
- **Not examined — the actual seller agreement text.** The intake answer says terms are "silent," but no one has read the agreement. If it contains a broad setoff or operational-changes clause, the amendment may be simpler than assumed. If it contains an affirmative-consent requirement for amendments, the seller-cohort migration timeline changes.
- **Not examined — state money-transmission statutes beyond the federal framing.** The analysis assumes a US-centric perimeter question but does not analyze any specific state's money-transmitter licensing statute. If the funds-flow mapping raises a perimeter question, state-by-state analysis (starting with the states where Mosaic Relay has the most transaction volume) would be required.
- **Not examined — the marketplace's existing buyer-facing refund policy.** The consumer-protection analysis assumes the marketplace may not have adequate refund disclosures, but the actual policy text has not been reviewed. If it already covers partial refunds and multi-seller orders, the disclosure workstream shrinks significantly.
